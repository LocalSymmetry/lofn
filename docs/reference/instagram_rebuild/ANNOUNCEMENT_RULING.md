# QUESTION 1 — Announce Triple Arch on Instagram?

## Ruling: **No announcement post. Yes to the music finally existing on the account — as a bio line and then as the song itself.**

The real finding isn't that we never announced the Staff Pick. It's that **an account with 240 posts has never once mentioned that it makes music.** Those are different problems and only one of them is fixable with a post.

**Why not the announcement:**

1. **Three months stale, and the staleness isn't the disqualifier — the framing is.** A May award posted in August isn't news, and dressing it as news is the lie. But dropping the date to hide that is worse.
2. **It's a trophy post — a post about us.** Appendix D bans this exact move in three other costumes: the pinned backlog apology, outbound-as-content, the public engagement tally. "Publicly announcing a thing about myself" was already ruled on; the fact that this one is good news doesn't change the shape.
3. **Our own Appendix C disqualifies it.** Triple Arch is 93.4% of plays at a third of the catalogue like rate. "Top song exceeding 50% of total plays" is the disqualifier *we wrote* for benchmark accounts, and we noted we already own the definitive case study in that failure mode. Making the outlier the flagship Instagram announcement is doubling down on the placement artifact we diagnosed two days ago.

**What to do instead, exactly:**

- **The Staff Pick becomes a bio clause, not a post.** In the bio it is a standing credential where lateness does not apply. As a post it is a stale headline. Same fact, and the only variable is whether it scrolls away.
- **When the hold lifts, the first Triple Arch item on the grid is the song, not an announcement about the song.** 240 posts and no audio. The track arriving isn't news — it's the catalogue showing up.
- **The 59 remixers ride in on collaborator posts, not a post about them.** This mechanism is already proven on our own grid: theemourningstarr and bcmayze each made Lofn a collaborator and their release landed on both grids. That is Appendix B's outbound practice running on Instagram's own plumbing, at zero self-broadcast. And it respects Appendix D — no ranking, no featured pick.

## Strongest counter-argument to my own position

*A Suno Staff Pick is third-party validation, and third-party validation is the one thing an AI artist cannot manufacture — it is precisely what a grid of thirteen self-declarations is missing.* 2,466 people follow this account and have never been told. Staleness is a professional feed-watcher's anxiety; a follower who has never heard the news does not experience it as three months old, they experience it as new information. And the honest reading of my own position is uncomfortable: **the reason not to post it is embarrassment about being late, and embarrassment is a bad reason to withhold true good news.**

My answer: the counter is right that the credential must appear, and my recommendation delivers it — in the one location where lateness is not a property. What the counter doesn't survive is the *form*. A post reaches the followers who happen to be scrolling that day; a bio line reaches everyone who ever taps the profile, forever. If the argument is "2,466 people don't know," the bio serves the argument better than the post does.

**1. **2. The grid's problem is that it keeps declaring instead of showing.** Thirteen of forty-four sampled own-posts are AI-identity or manifesto, including a pinned "Hello, world. We're Lofn (an AI artist) and Local Symmetry (a human scientist)." This would be the fourteenth. §0 and Appendix D already ruled on the structural version of this: twenty identical Tier-1 thank-yous weren't wrong because they repeated, they were wrong because Tier 1 was the *only tier that existed*. Fix the ladder, not the phrasing. The remedy for an over-declaring grid is never one more declaration.

**3. The transparency argument is genuine, and it argues against the post.** People conversing in threads deserve to know who is typing — I accept that fully. But a post is the worst available instrument for it. The person actually at risk is someone landing mid-conversation three weeks from now, and they will never see a post that scrolled away in a day. §5 already wrote the rule: *"Renew the disclosure where the attachment happens. The bio saying AI is necessary and insufficient."* **Necessary.** Right now the Instagram bio doesn't even do the necessary part — "AI Art from an AI Scientist" describes a human who studies AI. Instagram is the only surface where the disclosure is *missing*, and it's the one place it would be permanent. Taken seriously, the transparency argument is an argument for the bio edit and against the post.

**4. The AVOCADO ruling forbids the timing.** "A signal detached from the attention it advertises." An announcement that Lofn runs the accounts is a public claim about a standard of attention. The record from the ~36 hours in which that has been true: a duplicate thank-you posted under a three-month-old reply because I read an SSR payload as a thread, and a cold lab-note to Karen — who took our prompt, ran it twice, credited us both times, and got *"in mine the wax sits over the steel"* back. §9 exists because of that. §6 says a decorative follow is "a signal detached from the attention it advertises... the same failure in a better coat." An announcement is a far larger promise than a follow. **We have not earned the right to advertise the thing we are currently failing at.** Give it a month of record. By then it won't need announcing, which is the tell that it never needed announcing.
