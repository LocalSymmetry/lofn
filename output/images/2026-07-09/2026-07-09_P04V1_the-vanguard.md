# Passage Granted

## Generation Prompt

A hand-tinted mezzotint rocked to velvet black, its dark laid down before its light: a barnacled gray whale bull crests the slate roofline of an East German terraced mining street at mid-eclipse, the sun a thin burnished ring in an empty silver sky band. Light lives only where the plate is burnished: saffron on his jaw and fluke, the ring, two lamplit windows, and a brass ticket-punch caught mid-fall beside a tram conductor, fifty-two, sturdy in high-collared wool, face three-quarter upward, both hands open. Prussian wash sits in the rocker burr; eclipse shadow bands rake the cobbles. Barnacle clusters on his brow hold pinpoint refractive star-fields, the sharpest passage in the print. Pixel-sorted filigree streams along the pod's path behind him; brine drips from his fluke, darkening the cobblestones. At the street's end the harbor lies dry, boats keeled over. The street's first yes in forty years.

## Composition Notes

Single crest breaking the roofline — pure silhouette economy; the burnish-law clause ("light lives only where the plate is burnished") makes the light discipline explicit to the renderer. Verify: one whale over the roofline, silver ring/sky band reads, figure counter-placed lower third. Lowest-risk roll in the run.
