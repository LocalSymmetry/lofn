# Six Hundred Miles, Arrived

## Generation Prompt

Verre églomisé reverse-painted glass, palladium leaf verso deep beneath the sea, gold verso under one kiln-warm ember lantern, frames an adobe courtyard's second door in carved color planes, holding the Pacific to its horizon: a windless vertical face of water, still as poured glass, taller than the woman before it, no crest, no spray, not spilling, horizon high inside the doorframe. One white line of foam along the doorsill grain does the work of the whole wave. Esperanza, 68, Nuevomexicana weaver in black mourning gloves, dark wool dress, handwoven shawl, stands small in the lower third, face three-quarter, a letter loosening from her gloved fingers. Suspended galaxies hang in the sea-wall's teal depth; holographic sheen rides only the seam where water meets sill, edges crisp as knife cuts. The second chair sits empty; the door's paint half-scraped. Storm-held sky bare above. Six hundred miles stood up to greet her.
