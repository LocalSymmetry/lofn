# 01 — The Bearing Line

## Generation Prompt

An architectural assemblage portrait in matte ash-casein plaster, charred timber, blackened copper wire, crackle-glazed celadon ceramic, brocade selvage, bone linen, and dull pewter stress foil holds a compressed room under visible load. An invented 46-year-old brown-skinned craftswoman with broad shoulders, close-cropped salt-and-pepper curls, one white forelock at her left temple, a long angular face, deep-set dark eyes, and a plain bone-linen work dress with a single black-and-celadon cuff on her left wrist. She sits in profile beneath one black brace aligned with her spine, left shoulder raised under a heavy copper arc while both hands tighten one palm-sized eyelet. Four endpoint plates remain visible but slack at the frame corners. Unequal nested loops return toward the brace; a bowed ceiling seam and rubbed plaster prove weight. Dry side light from the left cuts charcoal, copper, celadon, and bone. Her neutral mouth and lifted ribs show practiced effort.

## Composition Notes

Compressed profile, one shared vertical bearing line, minimal headroom. The adult and the black brace must read before the wire detail. This is the chronological opening, not the separate promotional cover.

## Continuity Locks

Brace intact and loaded; all four endpoints present but slack; left cuff at the active eyelet; shoulders and ribs lifted.

## Bold Choice

The room’s beautiful support system visibly depends on one ordinary adult posture.

## Reject If

The brace reads as décor, the wire forms a halo or cocoon, or the endpoints disappear.
