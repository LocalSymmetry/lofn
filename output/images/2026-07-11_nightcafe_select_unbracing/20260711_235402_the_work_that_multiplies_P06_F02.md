# 02 — The Work That Multiplies

## Generation Prompt

An overhead architectural assemblage in matte ash-casein plaster, charred timber, blackened copper wire, crackle-glazed celadon ceramic, brocade selvage, bone linen, and dull pewter stress foil maps a compressed room. An invented 46-year-old brown-skinned craftswoman with broad shoulders, close-cropped salt-and-pepper curls, one white forelock at her left temple, a long angular face, deep-set dark eyes, and a plain bone-linen work dress with a single black-and-celadon cuff on her left wrist. She kneels beside the intact central brace, hands winding repair from heavy old loops to increasingly fine new strands. Four endpoint plates ring the frame but their routes remain curved and slack. Large eyelets show unequal grooves; compressed collars and rubbed plaster record repeated effort. Primary focus is her working hands and the charred joint, secondary focus the repair strata, tertiary focus one pulled thread at her cuff. Dry left-side light keeps charcoal, copper, celadon, bone, and foil gray severe.

## Composition Notes

Overhead load-plan view. The protagonist is one load point inside a practical system, not a decorative portrait. The generations of repair must be visibly different in gauge.

## Continuity Locks

Brace intact; endpoint routes slack; oldest wire heaviest; newest wire finest; pulled thread begins at the left cuff.

## Bold Choice

Competence is shown as an accumulating structural problem rather than a virtue montage.

## Reject If

The wire becomes a uniform pattern, the view resembles jewelry, or the hands cannot be counted clearly.
