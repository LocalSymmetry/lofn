## PAIR PREAMBLE — the two things I had to solve before any step could run

**(a) A lithophane wants to be flat. A carried lamp wants to be round.**
The whole material argument dies on a cylinder: a portrait wrapped around a tube is illegible at
every angle and catastrophic at 16px. So the lamp is not a tube. It is a **faceted sleeve** — five
flat pressed plaques joined at hard vertical arrises around a squat foot. One broad facet faces the
viewer square-on and carries the picture; the next runs away into the dark and proves the object has
volume. The arrises are exactly where a two-part plaster mould leaves its seam, so the geometry that
saves legibility is the same geometry that carries the Mould Seam flair. This is the pair's single
biggest structural decision and it is recorded here, before step 06, because every later step assumes
it.

**(b) My arm is ACCESSIBLE and my axis draw is cold.**
`parian` + `a cold electric filament, too blue for the clay` is the intense palette by any plain
reading, and the ACCESSIBLE arm is defined in `core_seed.md` as bone-china warmth, candle-amber,
*light as gift*. **I am not going to pretend that friction is not there.** My resolution — and I
believe it, it is not a dodge — is that this pair earns the warm arm along a **time axis instead of a
hue axis**. The lamp is cold and clinical when it is full and it warms as it dies. The gift arrives
at the end, and it costs everything to reach. The four variations therefore run a hard colour arc:

> **cold blue-white → the stepped turn → all amber → grey shadow.**

That is *light as gift* stated literally rather than tastefully, which is precisely what the Context
Skeptic demanded. See step 09 for the bench's ruling on whether this survives.

## THE SIX TERRACES — this pair's fixed arithmetic

Published once here so all four prompts obey one number (the Medium Skeptic's exact demand: *pick
one number, publish it, and every part obeys it*).

**Index convention, corridor-wide:** T1 is the **thickest and darkest** rung and T6 is the
**thinnest and brightest**, the same direction P1, P4, P5 and P6 publish. The millimetre scale below
is this pair's own; only the direction is shared.

| rung | wall thickness | behaviour when driven | behaviour when dying |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | ~6.0 mm | opaque, dead black | always black |
| **T2** | ~5.2 mm | legible, near-black | dies first |
| **T3** | ~4.4 mm | legible | dies second |
| **T4** | ~3.6 mm | legible | dies third |
| **T5** | ~2.8 mm | second to fuse | dies fourth |
| **T6** | ~2.0 mm | first to fuse into flat white | **last to stay alive** |

**The consequence I built the whole pair on:** T6 is the extreme at *both* ends. The thinnest carve
is the first thing a full lamp burns away and the last thing a dying lamp still shows. So the same
detail is invisible when the lamp is new and is the only thing visible when the lamp is finished.
That is "both extremes are blindness" turned into a story beat rather than a caption.

**What T6 carries:** the carver's hands, and the small faceted lamp she is holding in them.
You cannot see what she is holding when your lamp is full. You see nothing else when it is nearly out.

**The remaining amount, without a meter:** you read the charge by **how many rungs of her portrait
are still alive.** Full = the two thinnest rungs have fused and the picture is over-driven. Half = all six
present, the correct exposure. Nearly gone = one rung. Dead = none, and the object is now only its own
shadow. There is no bar, no number, no icon anywhere in this pack. The gauge is the picture of a
woman going out.

# STEP 06 — JUDGING FACETS

Five facets against which every P2 prompt is scored. Sensory, and each one is failable.

1. **The thickness is doing the work.** The picture must be visibly *cut into a wall that has a
   measurable depth* — you can count the shelves with your eye and read the object's edge as a rim of
   real clay. If the picture could have been silkscreened onto the outside of the sleeve, the prompt
   has failed the first facet.
2. **Brightness arrives in rungs with hard tops.** Between any two adjacent light values there is a
   crisp step you could catch a fingernail on. No value slides into its neighbour anywhere in the
   frame, including at the object's silhouette, where the emission stops dead at the clay and the
   surrounding field records nothing.
3. **The object is dying and you can tell how far along it is without being told.** A viewer with no
   caption must be able to rank two frames of this lamp by remaining charge in under two seconds,
   using only how much of the woman's face is still alive.
4. **The damage is load-bearing, not decorative.** The iron staples, the seam fin, the worn-mould
   softness — each must destroy or protect a *specific* piece of the picture. A scuff added for
   texture's sake is the Concept Skeptic's craft-as-alibi and is rejected on sight.
5. **It is parian, in the hand, at phone size.** Chalky, dry, faintly sugary, unglazed, absolutely
   nothing wet or metallic-specular on the porcelain; and at 16px it still reads as *a small pale
   object with a lit picture inside it,* silhouette held by five flat facets and a squat foot.

{ "facets": [
  "The picture is legibly cut INTO a wall of measurable depth — the shelves are countable by eye and the rim of real clay is visible at the object's edge.",
  "Every change in brightness is a hard-topped rung with a crisp step between it and its neighbour, and the emission stops dead at the clay's silhouette.",
  "Remaining charge is readable in under two seconds from how much of the carver's face is still alive, with no bar, number or icon present anywhere.",
  "Each flaw — iron staples, mould-seam fin, worn-mould softness — destroys or protects one named piece of the picture rather than decorating the surface.",
  "The body reads as dry unglazed parian, chalky and faintly sugary with no specular point, and holds its five-facet silhouette at 16 pixels."

# STEP 09 — THE BENCH (artist-refined pass)

Three seats from the supplied Panel Ledger. No new panel invented.

**THE THICKNESS (after the Wedgwood lithophane/jasperware lineage) — on V1.**
*Working from the documented thin-body and relief record:* the draft is correct about the thing most
people get wrong. A new lithophane driven hard is **worse**, not better, and the loss lands on the
finest carve first. Hold the fusing to exactly the top two rungs — if three fuse, the sleeve reads as
a plain white lamp and the pair has no picture at all. And press harder on the mould: this is not
carved, it is **pressed from tooling that has been used too many times.** The softness in the corner
of her mouth is not artistic; it is wear in the plaster. Say so in the prompt or an image model will
render a crisp hand-carving and the entire second axis evaporates.
→ **Amended into V1 and carried to all four.**

**THE FALLOFF (after Swink) — on V2. This is the pair's near-death and I am recording it in full.**
*Working from the readability-as-gameplay decomposition:* stop. *"The blue fades warmly into amber up
the sleeve"* is the exact sentence the style contract exists to prevent. You have written a gradient
and called it a Celadon Turn. It is not a compromise, it is the failure.

The fix is in your own arithmetic and it is better than the draft. You have six shelves. **Assign the
colour per shelf.** T2 and T3 emit blue-white; at the T3/T4 boundary the colour changes to amber and
stays amber on through T6. The turn happens **on a shelf edge** — a hard line you could catch a
fingernail on — because the light is travelling through a measurably different wall thickness on
either side of it and the clay's own body colours it differently at depth. That is physically honest,
it is unmistakably ours, and nobody else's frame will look like it.

> **RECORDED REROLL — V2**
> **BEFORE:** *"...the cold blue of the filament fades warmly into amber as it rises up the sleeve,
> a soft transition across the porcelain."*
> **AFTER:** *"...the emitted colour is assigned per shelf and changes on a shelf edge: the two
> deepest shelves pass cold blue-white, and at the hard boundary where the wall steps thinner the
> colour changes over to warm amber in a single crisp line and stays amber through every shallower
> shelf, so the turn is a countable edge across her face rather than a blend."*

**THE FALLOFF — on V3, same failure, smaller.**
*"A soft amber glow around the lamp's foot"* is a bloom. The emission stops at the clay. The field is
unlit and records nothing.

> **RECORDED REROLL — V3**
> **BEFORE:** *"...a soft amber glow spilling around the lamp's foot onto the ground."*
> **AFTER:** *"...the amber stops dead at the clay's outer edge; the deep-navy field immediately
> outside the silhouette stays completely unlit and unmarked, with no halo, no spill and no cast
> shadow, so the object's outline is a hard cut against the field."*

**THE THUMBNAIL (after Kare) — finding B, on V4.**
At 16px a dead lamp against navy is **a pale smudge**. Say it plainly: **V4 does not satisfy the
thumbnail law and cannot be fixed into satisfying it,** because satisfying it would mean emitting
light, and this is the frame where the light is gone. I am not going to smuggle a residual ember in to
game the gate.

**Ruling:** V4 ships as a *state part only*, tagged `promo:false`. The thumbnail law is carried in this
pair by **V3** (a single amber shape on black — the pack's best 16px read by a wide margin) and by
V1. That exclusion goes into the prompt's operator block so nobody grabs V4 for a store page.
Compensation inside the frame: coarsen the shadow staircase so the six steps survive downscaling as
*ridges*, and let the arris catch a hard white edge so the silhouette holds even when the interior
mushes.
→ **Amended into V4.**

**THE THUMBNAIL — finding A, on all four.**
The technique line in the assignment says *the object held*. A rendered hand is a scene, it ruins
border-connected flood fill, and it will drag a wrist, a sleeve and a room in behind it. **Carry
"held" through orientation and scale only** — three-quarter, close, at the height and angle of a thing
raised to read by — and render no hand.
→ **Amended into all four. Explicit in every body.**

**THE SLOW OBJECT (after de Waal) — on the pair as a whole, and on my preamble (b).**
*Working from the porcelain vitrine practice:* I want to test the claim that a cold draw can serve the
warm arm. The claim holds, on one condition: **the arc has to actually run.** If V1 is merely
"blue-ish" and V3 merely "orange-ish," it is a hue wobble and the arm is unearned. Push the ends
apart. V1 should be genuinely unpleasant — clinical, over-charged, a lamp behaving like an instrument.
V3 should be the warmest frame anyone in this run produces. The warmth is only a gift if the thing
before it was cold.
→ **Accepted. V1's palette hardened toward slate; V3 reduced to three tones with amber dominant.**

**THE CONTEXT SKEPTIC (after Kalman) — the last word.**
The recursive detail — she is holding a lamp exactly like this one — is the pair's best asset and it
is one sentence away from being a gallery text. Do not explain it. Render it and shut up. A viewer
either notices that the last thing they see before their light dies is a woman holding the same lamp,
or they do not. The moment a prompt says *"symbolising the transmission of craft"* it is dead.
→ **Accepted. No prompt body contains a word of interpretation.**

# NEGATIVE-GATE SELF-CHECK

Each of the six clauses quoted from `CREATIVE_CONTEXT.md` §THE NEGATIVE GATE, with how these four
prompts avoid it.

**1. "NOT printed/painted/decaled/projected onto porcelain — the relief IS the image"**
✅ Every body states the picture *"exists only as thickness in the clay itself, cut as six flat
depth-shelves"* and names the millimetre range. V1 and V2 additionally instruct that the shelves are
countable by eye and that the rim of real clay is visible at the object's edge. V4 removes light
entirely and the picture is still there as ridges — which is only possible if the relief *is* the
image. No prompt contains the words print, paint, decal, projection, decoration or surface pattern.
Facet 1 is written specifically to fail any prompt that could have been silkscreened.

**2. "NOT alpha-gradient glow, bloom, god-rays, soft falloff, lens flare — light is CARVED in hard
terraces"**
✅ This clause killed two of my four drafts and both kills are recorded in step 09 with before/after
text. Every prompt says brightness arrives as *"a crisp step you could catch a fingernail on"* and
that *"brightness stops dead at the clay's outer edge; the navy field stays completely unlit and
unmarked, with no halo, no spill and no cast shadow."* The Celadon Turn in V2 was explicitly rebuilt
from a fade into a **per-shelf colour assignment that changes on a hard edge**. No prompt contains
glow, bloom, ray, halo (except to forbid it), falloff, flare, fade, blend, or gradient.

**3. "NOT a post-process filter over ordinary 3D"**
✅ The banding is described as geometry throughout — depth-shelves, millimetre thicknesses, hard-topped
rungs, lips and arrises that throw shadows. V4 is the structural guarantee: it renders the *same six
values as physical ridges with no light passing through them*, which no post-process banding filter
could produce. Nothing anywhere describes a look, a pass, a grade or an effect applied to the frame.

**4. "NOT PBR metal, plastic, wet gloss, or subsurface skin — porcelain is matte, dry, slightly
sugary, and it TRANSMITS"**
✅ Every body states *"matte, chalky and faintly sugary with no wet highlight and no metallic sheen."*
The one metal in the pack — the iron staples — is specified as *"oxidised, dry and dull with no
shine"* every time it appears, and is defined by what it does to light (stops it) rather than by a
reflectance model. The body is unglazed parian throughout; no glaze skin, no gloss, no clearcoat, no
subsurface glow term appears. Transmission is stated positively in V1, V2 and V3 and its absence is
stated positively in V4.

**5. "NOT a composed scene, room, HUD, typography, score, control or environment"**
✅ Every body contains the explicit clause *"no hand, no arm, no table and no room — the object floats
alone."* This is the step-09 finding-A amendment; I removed the literal hand that the assignment's
"held" technique implied and carried "held" through orientation and scale instead. The field is a flat
unlit deep-navy matte with declared pixel clearance on all four edges and no cast shadow touching it,
so border-connected flood fill has a clean run. No text, no numerals, no bar, no icon, no meter, no
button, no frame furniture. The charge gauge is the picture itself.

**6. "NOT a documentary of a real pottery workshop (L2 HIGH: realism loses to impossibility in
object-world fields)"**
✅ No kiln, no wheel, no studio, no shelf of drying ware, no tools, no potter at work. The object is
an invented one that does not exist: a **consumable pressed-parian reading lamp whose remaining charge
is legible as how many rungs of a portrait are still alive.** The impossibility gradient is carried by
three invented behaviours — the finest carve being destroyed by *too much* light, the emitted colour
changing on a shelf edge rather than across a blend, and the relief showing a different face in shadow
than it showed in light. The recursive detail (she is holding this same lamp) is rendered flat and
unexplained, per the Context Skeptic's ruling.

**NEGATIVE GATE: CLEAN — 6 / 6.** Two blocking hits were caught during step 09 (clause 2, twice) and
both are fixed with quoted before/after text rather than silently rewritten.

# HONEST PROBLEMS — reported, not smoothed over

1. **V4 does not satisfy the thumbnail law, and cannot.** A dead lamp emits nothing; making it
   thumbnail-legible would require adding light, which would destroy the state. It ships tagged
   `promo:false` and the pair's thumbnail obligation is carried by V3 and V1. I chose to flag this
   rather than smuggle in a residual ember to pass a gate.
2. **The ACCESSIBLE-arm assignment fights the axis draw.** `parian` + `cold filament` is the intense
   palette, not the warm one. I resolved it on a time axis (cold → turning → amber → grey) so the warmth
   is the reward for depletion, and the bench (THE SLOW OBJECT seat) accepted it *on condition the arc
   actually runs at full amplitude*. If the renders come back with V1 merely blue-ish and V3 merely
   orange-ish, this pair's arm assignment has not been earned and V1 should be re-rolled colder before
   anything else is touched. That is the render-gate watch flag for P2.
3. **All four share one camera, one box and one registration.** This is deliberate — the four states
   are a swappable strip in a single quad — but it means the pack has less framing variety than a
   four-prompt pair normally would. The variation is carried entirely by light. I believe this is
   correct for a state family and wrong for any other kind of pair, so it should not be copied.
4. **Renderer risk, named:** an emitting object is the single strongest bloom attractor in any image
   model. Every body carries an explicit hard-edge/no-spill clause, but this is the thing most likely
   to come back wrong. **At the render gate, reject any V1/V2/V3 output where the navy field is
   brighter within fifty pixels of the silhouette than it is at the frame edge.** That is a measurable
   test, and it is the one I would run first.
