# THE DEBATE — baseline configuration

**THE TRANSMITTED (after Brandt):** *Working from the Bauhaus metal-workshop record —* the brief
already contains its own answer and I want to name it before anyone decorates it. A lithophane is
an object where the fitting and the light are not separable. There is no "image" and then
"lighting of the image." **The image is a thickness.** So the first rule is material truth: every
part must be describable as a *depth map in clay*, never as a picture applied to a slab.

**THE RELIEF (after Whiteread):** *Working from the casting practice —* and note what that makes
the picture. It is not carved *onto* the porcelain. It is carved *out of* it. The bright parts of
the image are where the clay is **absent**. You are looking at a cast of a void.

That is the concept in one line: **the picture is the missing clay.** Everything bright in this
game is something that was taken away.

**THE LANTERN:** *Working from the andon-e lantern-picture tradition —* which gives the corridor
its ethic. In that practice the picture is not owned, it is *attended*: someone must keep the wick
alive or the image ceases to exist. It is not stored anywhere. A lithophane corridor is a place
where **images require maintenance to remain real.**

**THE SKEPTIC — CONCEPT (after Loos):** *Working from the Ornament and Crime polemic —* stop.
Three turns of poetry and nobody has said a useful production word.

You are describing a game where the player spends most of their time looking at **blank white
slabs**. That is what an unlit lithophane is. Not mysterious — *blank*. You have chosen a material
whose default state is the absence of information, for a medium where the player is staring at it
for twenty minutes.

And craft is being used as an alibi here. "Kiln warp, thumbprint, mould seam" — that is a
vocabulary of *excuses for imperfection*, dressed up as meaning. Show me why the unlit state is
worth looking at or the whole run is a beautiful screenshot attached to a boring game.

**THE THRESHOLD (after Matta-Clark):** ...*Wait.* He is right, and the fix is in my own practice.

When I cut a building, the cut is visible **in daylight** — as shadow, as edge, as relief. It does
not need to be backlit to be legible. A carved lithophane has *physical relief on its face*. In
raking light, unlit, you can see the carving as **shadow.**

So the panel has been designing one image when the object has two:

> **Unlit, the relief reads by SHADOW. Lit, it reads by TRANSMISSION. Same clay, two completely
> different pictures — and they do not have to agree.**

**THE ARCHIVE (after Sugimoto):** *Working from the Theaters series —* and that is where the
game's whole idea lives. In Theaters, a full film is exposed onto one frame and the screen comes
out **blank white** — every image at once averaging to nothing. Blankness as maximum information,
not absence of it.

So: the shadow image and the transmitted image should **show different things.** The relief you
can see unlit is the door's *surface claim*. The picture that blooms when you spend light is what
is *actually* behind it. When they disagree, the player has learned something and it cost them.

**THE SKEPTIC — CONCEPT:** *Actually* — that I will accept, and it repairs my objection instead of
dodging it. The unlit state is no longer blank; it is a **carved surface under raking light**, and
it is doing narrative work. Withdrawn. Keep the imperfection vocabulary only where it *carries
information* — a firing crack that splits the image is a fact about the object, not an excuse.

---

**THE KILN (after Leach):** *Working from A Potter's Book —* medium panel, and I want to hold the
concept panel to something. If the fire is a collaborator, then no two panels in this corridor are
identical even from the same mould. Warp is not decoration, it is **the record of a firing.**
Practically: the tiling must never be a clean repeat.

**THE TILE (after Frank):** Which is my whole discipline and it is the hard technical problem
here. *Working from the pattern-repeat practice —* a corridor is a tiled surface, and a repeat you
can find is a repeat that kills the illusion of made objects. If every wall panel is the same
lithophane, the player is in a video game. If each is *slightly* warped, they are in a kiln's
output.

**THE THICKNESS (after Wedgwood):** *Working from the documented thin-body and relief lineage —*
and here is the number that makes this buildable rather than poetic. A real lithophane works in a
thickness range of roughly **2–6 mm**: below that it burns out to white, above it stops the light
entirely. That is not a wide range. It means the medium has **very few available values** — it is
closer to a woodcut than a photograph.

**THE FALLOFF (after Swink):** Which solves the style contract's hardest constraint, and I want to
be explicit because it is the single most useful production rule to come out of this room.

The consuming standard **bans alpha gradients** — light must be carved, never faded. Everyone has
been treating that as a restriction. It is not. It is what the material actually does. Thickness
is quantized by the carving tool, so the transmitted image comes out in **stepped terraces, like a
topographic map** — discrete bands of brightness with hard edges between them.

> **The signature is contour lines.** Light in this game steps; it never fades. Nobody else looks
> like that, and it is the physically honest result.

**THE RAKING LIGHT (after Hubert):** *Working from the fast-environment method —* and it is cheap,
which matters. You do not model a lithophane. You author a **height field** and let it drive both
states: shadow when lit from the side, transmission when lit from behind. One asset, two
behaviours. Darkness does the rest of the corridor for free.

**THE SKEPTIC — MEDIUM (after Muratori):** *Working from the published critiques of decisions
taken for fashion over measured constraint —* the terrace idea is good and I am about to make it
survive contact.

If brightness comes in discrete bands, **how many bands?** Say it now or every artist picks their
own and the corridor looks like six different games. And the thickness range gives you the answer
rather than taste: 2–6 mm at a plausible carving step is **five to seven usable values.** Pick
one number, publish it, and every part obeys it.

Second: this must not ship as a **filter**. If the "look" is a post-process pass over ordinary 3D,
it will read as an Instagram preset and die. The banding has to be *in the asset* — baked into the
height field — not applied to the frame.

**THE FALLOFF:** Agreed. Call it **six values**, and it is not arbitrary: it is the number of
distinct brightnesses a hand can reliably carve in a 2–6 mm body, and it is enough to read a face.

---

**THE ONE SENTENCE (after Clark):** *Working from the capsule-art analyses —* context panel. The
sentence is already written and it is unusually good: *"Every surface is backlit porcelain that is
invisible until you light it."* That is sayable, specific, and it makes a promise the game
actually keeps. That is rarer than it sounds.

**THE THREAD:** *Working from the documented reception of the embroidered game —* 5.7K reactions
and 532 shares for *"every frame hand-embroidered with real thread."* Note what that sentence is:
a **cost**, publicly borne. People did not share a look. They shared somebody's willingness to do
something absurd and slow.

"Invisible until you light it" is the same shape of sentence — it names a **constraint accepted**,
not a style applied.

**THE THUMBNAIL (after Kare):** Then the risk is obvious and nobody has said it. At 16px, a
featureless pale slab is **nothing**. The lit door is the only frame that survives a thumbnail.
So: every promotional frame, and the first frame of the game, must contain **at least one lit
panel against dark** — and the terrace banding must be coarse enough to survive downscaling. Fine
contour lines will alias into mud.

**THE SLOW OBJECT (after de Waal):** *Working from the porcelain vitrine installations —* I want
to defend the pale slabs, because the panel is treating them as a liability to be minimised.

Many near-identical pale objects, repeated, accrue meaning precisely *because* they are almost the
same. A corridor of unlit lithophanes is a vitrine. The player walking past forty pale doors and
choosing to light one is doing the thing my practice is about: **attention as the scarce resource,
not spectacle.**

**THE SKEPTIC — CONTEXT (after Kalman):** *Working from the critiques of professionalized taste —*
and there it is. That is the most tasteful sentence anyone has said today and it is the one that
will kill this.

"A corridor of pale objects where attention is the scarce resource" is a gallery text. It is
beautiful and it is *forgettable*, which is the specific failure I care about. The embroidery game
did not win by being tasteful. It won by being **almost stupid in its literalism**: real thread,
every frame, look at it.

So be literal. Not "attention as scarce resource" — **you have three matches.** Not "images
require maintenance" — **the light dies and the picture goes with it.** The material is already
strange enough to carry the whole thing. Do not make it elegant on top of strange; elegance will
sand off the liability that makes it shareable.

**THE ONE SENTENCE:** That is right and it changes the copy. Lead with the physics, never the
metaphor.

# TRANSFORMATION 1 — GROUP CHOICE: **BRIDGE**
*The baseline panel proposed Bridge: find a distant domain with the analogous problem.*

The analogous problem — *"an image that must be paid for in a consumable resource, and reading it
destroys it"* — is not a ceramics problem. It is **film photography**. Three bridge seats:

**THE DARKROOM (after Sally Mann, b. 1951):** *Working from the wet-plate collodion practice —*
in that process the plate's flaws are not tolerated, they are the *subject*. And crucially the
image arrives **during development**, in front of you, and the same light that lets you see it can
destroy it. Bridge insight: **the reveal should be a process with duration, not a state change.**
A door should not snap from unlit to lit. The picture should *come up*, terrace by terrace, the
way an image rises in a tray — and rise from the thin parts first.

**THE FUSE (after a match-and-safety-lamp lineage — role-anchored):** the miner's lamp is the
literal object: a light you carry that is *finite*, that tells you about the room, and whose
failure is the danger itself. Bridge insight: the light source is not a tool the player uses; it
is **a thing that is dying** while used. Give it a body and a visible remaining amount.

**Adopted from the bridge:** the reveal has duration and rises thin-parts-first; the light source
is a depleting physical object, not a UI meter.

# TRANSFORMATION 2 — SKEPTIC CHOICE: **REFLECT**
*The three Hyper-Skeptics jointly chose Reflect: seat panelists holding the OPPOSITE foundational
assumption.*

Every seat so far assumes **light reveals**. Reflect seats the opposite: **light conceals.**

**THE OVEREXPOSED (after Hiroshi Sugimoto, reflected):** past a threshold, more light does not
reveal more — it **burns the image out to white**. A lithophane over-lit is as blank as one unlit.

That is the reflection's gift, and it is a mechanic:

> **Both extremes are blindness.** Too little light and the door says nothing; too much and it
> whites out. There is a *correct amount* of light, and finding it is the skill.

**THE SKEPTIC — MEDIUM:** That also kills the last cheap option. You cannot just "turn the
brightness up" to make a frame look good. Exposure is a design parameter with a right answer per
panel, which means the artist must specify it per part.

# CROSS-PANEL SYNTHESIS

**1. The object has TWO images that need not agree.** Unlit it reads by **shadow** (relief under
raking light) — the door's surface claim. Lit it reads by **transmission** — what is actually
behind. Disagreement between them is information the player paid for. *This repaired the
Skeptic's strongest objection: the unlit state is not blank, it is carved.*

**2. The picture is the missing clay.** Bright = absent material. Everything luminous in this game
is something that was taken away.

**3. Light steps, it never fades — SIX VALUES.** Thickness quantizes to roughly six carvable
brightnesses in a 2–6 mm body. **The signature is contour lines.** This satisfies the style
contract's alpha ban not as a compromise but as physical honesty. The banding is **baked into the
height field, never a post-process filter.**

**4. Both extremes are blindness.** Unlit says nothing; over-lit burns to white. There is a
correct exposure per panel and finding it is the skill.

**5. The reveal has duration.** The image rises like a print in a developing tray, thin parts
first. It does not snap on.

**6. The light is a dying body, not a meter.** A carried object with a visible remaining amount.

**7. Be literal, not tasteful.** *"You have three matches"* beats *"attention is the scarce
resource."* The material is strange enough; elegance would sand off the liability that makes it
shareable.

**8. Thumbnail law.** Every promotional frame and the first frame of the game carries at least one
lit panel against dark, with banding coarse enough to survive downscaling.

**9. No two panels identical.** Warp is the record of a firing; a findable repeat kills the
illusion of made objects.
