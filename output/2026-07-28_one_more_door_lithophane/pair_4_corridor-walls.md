## THE PAIR'S TWO STRUCTURAL DECISIONS

Made before a word of prompt, because everything downstream hangs off them.

### Decision 1 — what "a place that no longer exists" is, in a medium whose signature is contour lines

The synthesis says the transmitted image arrives in six hard-edged terraces *like a topographic map*.
Every other pair has to fight that resemblance or ignore it. This pair makes it **diegetic**: the
vanished place is **a stepped orchard hill that was quarried away.** The six terraces are literally
its six surveyed elevations. The summit is gone, so the innermost contour ring is the **thinnest**
clay — the brightest thing on the panel is the hole where the top of the hill used to be.

That collapses three requirements into one form:
- The Missing Clay stops being a metaphor. Bright *is* removed material, at both scales at once —
  removed from the clay, and removed from the hill.
- The contour-line signature is no longer a stylistic tic the artist chose; it is what the object
  is *of*.
- It gives the height field a non-arbitrary geometry: nested lens-shaped rings, offset, never
  concentric, notched once where a haul road cut in.

**Hard rider:** a survey has numbers on it. This one does not. No annotation, no numerals, no legend,
no letterform anywhere — the contours are carved risers only. This is the gate clause most likely to
be violated by accident in this pair, and it is banned explicitly in all four prompts.

### Decision 2 — how "another lithophane, lit in turn" survives the alpha ban *and* gate clause 1

Axis 3 is the one draw in this pair that fights the negative gate, and it is worth saying plainly
rather than smuggling. If a neighbouring panel's picture lands on this panel's face, the rendered
result is *an image projected onto porcelain* — gate clause 1, blocking.

**Resolution, and it is better than the problem:** the picture does not survive the crossing. At
150 mm of separation only the source's coarsest terrace boundaries hold together; everything finer
dissolves. So what arrives on the neighbour is **not a picture — it is ordnance.** Hard bright bars
separated by hard dark bars, with no penumbra anywhere, and every bar *breaks and steps sideways*
at every riser of the receiving relief. The break is the proof: a projection would ride over the
surface; this one is cut by it.

That is also the pair's dread. Each panel is lit by another panel and **still cannot read it.**
Light crosses the corridor; meaning does not.

# STEP 07 — ARTISTIC GUIDES
*Six guides. Four map to the assigned variation angles; two are exploratory and are consumed at
step 10 to strengthen the weaker assigned angles. Each carries mood, light, palette, and story cue.*

**G1 · THE RACK OF FOUR (assigned angle 1).**
Four abutted panels, dead-on, unlit. Mood: patient dread with the dread not yet arrived — the
vitrine before anyone spends anything. Light: a single grazing source from image-left at about 8°
above the panel plane; no other light of any kind. Palette: cool buff-grey stoneware, iron speckle,
navy void. Story cue: four objects that came out of the same fire and out of it differently. The
sharpest available inversion — three panels are visibly warped and the fourth is nearly true, so
**the flat one reads as the anomaly.** Repetition making meaning, exactly de Waal's argument, turned
so it does not read as a gallery text.

**G2 · THE NEIGHBOUR'S ORDNANCE (assigned angle 2).**
Same rack, panel 3 lit from behind. Mood: the moment the cost is paid and the return is partial.
Light: transmission out of panel 3 at correct exposure, plus that transmission arriving on panels 2
and 4 as hard bars. Palette: dirty ochre-amber transmission — grog is iron-bearing, so what gets
through is never clean white — against unlit buff-grey and navy. Story cue: the panel next door is
on fire with information and you still cannot read it from here.

**G3 · THE BODY LOSING (assigned angle 3).**
One panel, backlit at an exposure that would be correct for bone china and is wrong for this body.
Mood: dread with the awe stripped out. Light: transmission from directly behind, plus a weak
grazing second source that keeps the relief alive where the transmission has died. Palette: four
terraces collapsed to one dead grey-brown, one terrace surviving only as granular static, one
terrace open and peppered. Story cue: **the six values collapse to two and a half** — the object
still has its whole picture carved into it and can no longer say it.

**G4 · THE REFUSAL (assigned angle 4).**
Macro, vertical, the joint between two warped panels. Mood: awe at a millimetre. Light: raking from
above-left along the arrises, plus a blade of light coming through the gap from behind. Palette:
near-monochrome buff and navy with one hot slot of amber-white. Story cue: the brightest value in
the frame is **not clay at all** — it is the gap where clay failed to meet, and it carries no
picture whatsoever. Information-free brightness. The failure out-shines the image.

**G5 · THE LIFTED CORNER (exploratory).**
A single panel seen from a shallow oblique, one corner risen 4 mm off its seat, the shadow beneath
the lift a hard black wedge. Mood: the object refusing its housing. Useful, but it wants a frame
part in shot to explain the lift — that is P6's family, and the borrow costs more than it returns.
**Consumed into G4** at step 10: the lift explains the wedge, and the seam macro already carries it.

**G6 · THE EIGHT-PANEL VITRINE (exploratory).**
Eight panels in a 4×2 grid at small scale, a deliberate thumbnail-law stress test. Mood: the pale
repeated object. Verdict: at 16 px, eight unlit slabs are eight grey rectangles — the Thumbnail seat
is right and this frame is *nothing*. **Consumed into G1** at step 10: its one surviving contribution
is the scale discipline — make the terrace risers coarse enough that the shadow they throw survives
downscaling, and cut the count from eight to four so each slab gets real pixels.

# STEP 09 — ARTIST-REFINED PASS

**Refining voice: after Ruth Duckworth (1919–2009)** — chosen for step 09's artist loop, deliberately
*not* one of the 18 panel seats. Grounding: the documented large unglazed stoneware **wall reliefs**
and murals; a practice in which a wall is a single continuous carved landform rather than a picture
hung on masonry, read entirely by its own shadow. This is the only body of work that is already
exactly this part family.

> *Panel and artist voices in this document are model-generated interpretive constructs "after" named
> source figures' published work. No statement is a quotation of, or endorsement by, the named person.*

**What the Duckworth read changes in every prompt:**
1. Stop describing the panel as a slab bearing an image. **Instruct the renderer to build a landform
   and then slice it flat.** The wall is topography that has been given a boundary, not a picture
   that has been given a frame.
2. The unlit read is the *primary* read, always — shadow first, transmission second, even in the lit
   frames. A relief that only works backlit is a relief that was never carved.
3. Unglazed means unglazed. No sheen anywhere. The surface should look like it would take chalk.

**Friction from the supplied ledger, applied:**

- **THE TILE (after Frank)** on R1: *four abutted panels with a shared contour survey is a findable
  repeat waiting to happen — the survey itself becomes the grid.* → Fix: the survey **misregisters at
  every seam by a different amount** (3, 8, 5 mm), and one panel is installed a quarter-turn out of
  survey. The continuity is broken four different ways, so there is no repeat to find.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, MEDIUM (after Muratori)** on all six: *if you do not publish the terrace numbers,
  every render invents its own and the corridor looks like six games.* → Fix: **the six-value ladder
  is stated numerically in every prompt** — T1 6.0 mm through T6 2.0 mm, 0.8 mm risers, sharp arris.
  Non-negotiable, identical text across the pair.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, MEDIUM** on R2: *"spill" is where the alpha ban dies.* → Fix: the word is banned
  from this pair's vocabulary. What crosses the gap is **bars**, and every bar boundary is a hard
  line with no penumbra. Stated as an explicit rendering order, not as an adjective.
- **THE KILN (after Leach)** on R1/R2: *warp is the record of a firing, so each panel must warp in a
  different mode.* → Fix: one saddles, one cups, one twists, one is nearly true.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, CONTEXT (after Kalman)** on G6 and on my own first draft of R1: *"a vitrine of
  pale objects" is the most tasteful sentence in the room and it is the one that kills this.* → Fix:
  G6 cut. R1 keeps the repetition but earns it by inversion — the true panel is the strange one.
  Literal, not tasteful.
- **THE THUMBNAIL (after Kare)** on R1: *at 16 px this is four grey rectangles.* → **Accepted, not
  fixed.** R1 is a library/tiling part and is disqualified as a hero frame. R2 carries the thumbnail
  law for this pair. Recorded in Problems.

## PROMPT 1 — `wall.strip.grog.unlit`
### logical part box: **2048 × 512 px** (four abutted panels, each 512 × 512 px; in-world each panel 300 × 300 mm × 6 mm)
### state: **UNLIT / SHADOW.** Zero terraces legible as light. All six terrace risers legible as shadow.

```
Build a horizontal strip of four square wall slabs of grogged stoneware standing edge to edge, and
render it dead-on and orthographic with the slab faces exactly parallel to the image plane. Use no
perspective convergence, no vanishing point, no floor, no ceiling, no corridor, no room.

Construct each slab as a landform first and only then cut it to a square: carve into the face a
nested field of closed contour terraces describing a stepped orchard hill that was quarried away.
Make the rings lens-shaped and offset, never concentric circles, and notch each ring once on the
same flank where a haul road cut in. Carve the innermost ring thinnest, because the summit was
removed — the missing material is the subject.

Quantize the relief to exactly six thicknesses and no others: T1 = 6.0 mm, T2 = 5.2 mm, T3 = 4.4 mm,
T4 = 3.6 mm, T5 = 2.8 mm, T6 = 2.0 mm. Cut each riser between terraces as a vertical 0.8 mm wall
with a sharp unbroken arris. Do not blend, ramp, feather or dither between terraces anywhere.

Light the strip with one grazing source from image-left at 8° above the slab plane and nothing else.
Let that raking light do all of the work: throw a hard black shadow off the left face of every riser
and leave the right face bare, so the contour field reads as a stepped survey drawn entirely in
shadow. Make the risers coarse enough that the shadow bars survive being shrunk to thumbnail size —
no hairline contours.

Give the surface the history of a machine badly corrected by a hand. Lay horizontal print
striations at 0.25 mm pitch across the full width of every slab and let the grazing light catch them
as a fine corduroy. Run one vertical start-stop scar up each slab in a different position. Then
scrape irregular flats where a metal rib knocked the corduroy off, leaving islands of smooth against
islands of striation. On one slab only, allow a single riser to have been over-scraped into a
45° chamfer — keep it a hard-edged facet, never a gradient, and let it read as damage.

Warp each slab differently, because each was fired separately: make the first saddle with opposite
corners lifted, make the second cup into a dish, make the third twist, and make the fourth nearly
true so that the flat one is the strange object in the rack. Open the three seams between them to
different widths — roughly 1 mm, 4 mm and 2 mm — and let each seam be a hard black wedge that
changes width along its length. Misregister the contour survey across every seam by a different
amount, three to eight millimetres, and install one slab a quarter-turn out of survey so no repeat
can be located anywhere in the strip.

Keep the body matte, dry and slightly sugary, buff-grey and peppered with dark iron grog particles,
completely unglazed, with no sheen, no wet highlight, no plastic or metallic response of any kind —
a surface that would take chalk. Do not paint, print, decal or project any image onto it; every
feature must be geometry.

Include no numerals, no lettering, no survey annotation, no legend, no symbols, no interface, no
hands, no tools, no workshop.

Place the strip on a single flat uniform deep-navy field at #0B1220, one exact value everywhere,
with no vignette, no gradient, no texture, and no shadow cast onto it. Let that navy connect
continuously to all four image borders so a border-connected flood fill removes it in one pass, and
leave at least 24 px of clean matte on every side. Transparent background is equally acceptable.
```

**Flairs solved:** *Kiln Warp* — four firing modes, four seam widths, a survey that misregisters four
different ways. *Raking Light* — the entire read is the 8° source; there is no other light. *Six
Terraces* — the ladder is present as pure shadow, proving the height field before any light is spent.
*The Vitrine* — four near-identical pale objects, with the meaning carried by the inversion that the
true one is the anomaly.

**Why this wins:** it answers the Concept Skeptic's blank-slab objection in the very frame that has
no light in it at all — the unlit state turns out to be the densest surface in the run.

## PROMPT 2 — `wall.strip.grog.neighbourlit` **(pair hero — carries the thumbnail law)**
### logical part box: **2048 × 512 px** (four abutted panels, each 512 × 512 px)
### state: **ONE PANEL LIT / MID-CROSSING.** On panel 3: T6 and T5 fully open, T4 marginal, T3–T1 dead. On panels 2 and 4: zero terraces lit, relief crossed by hard bars.

```
Render the same orthographic four-slab strip of grogged stoneware, dead-on, faces parallel to the
image plane, no perspective, no room, no corridor, no floor.

Carve every slab as before: six thicknesses and no others — T1 = 6.0 mm, T2 = 5.2 mm, T3 = 4.4 mm,
T4 = 3.6 mm, T5 = 2.8 mm, T6 = 2.0 mm — with 0.8 mm vertical risers and sharp arrises, cut into a
nested lens-shaped contour survey of a quarried orchard hill whose summit is the thinnest and
therefore brightest region. Do not blend between terraces anywhere.

Now put a light behind the third slab from the left and behind nothing else. Drive its
transmission out of the face in flat hard-edged plateaus: open T6 to a bright dirty ochre-amber,
open T5 one full step darker, let T4 arrive only just, and hold T3, T2 and T1 as completely dead
stone. Make the boundary between any two terraces a single hard line. Because the body is grogged,
pepper every lit plateau with opaque black flecks where grog particles stop the light — the
transmission must look granular and hard-won, never clean and never creamy.

Take the light that leaves that slab and lay it across its two neighbours as bars, not as glow.
Cast a set of hard bright bars separated by hard dark bars onto slab 2 and slab 4, at a grazing
angle of about 15°, and break every bar sideways at every riser it crosses so the bars step across
the relief instead of riding over it. Give no bar a penumbra: every light-to-dark boundary in this
image is a hard line. Carry no picture in those bars — the neighbour's image does not survive the
crossing, only its coarsest banding does, so the bars must read as banded illumination and never as
a projected picture.

Keep the two lit-upon neighbours legible as relief, not as screens: their contour risers still throw
their own hard shadows inside the bright bars.

Keep the warp: one slab saddled, one cupped, one twisted, one nearly true, with the three seams
opening to different widths as hard black wedges. Let the lit slab's warp bow it away from its own
light source on one side so its terrace values shift by one full step across the bow. Misregister
the survey at every seam by a different amount.

Keep the machine-then-hand history: 0.25 mm horizontal print striations, one vertical start-stop
scar per slab in a different position, scraped flats interrupting the striations, and one riser
over-scraped to a hard 45° chamfer.

Keep the body unglazed, matte, dry, slightly sugary, buff-grey with iron speckle, with no sheen, no
wet gloss, no plastic, no metal, no skin-like scattering. Do not paint, print, decal or project any
image onto any slab; every feature is geometry. Use no bloom, no halo, no lens flare, no glow
around the lit slab, and no atmospheric haze in the gaps.

Include no numerals, no lettering, no survey annotation, no legend, no symbols, no interface, no
hands, no tools, no workshop.

Place the strip on a single flat uniform deep-navy field at #0B1220, one exact value everywhere,
with no vignette, no gradient, no texture, and no shadow cast onto it. Let that navy connect
continuously to all four image borders so a border-connected flood fill removes it in one pass, and
leave at least 24 px of clean matte on every side. Transparent background is equally acceptable.
```

**Flairs solved:** *Six Terraces* — the ladder is legible as light for the first time in the pair, and
legible as *steps*, not as a fade. *Raking Light* — solved twice over, because the raking light here
**is another panel**; the side light has a picture in it that cannot be read. *Kiln Warp* — the bow of
the lit slab moves its own terrace values by a full step across its face. *The Vitrine* — three mute
slabs and one speaking one, so the repetition is what makes the single lit panel mean anything.

**Why this wins:** one lit slab against dark with terrace banding coarse enough to survive 16 px —
the thumbnail law satisfied in the only frame in the run where the light source is itself an image
nobody can read.

## PROMPT 3 — `wall.panel.grog.transmit_fail`
### logical part box: **1024 × 1024 px** (one panel, authored at 2× for the atlas; in-world 300 × 300 mm)
### state: **LIT AND FAILING.** T6 open but peppered; T5 surviving only as granular static; T4, T3, T2, T1 collapsed into a single dead value. Six terraces reduced to two and a half.

```
Render one square wall slab of grogged stoneware filling the frame, tilted about 10° so its own bow
leans toward the camera, otherwise dead-on with no perspective, no room, no corridor, no floor.

Carve it with the same six thicknesses and no others — T1 = 6.0 mm, T2 = 5.2 mm, T3 = 4.4 mm,
T4 = 3.6 mm, T5 = 2.8 mm, T6 = 2.0 mm — 0.8 mm vertical risers, sharp arrises, no blending anywhere.
Cut into it the nested lens-shaped contour survey of the quarried orchard hill, offset rings, one
haul-road notch, the summit ring carved thinnest because the top of the hill was taken away.

Now light it from directly behind at an exposure that would be correct for a fine white body and is
wrong for this one, and show the body losing. Collapse T4, T3, T2 and T1 into one indistinguishable
dead grey-brown so that four carved elevations of the hill deliver exactly one value and the
information in them is simply gone. Let T5 arrive only as granular static — resolve it as a scatter
of opaque black grog flecks with hard edges, so the terrace reads as noise rather than as an area.
Open T6 alone into real light, a dirty ochre-amber, and pepper even that with flecks.

Use the slab's own warp against it: because it bows toward the camera, hold the far half a full
terrace darker than the near half, with the change happening along a hard line where the bow turns.

Add one region, near the lower right, where the printer under-extruded and the hand-finisher sanded
straight through: make it accidentally thinner than T6 and let it burn out to flat featureless
white. Give that region no carved detail at all — it is the brightest area on the object and it
carries nothing.

Rescue the frame with a second, weak grazing source from image-left at 8° so that wherever the
transmission has died the relief takes over: the collapsed terraces must remain visible as
hard-shadowed risers even though they no longer transmit. Let the shadow picture and the light
picture disagree — the shadow still describes the whole hill, the light describes almost none of it.

Keep the machine-then-hand history: 0.25 mm horizontal print striations catching the grazing source
as corduroy, one vertical start-stop scar, scraped flats interrupting the striations, one riser
over-scraped into a hard 45° chamfer that reads as damage.

Keep the body unglazed, matte, dry, slightly sugary, buff-grey, densely peppered with dark iron
grog, with no sheen, no wet gloss, no plastic, no metal, no skin-like scattering. Do not paint,
print, decal or project any image onto it; every feature is geometry. Use no bloom, no halo, no
god-rays, no lens flare, no soft falloff at any brightness boundary — every boundary is a hard line.

Include no numerals, no lettering, no survey annotation, no legend, no symbols, no interface, no
hands, no tools, no workshop.

Place the slab on a single flat uniform deep-navy field at #0B1220, one exact value everywhere, with
no vignette, no gradient, no texture, and no shadow cast onto it. Let that navy connect continuously
to all four image borders so a border-connected flood fill removes it in one pass, and leave at
least 24 px of clean matte on every side. Transparent background is equally acceptable.
```

**Flairs solved:** *Six Terraces* — solved by their **collapse**; this is the only frame in the run
where the ladder is shown by its own destruction. *Kiln Warp* — the bow is not decoration here, it
costs a full terrace of value across the face. *Raking Light* — promoted from a look to a rescue: the
shadow read is what keeps the object legible after the transmission dies. *Missing Clay* — the
brightest region is a sanding accident that carries no picture, so absence illuminates and says
nothing.

**Why this wins:** it is the only part in the pack where you can watch the material lose, and the two
images finally, flatly disagree — the shadow still knows the whole hill and the light has forgotten
almost all of it.

## PROMPT 4 — `wall.seam.grog.macro`
### logical part box: **512 × 1024 px** (vertical seam element; in-world ~40 mm of joint across the full 300 mm panel height)
### state: **MID-REVEAL, EDGE-ON.** On the two arrises: T1 and T2 only, read as shadow. Through the gap: a blade of light brighter than T6 and carrying no terraces at all.

```
Render a tight vertical macro of the joint where two grogged stoneware wall slabs meet and fail to,
filling the frame top to bottom, orthographic, no perspective, no room, no corridor, no floor.

Show only the two facing edges and the air between them. Cut the left slab's edge square and true;
cut the right slab's edge warped so that the two do not agree: let the gap between them run from
nearly closed at the top to a 4 mm wedge at the bottom, and make the wedge boundary hard on both
sides — no rounding, no soft contact, no ambient occlusion smudge.

Chip the right slab's arris in two places so the broken edge shows the body in section: expose the
thick end of the ladder, T1 at 6.0 mm and T2 at 5.2 mm, as raw fractured stoneware with visible grog
particles standing proud of the break like grit in a broken biscuit. Keep the ladder's full
definition legible — T1 = 6.0 mm, T2 = 5.2 mm, T3 = 4.4 mm, T4 = 3.6 mm, T5 = 2.8 mm, T6 = 2.0 mm,
0.8 mm risers, sharp arrises, no blending — and let the first two risers run away from the edge into
the frame's shallow depth as hard-shadowed steps.
```

# NEGATIVE-GATE SELF-CHECK
*Each of the six ICB clauses quoted, then how these four prompts avoid it.*

**1. "NOT printed/painted/decaled/projected onto porcelain — the relief IS the image."**
Every prompt carries the literal order *"Do not paint, print, decal or project any image onto it;
every feature must be geometry,"* and every prompt builds the picture as a carved six-thickness
contour field before any light exists. The near-miss in this pair is axis 3, since a neighbouring
panel throws light onto a slab in Prompt 2 — so Prompt 2 states outright that **no picture survives
the crossing**, that what arrives is banded illumination and never a projected picture, and that every
bar **breaks and steps sideways at every riser it crosses**. A projection rides over a surface; this
one is cut by it, which is the proof the relief is real geometry. **Clean.**

**2. "NOT alpha-gradient glow, bloom, god-rays, soft falloff, lens flare — light is CARVED in hard
terraces."**
All four prompts publish the six-value ladder numerically with 0.8 mm risers and sharp arrises and
forbid blending, ramping, feathering and dithering between terraces. Prompt 2 orders *"every
light-to-dark boundary in this image is a hard line"* and bans penumbra on the bars. Prompt 3 bans
bloom, halo, god-rays, flare and soft falloff by name. Prompt 4 bans bloom, halo, flare and shafts in
the air and gives the gap-blade knife edges. The only soft form permitted anywhere in the pair is a
**45° chamfer scraped by a clumsy hand** — a hard-edged facet, explicitly framed as damage, never a
gradient. **Clean.**

**3. "NOT a post-process filter over ordinary 3D."**
The banding is specified as **thickness in millimetres**, not as a tonal treatment: T1 = 6.0 mm down
to T6 = 2.0 mm. Prompt 1 proves the same height field with **no transmission at all**, purely as
shadow under an 8° rake, and Prompt 4 shows the ladder **in section at a fracture**. A filter cannot
produce either of those frames. The band structure is also disrupted per-instance by warp — one slab
loses a full terrace across its bow — which no uniform post-process pass would ever do. **Clean.**

**4. "NOT PBR metal, plastic, wet gloss, or subsurface skin — porcelain is matte, dry, slightly
sugary, and it TRANSMITS."**
Every prompt specifies unglazed, matte, dry, slightly sugary buff-grey stoneware with iron speckle and
explicitly excludes sheen, wet gloss, plastic, metal and skin-like scattering; Prompt 1 adds *"a
surface that would take chalk."* Transmission is present and is deliberately **grudging and granular**
— opaque grog flecks inside the light — which is the opposite of subsurface creaminess. Note on the
draw: grogged stoneware is not porcelain, so all four prompts treat it as a high-porcelain-fraction
stoneware thinned into lithophane range, which keeps the matte-dry-transmitting requirement while
honouring axis 1. **Clean.**

**5. "NOT a composed scene, room, HUD, typography, score, control or environment."**
Every prompt is a single separable part on a matte with a stated logical part box, and every prompt
bans floor, ceiling, corridor, room and perspective convergence by name. Prompt 4 additionally bans
frame, fixing and bracket, since that armature is P6's family. The typography risk in this pair is
real and specific — the image is a **survey**, and surveys carry numerals — so all four prompts carry
the explicit order *"no numerals, no lettering, no survey annotation, no legend, no symbols, no
interface."* **Clean.**

**6. "NOT a documentary of a real pottery workshop (L2 HIGH: realism loses to impossibility in
object-world fields)."**
No hands, no tools, no kiln, no bench, no workshop — banned by name in all four. The subject is not a
real object: it is a wall slab carrying the contour survey of a hill that was quarried away, lit by
another slab, with the brightest value in the pack being a gap rather than clay. That is the
impossibility gradient, not a document. **Clean.**

**Result: 6 / 6 clean. No blocking hit.**
