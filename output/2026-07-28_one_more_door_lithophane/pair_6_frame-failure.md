## THE PAIR'S THREE STRUCTURAL DECISIONS

Made before a word of prompt, because every prompt below hangs off them.

### Decision 1 — the armature is the one thing in this world that light cannot get through

Five pairs make light. This pair makes **obstruction**. That is not a consolation prize; it is the
only seat in the run from which the material's whole premise can be seen from outside.

Every other part in ONE MORE DOOR is a body that transmits. The frame is cast iron: it stops the
light absolutely, at every thickness, forever. It has no unlit state and no lit state — it has *one*
state, and its silhouette is identical in both. The armature is the **anti-lithophane**, and putting
it in the same frame as the clay is what proves the clay is doing something.

**The consequence that runs the whole pair:** the mend is made of the same stuff as the frame. An
iron staple driven across a fracture is a piece of armature *placed inside the picture*. So —

> **In a picture made of light, the repair is the dark.** Every staple is a line of image that will
> never come back. The mend is not a scar on the object; it is a subtraction from the image.

This is the pair's best idea and it is the inversion of the flair as usually reached for. Staple
Repair, in the tasteful reading, is a beautiful visible mending. Here it costs you five sentences
of her face, permanently, and it did not even close the crack — it only stopped the crack travelling.

### Decision 2 — the portrait is never whole in this pair

Axis 4 (*the person who carved it*) is shared with P2, and P2 owns the intact reading: her face on
the lamp. This pair therefore takes the **only** other structurally available position, and it is
native to a failure-states family:

> **She is never assembled in any frame this pair produces.** Prompt 1 has the frame she should be
> in and she is gone. Prompt 2 has her stapled back together with bars across her. Prompt 3 has her
> in section, where a face is 4 mm of nothing. Prompt 4 has her in seven pieces, deliberately laid
> out so no fragment sits next to its true neighbour.

**Likeness is continuity, not convergence.** P2 wrote the carver first and this pair adopts its
likeness verbatim — *a woman in her sixties, East Asian, broad-shouldered, hair pinned back, in a
collarless work smock, seen from the shoulders up and looking down.* Two parts in one game showing
the same person must show the same person. What must not be shared is the **treatment**, and it is
not: P2 has her intact and frontal on a lamp with her hands burned out by over-drive; this pair never
assembles her at all.

And the worn mould does the rest — but **not** by rounding off a named feature, because P2 already
took that move on the same axis draw (its worn tooling softens the corner of her mouth). This pair
takes the *systemic* consequence instead. A plaster mould loses depth unevenly across its whole face,
so a slab pressed late in that mould's life does not lose a detail; it loses **terraces**.

> **She does not arrive at one exposure.** One half of her is a six-step picture and the other half
> is a four-step picture — no T6 and no T5 anywhere on that side — and no candle setting can repair
> it, because the missing values were never pressed.

She is not being erased by damage. She is being erased by being copied, and the erasure is measured
in terraces.

The seam itself runs down the exact centre-line of her face as a proud ridge of clay. **The portrait
was divided before it ever broke.** The firing crack begins on that seam — and then leaves it, and
wanders into the thin terraces where the clay had least to hold with. It does not stay tidy. A
symmetrical crack down a centre-line is the tasteful version and the Context Skeptic would kill it.

### Decision 3 — how "a single candle, guttering" is rendered without a single soft edge

Axis 3 is the draw most likely to smuggle in a gate violation, because "candlelight" fetches glow.
Ruling, stated once and applied in all four prompts:

- **A candle flame is small.** At the working distances in this pair, its penumbra is negligible.
  Shadow edges stay hard. What a close point source *does* give — and a sun does not — is
  **divergence**: shadows are larger than their casters and splay outward from a single origin. That
  is the physically honest tell, and it costs no softness.
- **Guttering is not blur.** A flame that leans and stays does not smear the image; it moves one
  terrace across its exposure threshold. So the candle's mortality appears as **one band caught
  half-open along its own length, breaking at a hard vertical** — the same carved terrace, open on
  one side of a line and dead on the other. A step, not a fade. That is The Dying Body written into
  the picture instead of into a meter.
- **A candle leaves a body behind.** Carbon. Soot lies in the trough floors of the deepest cuts and
  nowhere else — not on risers, not on plateaus, because it falls. So the deepest carvings read
  darker than their thickness alone would give.

  > The light you spend to read the picture is slowly filling the picture in.

  Awe and dread in one deposit. Used in Prompts 1, 2 and 4.

**Held apart from P1, which draws the same candle.** P1 reaches carbon too, and it had to — soot is
the honest residue of this axis and neither pair invented it. The two are separated by **where it
lies and what it costs**. P1's soot is a surface patch with a scalloped tide line, and its job is to
record that somebody already spent light on that door. This pair's soot is governed by gravity: it
sits in trough floors only, never on a riser, never on a plateau, so it does not mark a surface — it
**removes brightness from the deepest cuts specifically**, which is exactly where the ladder was most
expensive to carve. Same residue, opposite consequence: theirs records a visit, this one takes values
back. The light-boundary devices are cleanly different too — P1 solves the candle's reach as one
hard-edged scalloped ellipse across which *every* terrace drops a step, a spatial shape; this pair
solves the candle's *mortality* as a **single** terrace caught half-open at a hard vertical, a moment.

# STEP 07 — ARTISTIC GUIDES
*Six guides. Four map to the assigned variation angles; two are exploratory and are consumed at
step 10 to strengthen the assigned four. Each carries mood, light, palette, story cue.*

**G1 · THE SEAT (assigned angle 1).**
One door armature, dead-on, orthographic, with no panel in it. Mood: patient dread with nothing yet
to dread — a hole cut in iron that expects something. Light: one small warm point source low at
image-left, 10° above the plane, close; hard shadows, splayed. Palette: blackened iron, matte
carbon, one fringe of warm bone-white, navy void. Story cue: **the panel is gone but its silhouette
is still here** — years of candle smoke blacked the rebate everywhere the panel did not cover, so
the seat carries a clean grey rectangle of bare metal, the exact shape of what is missing, edges
slightly ragged where a warped panel let smoke under. The only picture in this frame is made
entirely of absence, and it is not on clay at all.

**G2 · THE MENDED FACE (assigned angle 2).**
The same armature with a panel seated in it, lit from behind, the panel cracked and stapled. Mood:
awe and dread arriving in the same second — the object is beautiful and five parts of it are gone.
Light: candle behind the panel at the exposure that is correct for bone-china and no brighter.
Palette: cream-white through amber to deep oxblood-brown in the clay; absolute iron black in the
staples; navy. Story cue: five black bars lie across a woman's face and one of them crosses her
lowered eye. The crack still leaks a hairline of unstoppable light because a fracture has no
thickness — but the frame's **subject is the dark**, not the bright.

**G3 · THE SECTION (assigned angle 3).**
A physical cut straight through the assembly, edge-on, laid horizontally. Mood: awe at a millimetre,
delivered as a fact. Light: a raking key from above-left at 12° for the cut faces; one small
backlight at the far right behind the thin end only. Palette: near-monochrome iron and bone with one
warm edge. Story cue: **the thing that holds the picture is 14 mm of solid iron; the picture is 4 mm
of clay.** The armature is three times the substance of everything it exists to serve, and the
section is the only frame where you can see that both objects are being measured by the same ruler.

**G4 · THE SCATTER (assigned angle 4).**
Seven fragments of one shattered panel, individually backlit, laid out in a row with none of them
adjacent to its true neighbour. Mood: the vitrine after the accident. Light: one warm point source
behind the sheet plane, common to all seven. Palette: seven warm-lit shards on navy, one of them
burned to featureless white, two of them dead black. Story cue: the picture is complete across the
sheet and whole nowhere in it — and two fragments are **still stapled to each other**, so the mend
outlived the object it was made to save.

**G5 · THE STAPLE, MACRO (exploratory).**
Extreme macro of one iron staple bridging a lit crack: drilled pockets, sunk legs, the backs of the
legs standing proud, absolute black across a field of amber. Mood: the mend as a wound. Verdict:
**the best single image in the pair and it cannot stand alone** — at this magnification the reader
loses the ladder, loses the face, and loses the proof that the black thing is iron and not a painted
stripe. **Consumed into G3** at step 10: the section shows the same staple with its legs sunk to
4.5 mm inside a 6.0 mm body, and the section is where "how nearly this went through" becomes visible.

**G6 · THE RACK OF EMPTY FRAMES (exploratory).**
Six armatures in a row, all empty. Mood: the corridor as inventory. Verdict: **cut, twice over.** It
is P4's vitrine strategy in a different material — a straight motif collision — and at 16 px six iron
rectangles are six iron rectangles. **Consumed into G1** at step 10 as a discipline only: one frame,
large, and everything the rack was going to say by repetition gets said instead by the soot ghost,
which is repetition compressed into a single object's history.

# STEP 09 — ARTIST-REFINED PASS

**Refining voice: after Louise Nevelson (1899–1988)** — chosen for step 09's artist loop, deliberately
*not* one of the 18 panel seats. Grounding: the documented assemblage wall practice — compartmented
constructions unified under a single matte black, where every form is read exclusively by the shadow
it casts inside its own box, and where the black is not a colour applied to objects but the condition
that turns objects into one object.

**Second grounding, a documented craft tradition rather than a person: staple-repaired ceramic
(*ju ci* / 鋦瓷).** Grounding: the historical East Asian practice of drilling blind pockets either
side of a fracture in a vessel and bridging them with sunk metal staples, so the mend is structural,
visible, and permanent. This is the exact physical vocabulary axis 5 draws, and it is the reason the
staples in this pair have **drilled pockets and sunk legs** rather than being clamps laid on a surface.

> *Panel and artist voices in this document are model-generated interpretive constructs "after" named
> source figures' published work and documented traditions. No statement is a quotation of, or
> endorsement by, the named person.*

**What the Nevelson read changes in every prompt:**

1. **Stop rendering iron as metal. Render it as the absence of light.** The single most useful
   instruction in this pair is not "cast iron" — it is *hold the armature at one dead matte value
   whose brightest point is still darker than the darkest clay in the frame.* That resolves the PBR
   clause, kills the chrome reflex, and makes the pair legible in one move.
2. **The armature's job is to make a compartment.** So its inner arris must cut the lit field to an
   exact hard rectangle with nothing spilling past it — the frame is what gives the light an edge.
3. **A black object with no highlight is read entirely by its own cast shadow.** So the raking key
   is not atmosphere in this pair; it is the only reason the iron has form at all. Under-light it and
   the armature becomes a silhouette hole.

**Friction from the supplied ledger, applied:**

- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, CONCEPT (after Loos)** on R1: *"an empty frame" is craft-as-alibi in its purest
  form — a beautiful nothing, and you will tell yourself the emptiness is meaningful.* → **Accepted
  in full.** Fix: the frame is not empty. It holds (a) a **fringe of snapped bone-china still gripped
  in the rebate all the way round**, so the ladder is present in section as shadow, and (b) a **soot
  ghost** — a hard clean rectangle of bare metal inside a field of carbon, the exact shape of what
  left. R1 now contains the ladder and a picture; neither is what the frame was built to hold.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, MEDIUM (after Muratori)** on all six: *publish the terrace numbers or every render
  invents its own.* → Fix: the six-value ladder is stated numerically and identically in every prompt
  — T1 6.0 mm through T6 2.0 mm, 0.8 mm risers, sharp arris — **the same numbers P4 published**, on
  purpose. One corridor, one ladder. What changes with the body is not the geometry but the *correct
  exposure*: bone-china is far more translucent than grog, so this pair runs its candle roughly one
  stop lower and states so in each spec header.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, MEDIUM** on R2: *"candlelight" is where the alpha ban dies in this pair.* → Fix:
  glow, bloom, halo, flare, god-rays, haze and soft falloff are banned by name in all four; the
  candle appears only as shadow divergence, hard shadow edges, one threshold-caught terrace and
  carbon deposit. The word *flicker* is banned from this pair's vocabulary because it fetches motion
  blur; the instruction is a **hard vertical break in one band**.
- **THE RELIEF (after Whiteread)** on R1: *if the bright thing is the missing clay, then the soot
  ghost is the same argument at the armature scale and you should say it that way.* → Fix: the ghost
  is specified as a deposit shadow with a hard boundary, ragged along the two edges where a warped
  panel let smoke under — absence doing the drawing, with the warp as its pen.
- **THE THICKNESS (after the Wedgwood lineage)** on R3: *a section is the only chance the whole run
  gets to prove the ladder is thickness and not tone — do not waste it on a pretty edge.* → Fix: the
  section carries the armature and the panel at the same scale in the same cut, so the 14 mm of iron
  and the 4 mm of clay are measured against each other with no annotation.
- **THE ARCHIVE (after Sugimoto)** on R4: *seven fragments risk reading as seven objects rather than
  one loss.* → Fix: the fracture faces are **fresh, clean and unsooted** while the carved troughs are
  **black with carbon** — so a single frame carries two ages, and the eye is told the blinding is old
  and the break is new.
- **THE ONE SENTENCE (after Clark)** on R2 and R4: *both are sayable, and R4 is sayable in fewer
  words.* → Recorded in the step-10 ranking.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, CONTEXT (after Kalman)** on R2: *"visible mending" is the most tasteful phrase
  available to this pair and it is the one that will kill it — kintsugi with the gold filed off is
  still kintsugi.* → Fix: the staples are **not beautiful and not gold**. They are dead matte iron,
  they take a piece of a face each, and the prompt is instructed to make the reader notice the loss
  before the craft. Literal, not tasteful.
- **THE THUMBNAIL (after Kare)** on R1 and R3: *at 16 px, an iron rectangle is a rectangle and a
  section strip is a line.* → **Accepted, not fixed.** R1 and R3 are library parts and are
  disqualified as hero frames. R4 carries the thumbnail law for this pair; R2 backs it. Recorded in
  Problems.

**Why G4 outranks G2, stated plainly, because it is not the obvious call.** G2 is the more complete
image and the more moving one. But seven lit shards against dark is a **constellation** at 16 px,
where one lit rectangle is a rectangle; and G4 is the only frame in the pair where one exposure is
provably wrong in two opposite ways at once — a fragment that is all thick clay and a fragment that
has almost no clay left, both dead at the same setting, one because there is too much body and one
because the candle's own carbon has filled it. Synthesis point 4 gets proved rather than described,
and it gets proved on this pair's own side of the argument: both failures here are **darkenings**.
G2 remains the emotional hero and the run's best single object; G4 is the one that survives a phone.

**The two syntheses, stated:**

- **Prompt 1 = G1 + G6.** G6 died twice (motif collision with P4's vitrine, and it is nothing at
  thumbnail) but left one usable rule: *one object, large, with enough pixels to carry its own
  history.* G1 absorbs it and stops trying to say "many" by counting. What the rack would have said
  by repetition, the soot ghost says by duration — the panel sat in this seat long enough to mask
  years of carbon, and that is repetition compressed into one object.
- **Prompt 3 = G3 + G5.** G3 alone is a measured fact and no more. G5's staple is grafted into the
  cut so the section stops being a specification and becomes a near-miss: **the legs are sunk to
  4.5 mm inside a 6.0 mm body, leaving 1.5 mm of clay under each tip.** The frame now shows that the
  repair very nearly destroyed what it saved, which is the same sentence the whole pair is making,
  said in millimetres.

**Editorial rules imposed across all four at this pass:**

1. The ladder text, the no-penumbra order, the annotation ban and the matte contract are **verbatim
   identical** in all four prompts. These are the mechanical clauses; convergence there is intended
   and corridor-wide.
2. Every motif-carrying sentence is written in a shape no other pair's draw would produce. This
   pair's motif vocabulary is **iron, soot in trough floors, sunk staple, saw cut, snapped arris,
   shallow-pressed half**. None of it appears in P3's (pebble, sgraffito, glaze pool, chip) or P4's
   (grog, print striation, contour survey, seam wedge).
3. **One deliberate divergence from P4, forced.** P4's Prompt 4 lands on *the brightest value is a
   gap and it carries no picture.* This pair could trivially land on the same sentence via the crack.
   It does not: the crack is written as a **hairline**, held deliberately modest, and every prompt
   states that the frame's subject is **the black across the picture, not the bright inside it.**
   Where P4's failure out-shines the image, this pair's failure **out-darkens** it. Opposite move,
   same flair family, no collision. Enforced down to the cell: Prompt 4's cell 7 is the fragment
   with the least clay on the sheet and it is the darkest clay in the frame, because the candle's
   carbon got there first. The one place in this pair that could have burned out, doesn't.
4. An earlier draft of Prompt 2 had a slot of light escaping between a warped panel corner and its
   rebate. **Cut** — it is P4's gap-blade with the serial numbers filed off. Replaced with the
   physically better answer: the standing corner sits *further from the candle*, so it loses a full
   terrace of value across that corner. The warp costs exposure instead of leaking light.

## PROMPT 1 — THE SEAT

```
SPEC — part key: frame/armature_01__empty__candle_shadow
Logical part box: 1024 x 1536 px, single part centred, minimum 24 px of clear field on every side.
In-world: outer 380 x 540 mm, opening 300 x 460 mm, members 40 mm wide and 14 mm deep, rebate lip
5 mm.
State: UNLIT / SHADOW. Nothing in this frame transmits.
Terraces legible: zero as light. T1 through T6 legible as SECTION ONLY, along the snapped inner
edge of the china fringe still gripped in the rebate — six stepped thicknesses read end-on as a
staircase of shadow, coarse enough to count.
Exposure verdict: there is nothing here to expose. The only correct exposure for this part is the
raking one, and the frame must be worth reading with no light spent.
Axis draws in force: bone-china as the broken material · pressed from a worn plaster mould with
seams visible · a single candle guttering · the image is of the person who carved it (absent from
this frame, and its absence is the subject) · repaired with visible staples.

Render one door armature as a single isolated part on a flat field. Not a scene, not a wall, not a
doorway.

Build a rectangular iron armature with four members halved together at the corners: 380 mm tall
overall, 280 mm wide overall, each member 40 mm wide and 14 mm deep, cut all the way round its
inner edge with a 5 mm rebate — a seat, a step of iron that a slab would drop into. Show it dead-on
and orthographic with its face exactly parallel to the image plane. Use no perspective convergence,
no vanishing point, no floor, no ceiling, no wall, no corridor, no door leaf, no handle.

Make the iron a light-eater. Render it as sand-cast iron, later tinned and then blackened by long
use: completely matte, dry, faintly powdery, absorbing. Give it no specular highlights, no
reflections, no environment mirroring, no polished sheen, no chrome, no rim-light. Hold the whole
armature at one dead low value and keep its brightest point darker than the darkest clay anywhere
in the frame. It has form only because of the shadow it casts.

Carry the evidence that it was moulded. Run a raised flash line down the outer edge of every member
where the two halves of a worn sand mould met, and let a hand have dressed that flash off unevenly
— proud and untouched along some stretches, filed flat and slightly bright-grey along others,
skipped entirely at the corners.

Now put the picture in the seat, and make it out of what is not there. Lay matte black candle
carbon over the whole rebate floor, then hold a hard-edged clean rectangle of bare grey metal in
the middle of it, exactly the size and shape of the slab that used to sit there, because the slab
masked the smoke for years. Cut the boundary of that rectangle hard on two edges and let it go
slightly ragged and irregular on the other two, where a warped slab did not seal and smoke crept
under. The only image in this frame is the silhouette of the missing thing.

Leave the slab's remains gripped in the rebate all the way around the opening: a fringe of broken
bone-china between 6 and 22 mm wide, its outer edge still bedded in hardened putty and its inner
edge a jagged snapped arris running the whole perimeter. Cut that snapped edge so the body's
thicknesses are readable end-on. Hold the body to six thicknesses and nothing between them —
T1 6.0 mm, T2 5.2 mm, T3 4.4 mm, T4 3.6 mm, T5 2.8 mm, T6 2.0 mm — with each step falling to the
next across a vertical 0.8 mm riser cut to a sharp arris. Do not blend, ramp, feather or dither
between thicknesses anywhere.

Give the china fringe the warm bone-white of unglazed high-translucency porcelain: matte, dry,
slightly sugary, with a fresh clean fracture face along the break and old candle carbon lying in
the trough floors of the deepest cuts and nowhere else — never on a riser, never on a plateau.

Press the history of hands into the bedding putty along the rebate floor: many overlapping
thumb-marks in the hardened putty, different sizes, at different angles, at least one pressed
squarely over another, each one a shallow dished oval with a fine ridge pattern that the raking
light picks out. Different people seated slabs in this frame for years and none of them are here.

Mend one corner and leave the mend crooked. Open the halved joint at the lower left by about
1.5 mm, bridge it with a flat iron strap and four sunk rivets, and set the strap a few degrees out
of square with the frame so it reads as done in a hurry by someone who could not get it right.

Light the part with one small warm point source at image-left, low, 10 degrees above the plane of
the frame, and nothing else. Place it close, so every shadow splays outward from a single origin
```

# NEGATIVE-GATE SELF-CHECK
*Each of the six ICB clauses quoted, then how these four prompts avoid it.*

**1. "NOT printed/painted/decaled/projected onto porcelain — the relief IS the image."**
Every prompt carries an explicit order — *"Nothing here is applied. Every mark on the clay is a
change of thickness; if it could not be cut, it does not exist"* in Prompts 1 and 2, and *"Build it
only as thickness. Nothing here is applied; every mark is a change of depth"* in Prompts 3 and 4 —
plus a literal ban on paint, print, decal, stencil and projection in all four. The near-miss in this
pair is the **soot ghost** in Prompt 1, which could be rendered as a painted rectangle. It is
specified as a *deposit shadow*: carbon lying over a rebate floor with a clean rectangle of bare
metal where a slab masked it, hard on two edges and ragged on the other two where the warp let smoke
under. That boundary can only be produced by an object having sat there, never by a brush. Soot in
the troughs is also constrained by gravity — trough floors only, never risers, never plateaus — so
it reads as fall and not as line. **Clean.**

**2. "NOT alpha-gradient glow, bloom, god-rays, soft falloff, lens flare — light is CARVED in hard
terraces."**
All four prompts publish the ladder numerically with 0.8 mm risers and sharp arrises and forbid
blending, ramping, feathering and dithering. All four ban glow, bloom, halo, haze, god-rays, light
shafts, lens flare and soft luminous falloff by name, and ban bokeh, shallow depth of field and soft
focus so the edges cannot be lost to lens behaviour instead. The pair's specific hazard is that a
candle fetches softness, so the candle is written entirely as **hard physics**: shadows splay from
one close origin and are larger than their casters; every shadow edge is a knife; guttering is *one
terrace open on one side of a hard vertical and dead on the other*, with smearing, blurring,
streaking and gradients banned in that sentence. The word *flicker* is deliberately absent from all
four prompts because it fetches motion blur. **Clean.**

**3. "NOT a post-process filter over ordinary 3D."**
The banding is specified as **millimetres of thickness**, never as tone. Prompt 1 proves the ladder
with **zero transmission**, purely as a snapped edge read end-on under a 10° rake. Prompt 3 shows the
ladder as a **physical staircase in profile with a saw cut through it** and a staple leg sunk 4.5 mm
into a 6.0 mm body. Prompt 4 shows it end-on again at conchoidal fracture faces. No post-process pass
can produce a section, a fracture profile, or a mould seam that costs 0.4 mm of brightness. The band
structure is also disrupted per-object — a warped corner drops a full terrace in Prompt 2 because it
moved further from the source — which no uniform filter would ever do. All four additionally ban
photographic filters, film grain, chromatic aberration and colour grade outright. **Clean.**

**4. "NOT PBR metal, plastic, wet gloss, or subsurface skin — porcelain is matte, dry, slightly
