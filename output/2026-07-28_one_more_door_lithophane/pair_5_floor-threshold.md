## THE PAIR'S FOUR STRUCTURAL DECISIONS

Taken before a word of prompt, because every sentence downstream hangs off them.

### Decision 1 — the celadon inverts the object, and that is the whole pair

This is the finding. A celadon glaze is a thin iron-bearing skin over a white body. It behaves in
two documented ways that matter here: **it pools thick in a hollow and thins to nothing over an
edge**, and where it thins over an arris it **breaks to white-rust**.

Now put that glaze over a gouged lithophane and the consequences are violent:

- The **deepest gouges** — the thinnest clay, therefore the **brightest** terraces when lit — are
  exactly where the glaze **pools thickest**, therefore the **darkest** areas when unlit.
- The **untouched plateaus** — the thickest clay, therefore **dead black** when lit — carry the
  **thinnest** glaze, therefore read **palest** when unlit.

> **Unlit, this floor is a photographic negative of itself lit.** The dark map and the bright map
> are the same carving read backwards. Not "two images that need not agree" — two images that
> *cannot* agree, by the physics of the skin.

That is the panel's synthesis point 2 satisfied without anyone deciding to satisfy it, and it is a
solution no other pair can reach, because no other pair drew a glaze.

Second consequence, free: because the glaze breaks white-rust on every arris, **the terrace
boundaries draw themselves as hard pale contour lines even in total darkness.** The Concept
Skeptic's blank-slab objection dies here in a way it does not die anywhere else in this run — P4
answers it with shadow, which needs a light; this pair answers it with **colour, which needs
nothing.** An unlit tile in this family is a contour map in white on jade.

### Decision 2 — "the corridor one moment later," carved into a floor, is your next footfall

Axis 4 is a trap. A corridor rendered into a tile is a **scene inside a part**, and gate clause 5
is blocking. The axis has to be honoured at the scale of the part or not at all.

A floor, one moment later, differs from a floor now by exactly one thing: **where your foot lands
next.** So the transmitted image on every tile in this family is a **single unworn footfall
hollow, carved thin, sitting ahead of the wear.** The clay knows the step you have not taken.

That is literal rather than tasteful, which is the Context Skeptic's whole demand, and it is
legible at 16 px as one bright foot-shape in a dark square.

And it sets up the pair's disagreement precisely:

- **Unlit**, raking light finds the **worn tread** — a scuffed traffic path where generations of
  feet abraded the glaze clean off and exposed raw white body. That is the floor's *surface claim*:
  *this is where people went.*
- **Lit**, transmission finds the **carved footfall**, which is **not on the worn path.** That is
  what is actually true: *this is where you go next.*

The floor has a history and a prediction and they point different ways. The player paid light to
learn that.

Third free consequence, and it is the sharpest image in the pair: the worn path **has no glaze
left on it**, so when the light comes through, the traffic path does **not** turn green. It comes
out the raw colour of whatever is underneath, while the entire rest of the tile turns sea-green.
**The path of everyone who came before glows a different colour from the picture.**

### Decision 3 — the wheel gives a floor tile a spiral, and a spiral cannot repeat

Axis 2 says *wheel-thrown*, and a floor tile is square. That is not a contradiction to work around,
it is a manufacturing story: each tile is **thrown as a wide shallow disc on the wheel, then cut
square from the disc while leather-hard.** Every tile therefore carries, under everything else, a
continuous **throwing spiral at ~1.5 mm pitch** left by the potter's fingers climbing the clay —
and the square cut runs straight across it, proving each square was once a circle.

The spiral does the work three ways:
- It is a **single continuous line**, so it is structurally incapable of being a findable repeat.
- Its **origin is never centred** — no potter centres perfectly — so each tile's spiral eye sits in
  a different quadrant, and a floor laid from these tiles never rhymes.
- The gouging is cut **across** it, so every terrace floor still carries the spiral and every riser
  interrupts it — evidence, under raking light, that the carving came second.

### Decision 4 — the thing under the floor is never shown, only its bad manners

Axis 3, *something alive and dim that is not identified*, is the draw most likely to produce either
a cliché or a gate violation. Ruling, applied in all four prompts:

1. **The source is never in frame.** Not a creature, not a limb, not a silhouette with a shape you
   could name. Nothing is under the floor in the picture; the floor is the whole part.
2. **The tell is a boundary that ignores the geometry.** The transmission satisfies the terrace
   ladder to *different depths in different regions of the same tile*, and the boundary between
   those regions is a **hard knife edge that follows no carved line.** Ordinary backlighting cannot
   do that. Something with a body and an opinion can.
3. **The second tell is over-drive.** In one small region the tile is pushed past what its
   thinnest terrace can carry and burns to featureless white — because whatever is down there came
   close. Burn-Out, local, and it is the dread.
4. **The third tell is occlusion with no name.** One dark region where the light is *blocked* by
   something not carved, hard-edged, and explicitly forbidden from resolving into a recognisable
   form.

Warmth in, green out: the source is warm before the glaze and green after it. Colour is the only
thing about it we are allowed to learn.

# STEP 09 — ARTIST-REFINED PASS

**Primary refining voice: after Katharine Pleydell-Bouverie (1895–1985).** Chosen for step 09's
artist loop and deliberately *not* one of the 18 panel seats. Grounding: the documented ash-glaze
research programme — hundreds of small test tiles, each fired and then recorded against exactly
what had been burned to make its glaze, treating the finished surface as a **readable record of a
specific fire and a specific plant** rather than as a chosen colour.

**Secondary voice, consulted for R3 only: after the Cosmati pavement workshops (role-anchored,
12th–13th c. Rome).** Grounding: the documented practice of setting salvaged stone of unrelated
origins into a single floor, with joints that are visibly negotiated rather than designed — the
tradition in which *badly met materials* are the honest condition of a floor, not a defect in it.

### What the Pleydell-Bouverie read changes in every prompt

1. **Colour is evidence, never decoration.** Every green in these prompts must be caused, on the
   page, by a stated glaze thickness, which is itself caused by a stated gouge depth. Where the
   prompt says *deep green*, it must first have said *deep gouge*. A colour that arrives without a
   cause is craft-as-alibi and the Concept Skeptic is right about it.
2. **State the order of making.** Thrown, cut square, gouged, glazed, fired, cracked, walked on.
   The renderer builds an object with a history instead of a square with features.
3. **The break-line is the signature and it is free.** Do not ask for contour lines. Ask for a
   glaze thin enough on every arris that it breaks — the contour lines are then *inevitable*, and
   they are inevitable in the asset rather than in a filter.

### Friction from the supplied ledger, applied

- **THE RELIEF (after Whiteread)** on R1: *if the bright thing is absent material, then a glazed
  lithophane inverts your own doctrine — the missing clay is the darkest area on the unlit tile.*
  → **Not fixed, promoted.** The inversion is stated explicitly in R1 and R2 and is the pair's
  central claim. Bright is still absent material; the skin just makes you pay light to see it.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, MEDIUM (after Muratori)** on all four: *publish the numbers or every render
  invents its own.* → The eight-row ladder above appears in every prompt as identical numeric text,
  including the worn-tread row, which is the row most likely to be improvised otherwise.
- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, MEDIUM** on R2: *"lit from beneath" plus "something alive" is one careless
  adjective from bioluminescent glow, which is the alpha ban's death.* → The words *glow, aura,
  halo, shimmer, radiance* are banned from this pair's vocabulary. The source is never in frame,
  the illumination boundary is a **knife edge**, and the tell that it is alive is that the boundary
  **follows none of the carving** — a geometric fact, not an atmospheric one.
- **THE KILN (after Leach)** on R1 and R4: *the fire is a collaborator, so the crack is not damage
  applied afterwards — it is a decision the fire made, and the glaze was still moving when it
  happened.* → Hence celadon **ran into the open crack while molten**, choking some stretches and

- **THE FALLOFF (after Swink)** on R2: *if the terraces arrive partially, the player must still be
  able to count them.* → Every partially-arrived terrace is specified as a **flat plateau at a

- **HYPER-SKEPTIC, CONTEXT (after Kalman)** on my first draft of G2, which read *the floor

## PROMPT 1 — `floor.tile.celadon.unlit`
### logical part box: **1024 × 1024 px** — one floor tile, square to the frame; in-world 300 × 300 mm, 12 mm apron, carved central field
### state: **UNLIT / SHADOW + GLAZE.** Zero terraces legible as transmitted light. **All six terraces legible without any light at all**, by pooled glaze colour and by the white-rust break-line on every arris. The seventh, unauthored wear terrace legible as abraded raw body.
### axis draws in force: celadon-glazed body · wheel-thrown then hand-gouged · the unnamed dim living source (absent from this frame — it has not been spent yet) · the corridor one moment later (present as the carved footfall, unlit and therefore silent) · a firing crack (present only as a hairline that has not yet been paid for)

```
Build this object in the order it was actually made, and let every later step be caused by an
earlier one.

First throw a wide shallow disc of white porcelaneous stoneware on a wheel, leaving a continuous
finger-drawn spiral ridge at 1.5 mm pitch across the whole face, and place the spiral's eye
off-centre in the upper-left quadrant. Then cut a 300 mm square out of that disc while the clay is
leather-hard, so the straight edges slice cleanly across the spiral and prove the square was once
a circle. Leave a 12 mm-thick unmarked apron 20 mm wide around all four edges.

Now gouge the central field with a loop tool to exactly six thicknesses and no others: T1 =
5.6 mm, T2 = 4.8 mm, T3 = 4.0 mm, T4 = 3.2 mm, T5 = 2.4 mm, T6 = 1.6 mm. Cut every riser between
terraces as a vertical 0.8 mm wall with a sharp unbroken arris. Do not blend, ramp, feather or
dither between any two terraces anywhere. Cut the terraces across the throwing spiral so the
spiral still runs along every terrace floor and is interrupted at every riser — the carving must
visibly have come second.

Shape what you gouge as a single foot-shaped hollow, roughly 270 mm long, carved thinnest at its
ball and heel, with the terraces nesting outward from it in irregular offset rings that follow the
foot's outline rather than making circles. Do not draw a shoe, a tread pattern, a print, or a
mark of any kind — this is a shallow hollow in a floor, described only by its six carved
elevations.

Then dip the whole face in a dry, matte celadon and let it settle the way a glaze actually
```

# NEGATIVE-GATE SELF-CHECK
*Each of the six ICB clauses quoted, then how these four prompts avoid it.*

**1. "NOT printed/painted/decaled/projected onto porcelain — the relief IS the image."**
All four prompts carry the explicit order *"Do not paint, print, decal, transfer or project any
picture onto the tile: every feature is geometry or glaze thickness,"* and all four build the object
as a **causal chain of making** — thrown, cut, gouged, glazed, fired, worn — so that no visual
feature exists without a physical cause stated earlier in the same prompt. This pair's specific
near-miss is the glaze, because a coloured skin is one careless sentence from decoration: the
counter-move is that **every green in every prompt is caused on the page by a gouge depth**, and
every pale contour line is caused by an arris thinning the glaze until it breaks. A painted tile
cannot produce a white-rust line that appears only on arrises, and it cannot produce a colour map
that inverts when the light moves behind it. **Clean.**

**2. "NOT alpha-gradient glow, bloom, god-rays, soft falloff, lens flare — light is CARVED in hard
terraces."**
The six-value ladder is published numerically in all four prompts — T1 5.6 mm through T6 1.6 mm,
0.8 mm risers, sharp arrises — with blending, ramping, feathering and dithering forbidden by name
between any two terraces. Prompts 2, 3 and 4 additionally ban glow, aura, halo, bloom, god-rays,
lens flare, shafts in the air, haze and soft falloff, and each states that **every boundary in the
image is a hard line**, including the hue boundaries the celadon introduces and the boundary where
the light stops at the threshold. The words *glow, aura, halo, shimmer* and *radiance* were removed
from the pair's working vocabulary at step 09. Prompt 2's illumination limit is specified as a
knife edge; Prompt 4's crack has hard fracture walls and switches colour along hard boundaries.
The only rounded form permitted anywhere in the pair is a **riser worn down to a stub by traffic**,
which is explicitly framed as damage with a fresh break-line either side of it. **Clean.**

**3. "NOT a post-process filter over ordinary 3D."**
The banding is specified as **clay thickness in millimetres**, and the colour is specified as
**glaze thickness governed by that clay thickness**. Prompt 1 proves the entire height field with
**zero transmission** — the terraces are legible purely as pooled colour and break-lines under an
8° rake — which no tonal filter can generate, since a filter has no access to where an arris is.
Prompt 3 shows the ladder **in section at a chipped lip**, with the glaze visible as a distinct
layer over the body. Prompt 4 **misregisters the entire band structure by 3 mm across a fault**,
which is a per-object geometric event that a uniform pass could never produce. Prompt 2's
illumination boundary deliberately **disagrees with the geometry**, which is only meaningful
because the geometry exists independently. **Clean.**

**4. "NOT PBR metal, plastic, wet gloss, or subsurface skin — porcelain is matte, dry, slightly
sugary, and it TRANSMITS."**
Every prompt specifies a **dry, matte celadon** with no specular highlight, no mirror, no wet sheen
and no reflection of the source, and states that colour comes from glaze depth rather than from
reflection; plastic, metallic and skin-like responses are excluded by name in all four. Exposed
raw body — inside the worn track, at the wiped-back band, at the chipped lip and on the fracture
face — is specified matte, dry and slightly sugary. Transmission is present in three of the four
and is always delivered as **flat hard-edged plateaus**, which is the opposite of subsurface
creaminess. **This is the pair's most honest tension and it is named in Problems below**: a glossy
celadon would breach this clause outright, so the prompts demand the satin-to-dry end of the glaze
family and forbid the reflective end explicitly rather than hoping. **Clean, conditionally — the
condition is stated in the prompt text, not assumed.**

**5. "NOT a composed scene, room, HUD, typography, score, control or environment."**
Every prompt is a single separable part on a matte with a stated logical part box, and every prompt
bans wall, doorway, room, corridor, adjacent tile, grout, skirting and ceiling by name. Prompt 3 is
the risk case, since a threshold implies a door: it therefore permits only **the bottom 20 mm of an
unglazed foot** and bans the door, its panel image, its carving, its frame, its hinge, its hardware
and any threshold plate. The typography risk in this pair is lower than P4's — nothing here is a
survey — but the order *"no numerals, no lettering, no annotation, no legend, no symbols, no
arrows, no interface"* is carried verbatim in all four anyway, and the footfall is explicitly
forbidden from becoming a tread pattern, a print or a mark, which is the one place a glyph could
sneak in. **Clean.**

**6. "NOT a documentary of a real pottery workshop (L2 HIGH: realism loses to impossibility in
object-world fields)."**
No hands, no feet, no tools, no kiln, no shelf, no workshop, no bench — banned by name in all four.
Every prompt describes a *making history*, which is not the same as depicting a studio: the
consequences are in the object, the process is nowhere in frame. And the object itself is not

# PROBLEMS — reported honestly

1. **Celadon fights the material clause, and the fix is narrow.** Axis 1 draws a *glaze*, and the
   negative gate says porcelain is matte and dry. A normal reduction celadon is a glassy, satin-to-
   glossy surface — that is most of why people fire it. The prompts resolve this by demanding the
   **dry/matte end of the celadon family** and banning specular highlight, mirror and wet sheen by
   name. Those glazes are real but they are a narrow band, and this is the instruction most likely
   to be quietly ignored by the renderer. **If any returned image shows a reflection of the light
   source in the green, it is a gate-4 failure and must be rerolled, not accepted.** I believe the
   prompts as written; I do not believe the renderer will obey them first time.

2. **Axis 4 is honoured at the scale of the part, not literally.** *"The corridor you are standing
   in, one moment later"* cannot be a picture of a corridor inside a floor tile — that is a scene
   inside a part and gate clause 5 is blocking. It is rendered instead as the one thing about a
   floor that changes in a moment: **the next footfall.** I think this is the stronger reading and
   it is certainly the shippable one, but it is a reinterpretation and should be recorded as such
   rather than presented as compliance.

3. **Axis 3 is the highest reroll risk in the pack.** *"Something alive and dim that is not
   identified"* is one hallucination away from a creature under the floor. Prompt 2 bans lamp,
   filament, creature, limb, shape and silhouette, and forbids the occlusion from resolving into a
   recognisable form — but a generative renderer wants to draw a thing when you describe a thing's
   effects. Expect to discard frames where the occlusion has grown a hand.

4. **Prompt 1 is not a hero frame, though it fails the thumbnail law less badly than an unlit part
   normally would.** The Thumbnail seat is right that a pale unlit slab at 16 px is nothing — but
   this pair's unlit state carries a **hue** contrast (white-rust lines on bottle-green) rather than
   only a value contrast, so it survives downscaling as a legible contour map. It is still a
   library part. Prompts 2 and 4 carry the thumbnail law.

5. **Prompt 3 borrows one edge of another pair's family and I want that on the record.** A
   threshold needs something on the far side of it, and the far side is a door — P1's part family.
   The borrow is held to the bottom 20 mm of an unglazed foot with no panel, no image and no
   hardware, which is the minimum that makes the joint legible. If P1 ships a door foot with a
   different profile or a different body, this part will not seat against it and the two should be
   reconciled at QA rather than in the renderer.

6. **The "two states, always" rule is satisfied here but only for two of four prompts.** Prompts 1
   and 2 are a genuine unlit/lit pair off one identical height field, which is the rule as written.
   Prompts 3 and 4 are single-state parts with no partner, because the assigned angles specify four
   different frames rather than two state-pairs. Flagged for QA rather than solved by inventing
   prompts outside the assignment.

7. **Nothing was generated.** These are prompts only. Image generation requires founder cost
   approval, which is not held.

8. **The Celadon Turn is double-assigned and QA should confirm the ruling.** The assignment sheet
   gives that flair to P2 and to this pair. The two solutions are mechanically distinct — body
   thickness there, glaze presence here — and I believe both should ship, because a corridor where
   the same physical phenomenon shows up in a carried lamp and in the floor underfoot is coherent
   rather than repetitive. But it is a shared flair across two pairs and it is the one place in
   this pack where a reviewer could reasonably call convergence. Raising it rather than waiting to
   be caught.
