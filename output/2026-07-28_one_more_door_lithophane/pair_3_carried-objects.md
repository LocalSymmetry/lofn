# STEP 06 — FACETS (the five scoring facets for this pair's prompts)

Before the facets, two material findings that the rest of the pair is built on. Both came out of
holding the axis draws against the physics rather than around them.

### FINDING 1 — the eggshell body does not get 2–6 mm, and that is this pair's argument

A door panel has a 2–6 mm body and six terraces spaced ~0.7 mm apart. A slip-cast eggshell toll
piece has a **0.8–2.4 mm** wall. It still yields six carvable brightnesses, but each step is only
**~0.3 mm**. Consequences, all of them useful:

- The whole family sits closer to burn-out than any other part in the game. One slip of the loop
  tool and the step is gone to white.
- Indirect daylight — a weak source — is *enough* for the thin steps and never enough for the
  thick ones. This family is permanently mid-reveal under its own axis-3 light.
- The correct exposure window is narrower here than anywhere else in the corridor. A carried
  object is the part most easily blinded in both directions.

Terrace table, published so the four prompts obey one number:

**Index convention, corridor-wide:** T1 is the **thickest and darkest** rung and T6 is the
**thinnest and brightest**, the same direction P1, P2, P4, P5 and P6 publish. The millimetres below
are this family's own — an eggshell wall, not a door — but the direction of the index is the run's.

| step | wall | reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | 2.4 mm, slip intact | opaque — dead black |
| **T2** | 2.0 mm | near-black brown |
| **T3** | 1.7 mm | deep umber |
| **T4** | 1.4 mm | warm mid-tan |
| **T5** | 1.1 mm | pale amber-cream |
| **T6** | 0.8 mm | near-white bone-cream, one step from gone |
| **T0** | absent — a chip or a pierce | pure burn-out white, carries no picture |

### FINDING 2 — the hollow form is what lets the two images disagree

A dark slip over a thin wall has an obvious trap: if the only carving is the sgraffito on the
outside, then the unlit line-drawing and the lit glowing-line drawing are **the same picture**,
and synthesis point 2 dies.

The slip-cast body solves it, because a slip-cast object is **hollow and reachable**. So:

- The **outside** carries the sgraffito — scratched through slip and shallowly into the wall.
  That is the surface claim, and it is what you get for free, unlit, in raking light.
- The **inside** carries broad thinning cut from within the cavity, where the tool could reach.
  It leaves **no surface relief and no disturbance in the slip**. Unlit it contributes nothing.
  Lit, it comes up as broad terraces underneath the scratched lines.

Two pictures, one wall, and the second one is physically hidden until it is paid for. This is why
`slip-cast eggshell` and `sgraffito` are the right pair of draws and not a fight.

### THE PART FAMILY — the toll pieces

Five palm-sized hollow toll pieces: flattened river-pebble ovoids, ~70 × 50 × 18 mm, with an
open oval foot at the base where the casting aperture was never closed. All five out of **one
worn plaster mould** — so the identical seam ridge runs down the identical left flank of all
five. Outside coated in a dry iron-manganese slip, near-black with a brown-black mottling. Sgraffito
subject on each: **a close huddle of these same ovoid forms seen from behind, feet toward you,
fronts turned away.** In the dark they are indistinguishable; only light tells you which one you
are holding. That is the game, made small enough to carry.

## The five facets

1. **Does the pale line exist because clay was taken away?** Every bright mark must be legible as
   a *removal* — a slip scratched through, a wall thinned, a chip gone. If any bright mark could
   have been laid on with a brush, the prompt fails.
2. **Is the unlit object worth twenty seconds?** The unlit read here is a high-contrast scratched
   drawing under a hard raking key, with the seam catching light in the same place on every
   object. If the frame could be described as "pale blank slabs," the prompt fails.
3. **Do the terraces arrive as flat plateaus with knife edges, in a stated subset of T1–T6?** Any
   step that blends into its neighbour, any halo, any soft falloff, is a blocking failure.
4. **Does repetition carry the meaning, and does the break from it cost something?** Five
   near-identical objects should accrue weight by being nearly the same; the one that differs
   must differ for a *stated physical reason*, never for composition's sake.
5. **Is the flaw information rather than texture?** The seam proves the "unique" object came from
   a mould. The thumbprint is a permanent bright blotch. The pooled glaze killed a detail that
   cannot be recovered. A smudge with no consequence is craft-as-alibi and fails.

# STEP 09 — ARTIST-REFINED PASS (critic/artist loop, supplied panel seats only)

**V1 · critic — THE SKEPTIC, CONTEXT (after Kalman):** "Five pale objects in a row is the most
tasteful, most forgettable image anyone has proposed today. Where is the fact?"
**V1 · refiner — THE SLOW OBJECT (after de Waal):** The fact is the seam. One mould, five
objects, the same ridge in the same place on every one of them — put a hard raking light across
it and the repetition stops being an atmosphere and becomes *evidence*. Accepted: the seam is
promoted from detail to the frame's subject.

**V2 · critic — THE THUMBNAIL (after Kare):** "T6 and T5 only? At 16 px that is four dark blobs
and one slightly less dark blob. Nothing survives."
**V2 · refiner — THE LANTERN:** Then the rotated one must be *unmissable* — its whole cavity
full, T6 down through T2, a lit lantern beside four extinguished ones. One bright object against four
dark ones is a shape that survives any downscale. Accepted: the break in the set carries the
thumbnail, and the four unbroken objects are what make it mean something.

**V3 · critic — THE SKEPTIC, MEDIUM (after Muratori):** "You banned wet gloss and now you want a
glossy pool. Which is it? Say the rule or every artist picks their own."
**V3 · refiner — THE RELIEF (after Whiteread):** The rule is: the *body* is matte, dry and
slightly sugary, everywhere, always. The pooled glaze is not a shading choice — it is a physical
lens of vitrified glass sitting in a channel, and it is opaque to the picture. It is the only
specular event permitted in this pair's four frames, it is confined inside the trough, and it is
there because it killed something. Accepted, written into the prompt as an explicit exception
with a boundary.

**V4 · critic — THE SKEPTIC, CONCEPT (after Loos):** "A chip is a chip. You are dressing damage
up as meaning again."
**V4 · refiner — THE THRESHOLD (after Matta-Clark):** Not dressing — *cutting*. The chip is a
hole in a wall that was carrying a picture. It is not a step on the ladder of brightness; it is
off the ladder, past T6, and it carries no image at all. That is a fact about the object with a
consequence the player can read across a dark room. Withdrawn, on condition the prompt states the
chip as **T0 — absent material, hard-edged, brighter than any terrace, blank.** Accepted.

# NEGATIVE-GATE SELF-CHECK

Each of the six clauses is quoted from `CREATIVE_CONTEXT.md` / `04_metaprompt.md`, then answered.

### 1. *"NOT printed/painted/decaled/projected onto porcelain — the relief IS the image"*

The riskiest clause for this pair, because `sgraffito through a dark slip` sounds like a surface
colour technique. Answered in three places in every prompt: the slip is **clay applied as clay**,
not paint or decal; the picture is made by **scratching it away** with a loop tool that also cuts
into the wall beneath; and each prompt states explicitly *"every pale line in every drawing must
be a line of clay that was taken away"* and *"the drawing exists only as depth."* Every prompt
carries an explicit ban on painted, printed, decaled and stencilled marks. The V2 interior carving
is thinning of the wall from inside — pure thickness, no surface event at all. **CLEAN.**

### 2. *"NOT alpha-gradient glow, bloom, god-rays, soft falloff, lens flare — light is CARVED in hard terraces"*

Every prompt names its terrace subset numerically (T1–T6), describes each step as *"a flat plateau
of one exact brightness with a hard knife edge between it and the next,"* and forbids blending,
feathering, haloing and fading between steps. Every prompt carries an explicit ban on glow, bloom,
halo, haze, god-rays, light shafts, lens flare and soft luminous falloff, plus a ban on brightness
spilling past the edge of the clay. V4's T0 chip — the one place a renderer would reflexively add
a bloom — is specified as *"a hard-edged aperture of pure flat white … no falloff, no halo, no
bloom, no glow spilling outward."* **CLEAN.**

### 3. *"NOT a post-process filter over ordinary 3D"*

The banding is specified as wall-thickness in millimetres (0.8 / 1.1 / 1.4 / 1.7 / 2.0 / 2.4 mm),
i.e. as geometry, not as a look. V1 renders the *same* six depths with **no transmission at all** —
which a post-process banding filter physically cannot produce — and V2 renders the same object set
from the same geometry in a second light. Every prompt bans photographic filters, film grain,
chromatic aberration and colour grade by name. **CLEAN.**

### 4. *"NOT PBR metal, plastic, wet gloss, or subsurface skin — porcelain is matte, dry, slightly sugary, and it TRANSMITS"*

Every prompt states matte, dry, faintly sugary, unglazed-bisque body and bans glossy, wet, waxy,

channel*, it is *dead vitrified glass, not a highlight*, it *must not spread onto the surrounding

### 5. *"NOT a composed scene, room, HUD, typography, score, control or environment"*

1. **The axis draw does not fight the material — but only because the body is hollow.** If a
