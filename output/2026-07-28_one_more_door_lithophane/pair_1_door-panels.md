# THE STRUCTURAL FIND THAT ORGANISES THIS PAIR

Before the steps, the one thing this pair discovered and that the other five cannot arrive at,
because it falls out of *this* pair's technique (frontal orthographic portrait) crossed with *this*
pair's image-of axis (the room beyond) and *this* pair's failure axis (a corner too thick):

**One height field. Raking light reads the terrace EDGES. Backlight reads the terrace AREAS.**

Where the carving is edge-dense — many risers packed close, a table, two chairs, a window reveal —
the unlit relief is *loud* under a grazing candle and the transmitted image *averages to mid-band
mud*. Where the carving is a single wide plateau at the thin end of the ladder, the unlit relief is
**silent** — no risers, nothing to cast — and the transmitted image is the **brightest thing on the
door**.

So the two pictures disagree, and they disagree **because of the physics**, not because an artist
decided they should:

| | shadow claim (unlit, raking) | transmission truth (lit) |
|---|---|---|
| loud | the furniture — table, two chairs pushed in, a window reveal, a lamp | — |
| silent | the person-shaped void; the far door | — |
| bright | — | the person-shaped void; the far door standing ajar |
| murky | — | the furniture, averaged into one mid-band |

**The relief claims a furnished, occupied room. The light says: nobody is in there, and there is
another door.** That sentence is the game's title mechanic executed in clay, and it is *paid for*
in the only light the player has.

## The published ladder (Muratori's price — one number, every part obeys it)

Body 2.0–6.0 mm, six carvable values. **T6 = 2.0 mm** (brightest, one step off burn-out) ·
**T5 = 2.8 mm** · **T4 = 3.6 mm** · **T3 = 4.4 mm** · **T2 = 5.2 mm** · **T1 = 6.0 mm** (holds all
light, reads near-black in transmission).

**The blind corner is 9.0 mm — off the ladder entirely.** It is not T1; it is a seventh, illegal
value. It is *thicker than the ladder's own black*. That is what "permanent blind spot" means as a
number rather than as a mood, and it is what makes the failure axis a fact about the object rather
than an excuse for imperfection. (Concept Skeptic, satisfied: this imperfection carries
information — it is where the player will never learn whether the far door had anything standing
in it.)

## The candle solved without a gradient

A candle is finite and it gutters, so the panel is not evenly served. **Do not solve this with
falloff.** Solve it the way the material does: the candle's reach is a **single hard-edged ellipse,
scalloped, unfeathered**, and across that boundary every terrace drops **exactly one step**. A
light shape that is itself a terrace. No alpha anywhere in it.

And a candle burned behind clay leaves **carbon**. The soot deposit reads as a flat, ashy,
crisp-rimmed patch with a scalloped tide line — never a haze. It is the record that *somebody
already spent light on this door*, and it is the single reason the unlit state is worth a look.

# STEP 06 — THE FIVE JUDGING FACETS

The rubric every P1 prompt is scored against.

1. **Two pictures, one slab, and they disagree.** Does the frame make it plain that the ridged
   shadow-drawing on the face and the glowing bands inside the body are the same carving read two
   ways — and that they are not telling the same story? A frame where the shadow image is merely a
   dimmer version of the lit image has failed.
2. **The ladder is countable.** Can a viewer count discrete brightnesses with hard, unfeathered
   boundaries between them — the way a contour map has lines, not slopes — with no band narrower
   than a thumbnail can hold? Any soft edge anywhere is a failure, not a stylistic choice.
3. **Bright means gone.** Is every luminous region legibly *material that was removed* — a floor
   left low by a loop tool, with the tool's chatter still in it — rather than a pale colour laid on
   top of a slab?
4. **The clay is dry in the hand.** Does the surface read matte, sugary, faintly chalk-dusty, warm
   ivory, absorbent — a fired body that *transmits* — with no wet sheen, no clearcoat, no plastic,
   no waxy skin?
5. **It reads at the length of a corridor glance.** At phone size, five seconds, without a caption:
   a slab of clay with a picture inside it, and the picture is made of light. And in the unlit
   frames: a slab of clay somebody has already carried a flame behind.

# STEP 10 — CRITIQUE, RANK, REVISE, SYNTHESISE

**Critics seated from the supplied ledger** (no new panel): **THE THICKNESS** (after the
Wedgwood/lithophane lineage) for the numbers, **THE THUMBNAIL** (after Kare) for survival at 16 px,
with **THE SKEPTIC — CONCEPT** (after Loos) holding the one question that can still sink this pair:
*is the unlit state worth looking at?*

**Essence, one sentence:** a hand-cut slab of bone china that claims, in shadow, to be a furnished
room, and admits, in candlelight, that the room is empty and has another door in it.

**Two best ways to leverage the medium:** (1) let edge-density and plateau-flatness carry the
disagreement, so one carving genuinely produces two pictures; (2) let the failure — a corner
carved 3 mm too thick — be the only region that survives over-exposure, so the flaw outlives the
image it ruined.

**THE THICKNESS on R3:** *"Finally a number I can build against. Six values, published, and a
seventh that is explicitly illegal. A panel that says 9 mm rather than 'too thick' can be moulded
tomorrow. Ranked first."*

**THE THUMBNAIL on R1:** *"At sixteen pixels this is a pale rectangle. I am not going to pretend
otherwise and neither should the pair. Keep it — it is a part, and parts are not hero frames — but
it must never be the frame that represents this game anywhere. The soot patch and the proud corner
are what stop it being nothing, and they must be coarse."*

**THE SKEPTIC — CONCEPT on R1, on the same question he raised in the debate:** *"Edge-density
carrying the shadow image is the answer I was owed. The unlit panel is not blank; it is a
ridge-drawing of furniture that the light will later contradict. And the soot is the part I did not
expect — it is evidence, not texture. Craft is not being used as an alibi here. Withdrawn, again."*

**THE THUMBNAIL on R4:** *"The reversal is the best single idea in the run. The dead corner becomes
the only readable thing. That survives downscaling because it is one hard black rectangle against
flat white — it is practically an icon."*

**RANK:** R3 · R4 · R2 · R1 · R6 · R5.

# NEGATIVE-GATE SELF-CHECK

Each of the six clauses quoted from the ICB, then how these four prompts avoid it.

**1. "NOT printed/painted/decaled/projected onto porcelain — the relief IS the image."**
Every prompt opens with the imperative *"Carve, do not paint"* / *"Carve, never paint"* and states
that everything produced must be explicable as a thickness of clay. Every value in V2, V3 and V4 is
bound to a named millimetre depth (2.0 / 2.8 / 3.6 / 4.4 / 5.2 / 6.0), so a brightness cannot be
specified without specifying the removal that causes it. V1 states the shadow image is made
*entirely of risers*. No decal, transfer, print, projection or applied pigment appears anywhere.
**Clean.**

**2. "NOT alpha-gradient glow, bloom, god-rays, soft falloff, lens flare — light is CARVED in hard
terraces."**
All four prompts carry an explicit FORBIDDEN block naming bloom, halo, glow, god-rays, lens flare,
soft falloff, alpha gradient and depth-of-field. Every band boundary is specified *hard and
unfeathered*. The candle's uneven reach — the one place a gradient would normally creep in — is
solved as a hard-edged scalloped ellipse across which each terrace drops exactly one step, i.e. the
light shape is itself a terrace. In V2, unarrived terraces are specified as **absent, not faint**,
which removes the wording that would otherwise invite a ramp. In V4 burn-out is defined as a
**merge of adjacent flat values with their boundaries deleted**, explicitly not a spread of light,
with brightness stopped dead at the arris so nothing leaks onto the matte. **Clean.**

**3. "NOT a post-process filter over ordinary 3D."**
Every band is tied to a carved depth, so the banding lives in the height field and cannot be applied
afterwards. Each prompt forbids photographic grade, diffusion filter and depth-of-field by name. V3
and V4 further require the loop-tool chatter to persist *inside* the bands as physical ridged
texture and to disappear where clay burns out — behaviour a post-process pass could not produce,
because it depends on the geometry rather than on the frame. **Clean.**

**4. "NOT PBR metal, plastic, wet gloss, or subsurface skin — porcelain is matte, dry, slightly
sugary, and it TRANSMITS."**
Each prompt's MATERIAL block specifies matte, dry, faintly sugary, minutely chalk-dusty and
absorbent, and forbids gloss, clearcoat, wet sheen, plastic and metal by name. V4 additionally
forbids the body reading as paper, backlit film or an LED panel at high brightness — the specific
failure that exposure invites. Transmission is stated as the body's own behaviour, never as a
surface effect. **Clean.**

**5. "NOT a composed scene, room, HUD, typography, score, control or environment."**
This is the clause my image-of axis draws nearest, and I am stating the resolution rather than
assuming it. The room on the other side of the door exists **only as a carved height field inside
one separable part**. The camera is never inside a room; the panel floats on a flat navy matte;
every prompt forbids floor, wall, room, table, hands, props, interface, text and numerals as
*render content*, and every prompt names a logical part box in pixels precisely so live code owns
layout. The word "room" appears only as a description of what was cut into the clay. **Clean — and
flagged as the clause a careless reroll would break first.**

**6. "NOT a documentary of a real pottery workshop (L2 HIGH: realism loses to impossibility in
object-world fields)."**
All four prompts forbid workshop, kiln, potter, studio, shelving and props by name. No frame
contains a maker, a bench, a tool in shot or a visible flame — the candle is named as a cause and
banned from appearing. The object itself is impossible rather than documentary: a real lithophane
is carved on the back and flat on the face, whereas this panel is specified so that one terraced
field yields two *contradicting* pictures depending on where the light stands. That is the
impossibility gradient, and it is in the object rather than in the staging. **Clean.**

**Verdict: 6/6 clean, no blocking hits.**

# HONEST PROBLEMS

1. **V1 is the run's weakest thumbnail and I will not pretend otherwise.** At 16 px an unlit
   bone-china panel is a pale rectangle. The soot tide line and the proud corner are what stop it
   being nothing, and they are specified coarse for that reason — but V1 is a *part*, not a hero
   frame, and it must never be the image that represents this game anywhere. THE THUMBNAIL's note
   above is recorded, not softened. The pair's hero is V3; V4 is the better icon.

   highest-risk instruction in the pair. If a render comes back with a feathered reach boundary it
   is a negative-gate hit on clause 2 and must be rerolled, not accepted.

   *because* it is cooperative, this pair is the most likely to drift pretty. The person-shaped
