# UNBRACING — Character, Room, and Topology Bible

This is the continuity authority for all eight renders. Paste the locked identity sentence unchanged into every prompt, then use the selected hero render as the character reference.

## Locked protagonist

**Verbatim identity sentence:**

> An invented 46-year-old brown-skinned craftswoman with broad shoulders, close-cropped salt-and-pepper curls, one white forelock at her left temple, a long angular face, deep-set dark eyes, and a plain bone-linen work dress with a single black-and-celadon cuff on her left wrist.

- Adult, reserved, competent, physically grounded.
- Neutral mouth; no smile, tears, closed-eye serenity, prayer pose, or victory gesture.
- Left wrist always carries the patterned cuff; right wrist is plain bone linen.
- Her shoulders begin level but raised, then settle unevenly. Her hands move from repair, to pause, to hovering check, to an ordinary fold.
- No jewelry, headwear, tattoos, borrowed cultural markers, or real-person resemblance.

## Locked room and material language

- **Medium:** tactile architectural assemblage portrait.
- **Walls:** matte ash-casein plaster, bone-gray, rubbed and scratched where prior repairs met the room.
- **Central member:** one charred timber brace on the vertical bearing line; one soot-dark base socket. After Frame 5, a forearm-length broken black fragment remains caught above the old line.
- **Primary network:** blackened copper wire. Heavy perimeter gauge carries force; three visibly finer interior fans distribute it. Never use one uniform gauge.
- **Joints:** palm-sized celadon ceramic eyelets with crackle glaze and bearing wear.
- **Collars:** black-and-celadon brocade selvage compressed around eyelets; structural textile, never jewelry.
- **Stress witness:** dull pewter foil appears only as crushed ridges at loaded contacts.
- **Palette:** charcoal, blackened copper, muted celadon, bone linen, foil gray. No radiant gold, neon, or color-only emotional progression.
- **Light:** dry hard side light from frame left. It reveals deflection and texture; it never becomes a halo or supernatural effect.

## The four anchor families

Every structural frame must show at least three endpoints; the wide proof frames show all four.

1. **Upper-left route:** cracked ceiling seam to a bowed upper-left wall plate.
2. **Upper-right route:** cracked ceiling seam to a bowed upper-right wall plate.
3. **Lower-left route:** network edge to a planted lower-left floor anchor.
4. **Lower-right route:** network edge to a planted lower-right floor anchor.

Fine strands may cross between these four heavy routes only through visible celadon eyelets. No wire ends in empty space or exits the frame without a readable receiving support.

## Composition progression

1. Compressed profile / shared bearing line.
2. Overhead load plan / hands as one load point.
3. Profile body-room cutaway / repair becomes enclosure.
4. Material-action three-quarter / irreversible choice.
5. Grounded three-quarter / quiet break and catch.
6. High oblique plan-to-ground / visible redistribution.
7. Wide ground-level proof / earned negative space.
8. Wide structural rhyme / ordinary aftertask.

## Reject the render if

- the wire reads as a cocoon, chandelier, halo, jewelry, celestial web, or decorative nest;
- fewer than three anchor endpoints are visible in Frame 5;
- eyelets become beads or collars become costume trim;
- the ceiling shows no compression, lift, deflection, or settled bearing evidence;
- the woman changes age, face, hair, skin tone, cuff side, dress, or body type;
- the room becomes luminous fantasy, generic steampunk, or polished digital décor;
- relief is communicated by a smile, open arms, glowing light, or instant serenity;
- any frame lacks a distinct causal state.
