## Winning proposition

One adult and one rigid brace begin as the room’s hidden foundation. Her accumulating repairs become an enclosure. When she chooses not to add another repair, the brace breaks quietly and the same wirework redistributes its load into four visible anchor families. The room learns to hold itself before her posture does.

This gives the entry two simultaneous reads:

1. **Feed read:** woman + broken black support + taut copper routes + changed ceiling.
2. **Judge read:** one transformed motif, causal material behavior, stable identity, eight non-redundant states, and an ending that refuses instant healing.

## Artist statement

*Unbracing* is about the strange interval between being safe and believing you are safe. A craftswoman has spent years treating her body as part of a failing room: every crack becomes another wire, every sag another reason to keep her shoulders raised. When the central brace breaks, her repairs do not collapse. They catch, divide the load, and become a permeable structure the room can carry through many small relations. Across eight images, wire changes from binding to support, broken timber changes from threat to witness, and her hands move from compulsory repair toward an ordinary fold of cloth. The ending does not claim she is healed. One finger still checks the ceramic joint. The room has learned to hold itself before her body has learned that it no longer must.
