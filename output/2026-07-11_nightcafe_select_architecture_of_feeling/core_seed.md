# GOLDEN SEED — UNBRACING

> **A body learns that an opening is not the same thing as a collapse.**

## Seed lineage

Primary anchor: **SEED 15 — A Surreal Portrait Reflecting Our Souls**.

Supporting structure: SEED 3's decisive-moment series discipline and SEED 14's visible emotional turn. The seed is chosen before concept generation; no later concept may retrofit a different Golden Seed.

## Emotional engine

- **Primary:** relief after prolonged vigilance.
- **Secondary:** acceptance that safety does not require perfect repair.
- **Residual:** unease—the body checks whether the released load will return.

The locked phrase is **unbracing**: the first full breath after years of carrying a structure that no one else could see. This is not serenity, recovery-as-smile, or triumphant liberation. Relief arrives while the old posture remains legible.

## Core world law

One invented adult woman's body and one constructed interior exchange physical load across a sequential Body of Work. Architecture is never background and never a decorative metaphor. Every wall, seam, vault, threshold, floor, and opening determines what she must hold, where she can stand, or how breath moves.

Each frame changes at least one spatial property—compression, permeability, symmetry, support, interior/exterior relation, or direction of light. A recurring seam records the transfer. Ornament is forensic evidence, not garnish.

## The story move

1. Establish an immediately readable impossible load.
2. Show the protagonist's practiced competence at carrying it.
3. Reveal that her beautiful repairs have become part of the enclosure.
4. Force a rupture that cannot be solved by adding another repair.
5. Let her choose one opening and refuse to close it.
6. Redistribute the load through space and material rather than erasing it.
7. End with a visual rhyme to the opening: the architecture remains, but it no longer requires her body as its hidden foundation.

The exact protagonist, room type, material system, and action are discovered downstream. The ending must retain one unrepaired trace.

## Dangerous permission

- Beauty may become oppressive before it becomes useful.
- A crack may be an exit rather than damage.
- The protagonist may look uncertain in the final frame; relief is allowed to remain vigilant.
- The sequence may let one frame become radically sparse after dense ornament, provided the visual identity survives.
- The architecture may appear to disobey physics only when the emotional causality is perfectly clear.

## Five fresh constraint axes

### Axis 1 — Load-bearing anatomy

Choose one dominant system per concept:

1. rib-vault pressure held beneath the sternum;
2. shoulder-borne ceiling with visible settling lines;
3. throat-hinge controlling a narrow interior door;
4. spine-stair whose risers register each held breath;
5. palm-foundation supporting a miniature floor plan;
6. shadow-column taking weight the body cannot acknowledge.

### Axis 2 — Material transformation sequence

Choose one sequence and make every stage visible:

1. cracked egg tempera → gilded seam → opened linen backing;
2. blue ceramic tesserae → loosened grout → water-drawn contour;
3. black urushi lacquer → scraped gesso → warm raw wood;
4. etched copper → verdigris bloom → perforated light-screen;
5. stained glass → missing lead came → clear air aperture;
6. embroidered brocade → pulled thread → load-bearing net.

### Axis 3 — Spatial rule

Choose one causal rule per concept:

1. walls thicken whenever she performs composure;
2. every repair lowers the ceiling by one visible course;
3. doors appear only after she stops facing them;
4. the floor plan redraws itself around her exhale;
5. symmetry holds while she grips the seam and breaks when she releases it;
6. light can enter only through material she refuses to restore.

### Axis 4 — Artifact of imperfection

Choose one small recurring witness:

1. one missing tessera kept in her pocket;
2. a loosened cuff thread she never cuts;
3. a crescent cup-ring repeated on each floor;
4. a dull brass latch with no corresponding key;
5. a black seed lodged in a wall joint;
6. a square of unpainted backing cloth.

### Axis 5 — Frame grammar

Distribute these across the series rather than repeating one portrait:

1. frontal icon with a severe clear silhouette;
2. profile cutaway where body and room share one boundary;
3. overhead plan in which the protagonist is a load point;
4. threshold silhouette with aggressive negative space;
5. close material detail that advances the plot;
6. final wide echo in which support has visibly changed hands.

## Personality Genre DNA Constraint — visual register

The orchestrator must select a valid personality YAML. The strongest library match is expected to be **LOFN-ArtCore**, but selection remains Phase 1.

If LOFN-ArtCore is selected:

- **ACCESSIBLE arm:** serene neo-baroque clarity; legible adult figure or emblem; midnight teal, bone, ember-gold, celadon; cracked tempera, textile, lacquer, restrained filigree; one impossible law readable instantly.
- **AMBITIOUS arm:** fractured fresco, black-glass voids, scraped supports, holographic foil used as damage evidence, severe asymmetry, material abrasion, cosmic scale compressed into anatomy.
- Generic fantasy portraiture, fairy-core, generic photorealism, castle imagery, soft-focus celestial glamour, and interchangeable digital illustration are forbidden substitutes.

## Non-negotiables

- Adult protagonist only; no children.
- Invented subject; no identifiable real person.
- No borrowed ethnic, tribal, sacred, or grief-specific identity.
- No text inside the image.
- No fairy wings, princess, witch, cute familiar, generic apocalypse, heart symbol, key-as-obvious-metaphor, clock, lighthouse, phoenix, or cyberpunk city.
- No living-artist names in generation prompts.
- One coherent protagonist and material law across the final sequence.
- Feeling is shown through posture, load, space, and transformed material; it is not named in prompt prose until an optional final craft note outside the render text.
- Cover-first legibility and second-read depth must coexist.
