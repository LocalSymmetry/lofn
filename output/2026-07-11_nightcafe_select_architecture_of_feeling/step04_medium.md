## Pair-by-pair selection and review

### C01 — The Seam She Polishes

**Selected medium:** coarse linen stretched over visible timber ribs, painted in matte midnight-teal egg tempera; a thick rubbed ember-gold leaf seam crosses garment-shadow-wall alignment and is fixed with oxidized copper pins.  
**Why it fits:** the tempera crackle records repeated care; rubbed leaf can appear heavy; linen and timber make the final transfer legible. It supports high texture and one clear silhouette.  
**Revision:** replaced polished gold with rubbed, abraded leaf so beauty carries labor rather than glamour.

### C02 — The Ceiling’s Receipt

**Selected medium:** hand-cast bone-white plaster relief modules mounted close to a raw timber ceiling, each module edged with dull gold leaf and separated by deep raked shadows.  
**Why it fits:** modular depth makes accumulated pressure measurable without text; the omitted unit can transform the grid into an arch.  
**Revision:** removed written marks and stamped numbers; shadow depth is the ledger.

### C03 — Selvage Ledger

**Selected medium:** handwoven indigo linen-and-silk brocade with coral and tarnished-gold supplementary warp, an enlarged raw selvage, and open-warp intervals that expose the dark room beyond.  
**Why it fits:** the medium literally owns tension, pull direction, and permeability; coral thread gives a traceable causal line.  
**Revision:** removed wrist cords; all force now converges through shuttle position and cloth geometry.

### C04 — Air Through the Pattern

**Selected medium:** hand-knotted indigo linen cord backed by fragments of matte brocade, with coral bearing rosettes, bone-colored knots, and hard daylight crossing the opened lattice.  
**Why it fits:** knots make recoil and redistribution visible; the open field can change frame by frame without becoming decorative lace.  
**Revision:** reduced floral motifs to structural rosettes and limited them to three scales.

### C05 — Pocket Weight

**Selected medium:** irregular crackle-glazed celadon and cobalt ceramic mosaic set in deep bone-gray lime grout, paired with a plain heavy cotton coat whose concealed tesserae form angular reliefs.  
**Why it fits:** a missing piece and grout line remain legible at feed scale; ceramic weight deforms clothing and supports the circular floor-plan ending.  
**Revision:** enlarged individual tesserae and reduced palette to celadon, cobalt, bone, and one ember-gold grout trace.

### C06 — The Tile Refuses

**Selected medium:** a spare celadon-tile wall with raked matte grout, one palm-sized chipped cobalt tessera, graphite landing marks on lime-plaster floor, and cool daylight sharpened by the final recess.  
**Why it fits:** the object remains ordinary and heavy; impact marks allow six landings to accumulate into a readable path.  
**Revision:** removed water and reflective glaze from the concept so the tile’s mass, not beauty, drives the image.

### C07 — Volume of What Wasn’t

**Selected medium:** a chalk-white casein-and-gesso negative cast with rough linen fibers and unfinished handprints, fitted beneath a polished black-lacquer stair with raw oak bearing feet.  
**Why it fits:** matte cast and reflective stair clarify positive/negative volume; rough handprints preserve residual labor; oak feet make the transfer concrete.  
**Revision:** reduced internal cast complexity to two arm hollows and one stepped outer contour for thumbnail decoding.

### C08 — Absent Wall in Her Arms

**Selected medium:** a black-lacquer timber assemblage whose concealed scraped-gesso joints separate into pale raw-wood ribs, with chalk pressure transfer on plain linen sleeves.  
**Why it fits:** monochrome mass can break into an intelligible construction grammar; the raw interior shifts the medium from opacity to permeability.  
**Revision:** removed doorway lighting; the open interval is defined by material separation rather than a luminous portal.

### C09 — Shadow Takes Attendance

**Selected medium:** etched copper ceiling plates with verdigris stress blooms above a black-glass floor relief whose shadow planes thicken into physical wedges, bounded by a narrow celadon plaster strip.  
**Why it fits:** copper visibly deflects; black glass gives the shadow an object-like edge; repeated etching marks can register contact migration.  
**Revision:** replaced smoky shadow with solid, sharply bounded planes to make bearing plausible inside the invented law.

### C10 — Load Crosses Noon

**Selected medium:** a hand-punched oxidized-copper screen, graphite-black sun bands, a verdigris plaster column, and one removable hammered-brass shoulder plate on coarse linen.  
**Why it fits:** the screen gives the protagonist agency over the line; the plate is a visible obsolete tool after transfer.  
**Revision:** kept the palette dry and architectural, eliminating gold haze and spectacle.

### C11 — Nested Brace

**Selected medium:** hand-looped copper wire of varied gauge joined by visibly stitched indigo-brocade tabs, anchored to a charred timber footing against a bone casein wall.  
**Why it fits:** different wire gauges show changing tension; stitched joints preserve the user’s textile signature; the body-shaped void stays clear.  
**Revision:** limited the vault to thirty-to-forty major loops rather than an undifferentiated mesh.

### C12 — Failure Becomes Mesh

**Selected medium:** one charred wood brace wrapped in fine copper wire that catches through repeated celadon ceramic eyelets across an ash-and-casein room, leaving a single black fragment suspended after redistribution.  
**Why it fits:** every component has a job and can change state; char, copper, ceramic, and ash produce a tactile palette with a strong cover silhouette.  
**Revision:** removed sparks, debris, and explosive motion; the break is quiet and the taut aftermath is the hero image.

## Feedback-loop conclusions

- C01 and C12 gained craft specificity without losing their simple impossible propositions.
- C03 now avoids literal restraint while retaining body-memory through the shuttle and curled hands.
- C06 improved most: removing watery beauty made the refused tile feel heavy, adult, and instructional.
- C07’s cast is now simpler and easier to decode, though it still asks more of the viewer than accessible slots.
- C09 remains the most technically risky model-generation challenge because the same shadow must read as both silhouette and bearing wedge.
- C02, C04, C08, and C10 remain viable but weaker against their paired rivals.
