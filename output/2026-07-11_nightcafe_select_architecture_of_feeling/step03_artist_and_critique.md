## C01 — The Seam She Polishes

**Historical lens:** Anna-Eva Bergman (1909–1987), used analytically for metal leaf as mass rather than luxury and for severe planar compression.  
**Contribution:** let gold behave as a heavy geological plane whose edge can bear or fasten, not as decorative sparkle.  
**Critique:** the concept has the clearest emotional causality in the field, but the “seam through shoulder” could become body-horror or a literal wound if the joint is anatomically invasive. Keep the boundary shared, not pierced. The woman’s long competence must be visible in the polished wear and tool logic.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 9.5; Craft potential 9.4; Cohesion 9.7; Intent 9.6.  
**Panel disagreement 1:** the Material-Wound Mason requested a visible scar; the Embodied Viewer argued that an actual wound would hijack the feeling.  
**Resolution:** make the gold line cross garment, shadow, and wall in perfect alignment without cutting skin; pressure is legible through posture and fabric compression.  
**Refined concept:** An invented middle-aged woman repeatedly burnishes a heavy ember-gold seam aligned across her linen shoulder and a low midnight-teal ceiling, until one loosened copper fastening shows that the seam has been pinning her posture to the room; she leaves the final hole open and exposed timber ribs accept the load while her empty hand remains raised from habit.

## C02 — The Ceiling’s Receipt

**Historical lens:** Mary Martin (1907–1969), used for modular relief, repeated units, and changing shadow logic.  
**Contribution:** repeated rectangles can become a measurable pressure system whose meaning changes through displacement.  
**Critique:** the ledger metaphor is clever but verbally dependent; at thumbnail size it may read as ornamental ceiling tiles. The blank unit must produce an unmistakable physical consequence, and the woman’s agency cannot be reduced to accounting.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 7.6; Craft potential 8.5; Cohesion 8.8; Intent 7.8.  
**Panel disagreement 2:** the Closure Editor liked the clear countable sequence; the Interpretation Skeptic called “receipt” a caption-dependent conceit.  
**Resolution:** remove all text and arithmetic; let each repair module descend by a visible depth increment, while the omitted module changes the whole vault geometry.  
**Refined concept:** Above an invented older repairer, rows of small gilded plaster modules descend farther with every completed crack until she deliberately leaves one bone-white recess empty, causing the rigid grid to hinge upward into a shallow arch while one raw fracture and her lowered tool remain visible.

## C03 — Selvage Ledger

**Historical lens:** Lenore Tawney (1907–2007), used for open warp, suspended fiber structure, and the expressive interval between threads.  
**Contribution:** the plain structural edge can outperform the ornate field; air is an active component of the textile.  
**Critique:** the concept belongs naturally to the user’s textile-and-body fusion language and has a superb causal thread, but wrist tethers risk literal bondage imagery. The pull should enter through work posture and cloth behavior, not restraints.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 9.2; Craft potential 9.5; Cohesion 9.3; Intent 9.2.  
**Panel disagreement 3:** the Tension Weaver wanted visible cords touching the hands; the Catharsis Auditor warned that cutting bonds is a familiar liberation trope.  
**Resolution:** connect tension to the shuttle and the remembered shape of her hands; she changes the weave rather than breaking free.  
**Refined concept:** An invented adult weaver tends a dense indigo brocade wall whose coral-and-gold warp lines converge through her shuttle and dictate the curl of her hands, until she draws one undecorated selvage thread across the whole field and the textile opens into a self-tensioning net while her fingers remain curved around an absent tool.

## C04 — Air Through the Pattern

**Historical lens:** Aurèlia Muñoz (1926–2011), used for knotted fiber volume, suspended architecture, and textile that structures space.  
**Contribution:** apertures can be held by knots and intervals rather than pictured as holes in a flat fabric.  
**Critique:** the recoil-before-release is psychologically exact and could make a powerful sequence, but floral repetition across body and wall risks elegant fashion imagery with no visible labor. The apertures need a specific making method and escalating consequence.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 8.3; Craft potential 9.0; Cohesion 8.7; Intent 8.5.  
**Panel disagreement 4:** the Poetics-of-Interior Keeper valued the body-pattern merger; the Weight Prosecutor said surface camouflage is not a load path.  
**Resolution:** replace printed flowers with knotted bearing rosettes whose spokes visibly tighten or relax around each opening.  
**Refined concept:** An invented seamstress unknots one bearing rosette at a time in a suspended indigo-brocade partition, each first aperture pulling the remaining knots tighter around her seated outline before the forces redistribute into a stable open lattice and a band of daylight reaches both empty palms.

## C05 — Pocket Weight

**Historical lens:** Jeanne Reynal (1903–1983), used for irregular mosaic surfaces, mobile light, and the expressive necessity of gaps.  
**Contribution:** grout and missing pieces can carry as much visual authority as the tesserae.  
**Critique:** the coat gaining weight while the room appears perfected is emotionally sharp, but pocketed tiles may look like theft or a cute reveal. The burden must be visible earlier through sag, soundless geometry, and repeated emptying gestures.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 8.7; Craft potential 9.2; Cohesion 9.0; Intent 8.8.  
**Panel disagreement 5:** the Memory Cell Builder favored secretly carried pieces; the Ways-of-Seeing Translator worried the pockets cannot be read in a grid preview.  
**Resolution:** let cobalt tesserae visibly press as angular reliefs beneath the plain coat and pull its hem downward in every frame.  
**Refined concept:** In a celadon mosaic chamber, an invented woman’s plain coat sags under the cobalt tesserae she has removed and concealed while making every wall appear complete; she finally pours the angular pieces into a circular floor contour, and the room rebuilds around one shoulder-height absence as her coat hangs weightless but permanently creased.

## C06 — The Tile Refuses

**Historical lens:** Margit Kovács (1902–1977), used for tactile ceramic rhythm, compressed figurative form, and glaze as emotional evidence.  
**Contribution:** one ceramic object can retain contact history through chipped edges, fingerprints, glaze wear, and repeated placement.  
**Critique:** this is the simplest story and the most memorable object law, which gives it audience advantage; however, a “magic falling tile” could feel whimsical unless each landing changes the plan and her understanding. Keep the tile ordinary, heavy, chipped, and materially specific.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 9.4; Craft potential 9.1; Cohesion 9.6; Intent 9.5.  
**Panel disagreement 6:** the Ways-of-Seeing Translator called it the cleanest one-breath retell; the Novelty pressure within the Concept panel worried it was too simple.  
**Resolution:** preserve the one-object simplicity but make the cumulative landing marks form a load-transfer drawing only visible across the sequence.  
**Refined concept:** An invented conservator keeps pressing one chipped cobalt tessera into a celadon wall and it falls to a different marked floor point after each repair, its six landings gradually drawing the room’s true load path; she reads the pattern, leaves the shoulder-height recess open, and holds the unused tile loosely as raked light crosses through the gap.

## C07 — Volume of What Wasn’t

**Historical lens:** Katarzyna Kobro (1898–1951), used for sculpture that organizes surrounding space and refuses a sealed, self-contained mass.  
**Contribution:** the cast should redirect the room around it rather than appear as a prop placed inside the room.  
**Critique:** carrying a negative volume is genuinely strange and intellectually strong, but the cast’s relationship to the missing stair space may be hard to parse in a single frame. The repeated arm hollows and eventual handprints provide continuity.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 9.0; Craft potential 8.8; Cohesion 9.3; Intent 9.0.  
**Panel disagreement 7:** the Negative-Cast Cartographer embraced absence as mass; the Context panel feared judges would need the statement to decode it.  
**Resolution:** match the cast’s outer contour exactly to the stair’s missing underside and show one unmistakable moment of fit before the set-down.  
**Refined concept:** An invented adult woman carries a chalk-white cast whose stepped outer contour exactly fills the missing underside of a black-lacquer stair and whose hollows preserve her bracing arms; when she sets it into the void, it redirects the stair load into raw-wood feet while her unfinished handprints remain and she stands beside it without a task.

## C08 — Absent Wall in Her Arms

**Historical lens:** Louise Nevelson (1899–1988), used for monochrome assemblage, recovered fragments, and shadow turning separate parts into one field.  
**Contribution:** the separating cast can reveal an internal grammar of ribs, ledges, and discarded support fragments.  
**Critique:** the walk-through ending is accessible, but this variation lacks the startling precision of C07 and could drift into a portal cliché. The forearm pressure mark is good residual evidence; the mechanism of separation needs to be triggered by her choice.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 7.9; Craft potential 9.0; Cohesion 8.4; Intent 8.1.  
**Panel disagreement 8:** the Support Choreographer favored the bodily passage; the Interpretation Skeptic argued that walking through ribs merely illustrates “moving on.”  
**Resolution:** make her remove one black-lacquer wedge from the cast, initiating the conversion from mass to frame; she later passes through only as a quiet consequence.  
**Refined concept:** An invented woman withdraws one black-lacquer wedge from the wall-shaped mass she carries, and the scraped-gesso object separates along hidden joints into raw-wood ribs that take their own footing, leaving a chalk pressure band on her forearms as she occupies the newly permeable interval without crossing it theatrically.

## C09 — Shadow Takes Attendance

**Historical lens:** Maria Helena Vieira da Silva (1908–1992), used for unstable interior grids, compressed perspective, and space behaving like a nervous system.  
**Contribution:** contact points can migrate through a gridded room while the figure remains legible as one dark-light exchange.  
**Critique:** the idea offers the most visually novel cover image, and the shadow’s changing allegiance externalizes residual vigilance without literal anatomy; its risk is arbitrary magic unless the same time, light aperture, and contact notation repeat exactly.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 9.3; Craft potential 9.3; Cohesion 9.4; Intent 9.1.  
**Panel disagreement 9:** the Counterweight logic excited the Medium panel; the Weight Prosecutor demanded evidence that a shadow can bear anything beyond symbolism.  
**Resolution:** the black-glass shadow visibly thickens into a planar wedge at each new bearing point while the copper ceiling deflection decreases.  
**Refined concept:** At the same hour across a sequence, an invented woman’s black-glass shadow thickens from a flat outline into etched planar wedges that transfer their contact points from her body to a deflecting copper ceiling, until the shadow bears the room alone and she enters a narrow celadon band of unshadowed floor, glancing back once.

## C10 — Load Crosses Noon

**Historical lens:** Nasreen Mohamedi (1937–1990), used for disciplined line, interval, restraint, and the sensation of time crossing a surface.  
**Contribution:** one line can carry the full temporal argument if its thickness and relation to the column change with precision.  
**Critique:** the concise sundial premise is elegant, but it may produce four nearly identical frames and has less tactile variety than the rest. The bearing plate helps, yet the woman currently waits for noon rather than making a decisive choice.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 8.0; Craft potential 8.6; Cohesion 9.0; Intent 7.7.  
**Panel disagreement 10:** the Emotion-First Cutter praised the restraint; the Memory Cell Builder found too little accumulated history.  
**Resolution:** let the woman rotate a small pierced copper screen that steers the line across the necessary bearing path; time supplies force, but she supplies direction.  
**Refined concept:** An invented adult woman rotates a pierced copper screen so a narrow noon shadow traverses her linen ribs and a verdigris column, thickening into a bearing plane where the screen’s pattern converges; once the column rises, she sets the obsolete shoulder plate on the floor and stands in the ordinary light beside it.

## C11 — Nested Brace

**Historical lens:** Gego (1912–1994), used for line as spatial structure, irregular networks, and permeability without loss of rigor.  
**Contribution:** the wire vault can expand by redistributing tension through uneven nodes rather than inflating like a cage.  
**Critique:** the body-fitted chamber is a strong first image, but an expanding wire room can become decorative spectacle. Show node-by-node transfer and keep the human action modest: one latch, one shift, one wider breath.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 8.9; Craft potential 9.6; Cohesion 9.1; Intent 8.9.  
**Panel disagreement 11:** the Void-Wire Engineer wanted maximum asymmetry; the Context panel requested a recognizable centered silhouette.  
**Resolution:** begin with a centered body-shaped void, then let asymmetry migrate outward while the central negative space remains the sequence anchor.  
**Refined concept:** An invented seated woman is exactly enclosed by a looped-copper chamber whose brocade-bound nodes touch the geometry of her bracing posture; she opens one hand-sized latch and the tension travels node by node into an asymmetric self-supporting vault, preserving her former silhouette as empty central air while she sits just outside it.

## C12 — Failure Becomes Mesh

**Historical lens:** Saloua Raouda Choucair (1916–2017), used for modular interdependence, stacked structural rhythm, and parts that gain meaning through relation.  
**Contribution:** the snapped strands should interlock through a repeatable modular rule, not convenient visual magic.  
**Critique:** failure becoming distributed support is the most faithful architecture-of-feeling mechanism, and the charred member supplies history, but the snap could become melodramatic. The decisive image should be the quiet instant after, when the strands are taut in many directions and her hands have not yet lowered.  
**Artist-lens score:** Concept 9.5; Craft potential 9.5; Cohesion 9.6; Intent 9.7.  
**Panel disagreement 12:** the Closure Editor wanted the broken brace absent in the finale; the Catharsis Auditor insisted the black fragment remain.  
**Resolution:** suspend one broken length above her lowered hands as an artifact, no longer a support and not erased.  
**Refined concept:** An invented craftswoman winds increasingly fine copper wire around a charred central brace until it breaks without explosion, releasing strands that hook through repeated ceramic eyelets into a taut open mesh across the room; the distributed network lifts the ceiling while one black fragment hangs above her slowly lowering empty hands.
