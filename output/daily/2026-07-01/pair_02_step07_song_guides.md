## Pre-draft questions (answered separately per variation, per the step contract)

### V1 — "Train car"

**Where is the body standing?** Inside a moving train car, upright, one hand on a cold vertical rail, weight shifting with the car's sway. An ad screen loops directly overhead or on the opposite wall — the body cannot NOT see it in this space; the car is a captive box built for exactly this kind of looking. Rails clatter underneath in a fixed, almost-metronomic pattern; the air is recycled, faintly warm, carrying a trace of something chemical-sweet that doesn't belong to a train car — the ad's own scent-marketing conceit leaking into the actual air the body breathes (Axis 2, entry sense: a smell that doesn't belong to the moment yet).

**What could hurt it here?** Nothing external and sudden — no crash, no crisis. The hurt is slower and worse: the realization that the body's own attention is feeding the exact machine it should distrust, and that it cannot look away because the screen was built by people who know precisely how bodies in train cars watch things they can't leave. Fear register: complicity (Axis 3, option 2) — reinforced structurally by the literal captive-audience setting; there is no train-car exit until the next stop, so the "choice" to keep watching is not really a choice.

### V2 — "Corridor of loops"

**Where is the body standing?** In an institutional corridor — fluorescent-lit, long, lined on both sides with looping displays rather than one central screen. The body walks (or stands still and lets the corridor's rhythm walk past it) between repeating architecture: screen, dark bezel, screen, dark bezel. Entry sense (Axis 2): the quality of light changing on a surface — screen-glow sweeping across the corridor walls and, faintly, across the body's own face reflected in each dead bezel between plays.

**What could hurt it here?** The specific horror of recognition-failure (Axis 3, option 5): in each dark bezel between plays, the body's own reflected face looks back at it, and it doesn't fully recognize what it sees — a face already a little changed by the act of watching, unfamiliar in a way that arrives quietly rather than all at once. Nothing chases the body down this corridor. The corridor is doing something slower: converting the body into an audience one loop at a time, and the reflection is the only evidence.

## Facets → Guides (5 facet guides + 1 wildcard, per Step-06 facets, 20-30 lines each)

### Guide 1 — Weight-Not-Tone Somatic Governance (facet weight 0.24)

**Storytelling technique:** Dramatic irony (the audience/body senses the wrongness in the body before the mind names it) paired with synecdoche (the sub-bass stands in for "the whole institution," a part carrying the weight of the system). **Mood/emotional arc:** begins as unnoticed physical pressure — a hum felt in the sternum before it's identified as sound — and never resolves into comfort or into obvious dread; it simply persists, indifferent to the listener's attention, the way institutional power persists whether or not you're watching it. **Instrumentation/texture:** a mono-centered 30-60Hz sine-and-growl hybrid, never gated fully off, riding beneath every section including the chorus — layered under train-rail clatter (V1) or corridor-hum fluorescent buzz (V2) as the field texture. Use Add Instrumentals sparingly to extend the sub-bass's felt continuity across section boundaries rather than letting it reset per section. **Production/effects:** side-chained lightly to the kick so it breathes with the too-perfect groove but never disappears; recorded/rendered to read as felt in the chest, not heard in the ear — no brightness added to this layer, ever. **Structure/dynamics:** present at full weight from :00 to the final second except the bridge, where it is the ONLY thing left (Source-3 form law). **Rhyme scheme note:** N/A (production facet). **Poetic devices to bind lyrically to this facet:** consonance (hard, low consonants — /b/, /d/, /g/ — clustered near lines that describe the sub-bass's physical presence) and synaesthesia (describing the bass as temperature or pressure, never as "sound"). **Hook technique tie-in:** the sub-bass's ONE full-alone moment (the bridge) functions as a structural hook — the true "drop" of this song is silence-of-topline, not noise.

### Guide 2 — Unbroken Glossy Surface / No-Rupture Integrity (facet weight 0.22)

**Storytelling technique:** Verbal irony sustained at the level of the whole arrangement (the music says "everything is fine" for the entire runtime while the lyric's meaning erodes) and understatement (the horror is stated plainly, never amplified by the production). **Mood/emotional arc:** stays major-key bright and "produced-to-perfection" throughout — no minor-key detour, no distortion swell, no tempo collapse; the arc lives entirely in what the words come to mean against an unchanging backdrop. **Instrumentation/texture:** glossy synth-pop chassis — clean digital keys, a bright quantized pluck pattern, Baile-Phonk-adjacent low-end discipline kept "too perfect" rather than aggressive; no guitar distortion, no noise-wall, no genre-inversion drop anywhere in the arrangement, including the final chorus. **Production/effects:** pristine, over-corrected mixdown — vocal comping with zero audible edit noise, a mix so clean it starts to feel like an ad itself (this cleanliness IS the argument, per the Cinematic Modernist's revised ruling). **Structure/dynamics:** dynamics rise only through arrangement density (more layers, wider stereo), never through genre or key shift; the final chorus is louder, not different. **Poetic devices:** epistrophe (a repeated closing phrase across choruses that sounds identical but means differently by the second time, tying directly to Guide 3). **Hook technique tie-in:** the "hook" is precisely how UNBROKEN the gloss is — a listener trying to find the "turn" in the music and failing to find one is the intended experience.

### Guide 3 — Textual Turn Legibility — the Curdling Harmony (facet weight 0.22)

**Storytelling technique:** Repetition-with-altered-meaning (the same words heard twice, believed the first time, caught-in-the-act the second) and free indirect discourse (the harmony voice occupies the narrator's own private, unspoken judgment of themselves). **Mood/emotional arc:** warm/admiring in the early pinned-line delivery → curdled/appraising by no later than the second chorus, per the Silence Skeptic's condition — the SAME processing chain throughout (no new reverb, no new distortion, no pitch-drop on the "cold" pass) so the change reads as a change of mind, not a change of equipment. **Instrumentation/texture:** the lead vocal stays bright and breathy start to finish; the doubled/harmonized layer sits just under and slightly behind the lead in the mix, close enough to be legible as the SAME words, not a new melodic idea. **Production/effects:** identical vocal chain (same compression, same reverb send, same pitch-correction curve) on both the warm and cold deliveries of the pinned line — the ONLY variable allowed to shift is performance: breath placement, consonant hardness, vowel length. **Structure/dynamics:** the pinned line ("look how much they spent" or an owned close variant) appears warm in an early verse or pre-chorus, then appears again — words identical — no later than the second chorus, now delivered with a colder, appraising weight; mix the harmony forward enough on BOTH passes that the repetition (and its shift) is audible, not just legible on a lyric sheet. **Poetic devices:** epizeuxis (the pinned phrase's literal repetition) and antanaclasis (the same words, a different sense). **Hook technique tie-in:** this repetition IS the hook — an adoptable line that only reveals its second meaning on the second listen, rewarding attention per the Hook Neuroscientist's prediction-error model, "a genuine neurological hook built on betrayal."

### Guide 4 — Named-Mechanism Precision (Boomerang Auditor standard) (facet weight 0.16)

**Storytelling technique:** Direct address crossed with clinical diagnosis — a line that sounds like it's confessing affection but is actually naming a mechanism, the way a case study names a failure mode. **Mood/emotional arc:** a single moment of terrible clarity, placed at or near the bridge/post-bridge return, where the narrator names precisely WHY the glossy thing worked on them — not "it was pretty," but the specific causal chain (production value reads as care; care reads as desirability). **Instrumentation/texture:** this line should land where the mix is at its most exposed — ideally just after the sub-bass-alone bridge moment, as the topline first re-enters, so the words have room. **Production/effects:** no vocal effect added here beyond the song's baseline chain — this line should NOT be the "big emotional swell" moment production-wise; its power is in what it says, not how loud it's sung, per the Zen Translator's "don't let production theatrics announce the twist before the words do." **Structure/dynamics:** placed once, precisely, not repeated verbatim elsewhere (though its logic can echo in the chorus's curdled pass). **Poetic devices:** chiasmus (care→value, value→desire, inverted back) or a controlled zeugma binding "spent" across two different registers (money and devotion). **Hook technique tie-in:** NOT the chorus hook — this is the thesis line, positioned to be quoted, not chanted.

### Guide 5 — Cold-Register Restraint (Indignation-Without-Tantrum) (facet weight 0.16)

**Storytelling technique:** the unreliable-but-not-lying narrator (someone reporting their own susceptibility honestly, without melodrama) and litotes (understatement used for a colder, more clinical anger than shouting would achieve). **Mood/emotional arc:** never crosses into pop-punk snarl, screaming, or maximal aggression — anger here reads as precision, as someone who has figured something out and is stating it plainly, which is scarier than a tantrum. **Instrumentation/texture:** vocal delivery stays close-mic'd and controlled even at its coldest; no vocal-fry snarl, no gang-vocal shout-chorus. **Production/effects:** dynamics communicate control, not loss of control — even the loudest chorus moment should sound curated, not unleashed. **Structure/dynamics:** the emotional "peak" of the song is the clarity of the named-mechanism line (Guide 4) and the cold second pass of the pinned line (Guide 3), not a volume peak. **Poetic devices:** anaphora used sparingly and evenly (not shouted) to build the sense of an argument being made calmly. **Hook technique tie-in:** the restraint itself becomes the song's signature — a listener expecting a "drop" gets composure instead, which is the indictment.

### Guide 6 — Wildcard: The Bezel/Screen as Confessional Architecture

Cross-disciplinary seed: think of Renaissance devotional painting's use of a mirror or window within the frame (Van Eyck's convex mirror in the Arnolfini Portrait) — a small reflective surface inside the composition that implicates the viewer as a second, watched watcher. Translate this into sound: the dark bezel between ad-loops (V2) or the ad screen's own faint audio-bleed reflection (V1, the screen's sound arriving a half-beat before its picture, like a mirror lagging) becomes a structural pause — not silence exactly, but a held, listening quality in the mix, as if the track itself is checking to see if anyone's watching it be watched. This wildcard licenses ONE brief moment (2-4 seconds, not a full section) where the too-perfect groove holds its breath — not a rupture, just a flicker of self-awareness in the machine — before resuming exactly as before. Use sparingly; this is atmosphere, not a third load-bearing device (the two-device economy — sub-bass + pitch-shift hook — remains the binding budget per the Raw-Production Skeptic's ruling).
