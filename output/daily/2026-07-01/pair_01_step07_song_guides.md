## Opening questions (mandatory, answered separately per variation)

### V1 — "Catwalk"

**Where is the body standing?** On an observatory catwalk — a narrow steel-mesh walkway bolted to the outside of a dome or instrument housing, high enough that the ground is a suggestion rather than a fact. The grating is cold enough to read as a texture before it reads as temperature. There is a railing, and the hand is on it.

**What could hurt it here?** The literal fall — the mesh underfoot is grate, not floor, and looking down shows the drop through it. The cold at altitude is fast; exposed skin loses heat before the mind catches up. And underneath both of those: the number itself, sung once, is a kind of vertigo with no railing — 6,000 light-years is a distance the body has no organ for, and standing under the light that traveled it is its own species of height.

### V2 — "Field"

**Where is the body standing?** In an open field at roughly 3am — no structure, no railing, grass gone stiff with dew, the kind of dark where the sky is not a backdrop but the largest object in the scene. Nothing between the body and what's directly overhead.

**What could hurt it here?** Exposure, plainly — no walls, no roof, alone in a dark field is its own low-grade animal fear before any cosmic content arrives. But the sharper hurt is retroactive: the light landing on the wet grass and on the skin left the source seven millennia before arrival — the catastrophe is already over, has been over the whole time the body has been alive to stand here, and standing in its afterglow does nothing to undo it. Aftermath-standing: the fear that there is no verb tense available for "helping now."

## Facet-by-facet guide (5 + 1 wildcard, ~20-30 lines each, both variations addressed within each guide)

### Guide 1 — Somatic Fact-Placement Fidelity

**Storytelling techniques:** (1) *Delayed revelation via physical anchoring* — the poem accumulates sensory data about the scene for a full verse before the number arrives, so the fact lands into an already-built body, not an empty one. (2) *Apostrophe without translation* — the singer addresses the star directly ("you," "your light") without ever narrating what the number means; Sebeok's redundant-body-encoding principle says feeling has to travel in the material, not the symbol.

**Mood & emotional arc:** Begin in plain sensory attention — cold (V1) or grass-static-and-silence (V2) — genuinely neutral, almost domestic in its ordinariness before the domestic is ruled out by the sky overhead. The fact arrives as an interruption, not a climax: it should feel like the moment breath catches, not the moment a chorus swells. After the fact, the song does NOT explain — it sits, unresolved, letting the physical sensation (cold, height, held breath) carry the freight the lyric refuses to translate.

**Instrumentation & texture:** The moment of the fact-delivery is where Reznor's "one small harsh-decay crack" (cut down from a full section per Rubin's economy ruling to a single texture-crack) lives — one instant of the wind-drone's granulation audibly glitching, immediately smoothing back into its steady presence. No new instrument enters here. The vocal drops to near-whisper exactly at the fact per the Minimal-Maximalist Validator (Eilish): quiet carries the number, power is saved and withheld even after.

**Production & effects:** Mix proximity does the emotional work Cage forbade the arrangement from doing with a fourth device — the drone should feel like it moves half a step closer in the stereo field at the fact-moment, present but not louder. Dry, close vocal; no reverb bloom timed to the number (a swelling reverb would be the "explaining" the rule forbids).

**Structure & dynamics:** Fact lands at roughly the 40-45% mark of total runtime (the true hinge, not the chorus peak). V1: the fact interrupts mid-verse-2, catwalk-cold already established. V2: the fact interrupts as the eyes finally lift from the grass to the sky, mid-verse-2 also, entry-sense (sound-before-sight) already paid off.

**Rhyming scheme:** Verses use slant/near-rhyme only (never full couplets) — the imperfect rhyme mirrors the imperfect resolution the ending withholds. The fact-line itself should NOT rhyme with its neighbors — it should sit metrically and sonically apart, an intrusion the form itself notices.

**Poetic devices:** Consonance (hard cold-consonant clusters — "grate," "grate-cold," "grip," "grip") around both hinge moments; caesura — a hard mid-line pause exactly where the fact interrupts, visible as a line-break the singer has to breathe through.

### Guide 2 — Three-Source Discipline & Bar-Count Drift

**Storytelling techniques:** (1) *Formal mimesis* — the FORM enacts the content: the listener's body feels the "off" of the drifting bar-count the way the singer's body feels cold-induced unsteadiness, without either being named. (2) *Refrain-as-echo, not refrain-as-hook* — the returning address-line to the star is never identical twice; each return sits on a different harmonic footing (per the Hook Neuroscientist/Levitin dopamine-prediction-error model, defended against the Silence Skeptic's "adoptable hook" objection by making the harmonic shift diegetic — it changes because the singer's understanding changes).

**Mood & emotional arc:** The limp in the bar-count should read as bodily, not glitchy — cold feet shifting for warmth (Questlove's pocket-as-argument), not a production error. It starts almost-imperceptible, compounds very slightly through the song (Glass's "drift, not repetition"), and tightens again briefly in the final verse — not to resolve, but to show the body trying and failing to steady itself.

**Instrumentation & texture:** THREE sources, no more: (1) whispered-to-mid female vocal, crystalline capacity held back; (2) never-resolving field-recorded wind-drone — catwalk-railing wind for V1, open-field wind for V2 — granulated and pitched down; (3) ONE percussive element sourced from the SAME foley family as the drone (metal-grating footfalls for V1, grass/fabric-rustle for V2), carrying the bar-count drift in its placement. No pad. No second harmonized vocal outside the bridge.

**Production & effects:** Field recording is the literal spine — record (or credibly simulate in the prompt) wind against a real resonant surface (railing mesh for V1, open grass for V2), then granulate and pitch down until it stops resolving into a musical pitch and instead sits as weather. The percussive element should be dry and close, almost too intimate, so its drift is audible as placement, not tempo-map error.

**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:15-3:45 runtime. Bar lengths in verses shift by roughly one beat every 2-3 repetitions (per `step04_medium.md`), never announced, never resolving into a clean meter before the song ends.

**Rhyming scheme:** Uneven line lengths mirroring the bar-drift — a verse's four lines should NOT scan identically; syllable counts drift by 1-2 per repetition, matching the device structurally, not just texturally.

**Poetic devices:** Anaphora on the address-line's opening words (e.g., repeated "You—" openers) so the ear tracks the return even as the harmony under it keeps shifting; polyptoton on a root word tied to light/burning across the song (light, lit, alight) to keep the vocabulary tight inside the three-source economy.

### Guide 3 — Terror-Adjacence Without Added Texture

**Storytelling techniques:** (1) *Named physical hazard, no metaphor laundering* — state plainly what could hurt the body (the grate underfoot for V1, the total exposure of the open field for V2) rather than only implying danger through cosmic scale. (2) *Withheld catharsis* — per the Structural Skeptic, the ending must NOT resolve into safety; the last image should be the same hazard from the opening, now impossible to un-notice.

**Mood & emotional arc:** V1 (scale vertigo): the fear should physically resemble height-vertigo — a lurch, not a swoon. V2 (aftermath-standing): the fear should resemble the specific dread of realizing help arrived too late to be help — not panic, a colder and more permanent unease. Neither variation gets to "settle." Per Zimmer's revision: "the drone doesn't resolve at all. It's still there in the outro."

**Instrumentation & texture:** Terror-adjacence is earned ONLY through how close the drone sits in the mix and how hard/soft the voice pushes — never a fourth device. In the final chorus, the drone should sit at its closest, most present placement of the whole song — not louder, closer.

**Production & effects:** No brightening EQ move in the outro (a "clarifying" high-shelf boost would read as comfort). If anything, let the vocal thin very slightly against the drone's continued fullness in the final 15-20 seconds, so the drone visibly outlasts and outweighs the voice.

**Structure & dynamics:** Final chorus returns the opening physical image (grate/height for V1; open field/dew for V2) recontextualized by the fact heard earlier — not softened, sharpened. Outro: voice drops out or thins to near-silence; drone remains at full presence through the literal last second of audio.

**Rhyming scheme:** The song's final couplet (if any) should be its least resolved rhyme — off-rhyme or no rhyme at all, denying the ear the click of closure a perfect couplet would offer.

**Poetic devices:** Diacope (repeating a phrase with a word or two between) on the hazard-word across the song's arc, so it accumulates weight without becoming a chorus hook; epizeuxis (immediate word repetition) reserved for exactly one moment — the closest the song gets to breaking, then it doesn't break.

### Guide 4 — Genre-Fusion Vocabulary Fidelity to GiGi FM's Exact Quote

**Storytelling techniques:** (1) *Texture-as-citation* — the palette must be traceable, in the finished prompt, to GiGi FM's literal words: "rooted in the bittersweet mix of rave sunrise bliss and bluesy soul sadness." (2) *Split-register melody* — the melodic line itself should carry both halves of the quote: a soul-inflected minor/modal descent (bluesy soul sadness) that, once per song (percussive element only, per `05_pair_assignments.md`), brushes against a brief, brighter pulse (rave sunrise bliss) without ever becoming a fourth instrument or breaking the three-source ceiling.

**Mood & emotional arc:** The "bluesy soul sadness" half governs 90% of the song's harmonic language — blue-note inflections in Dorian/Aeolian, not major-key comfort. The "rave sunrise bliss" half is licensed for exactly one moment: the percussive element gets a single instance of brighter, more insistent pulse-placement (not a new sound, just a change in how the existing foley-percussion is placed rhythmically) — a glimmer of dawn-adjacent hope inside an otherwise Aeolian frame, immediately reabsorbed by the drone's steadiness.

**Instrumentation & texture:** No synth pad, no arpeggiator, no "sunrise" cliché instrumentation (no glockenspiel, no bell-tone shimmer — those would drift toward the banned house-lexicon "crystalline arpeggios" territory). The brightness lives entirely in the percussive element's placement and the vocal's brief lift in register at that one moment.

**Production & effects:** Keep the vocal's "soul" quality via breath audible in the mic, blue-note pitch-bends into key syllables, and a slightly behind-the-beat phrasing (soul/blues pocket) contrasted against the metronomic drift of the bar-count device.

**Structure & dynamics:** The "rave sunrise" moment should land once, briefly (2-4 bars), ideally just before or overlapping the fact-delivery hinge — brightness arriving right where the wound also arrives, so the two halves of GiGi FM's quote collide instead of taking turns.

**Rhyming scheme:** The one "bright" moment can risk a cleaner internal rhyme than the rest of the song allows, since it is explicitly the exception the palette permits.

**Poetic devices:** Synaesthesia (describing the light in temperature/texture terms — "warm the way a bruise is warm") to fuse the "bittersweet" half of the quote directly into image-making; assonance on open vowel sounds during the bright moment to physically brighten the sung tone.

### Guide 5 — Unresolved Ending & House-Lexicon Cleanliness

**Storytelling techniques:** (1) *Circular non-return* — the song ends on the same physical image it opened on, but the listener now carries the fact, so it is NOT actually circular; it's a spiral that looks like a circle. (2) *Negative-space closure* — the last lyric line should be a question or an unfinished address, never a resolved statement, so the silence after the drone (which itself never fully fades) is where the "ending" actually lives.

**Mood & emotional arc:** No triumphant swell. No brightening. No modulation to a "hopeful" relative major in the final chorus. The emotional arc ends exactly where facet 3 (terror-adjacence) says it should — heavier, not lighter, than the opening, because the listener now KNOWS what they're standing under.

**Instrumentation & texture:** Confirm zero house-lexicon phrases anywhere in the draft: no "more sub and more sky," no "frost-air pad," no "starfield percussion," no "zodiacal glow," no "crystalline arpeggios," no "triple arch" language, no "small enough to understand," no "dew-bright," no "glass harmonica sheen," no "clear silver tone," no "make my little fear," no "small astonished laugh." (V2's field setting risks "dew-bright" specifically — use "dew-soaked" or "wet grass" instead, never the banned collocation.)

**Production & effects:** The literal final seconds of the audio (as described in the prompt) must include the drone at full, undiminished presence — describe it explicitly so a renderer can't default to a fade-to-silence-on-vocal-only ending.

**Structure & dynamics:** Outro: 8-15 seconds, drone-only or drone-dominant, vocal present only as a fragment or breath, never a resolved final phrase.

**Rhyming scheme:** The very last line should break whatever rhyme pattern the song established — an intentional formal refusal to "land."

**Poetic devices:** Aposiopesis (a sentence that trails off, deliberately incomplete) on the final sung line for both variations.

## Hook techniques (3 possible approaches, both variations)

1. **The Diegetic Return** — the address-line to the star ("you," direct apostrophe) returns 2-3 times across the song, each time landing on a different chord under the same or near-same words — Levitin's prediction-error hook, defended as diegetic (the harmony shifts because the singer's understanding shifts) rather than gamed repetition.
2. **The Whisper-to-Almost-Belt Arc** — vocal power is held back for the entire song (per Emotional Cadence flair #15) and only allowed to open up — not fully belt, just audibly more present — in the final pass of the return-line, immediately undercut by the drone's unmoved presence, so the "release" reads as futile rather than triumphant.
3. **The Percussive Foley Signature** — the single percussive element (metal-grating footfall for V1, grass/fabric-rustle for V2) becomes recognizable enough across repetitions that its ONE moment of "rave sunrise" brightness (Guide 4) functions as the closest thing to a chorus-hook the song allows, without ever adding a fourth instrument.
