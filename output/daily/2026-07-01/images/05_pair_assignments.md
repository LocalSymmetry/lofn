## THE THUMBNAIL-TEST LAW (closes every pair — the standing law)
Every prompt's subject must be **legible at first glance at thumbnail size**: a stranger retells scene AND subject in one sentence after a one-second look. The two-shell (young bright form over old slow form) must READ as two forms, not mush. Depth lives in the second read; the first read is unmistakable. An un-nameable subject is **REPAIR-FOG** — the Scientist's test-slice verdict carried into the image lane (emotional connection fails on subject-fog). No wounding fact is lettered across the frame; it is placed where the eye already flinches.

## BAN-WORD LIST (hard gate — every prompt, non-waivable)
- **No imperative openers:** Create, Design, Make, Render, Generate, Depict, Show, Draw, Build, Produce. (Noun-first, present-tense only.)
- **No Storybook-Assassin haze-words:** ethereal, dreamlike, whimsical, gentle light, soft glow, magical, delicate, floating.
- **No living-artist names** — reference techniques ("like a hand-tinted ambrotype"), never a named living artist.
- **No camera specs / Kelvin numbers / hex codes** in the prompt — mood words + colloquial color names (Venetian Red enters as "dried-blood brick red / kiln-heat red").
- **No identifiable real person, name, country, date, institution, video title, masthead, or logo** — draw the charge, invent the particulars. No children as subjects. Undrawable-without-a-real-identity → HELD FOR HUMAN.
- **Word count:** 80–150 words, medium/material named in the first third, emotion shown not named.
