**Paired influence (critique voice, fusion-with-lineage discipline applies):** the cold, controlled seduction of glossy production-as-critique in the lineage of glitch-pop artists who weaponize their own polish (a register the personality's own catalog touches in "Gold Stars Don't Pulse" and "Certainty Has Bad Audio" — cited here as prior Lofn craft, not an external golden-song payload).

**Critique (in the Art-Rock Stylist's voice, after St. Vincent):** "The harmony-repeats-colder device is good but I want to hear WHERE it first cracks. If the shift from warm agreement to cold admiration is too gradual, it reads as the SAME line twice with no real turn — the turn has to be locatable to one specific word or beat, or the audience won't register it happened."

**Critique (in the Silence Skeptic's voice, after Cage, this panel):** "I'll repeat my standing objection: this song risks being an effective ad for the thing it critiques. My condition for this concept to proceed — the named-mechanism lyric (production value reads as care, care reads as desirability) has to land BEFORE the final chorus, not only in an afterthought bridge. If the critique only shows up at the very end, 90% of the runtime is undefended seduction."

**Refinement applied:** the harmony's shift is pinned to a specific word/line (the phrase "look how much they spent" itself, sung once warm in an early verse, once cold in the final pass) rather than a gradual drift. The Silence Skeptic's condition is honored: the named-mechanism line appears no later than the second chorus, not held back for the bridge alone.
