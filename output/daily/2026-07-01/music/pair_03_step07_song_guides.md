## THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS (answered concretely before any guide directs)

**1. Where is the body standing?** Outdoors at night, directly under an open sky — no roof, no kitchen. The body stands on the flat cold ground of a scrubland field far from town, the survivor star burning low and unmistakable above it (the one that outlived its twin's shell). Per variation the exact footing shifts but the sky is always physically overhead: V1 the body has a wristwatch it keeps checking against the star (two clocks); V2 the body's own skin registers the warmth as heat off a fuse; V3 the body is at the field's edge where the idea "it may already be gone" arrives before the eye confirms; V4 the body watches the light change on the ground as if watching a rehearsal it was allowed to attend.

**2. What could hurt it here?** The clean, enacted danger is NOT abstract cosmic dread — it is the specific bodily fact that *the light warming this exact skin, tonight, is the same explosion that will one day kill the thing being sung to, and at 6,000 light-years that death may already be in transit and simply unseen.* The body is warmed by a fuse. The terror is staged in a couplet — the warmth on the arms is the countdown reaching the skin — never asserted with "and now I understand." Both deaths (the twin's past explosion; this star's coming one) are nameable to a stranger after one listen.
