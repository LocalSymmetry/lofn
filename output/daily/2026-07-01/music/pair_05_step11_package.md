It opens as sound before sight: a breath-forward female voice already fused into the tanpura-like microtonal drone, doubled with one take a quarter-tone beneath so the two takes beat against each other — the set's densest gamaka — wide and center, while a near-subliminal wooden tick drifts under the pulse, far-left and low. The voice addresses the doomed shells in second person, a prayer with no god and no rescue, un-belted and even, keeping company by refusing to look away. The raised seventh holds unresolved through a vow-refrain that loses its tail at each return. One late first-person line cracks — choice or trap — and the doubled voice splits audibly apart before re-fusing. The bridge is the act of watching with no object: the tick gone, drone alone, the seventh suspended. It stops mid-vow. No soprano, no lush pad, no comfort.

## Production Dramaturgy — Song D

- **The doubled voice (quarter-tone apart, fused with the drone):** D's 5-second ear-mark — no tick foregrounded, no drone-alone opening, but two takes of the same woman beating against each other, the witness already inseparable from the watch. The split at the crack is the one moment the vow doubts itself; the re-fuse is the choice made.
- **The near-subliminal tick (far-left, low):** the young layer barely admitted — in a prayer, time passing is the intruder.
- **The collapsing vow-refrain:** the refrain loses its tail at each return (full vow → shortened vow → the outro's fragment) — the gift shrinking in transit exactly as the hinge says it must; the mid-vow stop is the refrain's third and final collapse, structural, not a fade.
- **The refused seventh under the hook:** devotion denied a cadence; the prayer never gets its amen.
