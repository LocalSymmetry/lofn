# PAIR 04 — STEP 09 · ARTIST REFINEMENT
## SLOT P04 · the three gates run line by line · what changed and why

**Gates run, in this order, on all four drafts:**
- ⭐ **THE FLAT TEST (words):** say it out loud, flat, no music, to a person's face. Survives = simple.
  Dies = merely short.
- ⭐ **THE HUM TEST (music):** the song must be hummable with no words. The instrumental hook must exist
  **before the vocal enters and after it leaves.**
- ⭐ **THE REMARK TEST (attention):** would a listener say *"that was clever"*? If yes it is a toll.

## 2. THE FLAT TEST — three lines that died, and what replaced them

**⛔ A plain line is harder to write than a dense one.** Said aloud, flat, these three did not survive:

1. **V3, Verse 5:** ~~`That is not a mistake that anybody made.`~~ → **`That was not an accident. That was somebody's afternoon.`**
   The original is a double negative wearing a suit. The repair is the same claim with a **day and an
   object in it**, which is what the metaprompt demands of a hard thing: *an event with a day and an
   object and a person.*
2. **V4, Verse 4:** ~~`There's nobody answering now. That's the shape of it.`~~ → **`There's nobody answering now. Nobody's going to.`**
   *"That's the shape of it"* is a writer's phrase, not a person's. The repair is flatter, worse news,
   and easier to say to a face — which is the test.
3. **V2, Verse 4:** kept ~~as drafted~~ — `That isn't a thing that went wrong. That's the job. / That is the entire job.` **survives flat**, and the doubling is the point: she says it twice because she means it, not because the first one failed.

⭐ **Checked and kept:** `That is a decision. Somebody made it.` (V1) · `Nobody stood in for anybody.
Somebody stood up.` (V4) · `It isn't close and it isn't mine.` (V3). All three survive being said flat
to a face, and all three are said in **the same voice as the joke** — no hush, no ritard, no drop-out.

## 3. THE REMARK TEST — one cut, and it was the line I liked most

⛔ **V3, Verse 5:** ~~`The gap is the same size as the words that were in it.`~~ → **`You could put your hand in it.`**

The cut line *explains the device.* It is accurate, it is the best sentence in the draft, and it is a
**toll**: a listener who parses it will say *"that was clever"* and will have stopped listening to do it.
The replacement does the same work physically and asks nothing. **Nobody has ever been moved while
parsing.**

⭐ **Swept for the same failure across all four:** no lyric anywhere in this pair points at the
architecture, names the shrink, counts the syllables, or invites the listener to notice the hole. The
absence is stated **once per song, plainly, as a fact** — `There's nobody answering now` and its three
variants — and then the list continues with the missing thing inside it.

## 4. THE HUM TEST — passed, and the one production decision it forced

The vocal hook is **half missing by design** from the stop bar onward, so the song cannot rely on the
voice to be the tune. It does not: ⭐ **the chiming three-note synth riff is the tune-carrier**, it plays
**alone in bars 1–2 before any vocal**, it fences the right-hand side of every empty answer bar, and it
plays **alone in the last two bars after the voice has gone.** Hum the record with no words and the riff
is what comes back.

**Forced decision:** V1's outro originally had the chime under her three closing calls all the way out.
Changed so the vocal ends and the chime holds the final two bars alone. **Reason:** if the voice is the
last thing on a record whose whole subject is a voice leaving, the arrangement has argued against the
lyric. The tune has to outlive her, and now it does — in all four.

**Second check, THE SWAGGER:** the bassline is hummable in all four independently of the vocal (walking,
front of mix, moving between chords, **on** the grid while the drums sit 8–14 ms late). ⛔ No distortion
anywhere in the pair. Swagger is a rhythm section here, as it is supposed to be.

## 6. ⛔ THE HUMAN-SUBJECT SWEEP, RUN LAST AND RUN BY HAND

Every proper noun in the pair, listed exhaustively so a stranger can check it: **Della** — invented, the
stand-in who ran the desk, alive, fine, and thanked. That is the complete list. **No band is named. No
record is titled. No producer, label, town or date appears. The one on the sleeve has no name.** He
**shouted his voice away over a bad PA and the room was booked for one more afternoon** — an ordinary,
invented reason, and it is stated in the lyric of all four so no listener has to guess at a darker one.

⛔ **Nobody dies in these songs. Nobody is ill in these songs. There is no scandal, no cover-up and no
victim.** The substitution is a decision somebody made twice on purpose, and the song's entire argument
is that it was the right one. **REAL GRIEF IS NOT RAW MATERIAL** — and here there is no grief to take:
the shape was borrowed and everything hung on it was invented today.

The lineage debt is real and it is paid **once, in `DEDICATION`, as a class** — session singers, tape
ops and stand-ins whose voices are the most-sung bar on records issued under somebody else's name.
⛔ Never in a style field. ⛔ Never in a lyric. ⛔ No individual named.

## 7. WHAT DID NOT CHANGE, AND WHY IT NEEDED NO DEFENCE

- **The byte-identical chorus calls.** Four times per song, word for word, in all four songs.
  ⭐ *A byte-identical chorus needs no defence and no note* — this line is the note that there is no note.
- **The answer being the tail of her own line.** It is the oldest device in call-and-response and it is
  **felt, not noticed** — which is exactly why it can carry the whole architecture without charging a
  toll. It also does the reveal engine's work mechanically: the room can finish the line because the
  room was handed the words a beat ago, **and that is when you know it was never yours.**
- **The four failed eligibility properties.** 3/7 is the AMBITIOUS target and the misses are the design.
  ⛔ No repair was attempted on cognitive ease; making the hole easier would delete the piece.
