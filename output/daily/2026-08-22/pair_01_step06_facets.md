## 1. THE CONCEPT, IN ONE BREATH

The Marigold Suite — a function room on the first floor of a hotel in an invented town — is being set up
for a party. It is four o'clock. Half the chairs are still on the trolley. She has the seating plan on a
clipboard and she is reading it out loud, table by table, as **praise**: who is good at what, and where
she has therefore put them. One card in the box has a name on it for somebody who is not coming.
Everybody knows. **The party is going to be good anyway** — and that, not the empty chair, is the thing
she cannot stop noticing and will not apologise for.

**⛔ The seed test, applied to this concept before anything else:** could this list be read off a page in
an empty room? **No** — the whole conceit is that the room is empty *for one more hour* and everything in
the list is about to walk through the door. She is not remembering the party. She is **loading it**.
The clock is in the concept, not attached to it.

## 5. THE ABSENCE — ITS EXACT HARMONIC REALISATION
*(RULING B1 state→remove→keep pointing · RULING A2 two cycles, no hole in the first thirty seconds, the
hole must be inconvenient · RULING C1 how the body still feels it · RULING X it is named in the lyric)*

**The figure:** the twelve-bar's last four bars — the turnaround. In this bed it is
**V — IV — I — V**, bars nine to twelve, with the horns hitting the five chord on the front of bar nine
and again on the front of bar twelve to throw the form back to the top.

**STATE IT (B1 / A2).** The intro is **eight bars: the four-bar turnaround played twice, in full, with
the five chord both times.** First statement: upright bass, brushes, piano. Second statement: horns and
the twirling acoustic guitar added. At 102 BPM one bar is 2.35 seconds, so the two statements occupy
**0:00–0:19** — the ear owns the figure before a word is sung.

**REMOVE IT.** Verse 1 is the first sung twelve-bar cycle, bars 9–20, ending 0:19–0:47. Its own turnaround
falls at bars 17–20 — **0:38** — and **the five chord does not arrive.** ✅ Past the thirty-second floor,
measured, not estimated. Bar nine of every subsequent cycle goes to the **four chord** and bar twelve
**stays on the tonic**. The five chord is never played again in the record.

**WHY THE HOLE IS INCONVENIENT.** The five chord is the only thing in a twelve-bar that says *go back to
the top.* Removing it takes the song's own turn signal. Every cycle now arrives home with no push, and
the band has to start the next one on nothing but the count. That cost is the point: it is a gap in the
part of the form that was **doing the work**, not in the decoration.

**HOW THE BODY STILL FEELS IT (C1).** ⭐ **The bass keeps walking the turnaround's root motion under a
chord that never comes.** At the top of bar nine the upright still climbs and lands hard on the **fifth
degree** — the same note, the same weight, the same placement — while the band sits on the four or the
one above it. The listener's ear supplies the missing chord from the root alone; the hips still turn the
corner. That is not a metaphor: it is a bass note against a chord that disagrees with it, and it is in
the bar.

**NAMED IN THE LYRIC (X).** ⛔ Not as harmony. The absence is named **as the empty seat and the card**,
in words, plainly, **once per variation**, in the same voice as the joke — §6.

**THE SECOND, SMALLER HOLE (LEAVE THE HOLE).** The horns answer the hanging third line of every couplet
with one short dry stab. **In each variation they fail to answer exactly once, and that is where the
wolf goes.** ⛔ This is **not a band drop-out**: bass, brushes, piano and guitar do not change by a hair.
One answer does not come. The rhythm section walks straight over the gap.

## 6. THE WOLF — AN EVENT WITH A DAY, AN OBJECT AND A PERSON

**Object:** a place card, handwritten, in a shoebox with a rubber band round it.
**Person:** Nell. Invented, per §3.0 slot grammar. **Place:** invented. **When:** unspecified.
**What is never supplied:** why she is not coming. ⛔ No cause, no illness, no accident, no date, no
funeral, no explanation. It is not withheld for effect — **it is not the subject.**

**It resolves.** In every variation she does **one specific physical thing with the card, once**, and
then goes back to the list with the missing thing inside it. The band does not slow down; the horns
simply do not answer that one hanging line; the next tercet gets its stab back as if nothing happened.

⛔ **HUMAN SUBJECT STANDARD.** Invented person, invented room, invented town, unpinned date. No real
harmed person, no real official, no real case. **No child appears anywhere in this slot.** No line
mourns; no line explains; no line asks the listener for anything.

## 7. THE HOOK'S FUNCTION — what it must DO
*(⭐ its words are not written here. They are written in the song, from the concept, at step 08.)*

**Length:** four to eight syllables. **Reach:** no interval a normal throat cannot hit at volume while
out of breath and holding a tray.
**Grammatical shape:** an **instruction or a placement**, never a feeling. Something an actual person
says out loud in a function room while doing something with their hands.
**Ownership:** it belongs to the room. By its second appearance a stranger must be able to shout it back
without having heard the verse.
**Load:** ⛔ **the hook may not carry the thesis.** The forbidden section for this slot is the final
chorus, and a hook that states the reveal engine would land the thesis there four times. The hook is a
plain true sentence about furniture, doors, floors or lights; the reveal engine lands in a verse, a
bridge, a tag or an outro — never in the chorus.
**Distinctness:** four variations, four hooks, no shared stem, no shared verb, no shared object.
**The instrumental half (THE HUM TEST):** the hook has a **horn shape that exists before the vocal enters
and after it leaves** — stated in the intro's second turnaround and played again in the last eight bars.
⛔ If the tune is only in the voice, the song has failed and no lyric repair fixes it.

## 9. SOURCE-2 IMPORTS — verbatim, as production behaviour only
⛔ **Neither phrase may appear in a lyric.** Both are style-field and guide vocabulary.

1. **"twirling acoustic guitars"** — a flat-top acoustic and a small archtop playing a **triplet figure
   that circles the root, the third and the sixth of the chord the turnaround is missing.** It literally
   twirls around the hole: the guitars go round the five chord's notes without anybody playing the five
   chord. Dry, close, no reverb, panned a hand's width either side of centre. **It enters at the intro's
   second turnaround and it is the thing still spinning when the horns stop answering.**
2. **"an overall mood of quiet reverence"** — ⭐ **IMPORTED INVERTED, and it goes in the EXCLUDE field.**
   Reverence is exactly what this record refuses: it is the sound a band makes when it decides the sad
   thing needs help. So the phrase is used as a **negative production instruction** — no hush, no
   ritardando, no held note, no room tail, no dynamic dip anywhere near the card. The one place a normal
   record would go quiet, this one gets **one missing horn stab and nothing else.**

## 10. WHAT THIS PAIR IS BETTING, AND HOW IT COULD BE WRONG

**The bet:** that a **seating plan is a love letter with a floor plan**, and that reading one out loud is
an act with a cost and a clock — which is the answer to L38's *a catalogue has no verb*.

**The three ways it fails, named now so QA can measure them:**
1. **It goes sentimental.** The empty chair is the most over-written object in popular music. Guard: the
   card is a **work item** — it gets squared to a fork like everything else — and nothing in any lyric
   says why.
2. **It goes clever.** *Arrangement* means three things in this room (tables, flowers, band) and the
   reveal engine is named *the arrangement outlives its reason*. Guard: **the word is used at most once
   per song, plainly, about flowers or chairs, and never near the thesis.** THE REMARK TEST is applied
   line by line at step 09.
3. **It becomes one song four times.** Guard: §4's table, enforced at step 09 by a machine sweep for any
   line appearing in more than one variation, and by four different hooks, four different keys, four
   different closing moves, four different entry parades and four different acts on the card.
