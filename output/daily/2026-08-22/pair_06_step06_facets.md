# PAIR 06 — STEP 06 · FACETS
## `daily-music-2026-08-22-sunna-offbeat` · SLOT P06 · AMBITIOUS · NEWS · AWE · ⭐ NO WOLF

**Bed:** art-pop — dry brass stabs that answer a line and get out inside the bar, unreverbed · hard
programmed low end · a vocal that moves sultry→savage without changing person. **112 BPM exactly.**
**Setting:** an office being packed into boxes, seven in the evening, a stapler that belongs to nobody
now in her hand. ⛔ Not a kitchen.
**Absence (SPATIAL):** the centre of the stereo field — occupied for all of section one, then vacated.
**Reveal engine:** the settlement is the confession, and she takes the night anyway.
**Verse architecture:** list-stanzas of exactly five items, the fifth always a person.
**Forbidden section:** the thesis may not land in the outro.
**Sung fact:** *four hundred million* — once, in words, responded to and then declined.

## 2. THE OFFICE'S PHYSICAL INVENTORY — the shared world all four songs draw from

Written once, here, so the four variations can take **different objects out of the same room** rather
than four writers describing four rooms.

**Fixed geography.** Fourth floor, open plan, one glass meeting room at the north end, a partition with a
kettle behind it, a goods lift and a passenger lift, one door with a card reader, tall windows on the west
side with vertical blinds, a carpet in a colour nobody has ever named. **The floor is tile at the edges
and carpet in the middle.**

**In the middle of the floor:** the copier · the tower under her desk that has hummed for nine years ·
a small speaker somebody brought in years ago and never took home · the recycling drum. ⭐ **Everything
this pair vacates is something that was physically in the middle of this room.**

**On and in the desks:** hard rubber bands · keys that open nothing here any more · a chewed biro from a
fire drill · a soup sachet from a winter · a ruler with the numbers worn off · a tin of gold drawing
pins · the good scissors · a photograph of this floor with the wrong carpet · box tape · a stapler.

**Sounds the room actually makes:** chair legs on tile · the blind cord ticking on glass · box tape
tearing in one long note · the kettle behind the partition · the lift arriving · the door's green chirp ·
the copier's fan · the tower's hum · a stapler.

**Bodies:** five people who will never work together again, and the two who have already gone down for
more boxes. Nobody is crying. Somebody has taken their shoes off.

## 4. THE EXACT FIVE-ITEM LIST LOGIC, PER VARIATION

⭐ The five-item list is this slot's **most heavily specified object**, which makes it the one most likely
to become a template. So the *grammar* of the list is different in all four, not just its contents.

**V1 — BARE INVENTORY.** Five noun phrases, **no main verb anywhere in the stanza**, each carrying one
concrete defect or history (*gone hard as wire*, *worn off*, *chewed flat*). The fifth line breaks the
pattern by **attaching a person to an object already named in the stanza** — the person arrives as a
*trace*, not as a subject: whose handwriting, whose repair, whose hands it came back to.

**V2 — VERB-FIRST PAST TENSE, ONE ABSENT SUBJECT.** Five clauses that all begin with a past-tense verb
and share a subject that is **never stated** (*Paid for… · Signed… · Filed… · Kept… · Bought…*). The
fifth line **snaps into the present tense and names a person doing something now** — the tense change is
the arrival, and it is the only tense change in the stanza.

**V3 — LOG ENTRIES.** Five comma-stacked noun runs with **no articles and no verbs**, in the flat register
of a machine record (*day, hour, door · thumb, glass, chirp*). The fifth line is **another entry in the
same log that happens to be a person** — same grammar, same flatness, and that is exactly why it lands.

**V4 — FIVE SOUNDS, PRESENT PARTICIPLE.** Five things heard, each `<object> <-ing> <detail>`. The fifth
sound **is a person**: a voice, a count-in, a laugh that is described the way the other four sounds were.
⭐ The list is an **audio inventory**, which is why this is the variation where the room's own pitch gets
named.

**One rule shared by all four, and it is a rule about arrival, not shape:** the fifth item is never
introduced with *and then* or *and finally*. It arrives at full speed, in the middle of the list's own
momentum, and the list keeps going in the next stanza as if a person were an ordinary item — because in
this room, at seven at night, they are the only item that was ever worth anything.

## 5. ANTI-TEMPLATE SWEEP — what is forbidden across the four before drafting starts

⛔ No two variations may open a stanza with the same part of speech.
⛔ No variation may use another's appetite verb in a chorus.
⛔ The word *box* appears in all four (it is the setting) but never in the same syntactic position.
⛔ The stapler is F1 and belongs to V1 and V4 only; V2 and V3 may not use it as an instrument or an image.
⛔ Each variation's centre-event is a **different physical object** and each is named once, plainly.
⛔ No variation ends on a minor turn, an apology, a *but*, or a reconsideration. **There is no sad ending
in this slot.** The last thing heard is always somebody still doing something.

## 7. THE SPATIAL ABSENCE, MADE READABLE — and why it cannot rest on the mix

⚠️ **Stereo placement is one of the least reliable things a text prompt controls.** A generator that
flattens the field would delete this slot's entire identity if the identity lived only in the pan.

So the absence is carried **twice**, and the second carry is the one that survives:

1. **In the mix (asked for, not relied on):** lead vocal dead-centre for all of section one, then vacated;
   she sings from the sides for the rest; the two sides are **deliberately not symmetrical** so the ear
   keeps hunting the middle; the dry brass stabs are dead-centre throughout — **which means the horns are
   the only thing left in the hole.**
2. ⭐ **As an EVENT ANYONE CAN HEAR AND NAME (RULING X):** in each variation a **named object that was
   physically in the middle of the room** stops, at a named point, and never comes back — the tower's
   hum, the copier, the door's chirp, the speaker. It is **named once, in plain words, in the lyric**,
   in exactly the voice that sang the funny line, and **the band does not slow down.**

**RULING B1 — state → remove → keep pointing:** the centre is *occupied and audible* for the whole of
section one (well past thirty seconds at 112 BPM), so what is taken is a thing the ear had already
learned. **RULING A2 — the hole is inconvenient:** the room's own machine noise was the record's floor;
losing it costs the arrangement its bed, and the bass has to widen to cover a gap it was not written for.
**RULING C1 — how the body still feels it:** the two sides are unequal in content, level, bandwidth or
density, so the ear keeps trying to average them into a middle that is not there, and the dry centred
brass stab lands in that middle every two bars like a hand pointing at an empty chair.

## 8. NO WOLF — stated as a positive constraint, not an omission

⭐ **The news is the SETTING, not the wound.** Somewhere a very large number was paid so that nothing
would have to change. She responds to it **once** and then declines to be sad about it, and the record
does not come back to it. ⛔ There is no dark line, no late minor turn, no apology, no *but*, and no
redemption offered to the thing that got paid for.

⛔ **AND IT IS NOT PLEASANTNESS.** *Steady + warm + cohesive* is one bad decision from wallpaper.
**Appetite is a verb.** In every variation she **wants a specific thing and takes it** — the drawer and
the floor · the night as expenditure · a line in a log · the volume. If a listener cannot say what she
took, the variation has failed and gets cut, not softened.

## 9. SOURCE-2 IMPORTS → PRODUCTION BEHAVIOUR ONLY

⛔ Never in a lyric. Both phrases go in the style field, verbatim, as instructions.

- **"transmogrifies those voices into what are best described as sonic events"** → at a named point the
  stacked backing vocals **stop being words**: the same take is chopped to the grid on the sides and used
  as percussion — consonants only, no vowels, no meaning. It is the run's *"a voice that has become an
  event rather than a narrator"*, and here it is also **why the sides are not symmetrical.**
- **"glassy textures"** → a thin, brittle, high FM bell layer on **one side only**, tracking the brass
  stab a fifth up and arriving a sixteenth late. It is the one element in the record with no low end, and
  it exists to make one side measurably different from the other.
