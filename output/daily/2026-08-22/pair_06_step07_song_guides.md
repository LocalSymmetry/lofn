# PAIR 06 — STEP 07 · SONG GUIDES
## `daily-music-2026-08-22-sunna-offbeat` · SLOT P06 · four variations · **112 BPM exactly, all four**

**One bed, four arrangements.** ⛔ No gear-change, no genre-shift, no tempo change, no dynamic swell doing
a timbre's job. The drive is **steadiness**; every change in all four songs is a **named instrument
entering or leaving at a named point**, one at a time.

**Bar arithmetic used throughout:** 112 BPM, 4/4 → one bar = **2.14 s**, four bars = **8.6 s**,
eight bars = **17.1 s**, sixteen bars = **34.3 s**. Every timing below is computed from that, not estimated.

**Shared bed (all four):** dry brass stabs — one trumpet, one baritone sax, in unison an octave apart —
that **answer a line and get out inside the bar**, unreverbed, **dead-centre**. A hard programmed low end:
square-wave sub locked to the grid, moving **between** chords rather than sitting on roots, front of mix,
−2 dB against the lead. Programmed kit **8–14 ms late** against a sub that is dead on the grid. Handclaps
dry and dead-centre. Stacked harmony is a required instrument, not a garnish. Lead vocal close on a
dynamic mic, breaths left in, **no transparent pitch correction**, doubles misaligned 15–35 ms and ±6
cents. −14 LUFS with at least 10 dB of crest.

⛔ **THE FAILURE MODE, NAMED ONCE FOR ALL FOUR GUIDES:** *a pad labelled "horns."* Every brass instruction
in this pair is written as an **event with a start and a stop inside one bar**. Nothing is ever asked to
sustain, swell, hold, or bloom. The exclude fields blacklist the pad by name in all four.

⭐ **THE HUM TEST, satisfied structurally:** in all four songs the **brass stab figure is stated by the
instruments alone before any vocal enters, and it is the last musical thing heard** (except V4, where it
is deliberately the *second* to last — see V4's closing move). The tune of this record is not the voice.

## Arrangement — which instrument enters where, **one at a time**

1. **Bar 1, beat 1** — **stapler**, four dry hits, dead-centre. It is the count-in and it is an instrument.
2. **Bar 1** — **programmed kick and square sub**, hard, dead on the grid.
3. **Bar 3** — **muted guitar**, sixteenth chop, palm-damped, three notes not a chord, **left only**.
4. **Bar 5** — **brass stab figure**, dead-centre: three stabs answering an empty bar. Stated twice.
5. **Bar 7** — **handclaps**, dry, dead-centre, on the backbeat only.
6. **0:17** — **lead vocal**, close, **dead-centre**.
7. **0:51** — **stacked harmony**, girl-group thirds, entering only on the hook.
8. ⭐ **1:09** — **the tower's hum leaves and the glassy FM bell enters, right side only.** One thing out,
   one thing in, on the same downbeat. *(This is the trade the whole song is built on.)*
9. **2:00** — **tambourine**, right, sixteenths, for the Turn only, then gone.
10. **2:43** — **the room**: five unpolished voices, both sides, no centre, for its own section.

## The vacated centre — stated, removed, still pointed at

**STATE (RULING B1):** the lead vocal is dead-centre for the Intro, Verse 1 **and** Chorus 1 — three
statements, 0:00 to 1:09, so the ear has fully learned the middle. ⛔ No hole in the first thirty seconds.
**REMOVE:** on the downbeat of **Verse 2 (1:09)** she moves to the sides and stays there.
⭐ **THE NAMED EVENT THAT CASHES IT OUT:** somebody pulls the plug on **the tower under her desk**, whose
fan has hummed dead-centre in this room for as long as she has worked here. The hum stops on the same downbeat, in a way a
listener can hear and name without knowing anything about stereo. **She names it once, plainly, in the
lyric, in the same voice she used for the joke, and the band does not slow down.**
**KEEP POINTING:** the brass stabs stay dead-centre for the whole record — the horns are the only thing
left in the hole, and they land in it every two bars.

**Asymmetry of the two sides — one line:** **content and timing** — the left carries the dry muted-guitar
chop and the claps exactly on the grid, the right carries a glassy bell a fifth above the brass arriving
a sixteenth late, and **nothing ever plays on both sides at once.**

**LEAVE THE HOLE (the arrangement gap):** from Chorus 2 onward the **third** stab of the three-stab brass
figure never arrives; the sub walks through the space where it was.

## Chorus block

place. **Byte-identical on all three returns.** No numeral, no name, no thesis. Words fixed at step 08 and

2. **Bar 2** — **square sub**, low and dry, walking.

8. **2:05** — **a shaker**, left, for the Turn only.
9. **2:47** — **the room**, both sides, unequal, five voices.

## The vacated centre — stated, removed, still pointed at

**STATE:** the chirp is dead-centre and so is she, from 0:00 to 1:39 — Intro, Verse 1, Chorus 1, Verse 2.
The chirp recurs **five times** in that stretch, so the middle is not merely occupied, it is *learned*.
**REMOVE:** on the downbeat of **The Wedge (1:39)**.
⭐ **THE NAMED EVENT:** **the card reader's chirp** — the building's only opinion about who is present —
stops, because Ruthie has wedged the door with a box. Named once, plainly, and the band does not slow down.
**KEEP POINTING:** the glassy bell on the right plays the chirp's exact pitch a semitone flat for the rest
of the record, from the wrong place, so the ear keeps turning toward a middle that has nothing in it but
brass.

**Asymmetry of the two sides — one line:** **bandwidth** — the left is full-range and keeps every breath
and consonant, the right is the same take band-limited to a narrow midrange with the breaths gated out,
so one side sounds like a person and the other sounds like a building's record of one.

**LEAVE THE HOLE:** the handclap on the last backbeat before every chorus does not arrive.

## Chorus block

# GUIDE · V4 — **WHO PUT THE MUSIC ON**

**Appetite verb: RAISE.** She makes somebody turn it up and pushes the tables back.
**Key:** F Dorian. **Feel:** grinning and physical from bar one; savage by the last chorus, same person.

## Form map

|---|---|---|---|

| Chorus 3 | 12 | 2:39–3:05 | everybody |

## Arrangement — which instrument enters where, **one at a time**

1. **Bar 1** — **stapler**, four hits, dead-centre. Fen's count-in, and the record's tempo source.
2. **Bar 1** — **square sub and kick** together, hard, on the grid.
3. **Bar 3** — **brass stab figure**, dead-centre, three stabs, stated twice before any voice.
4. **Bar 5** — **chair leg on tile**, one squealed note, **left**, used as a pitched percussion hit.
5. **0:13** — **lead vocal**, close, **dead-centre**.
6. ⭐ **0:47** — **the speaker is lifted off the middle of the floor and put on the window ledge.** The
   lead moves to the sides on the same downbeat; **handclaps** enter dead-centre to sit in the empty
   middle beside the brass.
7. **1:04** — **glassy bell**, right, doubling the chair-leg note two octaves up.
8. **1:38** — **stacked thirds**, both sides, unequal.
9. **1:56** — **tambourine**, left, Turn only.
10. **3:05** — ⭐ **everything electric stops mid-bar. Five unaccompanied voices and a stapler finish the
    bar.** The brass stab is the second-to-last thing heard; the last is a person.

## The vacated centre — stated, removed, still pointed at

**STATE:** dead-centre for Intro and Verse 1 — 0:00 to 0:47, which is 47 seconds, comfortably past the
thirty-second floor, and the stab figure has cycled eleven times by then.
**REMOVE:** on the downbeat of **Chorus 1 (0:47)** — the earliest vacate of the four, and it is the point
of this variation: the music itself physically moves off the middle of the room.
⭐ **THE NAMED EVENT:** **the small speaker on the floor** is picked up and carried to the window ledge by
the person the song is in praise of. Everything that was coming from the middle now comes from one side.
Named once, plainly, at the end of Verse 1, in the same voice as the joke.
**KEEP POINTING:** the handclaps and the brass stab move into the empty middle at exactly that moment, so
the hole is not silent — it is **occupied by the wrong things**, which is worse and better.

**Asymmetry of the two sides — one line:** **density** — the left carries whole words, the right carries
only the clipped consonants of the same take, and **the right drops to silence for the last bar of every
phrase** while the left keeps going.

**LEAVE THE HOLE:** the sub drops out for the last two beats of every chorus; the room's feet cover it.

## Chorus block

Eight lines. Hook in lines one, three, five and seven — **four syllables**, a demand for volume with no subject and
no object, short enough to shout while lifting a table. **Byte-identical on all three returns.** Words
fixed at step 08.

## Source-2 imports, as production behaviour

**"transmogrifies those voices into what are best described as sonic events"** → the right side for the
entire back half: her own consonants, gated to sixteenths, no vowels, no meaning, pure rhythm.
**"glassy textures"** → the right-side bell doubling the chair leg's squeal two octaves up.
