# PAIR 03 — STEP 09 · ARTIST REFINEMENT
## `daily-music-2026-08-22-sunna-offbeat` · SLOT P03 · the three gates applied to the Step 08 drafts

**Method.** Every line of all four Step 08 drafts was read aloud, flat, with no music. Three gates, all
hard, all from the ICB:

- ⭐ **THE FLAT TEST (words).** Say it out loud, flatly, to a person's face. If it survives it is
  **simple**; if it dies it was merely **short**. Density hides a weak idea; six plain words expose it.
- ⭐ **THE HUM TEST (music).** The song must be hummable with **no words at all**. The instrumental hook
  must exist **before the vocal enters and after it leaves.** ⛔ A pad is not a riff.
- ⭐ **THE REMARK TEST (attention).** Would a listener say *"that was clever"*? **If yes, it is a toll —
  bury it or cut it.**

Plus the two slot-binding audits: ⛔ **NO WOLF / no dark line, in any variation**, and ⭐ **APPETITE IS A
VERB — she must want something and take it.**

**Twenty-four changes were made. Every one is listed below with its reason.** Nothing was changed for
neatness. ⚠️ A **second, separate pass** follows in Step 10 — a measurement pass that shortens verse
lines to bring the lyrics field inside the render cap. Those cuts are logged in Step 10's MAJOR
DEVIATIONS, not here, because they were driven by a counter rather than by an ear — and the rule that
governed them is that **no vowel-chain word may be cut**: where a trim would have taken one, a
different clause went instead. ⛔ The refrains were not touched: they are byte-identical across all three appearances in all
four variations, which is **payment (a)** on the return debt and needs no defence.

## ⭐ THE RETURN DEBT, RE-STATED AT THE POINT WHERE IT IS PAID

> **THE DEBT.** This slot's verse architecture is *long lines, one breath each, **no end-rhyme***.
> **Removal is a debt** (`lofn-music` L21): rhyme may be stripped only by naming what returns in its place.

**PAYMENT (a) — the byte-identical refrain.** Eight lines, three appearances, identical to the
character. The hook line occurs four times inside each refrain, so the room hears it twelve times across
the song and twice more in the outro. **Verified byte-for-byte in Step 10, in code.**

**PAYMENT (b) — the vowel-chain.** One vowel colour per variation, carried through the interior stresses
of every long line and landing on the **final stressed syllable** of most of them, with the **codas
deliberately varied so no two line-ends rhyme.** Nine of this pass's twenty-three changes exist purely
to strengthen (b), because a chain that is only present in half the lines is not a chain and does not
pay the debt.

# V1 — WHOSE COAT · melodica · she EATS · round "o"

### 1. ⛔ FLAT TEST FAIL — a metaphor, cut
- **Was:** *"The hour is a coat and I have got my arms in both of the sleeves."*
- **Now:** *"They can have the sky. I have got the warm room and both of the pockets."*
- **Why:** the old line is the only metaphor in the song and it dies the moment you say it flatly to a
  person. Worse, it *interprets* the situation instead of continuing it — ICB rule 1: **depth lives in
  the situation, never in the sentence.** The replacement is a plain preference stated out loud, which
  is the same idea with the decoding removed, and it puts the launderette **above** the meteor shower on
  purpose — which is the whole slot.

### 2. ⛔ VOICE FAIL — she apologised for her own good line
- **Was:** *"I am going to stand in front of it with my arms open like a fool."*
- **Now:** *"I am going to stand in front of it with my arms open, and I mean it."*
- **Why:** *"like a fool"* is the singer laughing at the end of her own line. ICB §3: **no laugh at the
  end of her own good line. She lands it and moves on.** And Sunna **has never been embarrassed in her
  life and regards this as a practice** — a self-deprecating tag is the one thing she cannot say.

### 3. VOWEL-CHAIN — line-end moved onto the chain
- **Was:** *"Somebody walks past outside and does not look in, and that is good."*
- **Now:** *"Somebody goes past the window and does not look in. That is no loss."*
- **Why:** *good* is off-chain and the sentence had two soft verbs. *Window* and *loss* both land the
  round "o", and the full stop makes it one breath and two flat statements instead of a clause pile.

### 4. REPETITION — the bridge was quoting the refrain
- **Was:** *"Warm at the sleeve. Warm across the back. Warm all the way down."*
- **Now:** *"The bench is warm from the machine and I have got my back on it."*
- **Why:** the refrain already owns *"Warm at the sleeve and warm across the shoulder."* Echoing it in
  the bridge spends the return **without adding a return** — it makes the refrain feel cheaper when it
  arrives. Replaced with a new praised object (the bench) and a body in contact with it.

### 5. COLLISION with V4 — *"the long way"* was being used twice in this pair
- **Was (V3 of the song):** *"And then I am going home the long way, in somebody else's coat."*
- **Now:** *"And then I am walking home in it, and the walk is the best part."*
- **Was (outro):** *"Not tonight, though. Tonight it is mine and it goes the long way home."*
- **Now:** *"Not tonight, though. Tonight it is mine and it is done up to the throat."*
- **Why:** *the long way* is **V4's hook and V4's appetite verb.** Two variations cannot share the
  phrase that identifies one of them. ⛔ **Do not write one song four times.** The replacements give V1
  its own closing image — a coat fastened all the way up — which is possession, V1's actual subject.

**V1 audits:** no wolf ✅ · appetite verb (**she eats**) intact and it is still the last event of Verse 1
✅ · absence named once, in Verse 2 ✅ · thesis not in the last verse ✅ · hum test: the bass figure opens
the record alone and closes it alone with the hole in it ✅.

# V2 — THE MACHINE THAT TAKES POUNDS · tenor sax · she SPENDS · "ow"

### 6. ⛔ REMARK TEST FAIL — the run's own source structure winking at the listener
- **Was:** *"The radiant is empty and so is machine three."*
- **Now:** *"Nothing in it. Nothing to do. Round it goes."*
- **Why:** this is the single worst line in the drafts. It smuggles **SOURCE 3** — *the radiant is
  empty* — into the lyric as a nod to a listener who has read our own brief. Nobody outside this repo
  can decode it, and anybody inside it says *"that was clever."* ⭐ **THE DEEP REFERENCE STAYS
  SUBVERSIVE: never explained, never winked at.** It was also sitting in the **chant**, the most
  room-ownable eight lines in the song, where a line the room cannot own is a dead bar. The replacement
  is three short flat statements the room can shout on the first hearing.

### 7. BREATH — the fact line was two breaths
- **Was:** *"The radio on the shelf says somebody counted one thousand seven hundred and six."*
- **Now:** *"The radio says somebody counted one thousand seven hundred and six."*
- **Why:** seventy-nine characters is not one breath at this tempo, and the shelf is already praised in
  Verse 1 — the detail was doing its work twice. The number is unchanged, still spelled in words, still
  on one line, and still **responded to** on the next: *good for them.*

### 8. FLATTER — a clause trimmed
- **Was:** *"I can feel it in the chair, in my back, in my teeth, and I paid for that."*
- **Now:** *"I can feel it in the chair, in my back, in my teeth, and I paid for it."*
- **Why:** *"paid for that"* points backwards at the sentence; *"paid for it"* points at the heat. Same
  length, one less instruction to the listener.

### 9. VOWEL-CHAIN — chant line moved onto "ow"
- **Was:** *"That is money on heat and I will spend it again."*
- **Now:** *"That is money on heat, and I will do it again in an hour."*
- **Why:** *again* is off-chain; *hour* lands it. It also makes the boast **future-tense and specific**,
  which is appetite rather than commentary.

### 10. VOWEL-CHAIN + closing move — the outro's last claim
- **Was:** *"Best pound I have spent all year and I would spend it now."*
- **Now:** *"Best pound I have spent all year, and I would put another one down."*
- **Why:** *now* is on-chain but the sentence repeated *spend* from two lines earlier. *Put another one
  down* is the physical act instead of the accounting word, and it ends the record on the **verb**.

**V2 audits:** no wolf ✅ · appetite verb (**she spends** — a pound into an empty machine) executes on
stage in Verse 2, not reported ✅ · absence named once ✅ · reveal engine in Verse 2, ⛔ not the last verse
✅ · the chant is now entirely chantable on first hearing ✅.

# V3 — THE OTHER PERSON IN HERE · trombone · she PULLS A STRANGER UP · "ee-ih"

### 11. ⛔ RULING X VIOLATION — the absence was named twice
- **Was (outro):** *"The bass keeps going where the trombone used to be." / "And it leaves the top of it
  open, and I hear it, and I like it."*
- **Now:** *"Nobody asked me for a single thing all night in here." / "And I gave one away anyway, and it
  did not cost me a thing."*
- **Why:** ⛔ **the absence is named in the lyric, in words, ONCE.** A second naming turns the song into
  a song **about its own device** — and worse, it makes the listener check the arrangement instead of
  feeling it. The replacement lines close on the **actual subject** (nothing was required of either of
  them and she gave something anyway), which is the angle's thesis and belongs in the outro rather than
  a technical note about the bass.

### 12. ⛔ SAME RULING, UPSTREAM — the instrument was named a third time, in Verse 1
- **Was:** *"The trombone has been going since I came in and it is the best thing here." / "It is doing
  the tune and the bass is underneath it, keeping in."*
- **Now:** *"The tune that has been going since I came in is the best thing in here." / "It sits up over
  the bass, and the bass keeps underneath it, easy."*
- **Why:** the praise stays — ⭐ **the list is said DURING, while the thing is still going**, which is the
  seed, and praising the instrument while it is present is exactly right. But the **proper noun** now
  occurs exactly **once in the whole lyric**, at the moment it leaves. That makes the naming land as an
  event rather than a running commentary, and it makes the rule countable by a stranger with `grep`.

### 13. BREATH — a three-clause line
- **Was:** *"He put his boots on a chair, and then he thought about it, and took them off."*
- **Now:** *"He put his boots on a chair, thought about it, and took them off."*
- **Why:** seventy-seven characters and three *ands*. The trimmed version is one breath and the comic
  timing is better: the pause is now in the punctuation, not in a conjunction.

### 14. VOWEL-CHAIN — "ee-ih" restored at the line end
- **Was:** *"Somebody's, not mine. Somebody who is not even in the room."*
- **Now:** *"Somebody's, not mine. Somebody who is not even in the building."*
- **Why:** *room* is off-chain (and *room* is a V1 word). *Building* lands the chain and is the truer
  fact — they are not in the launderette at all.

### 15. ⭐ APPETITE — the closing line was passive, and this pair cannot afford passive
- **Was:** *"Then it is me and nine machines and one of them still going."*
- **Now:** *"Then it is me and nine machines and the warm seat he was in."*
- **Why:** the old line leaves her sitting there being described. The new one has her **noticing a warm
  seat and taking it** — a second small verb after the big one, and it keeps the last image of the verse
  physical and warm instead of ambient. ⛔ *Steady + warm + cohesive is one bad decision from wallpaper.*

### 16. BREATH
- **Was:** *"His machine gets to the spin and the floor picks it up and my chair shifts."*
- **Now:** *"His machine hits the spin and the floor picks it up and my chair shifts."*

**V3 audits:** no wolf ✅ · appetite verb (**she pulls a stranger up**) happens on stage in the bridge,
with the release built into the invitation ✅ · *trombone* now appears exactly once ✅ · thesis in Verse 2,
⛔ not the last verse ✅.

# V4 — WALKING BACK · flute · she TAKES THE LONG WAY · "eye"

### 17–22. BREATH — six verse lines were past one breath
This variation's draft was the longest and the architecture is **one breath per line**. Six lines were
over. All six were cut at the clause that was carrying no object:

| was | now |
|---|---|
| *"It smells like the best thing that has ever happened out here in this life."* | *"It smells like the best thing that has happened out here all year."* |
| *"The bins are out in a line and one of them has gone over on its side."* | *"The bins are out in a line and one has gone over on its side."* |
| *"The streetlight by the postbox has been out since some time in the spring."* | *"The streetlight by the postbox has been out since the spring."* |
| *"The bass goes round and leaves the top of it empty every single time."* | *"The bass goes round and leaves the top of it empty every time."* |
| *"The hill road goes up past the allotments and it adds a mile to it."* | *"The hill road goes up past the allotments and it adds a mile."* |
| *"The flute had the tune the whole way down the road, and now it has stopped."* | *"The flute had the tune all the way down the road, and now it has stopped."* |

⭐ Note that **the chain words survived every cut** — *line, side, mile, time* are all load-bearing "eye"
sounds and none of them was in the removed clause. Where a trim would have taken a chain word, a
different clause was cut instead.

### 23. ⛔ NO-WOLF AUDIT — the one line in the pair that let the dark in through the back door
- **Was:** *"Nobody in the world knows exactly where I am standing right now." / "That is not a sad
  thing. That is the nicest thing in it."*
- **Now:** *"Nobody in the world knows exactly where I am standing right now." / "Which is mine. That is
  mine, and nobody can have it."*
- **Why:** ⛔ **no dark line, on any schedule, in any variation.** The old second line *denies* sadness,
  and a denial is an admission that the writer was worried — it puts the word **sad** into a song whose
  binding instruction is that there is no wolf in it. ⭐ **A gate that says "this is not the sad thing"
  has already made it the sad thing.** The replacement never raises the possibility: solitude is stated
  once and immediately **claimed as property**, which is the same move as V1's coat and V2's pound. It
  also converts the pair's most exposed moment from a defence into an **appetite**.

### 24. VOWEL-CHAIN — the last verse's opening
- **Was:** *"Nothing for a long while. The sky just sits there being big."*
- **Now:** *"Nothing for a long while. The sky sits there being quiet and high."*
- **Why:** *big* is off-chain and flat in the wrong way; *quiet* and *high* are both "eye" and the line
  now has the vowel twice. ⛔ Checked against the house lexicon: this is not *"more sub and more sky"*
  and does not go near it.

**V4 audits:** no wolf ✅ (and the one place it was leaking is closed) · appetite verb (**she takes the
long way**) executes in Verse 2 and is paid for in Verse 3 ✅ · absence named once ✅ · the meteor in the
last verse is an **event**, not the thesis — the thesis landed in Verse 2, ⛔ never the forbidden section
✅.

## THE HUM TEST, ANSWERED ONCE FOR ALL FOUR (no change required, verified)

The hummable hook is **the bass figure**, not the vocal line. It plays **alone for two cycles before any
other instrument exists**, and it plays **alone at the end after the voice has gone**, with the answer
slot empty. ⛔ **A pad is not a riff** — the lazily-played pads never carry the tune in any variation,
and the departing instrument never had the figure, only the **answer** to it. That is why the record is
still hummable after the instrument leaves, and why what is missing is audible as a **removal** rather
than as a thinner mix.

⛔ **One thing was deliberately NOT changed:** when the named instrument goes, the bass **does not gain a
note to compensate.** Every instinct in a refinement pass says fill it. Filling it deletes the song.

## WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY LEFT ALONE

1. **The refrains.** Untouched, byte-identical, three times each. ⛔ **A byte-identical chorus needs no
   defence and no note.**
2. **The plainness of the reveal.** *"Nothing is wrong. Nothing has gone wrong."* stays flat and
   unmarked. There is no band drop-out, no hush, no reverence anywhere near it. ⭐ **She'll say the line.
   She won't sell it.**
3. **The number.** *One thousand seven hundred and six*, once per song, in words, on one line, with the
   response on the next line in all four — and four different responses. It is never admired.
4. **The long unrhymed line.** No rhyme was quietly reintroduced anywhere to make a verse feel finished.
   The debt is paid by the refrain and the chain, or it is not paid.
