## 3. THE ABSENCE — PERFORMER LEVEL, AND HOW IT IS BUILT

**What goes:** the second voice. **Not** a hush, **not** a drop-out, **not** an instrument.

⭐ **THE MECHANISM THIS PAIR OWNS (and no other slot has call-and-response at all):**
> **The answer is always the tail of her own line, echoed.** She sings a line; the second voice hands
> back its last four syllables. So the listener is *taught the answer one beat before they need it*,
> every single time, for two full rounds.

Three consequences, and they are the reason this architecture was chosen over any other:
1. ⭐ **The room can fill the hole because the room already has the words** — they were in her mouth a
   beat ago. The reveal engine is not a metaphor; it is a mechanic.
2. ⭐ **The answer was never hers and was never the second voice's either** — it is a repeat of what was
   just said in the room. That is the AWE, and it arrives without being explained.
3. **C1 is satisfied structurally:** the hole is **in tempo** and its size is exactly the number of
   syllables just heard. **The ear counts it because it was just given the count.**

### B1 · STATE IT → REMOVE IT → KEEP POINTING
- **STATE:** two full rounds — verse and chorus, then verse and chorus again — with the answer present
  and doubled by a small dry group, so it reads as room-sized before it is taken.
- **REMOVE:** it shrinks first (see §4), then it stops on a named bar.
- **KEEP POINTING:** the skank upstroke and the break's snare play straight through the empty bar; a
  chiming synth figure lands on the downbeat *after* it, so the gap is fenced on both sides.
  ⛔ **The dub drop-outs never land on an answer bar.** A drop-out where the hole is would turn a
  subtraction into an effect. The band plays through the hole, every time, without exception.

### ⛔ AND NOTHING FILLS IT ON THE RECORD
No crowd, no double, no ad-lib, no instrument, no delay throw. **On the record the bars are empty. In the
song's story the car park fills them. In the listener's car, the listener fills them.** That is the entire
point, and it is why the answer had to be four syllables a normal throat can shout while out of breath.

*(Recorded for QA's HOLE-SWAP test against P02: **P02's gap is instrumental and nobody fills it. This
gap is vocal, pre-taught, and the listener fills it involuntarily.** Drop this block into a song with no
second voice and there is nothing to remove.)*

*(Recorded for the GENRE-TRANSPLANT test: the coordinator predicted this slot exposed, because a
skank-over-break absence would be made of its bed. **It is not.** The absence is a performer leaving a
call-and-response — that survives being re-recorded as gospel, as country, as a marching band. The
*bed* is a costume this song could change; the *architecture* is not.)*

## 4. ⭐ THE EXACT SHRINK SCHEDULE

The response is the tail of the call and it **runs out earlier every round**.

| round | response length | what the listener hears |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **four syllables** | the full tail, second voice + small dry group |
| **2** | **four syllables** | identical treatment — *this is the second cycle A2 requires* |
| **3** | **three syllables** | the same voice, one word short, no comment made about it |
| **4** | **two syllables** | a stub; still in tempo, still on the same beat |
| **5 onward** | **none** | bar-accurate silence, band straight through, forever |

**Rules that hold in all four variations:**
- The stop is **irreversible**. The second voice never returns — not in the outro, not as a tag.
- The stop bar is **different in each variation** and is written into that variation's guide.
- ⛔ **Nothing before 0:30.** The earliest shrink in the pair begins at bar 49 (1:38); the earliest full
  stop is bar 65 (2:10).
- ⛔ **The hole is inconvenient by construction:** it takes the chorus's answering half — the single most
  singable thing in the song — at the exact moment the song is biggest.

## 5. THE HOOK — ITS FUNCTION *(words deliberately not written here; each variation writes its own in Step 08)*

- **4–8 syllables in the call; exactly four in the answer**, and the answer is the call's own tail.
- **Room-ownable:** shoutable at volume, out of breath, standing up, by somebody who has heard it twice.
  No interval a normal throat cannot reach. No word that needs to be got right.
- **It is the hook whose response goes missing.** The hole is not in a bridge or a decorative bar — it is
  in the answering half of the chorus, which is the part the room came for.
- **Four different hooks, four different residues.** Each shrinks through its own three stages to nothing,
  and the two-syllable residue of each is a different kind of word: a place, a name, a possessive, a verb.
- ⛔ The hook never comments on the device. Nothing in any lyric says *listen to what is missing*.

## 7. SOURCE 2 → PRODUCTION BEHAVIOUR *(≥2 verbatim, ⛔ never in a lyric)*

1. **"detuned bliss"** → **the happiness is out of true on purpose and never corrected.** The guitar's
   top string sits a hair flat for the whole record; the chiming synth is a few cents sharp against the
   bass. Nothing tunes toward the middle at any point, including under the final chorus. This is the
   run's emotional thesis made mechanical: *delight with a deadline it is not pretending isn't there.*
2. **"a chiming synth riff"** → **the hook is an instrument, not the voice.** A three-note chiming figure
   is the song's tune-carrier. ⭐ It plays **alone before the first vocal** and **alone after the last**,
   which is the only way THE HUM TEST can be passed by a song whose vocal hook is half missing. It is
   also the fence on the right-hand side of every empty answer bar.

⛔ Neither phrase, nor any other Bandcamp sentence, appears in any lyric in this pair.

## 8. THE FACET SET *(the working list — every one of these is a decision on the record)*

1. **BED** — ska-punk skank over a dusty boom-bap break, sun-bleached, dub tape-echo at line ends.
   **118 BPM exactly**, no ramp, no half-time, no drop.
2. **STRUT AS NAMED ENTRIES (Ruling X)** — chiming synth alone (bar 1) → break (bar 3) → offbeat guitar
   (bar 5) → walking bass (bar 7) → second voice (verse 1) → melodica/trumpet stab (chorus 1) → handclaps
   (verse 2). **Seven timbres, one at a time, over one unwavering groove.** ⛔ No dynamic swell does a
   timbre's job.
3. **THE ABSENCE, NAMED IN THE LYRIC, ONCE** — one plain line per song stating that nobody is answering.
   Said in the same voice as the joke; the band does not slow down; nothing marks it.
4. **THE BASS IS THE SECOND LEAD** — melodic, front of mix, moving between chords, **on** the grid while
   the drums sit 8–14 ms late. If you can hum the bass, we have swagger.
5. **GUITAR AS PERCUSSION** — a three-note upstroke figure, clean, never a chord wall, never driven.
   ⛔ No distortion anywhere: this bed's whole lineage is a rhythm section, and drive would be the sound
   of the groove failing.
6. **HORNS PUNCTUATE** — one short trumpet stab and a melodica line that answer and are out inside the
   bar. Unreverbed. ⛔ **They never play in an answer bar** — the hole is not decorated.
7. **STACKED HARMONY AS AN INSTRUMENT** — the second voice is doubled by a small dry group in rounds 1–2.
   ⭐ **One moment where the two split and the room hears the gap open:** the first three-syllable answer.
8. **THE SUNG FACT** — *sixty-six degrees*, in words, once per song, **responded to and never repeated**.
   ⛔ No other number is sung in this pair.
9. **THE VOCAL** — full-chest alto, close on a dynamic mic, breaths in, no de-esser, no pitch correction,
   doubles misaligned 15–35 ms and ±6 cents. She sings it. The talky register is a colour and appears
   at most twice per song.
10. **THE MASTER** — −14 LUFS with ≥10 dB crest, contemporary and dry. ⛔ No period varnish: this is a
    1996-lineage body inside a present-day record, not a tribute to one.
11. **LEAVE THE HOLE, AND ONLY ONE** — the answer bars are the song's single arrangement gap. Every
    other bar is played.
12. **NO GEAR-CHANGE** — one bed, one tempo, one key, start to finish, in all four variations.
