## 0. THE SLOT, RESTATED IN MY OWN WORDS BEFORE ANY FACET EXISTS

A coach on the hard shoulder at eleven at night in the rain. A rail line was cancelled by people in a
different building, so a coach was sent instead. Everyone on board is polite. **The politeness is the
indictment.** The song is a list of specific, unembarrassed praise said **out loud, to faces, while the
thing is still happening** — and the anger is entirely in the words, over music that is physically
incapable of sounding cross.

⭐ **THE ONE RULE THIS SLOT LIVES OR DIES BY:** the skank cannot sound angry. So if the record sounds
cross, I have failed, no matter how good the words are. Anger goes in the **nouns**, never in the
**decibels**.

## 1. FACET — WHAT SPECIFICALLY GETS PRAISED (the inventory, before any line exists)

The seed says: *which bar, which snare, whose hands, what the light did.* Here that becomes **craft
under bad conditions, named to the face of the person doing it.** The praise pool, assembled first so
the four variations can divide it and not overlap:

**The driver's body and his craft**
- reversing a coach onto a wet verge between two cones with his hand on the luggage rack and his head
  right round — no camera, no shunt, no shout
- kicking the grit off the chrome step with the side of his boot before anyone puts a foot on it
- turning the heater up without being asked
- the word he uses for the passengers, and that he means it
- saying the true time instead of the comfortable time, and not dressing it

**The objects that work**
- a biro (works)
- a paper timetable typed by hand behind glass (has never been wrong)
- a stop pole that predates the line (has never lied)
- the fault light on the departure board (the only honest lamp on the platform)
- a flask lid used as a cup, going down the aisle

**The objects that do not work, and are newer than everything else**
- a handheld ticket machine, new in the spring, that cannot read a paper ticket
- a live-departure board, brightest object on the road, reporting a feed **two hours old**
- a journey-planner app that sent a coachload of adults to a stop that does not exist

**The people, praised as functions doing work**
- a man reading the board aloud for a woman whose eyes have gone
- a woman going down the aisle with a biro writing the claim word on the back of every ticket
- a man who gives his seat up and stands the whole way
- a woman who starts the singing, gets the low harmony wrong, and does not stop

⛔ **Not praised, ever:** the situation, the weather, resilience, or anything that would let the decision
off. **The praise is for named work, never for endurance.** Endurance-praise is how a song about a
system becomes a song that thanks people for putting up with it.

## 2. FACET — THE ROAD'S PHYSICAL INVENTORY (so no variation invents a second world)

Hard shoulder, eleven at night, August rain coming flat. One coach, hazards on, ticking. A recovery van
somewhere ahead with an amber beacon that goes round and paints every face the same colour. Two cones. A
soft, steep verge. Wet wool, diesel, and a heater running too hot. Windows fogged from the inside and
drawn on. Wipers that go double-speed at the top of the arc. A pneumatic door that sighs. A driver's
radio turned up so the coach can hear it. Across the road, a lit platform with a departure board and a
glass case with a paper sheet in it. In her hand: **a paper ticket, gone soft along the fold.**

**Invented, and it stays invented:** the line, the towns, the operator, every person. There is no real
route, no real official, no real company and no real dispute in any of the four variations, and **no
child appears anywhere.** The subject is **a decision made elsewhere that other people stand outside
for.** The case is not the subject and is never reconstructed.

## 4. FACET — THE ABSENCE, AND ITS EXACT RHYTHMIC REALISATION

**KIND:** rhythmic. **THE OBJECT:** the downbeat.

**STATE (RULING B1 · A2).** The record opens with the entire band in **unison on a straight
four-on-the-floor downbeat** — kick, bass, guitar, organ and baritone sax all landing the one together.
This runs the **eight-bar intro and the whole of verse one: two full eight-bar cycles, thirty-one
seconds at 126 BPM.** It is the loudest, squarest, most ordinary thing on the record and it is meant to
be learned in the body inside four bars.

⛔ **This is why the intro is long.** At 126 BPM one bar is 1.9 seconds; two cycles is 30.5 seconds; the
ruling says **no hole in the first thirty seconds.** A four-bar statement would put the hole at 0:07 and
break it. The intro length is not an aesthetic choice, it is arithmetic.

**REMOVE.** At the downbeat of the **Lift** — the four-bar approach into chorus one, bar seventeen,
0:30.5 — the one is deleted and **never played again on the record.** Guitar goes to a clean chop on the
offbeat only. Organ bubbles the *and*. Drums move to rim on three with hats on the upstroke, and the
kick never lands on beat one again for the rest of the song.

⭐ **AND IT IS INCONVENIENT.** The floor is removed **exactly where the chorus needs it** — the room's
own part, the chantable thing, arrives standing on nothing. A hole taken in a solo or an interlude is
decoration. This one takes the song's most load-bearing beam, at the worst moment.

**KEEP POINTING.** The **Lift** returns before every chorus. Every time, the ear goes to the one, and
every time it is not there. That is the pointing device, and it is structural, not a sound effect.

**HOW THE BODY STILL FEELS IT (RULING C1).** The bass leans a fat, forward pickup note onto the
*four-and* — **louder, longer and earlier than the one ever was** — so the body braces for a downbeat
that never arrives and then finds it has already carried the weight. The knee still does it. **The one
is unplayed and completely present.**

⛔⭐ **MITIGATION, HELD DELIBERATELY (the P02 × P04 hole-swap risk):** **this gap is INSTRUMENTAL and
NOBODY FILLS IT.** No handclap, no crowd chant, no tambourine, no vocal, no percussion of any kind ever
lands on beat one after 0:30. **In V4, where an entire coach sings, the singalong lands on the
upstroke** — and the lyric says so in words. If a room ever fills this hole, the slot has failed its own
falsification test.

## 5. FACET — WHERE THE ANGER LIVES, GIVEN THAT THE MUSIC CANNOT CARRY IT

The skank is physically incompatible with sounding angry. So the indignation is built out of four
non-sonic materials:

1. **THE FLAT INVENTORY.** Naming what works and what does not, in order, with no adjective doing a
   noun's job. *A pen works. A machine does not.* No verdict is stated; the list is the verdict.
2. **THE POLITENESS, QUOTED EXACTLY.** *Mind how you go. Sorry, love. Sit down, mate.* Said sincerely by
   people being decent at eleven at night — and it is the indictment precisely because nobody raises
   their voice. **Nobody is shouting. That is the worst of it.**
3. **THE ASYMMETRY, STATED AS FACT.** Somebody is warm. Somebody is asleep. Here is the rain. No
   comparison is drawn; the two facts are put next to each other and left there.
4. **THE ABSENCE OF A DEMAND.** The song asks for nothing. It does not petition, threaten, or resolve
   politically. **It just says what happened, at dance tempo.**

⛔ **Banned outright in all four:** shouting, sneering, sarcasm aimed at a person, a raised voice, a
chorus that accuses a named individual, distortion of any kind, and any line that tells the listener how
to feel about the decision.

## 6. FACET — THE REVEAL ENGINE, IN FOUR FORMS

**Engine (mine alone): the newest thing in the room is the broken thing.**

It is never explained. It is only ever demonstrated by putting two objects next to each other and
letting the audience do the arithmetic: a biro beside a handheld terminal; a hand-typed paper sheet
beside a live board; a pole beside an app. **The old thing is never sentimentalised** — it is praised
for a measurable property (it works, it has not lied, it has never been wrong), never for being old.

⭐ That is the guard against nostalgia, which the seed bans: **the song has no opinion about the past.
It has an opinion about a thing that was bought last spring and does not work.**

---

## 7. FACET — VERSE ARCHITECTURE AS A PHYSICAL CONSTRAINT

Quatrains. **Every line ends on a stressed monosyllable.** Internal rhyme falls on the offbeat syllable.

Why this is the right shape for this bed and not a rule I was handed: at 126 BPM the guitar chop lands
on every *and*, so a line that ends on an **unstressed** syllable falls into the chop and disappears. A
stressed monosyllable at the line end lands on a beat the band is **not** playing — the words keep
hitting where the music has gone quiet. **The verse form and the absence are the same idea.**

Practical consequences, accepted now rather than discovered at step 09:
- no *-ing*, *-ed*, *-er*, *-y* or *-tion* line endings, ever; no *tonight*, *again*, *instead*, *away*
- the vocabulary skews Anglo-Saxon and short: *rack, grit, boot, verge, cone, fold, stub, coin, sheet*
- the internal rhyme has to carry the music, because the end words are too short to do it alone

## 8. FACET — THE HOOK'S FUNCTION (⛔ no words here; those are written at step 08)

**Length:** four to six syllables. **Range:** inside a fifth, reachable in full chest by a coachload of
out-of-breath adults at midnight.

**Function:** each variation's hook must be **a sentence that is simultaneously a courtesy and an
accusation, and it must belong to the room, not to her.** It has to be something a real person would
actually say on that hard shoulder — not a slogan about the situation. When the coach sings it back, the
courtesy is doing the accusing, and nobody has raised their voice.

⭐ **Test applied at step 09:** could a stranger on that coach have said it first? If it sounds like a
line written *about* the coach rather than *said on* it, it is out. And a hook naming the decision, the
operator or the system is out on sight — **the indictment must arrive dressed as manners.**

⛔ Four different hooks, one per variation. The chorus is byte-identical **within** a song and shares no
line **across** the four.

## 9. FACET — THE PARADE (SWAG, and why it is legible as events)

The groove never changes: 126 BPM, no ramp, no key change, no drop, no half-time. The drive is in
**steadiness**. What changes is **which timbre is in the room**, one at a time, each entering at a named
point so the change is a countable event rather than a swell:

baritone sax → the chop → the organ bubble → trombone → tambourine on the offbeat → the stacked thirds,
and the one moment they split and the gap opens → the instruments leaving one at a time until the organ
figure is alone.

⛔ **No dynamic swell doing a timbre's job.** ⛔ **No standing tension lane** — no riser that never
resolves, no filter that only opens. The parade order differs across the four variations; the groove
does not.

---

## 10. FACET — THE INSTRUMENTAL HOOK (THE HUM TEST)

A three-note offbeat figure that is **an instrument, not the voice**, stated in the eight-bar intro
**before the vocal exists** and left playing alone **after the vocal has gone.** Different figure per
variation — baritone sax and organ in octaves; organ alone answering itself; a trombone slide; the whole
horn line in unison. ⛔ A pad is not a riff. If the vocal is the only tune, this song has failed, and the
failure will be inaudible to every text gate I own.

---

## 11. FACET — THE SUNG FACT, AND ITS RESPONSE

**The fact: *two hours old*.** A live-departure feed reporting data already stale while being the newest
object present. Sung once per song, **in words, never digits**, and **responded to, never recited** —
somebody says it, and somebody answers, and the answer is what the line is for. Four different responses:
*it is not his fault* · *thanks for saying so* · *well, there we are* · *sing louder*.

⛔ No second number is sung anywhere in any variation — no times, no counts, no fares, no ordinals. The
verse form's short vocabulary makes numerals tempting as line-enders; they are banned on sight.

## 12. FACET — SOURCE 2, AS PRODUCTION BEHAVIOUR ONLY

- ***"a sonic assault"*** — **imported as a REFUSED target.** The exclude field of all four names it in
  those words. This is the correct use of a phrase from a thrash review inside a ska brief: the
  reviewer's compliment is our failure mode, and naming the failure mode is worth more than borrowing a
  texture.
- ***"gnarled"*** — the Hammond drawbar tone. Not clean, not churchy: a rasping, slightly broken
  registration with key click left in, so the offbeat bubble has grit on it. **The one place on the
  record where something is allowed to sound damaged — and it is not the guitar, because distortion is
  what a record reaches for when the groove is not carrying it.**

⛔ Neither phrase appears in any lyric.

## 13. FACET — WHAT THE FORBIDDEN SECTION COSTS ME

The thesis may not land in **the bridge**. So in all four the bridge is **pure inventory** — objects and
hands, no verdict, no turn, no summary. This is a real cost: the bridge is the natural place to put the
knife, and I am giving it up. What the bridge gets instead is the **most physical writing in the song**
and the parade's densest event. The thesis lands in a chorus line or a late verse, which means **the
room sings the indictment** rather than hearing it delivered — which is better anyway, and is the reason
the constraint is worth obeying rather than working around.

---

## 14. FACET — THE GENRE-TRANSPLANT EXPOSURE, DECLARED

`05` predicts P02 is one of two slots exposed on RULING C2, because a rhythmic absence is **made of** its
bed. I accept the diagnosis and do not pretend otherwise. What survives transplant is the **situation** —
a coach, a decision, a signature, a screen that lies — and the **inventory of praise**, which would work
at half speed in any idiom. What would not survive is the deleted downbeat, because there is no downbeat
to delete in a form that never stated one. **That is the honest cost of being assigned ska, it is
recorded here before QA finds it, and it is not repaired by hedging the bed.**
