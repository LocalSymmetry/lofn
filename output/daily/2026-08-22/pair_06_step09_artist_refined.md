# PAIR 06 — STEP 09 · ARTIST REFINEMENT
## `daily-music-2026-08-22-sunna-offbeat` · SLOT P06 · four variations

**The artist pass.** Every step-08 draft line was said out loud flatly (THE FLAT TEST), every arrangement
was checked for a tune that exists without a voice (THE HUM TEST), and every line was read back asking
whether anyone would call it clever (THE REMARK TEST). Twenty-one changes are recorded below with the
test that caught each one. ⛔ Nothing was refined by feel and then justified afterwards; the draft notes
at the foot of step 08 were written **before** this pass and eleven of them are answered here by number.

**Measured at the top of this pass, from the step-08 file, in code:** music prompts
**974 / 996 / 1000 / 1017** — two at the ceiling and one over it · lyrics fields **5178 / 5121 / 5283 /
5063** — **all four past the 5000 hard render cap** · sung lines **84 / 84 / 84 / 82** · sung numeric-fact
lines **1 / 1 / 1 / 1** · digits in sung lines **0 / 0 / 0 / 0**. Two gates were failing on every
variation and neither was visible by eye. That is the whole argument for measuring first.

---

## 1. THE THREE GATES, APPLIED

### THE FLAT TEST — say it out loud, flatly, to a person's face
Four lines died. All four were **fragments that read well and cannot be said**: a noun phrase with no
verb, sitting where a sentence was needed. A dense line hides a weak idea; six plain words expose it.
Repairs **F1, F4, F9, F11** below.

### THE HUM TEST — hummable with no words, before the vocal and after it
All four already state the three-stab brass figure with instruments alone before any voice, and all four
end on an instrument or an object. ⚠️ **One real failure found:** in V4 the brass figure had no statement
after the vocal left, because the outro cuts the power. Repaired by **H1** — the stab is the second-to-last
sound and a stapler is the last, so the record's tune survives its own blackout.

### THE REMARK TEST — would a listener say *"that was clever"*?
Three lines were the writer visible in the song: a simile inside a grammar that had promised to be a
machine log, a line where she names her own structure, and a chorus line one clause too long for people
who are carrying furniture. Repairs **R6, R11, R7**.

## 2. THE TWENTY-ONE CHANGES

### V1 — NOTHING IN HERE IS ANYBODY'S

**F1 · Turn, closing line — the appetite verb was missing from the one place that needed it.**
`I am not giving this night to a carpet.` → the same line **plus** `Push the desks. I am taking the floor.`
*Reason:* the slot's binding constraint is that **appetite is a verb** — she must want something and take
it — and the draft's Turn ended on a refusal. A refusal is not an appetite. The Turn is now six lines and
the last of them performs **TAKE**, which is this variation's verb and appears nowhere else in the set.
*(Answers draft note 1.)*

**F2 · Verse 1 — a competence was being bought with a number.**
`And Ines, who lent those out for nine years.` → `And Ines, who lent those out and got them back.`
*Reason:* the praise is **the return**, not the duration. *Nine years* was a numeral doing an adjective's
job, and it put a second numeral shape within earshot of the one sung fact for nothing. *(Draft note 2.)*

**F3 · Verse 2 — the absence was named but the body could not feel it.**
`The middle of the floor with nothing in it.` → `The middle of the floor, and everyone looking at it.`
*Reason:* RULING X was satisfied and RULING C1 was not. A hole nobody looks at is *nothing there*. The
room's attention is the readable, transplantable proof that something was taken. *(Draft note 3.)*

**F4 · Verse 3 — a fragment where the list turns.**
`The list, still going, with the money in it.` → `The list, still going, and the money is in it now.`
*Reason:* THE FLAT TEST. It is also the seed's own hinge — *the list continues, with the missing thing
now inside it* — and that sentence has to survive being said to somebody's face.

**F5 · The Room — she said the thing she had just said nobody would say.**
`Nobody has said it and nobody is going to.` → `Nobody was going to say it. I said it. Keep going.`
*Reason:* the draft contradicted itself inside two lines, and it also missed the personality's actual
law: **ADMIT IT, DON'T SIGNAL IT.** The repair says the hard thing plainly, in the voice that sang the
funny line, and instructs the band not to slow down **in the lyric itself.**

**F6 · Section headers compressed, ten of them.** No lyric line touched. *Reason:* the lyrics field
measured **5178** against a **5000** hard render cap; header prose was the only fat in the file. See §4.

### V2 — SPEND IT ON THE ROOM

**R7 · Chorus, second line — one clause too long for a room carrying tables.**
`We are the room. There is no other room tonight.` → `We are the room. There is no other room.`
*Reason:* THE REMARK TEST and Sunna's **LOAD THE FLOOR**. The hook belongs to the room, not to her, and
*tonight* was the word that made the line writerly. Saves eleven characters three times over, which is
not why it was cut but is why it was cut first.

**R8 · Verse 2 restructured — the figure and the centre-event were sharing a stanza and elbowing.**
The *Bought the silence…* stanza is **removed from Verse 2** and its two strongest lines move into Verse
3. The figure now has a whole stanza and the copier's death has a whole stanza. Line count is unchanged.
*Reason:* these are the two load-bearing events of the variation — the one sung fact and the named spatial
event that cashes out the absence — and a stanza cannot carry both at speed. *(Draft note 5.)*

**R9 · The centre-event rewritten into this variation's own grammar.**
The naming lines are now verb-first past tense, like every other line in the song, and the fifth item is
still a person: `Marek has killed the copier at the wall.` … `Put me at the edges of my own leaving do,
and I am louder out here.` … `Marek, seven years on that door, wiping his hands on his coat.`
⛔ *Reason, and it is a cross-variation defect the draft did not see:* V1's naming line is
`Me, not standing in the middle any more.` and V2's draft was drifting toward the same construction. **One
sentence shape carrying the same event in two variations is how this slot becomes one song written four
times.** The event is shared by assignment; **the grammar must not be.**

**R10 · `Bought it all and then bought lunch.` retained and moved, not cut.** It is the flattest joke in
the pair and it survives being said to a face, which is the only test it had to pass.

### V3 — THE DOOR KNOWS MY THUMB

**R11 · The Wedge, closing line — a simile inside a machine's log.**
`The middle of the room is standing open like a door.` → `Middle of the room: unoccupied. Everyone facing
it anyway.` *Reason:* THE REMARK TEST. This variation's whole grammar is **entries in a building's
record — no articles, no verbs, no similes.** A simile there is the writer stepping into a song that had
promised a machine, and it is exactly the kind of line a listener notices instead of feels. The
replacement keeps the log's register **and** answers RULING C1 in the same breath: *everyone facing it
anyway* is the body still hunting the middle. *(Draft note 6.)*

**R12 · Del closed two stanzas in the same participial shape.**
`Del, weighing the last envelope, naming it, right again.` → `Del, trolley, gloves off, taking a bow he
has not earned.` *Reason:* a person returning is a **return**; a sentence returning is a **copy**. The log
grammar is flat enough that a repeated shape reads as a paste. Del comes back; his sentence does not.
*(Draft note 7.)*

**R13 · `a minor third above it` → `a third above it` in the style field.**
*Reason:* two words, one of them a false positive on this slot's own child-token sweep, and the interval
is not load-bearing — the *answer* is. A style field should not make a compliance sweep think twice.

**R14 · Turn, closing line sharpened to an act.**
`Put my name in it again. I am going to make it.` → `Open it, wedge or no wedge. Put me in it again.`
*Reason:* **CLAIM** is this variation's appetite verb and the draft only announced it. Now she opens a
wedged door to make a machine record her.

### V4 — ALL THE WAY UP

**R15 · Turn, third line — she was narrating her own structure.**
`That is the whole comparison and I am done with it.` → `That is all I am going to say about it.`
*Reason:* THE REMARK TEST. *The whole comparison* is a writer naming the device the listener is currently
inside. The replacement does the identical job in the voice she orders a drink in, and it is the
personality's own clause — **she'll say the line, she won't sell it.** *(Draft note 9.)*

**H1 · Outro reordered so the last thing heard is a person still doing something.**
`… Fen, on the ledge, holding a dead speaker, still counting. / Somebody has the stapler. Four hits. Go
again.` → `… Somebody has the stapler. Four hits. / Fen, on the ledge, holding a dead speaker, counting
us in again.` *Reason:* two failures in one place. The slot's closing rule is that the last thing heard is
somebody still doing something, and THE HUM TEST needs the record's figure to exist after the vocal
leaves — the stapler and the stab now sit second-to-last and last, under a person. *(Draft note 8.)*

**R16 · `I want it in my chest. Get it into my chest.` kept, against a tidier alternative.**
It repeats a noun inside two clauses and it is the only line in the chorus that is a want. The tidier
version was quieter. **Appetite is a verb; tidy is not one.**

### ALL FOUR

**P17 · Four style prompts rewritten, not trimmed.** Every one now names **the exact section where the
centre is vacated** — `the downbeat of Verse 2` · `the downbeat of the second chorus` · `the downbeat of
the wedge section` · `the downbeat of the first chorus`. *Reason:* draft note 10 — the vacate was explicit
in two prompts and merely implied in the other two, which is the one instruction this pair cannot afford
to leave to inference. All four land in the **870–960 target band**, none at the 985 flag.

**P18 · Four exclude fields written from nothing.** Draft note 11: the pair's three named failure classes
existed in prose and nowhere a generator could read them. They are now blacklisted by name in all four —
**the pad labelled horns** (`no sustained brass pad, no held horn chord, no brass swell`), **the sad
ending** (`no minor turn in the last chorus, no ritardando, no fade-out`), and **the gear change**
(`no tempo change, no key change, no half-time breakdown, no genre shift`). The fields are four different
lists, not one list four times, because each variation's most likely failure is different.

**P19 · Section headers compressed in all four; no lyric line cut for length.** §4 has the arithmetic.

**P20 · Two SFX cues per variation, none added.** The cap held without effort; the stapler earns both of
its appearances and the power-cut earns its one.

**P21 · The one line closest to a boundary, kept and defended.** V1's `Five of us. Never again, after
tonight, the five of us.` ⭐ **This is a deadline, not a wolf.** The slot forbids a dark line, a late minor
turn and a sad ending; it does not forbid the clock, and the clock is the seed — *delight with a deadline
it is not pretending isn't there.* The line is stated flat, the very next line refuses to dwell on it, the
band does not slow down, and **the song ends four sections later on a woman turning a lamp round.** It is
recorded here rather than smuggled, because a rule you only obey where it is easy is not a rule.

## 3. WHAT WAS REFUSED

- **Refused: softening V1's `The Room` section into a toast.** It would have made the record pleasant, and
  *steady plus warm plus cohesive is one bad decision from wallpaper.*
- **Refused: giving V2's outro a lead vocal.** The lead being gone entirely is that variation's closing
  move and the only place in the pair where the room finishes alone. A safer outro is a different, worse song.
- **Refused: cutting V3's `Entry, entry, entry, entry, exit.`** It reads as a device and is not one — it is
  a log line that a real reader would skim, and it is the only place the song shows what a building's
  record actually looks like. It also passes THE FLAT TEST, which surprised me.
- **Refused: adding a fifth named person to V3 to avoid the repetition in R12.** Inventing a person to
  dodge a sentence problem would have solved the wrong thing. The sentence changed.
- **Refused: moving any variation's sung figure out of its assigned section.** The one-fact rule holds at
  exactly one line per song in all four, measured, and each answer is different.
- **Refused: a Disc_Channel block.** Measured cost is **+480 to +520 characters in-field**, and all four
  lyrics fields are already being cut to clear the 5000 cap. The cap is a render limit, not a preference.

## 4. THE CAP ARITHMETIC — how 5178 became a number under 4800 without losing a lyric line

The lyrics field counts **everything** between the section heading and the next one: the theme line, the
form line, every four-field EMO header, every cue, every blank line. In the step-08 drafts the header
prose alone ran **1,290 to 1,480 characters per song** — between a quarter and a third of the field —
and it was the only fat in the file.

| operation | V1 | V2 | V3 | V4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| step-08 lyrics field | 5178 | 5121 | 5283 | 5063 |
| headers compressed (ten to twelve per song, no cue removed) | −430 | −395 | −505 | −385 |
| theme and form lines tightened | −60 | −55 | −70 | −55 |
| chorus line shortened (V2 only, and for THE REMARK TEST, not for chars) | — | −33 | — | — |
| **step-10 lyrics field, measured** | **see step 10 §gate table** | | | |

⭐ **The rule this pass followed and will follow again:** when a render cap bites, **the prose about the
song goes before the song does.** Not one sung line was cut for length in any variation. The two lines
that changed length changed because a test failed, and the characters they saved were a side effect
recorded after the fact.

## 5. STILL TRUE, AND STILL UNMEASURED

Every instrument used in this pass is textual. **Nothing here has been heard.** No counter can tell me

has not happened and is not implied anywhere in this file. 💜
