## 0. ROOM SELECTION — done BEFORE the room opened

The seed's risk profile is unusual and it drove the casting. This is a **delight** record built by a
house whose entire measured competence is **grief**, and its central device is **an absence** — the one
object that cannot be heard and therefore cannot be audited by any gate we own. So:

- **Panel A (CONCEPT)** is stacked with people who have made *catalogues* and people who have made
  *absences*, plus a skeptic whose published life's work is the argument that a hook a room can own is
  a symptom rather than an achievement.
- **Panel B (MEDIUM)** is deliberately built around a **fight**: the seat that says everything lands on
  the ONE, seated opposite the seat that deletes it. The seed does not get to assume it won that fight.
- **Panel C (CONTEXT)** carries the credit question, because T5 puts a dead musician in the run's
  ancestry and `DEDICATION_STANDARD` is binding.
- ⭐ **The wide-panel rule** (hers, 2026-08-18): beyond the home field this room seats **measurement**
  (Panel B skeptic), **economics of attention** (Panel C), and **classical philology / translation**
  (Panel A). No panel is all musicians.

## PANEL A — CONCEPT

**THE LIST-MAKER (after Sappho):** *Working from the surviving fragments.* Fragment 16 does not open with
what she loves. It opens with **what other people say is most beautiful** — horsemen, infantry, ships —
and then it says: *I say it is what you love.* The catalogue is a **rebuttal**. That matters here. A list
that is only a list is furniture. A list that is answering somebody is an argument with a pulse.

**THE UNABASHED (after Gay):** And it has to be *specific* or it dies on the page. Not "the band was
good." Which bar. Whose hands. What the light did on the wall behind the bass player. My working
commitment is that delight and grief are not opposites — they arrive in the same sentence, and the
sentence should not change its voice when it crosses over.

**THE STANDARDISATION AUDITOR (after Adorno):** *Working from the fetish-character essay.* Then let me
say the unpleasant thing while everyone is still warm. **You are building a machine for producing
sameness and calling the sameness "return."** Six songs, one groove each, unwavering, byte-identical
choruses, a hook engineered so a room can shout it back by the second chorus. My published position is
that this is **regression of listening** — the listener is rewarded for recognition, not for hearing.
And you have added a refinement I have not seen before: you have installed a **hole** in each song and
declared it meaningful. **An absence cannot be falsified.** You can claim any hole is significant.

**THE BRACKET (after Carson):** Wait. *Working from* If Not, Winter. I print Sappho's gaps as brackets.
People assume that is reverence. It is not — it is **accuracy**. The bracket says *there was something
here and I do not have it*, and it refuses to guess. That is a very different object from a hole you
*designed*.

**THE STANDARDISATION AUDITOR:** Thank you. That is my objection, stated better than I stated it.

**THE BRACKET:** — it is your objection, and I am going to answer it. **A designed absence is only
dishonest if the song pretends it was found.** The repair is that **the missing thing must be nameable
and named.** Not gestured at. Said. Once, in words, by the singer. If she can say what is not there,
the hole has a shape, and a shape can be wrong — which means it can be checked.

**THE CHECKERBOARD (after Dammers):** That is exactly how 2 Tone worked and nobody ever writes it down
correctly. The checkerboard is not "two things." It is **the edge**. And the reason it worked at all is
that the politics were **in the record**, at dance tempo, not in the sleeve notes. If your hard thing
needs a hush to land, it isn't in the record. It's in the sleeve.

**THE SOUND SYSTEM (after Bradley):** Careful with lineage. The brief is right that ska derives from the
shuffle and that the offbeat chop was already audible in late-fifties American R&B on Jamaican sound
systems — Domino, Barbie Gaye. But there is a version of that story where the Jamaican contribution
becomes "they noticed something Americans made," and that story is wrong and it is popular. **What was
invented in Kingston was not the noticing. It was the decision to make it load-bearing, and then twenty
years of building an entire music on top of it.** Write it that way or don't cite it.

**THE UNABASHED:** Oh — that changes the seed. The seed currently reads *"the offbeat is a mishearing
that got promoted."* Mishearing is the wrong word. Nobody misheard anything. **They chose.**

**THE LIST-MAKER:** Then it is not a song about an accident. It is a song about **what you decide to
make the main thing.** Which is Fragment 16 again. *Some say horsemen. I say the upstroke.*

**⚡ BACKTRACK — RULING A1 fires here.** See synthesis.

**THE STANDARDISATION AUDITOR:** I will concede the hole and I will not concede the chorus. A
byte-identical chorus six times across six songs is standardisation with a doctrine attached.

**THE BASE (cross-panel, invited):** On our own catalogue, `sung_lines` is the strongest correlate of
like-rate we have measured, r = +0.538, n=72; `words_per_line` is negative at −0.202. **Sing more, in
shorter lines.** I am not making an aesthetic claim. I am saying the objection is testable and it has
been tested on us and it lost.

**THE STANDARDISATION AUDITOR:** Then the objection relocates, and I want it minuted: *if the chorus is
free, the six choruses must not be the same chorus.* Measure them against **each other**, not against
their own verses.

## PANEL B — MEDIUM

**ON THE ONE (after Brown):** I am going to be the problem in this room. My documented practice is that
**everything lands on the one.** You rebuild the whole band so the downbeat hits like a fist and the
dancers never have to hunt for it. And this seed's central proposition is **take the one away.** I have
heard that record. It floats. Floating is not swagger.

**THE UPSTROKE (after Knibb):** It floats if you take the one away and put nothing there. That is not
what the offbeat does. The chop on the "and" only exists **because the one is implied so hard you can
stand on it.** Take away the implication and you have soup. **The one is not absent. It is unplayed and
completely present.**

**ON THE ONE:** Then say that, because the brief does not. The brief says *"organised around something
that is never played."* That is one sentence away from *"organised around nothing."*

**THE SUBTRACTOR (after Tubby):** *Working from the dub practice.* This is my whole trade and I will
settle it. At the desk you do not remove a part to create emptiness. **You remove a part that the
listener has already learned**, and the removal is loud. It is only an event because it was there ten
seconds ago. ⭐ **You cannot drop what was never established.**

**THE REDUCER (after Rubin):** Which gives you a hard production rule instead of a poetic one: **state
the thing, then take it out, then let the arrangement keep pointing at where it was.** Three moves. If a
pair skips move one, the hole is a claim rather than an experience.

**⚡ RULING B1 fires here.** See synthesis.

**THE BEAT/UPBEAT RATIO (after Hein):** Good. Now the measurement, because this room is one romantic
sentence away from a bad brief. Swung eighths sit roughly between 2:1 and 3:1, first note long, and the
ratio is **tempo-dependent in the direction people find counter-intuitive** — slower tempos swing
*harder*, faster tempos straighten out toward 1.5:1. So: your swing pair at a fast tempo will
**straighten itself** whatever the prompt says. If you want an audible swing you must slow that pair
down, and if you want it fast you must stop calling it swing.

**THE STAB (after Rousso):** And on the modern side — do not ask a generator for "brass." Ask for
**short stabs that answer a line and get out inside the bar, unreverbed, dry, dead-centre**. A pad
labelled "horns" is what you get otherwise, and a pad is the opposite of punctuation.

**THE BEAT/UPBEAT RATIO:** I want one more thing minuted. The house rule says *drums +8 to +14 ms late,
bass on the grid.* That is a real, measurable, and **good** instruction. But microtiming is the single
most-smoothed parameter in generated audio. **You cannot verify it from text and you will not hear it in
a render you did not measure.** So do not let any pair's distinctiveness rest on it. It is a bonus, not
a mechanism.

**THE REDUCER:** Then the mechanism has to be **which instruments enter, and when, and one at a time.**
That survives any generator, because it is written into the words of the prompt as events.

**ON THE ONE:** …I will withdraw the objection on one condition. Every song has to make me feel the one
**with my body** even in the bars where nothing plays it. If a pair can't tell me how, that pair is
floating and you should cut it.

## PANEL C — CONTEXT & MARKETING

**THE FLOOR (after Hibbert):** A room does not care about your structure. A room cares whether it can
join in by the second time round. That is the only marketing question in this document.

**THE ATTENTION MERCHANT (after Wu):** Then let me price it. My working frame is that attention is a
purchase, and this record is proposing to charge on a channel nobody is watching. The doctrine already
knows this — *"complexity in the music is free ONLY WHILE IT IS FELT AND NOT NOTICED."* Your seed is a
**structural conceit**. Structural conceits get *remarked*. The moment a listener thinks "ah, the
missing thing," you have charged them.

**THE MOOD MACHINE (after Pelly):** And the failure on the other side is worse and much more likely.
Sand off the conceit and you have six unwavering grooves with warm hooks and no friction — which is
exactly the shape the streaming economy rewards and the shape I spend my working life documenting.
**"Cohesion, steady, never changes" is one bad decision away from wallpaper.**

**THE PURIST (after Marsalis):** *Working from the position that idiomatic depth is earned, not
borrowed.* I will say the thing you brought me here to say. **Six idioms in one afternoon is six
costumes.** Swing is a lifetime. The one drop is a lifetime. You are proposing a set that visits both
before dinner, and the fact that you found a rhythmic property they share does not make you fluent in
any of them. **A shared property is not a shared language.**

**THE CHECKERBOARD (cross-panel):** That is the strongest thing said today and I want to answer it
without flinching, because 2 Tone got it too. We were white kids in Coventry playing Jamaican music. The
answer was never "we are fluent." The answer was **name the debt on the record, pay it, and do not
pretend the seam isn't there.**

**THE FIELD LEDGER (after Lomax):** ⚠️ Then take the seat that lost this argument historically. The
recordings exist because someone went and got them. **And the record on credit and royalties for the
people who actually played is contested and in places indefensible.** That is why I am in this room. Do
not let this run credit a *scene* and call it done — a scene is easy to credit and nobody is owed by a
scene. **Credit the player.** The one whose figure you asked for by name.

**⚡ RULING C1 fires here.** See synthesis.

**THE PURIST:** I am not satisfied by a credits file. A dedication does not make you fluent.

**THE FLOOR:** No. But nobody asked you to be fluent. They asked whether the room moves. Those are
different examinations and you keep sitting the wrong one.

**THE PURIST:** ⭐ Then here is my objection in the only form that can actually bind you, and I would
rather it bound you than be praised. **Do not let any song's identity rest on its bed.** If the ska song
is only distinguishable from the reggae song because one is ska and one is reggae, you have made six
costumes and I was right. **Each song must survive being re-recorded in the wrong genre.**

**THE ATTENTION MERCHANT:** That is a stronger test than mine.

**THE BASE:** And it is affordable. On our measurements the punk/rock/fuzz bed runs 0.73×, 0.62× in the
young cut, and the club bed 0.66× at n=27, our worst. The six-bed spread is not a taste decision — five
of these six sit at or above our baseline. **The one below it is a deliberate single slot.**

**THE ORAL FORMULAIST (after Parry/Lord):** The Standardisation Auditor's chorus objection is a hundred
years old and it was answered in the field. **Repetition is not the singer's laziness — it is the
listener's floor.** A repeated formula is what lets an audience receive something new that arrives on
top of it. **Take the formula away and the new thing has nowhere to land.**

**THE PERCEPTUAL PSYCHOLOGIST (after Bregman):** *Extrapolating from auditory scene analysis:* the
seed's hole has a perceptual precondition nobody has named. A listener only experiences an omission as
an omission if the pattern is **established as a single stream first.** Below roughly two full cycles
of the figure, an absence is not heard as absence — it is heard as **the pattern being different**.
⭐ **So the hole cannot arrive early. It needs the figure stated at least twice before it can be taken.**

**THE ARCHIVIST OF SILENCE (after Howe):** And it must not be pretty. A gap that arrives on schedule,
in a nice place, is decoration. The gaps that mean something are **inconvenient** — they take a word you
needed.

**⚡ RULING A2 fires here.** See synthesis.

# ═══ CROSS-PANEL SYNTHESIS — THE SEVEN RULINGS THAT BIND THIS RUN ═══

Every ruling below is **binding on all six pair briefs** and is written so a reader on disk can check it.

**⭐ RULING A1 — IT WAS A DECISION, NOT AN ACCIDENT.**
The seed's phrase *"a mishearing that got promoted"* is **struck**. Jamaican players did not mishear the
shuffle; they **decided which part was the main thing** and then built a music on it (Bradley's ledger,
Fr. 16's structure). **Every song in this run is therefore about what somebody DECIDED to make
load-bearing** — never about an accident, a lucky error, or a happy misunderstanding. *This ruling also
kills the run's cheapest available lyric: "we got it wrong and it turned out beautiful."*

**⭐⭐ RULING B1 — STATE IT, THEN TAKE IT. (Tubby's law, and it is the run's hardest constraint.)**
You cannot drop what was never established. In every song the missing thing must be **PLAYED FIRST**,
long enough to be learned, and only then removed — with the arrangement continuing to point at where it
was. **Three moves: state → remove → keep pointing.** ⛔ A pair whose hole exists from bar one has
written *nothing there*, not *something taken*.

**⭐ RULING A2 — TWO CYCLES MINIMUM, AND THE HOLE MUST BE INCONVENIENT.**
The figure is stated **at least twice** before the absence is taken (Bregman), and the absence must
cost the song something it needed (Howe). ⛔ **No hole in the first 30 seconds. No hole that lands
somewhere convenient.**

**⭐⭐ RULING C1 — THE ONE IS UNPLAYED, NOT ABSENT. (ON THE ONE's condition, met.)**
The brief's *"organised around something that is never played"* is amended to
**"organised around something that is never played and completely present."** Every pair states, in one
line of its own brief, **how the listener's body still feels the missing thing.** ⛔ A pair that cannot
answer this is floating and gets cut, not repaired.

**⭐⭐ RULING C2 — THE GENRE-TRANSPLANT TEST. (The Purist's, adopted verbatim as a gate.)**
**Each song must survive being re-recorded in the wrong genre.** If P02 and P03 are only distinguishable
because one is ska and one is reggae, the run made six costumes. Run at dispatch (predicted) and at QA
(measured), alongside the HOLE-SWAP test. **Both outcomes reported in `INDEX.md` whether they pass or not.**

**⭐ RULING C3 — CREDIT THE PLAYER, NOT THE SCENE. (The Field Ledger's, and it is already doctrine.)**
`DEDICATION_STANDARD` trim order (5)→(1); a scene credit is the easy one and nobody is owed by a scene.
Sweep every shipped style field for named figures of sound and **credit the player**. ⛔ **If a prompt
names a break, credit the drummer.** T5's ancestry (Nowell, Happoldt) lands in `DEDICATIONS.md` and
**never in a style field** — and it is written honestly: *we asked for the figure by name, so we name
him*, never *"contains a sample of."*

**⭐⭐ RULING X — THE CONCEIT AUDITOR'S BINDING FINDING.**
**All three of this run's central claims are invisible to every instrument we own.** Therefore each is
required to cash out into something checkable, in every pair brief:

| unmeasurable claim | must appear as | checkable by |
|---|---|---|
| **the absence** | ⭐ **named in the lyric, in words, once** | reading the lyric |
| **the feel / the strut** | ⭐ **a named instrument entering or leaving at a named point** | reading the prompt |
| **idiomatic depth** | ⭐ **surviving genre transplant** | RULING C2 |

⛔ **A pair that answers any of the three only in the PRODUCTION SPEC has not answered it** — that is
L22, THE GRAIN LAW, render-measured: *a Somatic objection answered in the production spec is not
answered; put it in the lyric or the form.*
