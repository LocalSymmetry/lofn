## 0. THE SLOT, RESTATED IN ONE SENTENCE SO IT CANNOT DRIFT

A woman stands on a hospital stair landing at six in the morning, between the twelfth floor and the
fourteenth, with a lanyard in her hand that isn't hers, and gives — out loud, at speed, to whoever is
coming up — a list of the specific operational competences of a colleague who was removed for saying
out loud what would happen *if*; and she will not say that colleague's name, because the building
already said it once, and a name is the only thing the building knows how to file.

**The engine is the rhythm section.** The syncopated riff against the straight kick is the song. The
drive is a colour that arrives for four bars and leaves. ⛔ **If the guitars carry this, the slot failed.**

---

## 1. FACET ONE — THE COMPETENCE, IN OPERATIONAL DETAIL

⭐ **Sunna's law R — RUTHLESS SPECIFICITY.** Never *"she was brilliant."* Which pump. Which sink. Which
hour. The praise has to be the kind only a colleague could give, and every item has to be a thing a
person actually *does*, not a quality a person *has*.

**The inventory this song praises from** (drawn on unevenly across the four variations — see facet 4):

| item | why it is praise and not description |
|---|---|
| reads the **pump**, not the screen the pump reports to | the machine is a witness, not an authority |
| **counts the sharps twice**, and once out loud | the second count is the whole profession |
| knows the **cylinder by weight**, not by the gauge | the gauge lies at the bottom and everyone knows it |
| tells the **alarms apart by ear** through a shut door | pattern knowledge nobody can hand you on a sheet |
| knows the **sats probe reads low on cold fingers** | stops a wrong escalation before it starts |
| knows which **sink runs hot** at four in the morning | the building is a body and she knows its plumbing |
| knows the **lift says coming and is lying** | the specific local truth that no sign carries |
| **stands between the family and the monitor trace** | a competence made entirely of where you put your body |
| **calls the blood gas back down herself** | she does not delegate the bit that goes wrong |
| **finds the sets that are not where the list says** | real stock knowledge vs. the inventory system |
| **takes the ugly bay**, every time, without saying so | the distribution of unpleasantness, chosen |
| **gives away the good pen** | the smallest possible generosity, done constantly |
| **writes the hour she actually did it** | the difference between a record and a defence |
| **never made you feel a fool for asking twice** | the reason the ward is safe: people ask her twice |
| knows **which porter will say yes** and does not ask the others | a social map, held privately, used for patients |
| **cleans the bay before the break**, not after | timing as generosity |
| **teaches the building on the stairs**, at speed | the transmission mechanism the song itself is using |
| **says the read-back** — *say it back to me* | closed-loop communication; the hook's ancestor |

⛔ **What is NOT praised anywhere:** heroism, sacrifice, "going above and beyond", vocation, angels,
care as a calling. Every one of those is a management word for unpaid labour and Sunna does not use
management words. The praise is **technical**.

## 2. FACET TWO — THE STAIRWELL, PHYSICAL INVENTORY

⭐ **This place is mine alone in this run. Nobody else's song may stand here.**

A concrete fire-stair in an invented hospital — **Kilnbrook** — at 06:00. Not the main stair: the one
behind the double doors that staff use because the lift is a rumour.

- **Underfoot:** grey non-slip vinyl with a black nosing strip on every tread, one strip lifted at the
  corner on the way up to fourteen and taped down with silver tape by somebody who did not have to.
- **The rail:** cold steel, painted, repainted, chipped back to primer at the turns where hands land.
- **The light:** a fluorescent batten with a slow ballast that ticks; the landing window is a wired-glass
  panel showing a car park and a sky just beginning.
- **The wall:** a fire-instruction notice in a frame, a fire extinguisher on a bracket, a hand-gel
  dispenser that is empty and has been for a month, and a **floor number stencilled at each landing**.
- ⭐ **The landing she is standing on has no stencil.** The count on the wall goes **twelve … fourteen**.
- **The sound:** a door banging two floors down; a trolley on a corridor floor overhead; the ballast tick;
  her own breathing, because she has come up on foot.
- **Smell:** hand gel, hot dust off the batten, and the specific cold of a stair that is outside the
  heated envelope.
- **In her hands:** a printed handover sheet folded in four, a biro, and **a lanyard that isn't hers** —
  plain navy fabric gone grey at the fold, with the badge still clipped on, photo side out.
- **⭐ THE OBJECT'S DEFECT (this is the whole of variation four):** the badge is **still live**. The person
  was removed; the permissions were not. It opens the lift, the drug room, the door onto fourteen and
  the roof door above it. **The building is worse at its own procedure than she is at hers.**

## 3. FACET THREE — THE FUNCTION-EPITHETS THAT STAND IN FOR THE NAME

⛔ **RULING B1, as this slot spends it:** the name is said **once, early, by somebody else** — by the
**overhead tannoy**, which is the building's own voice and not a character. After that it never arrives
again, and every person in the song is their function.

⭐ **The invented name is `Halloran`.** PERSON invented · PLACE invented (Kilnbrook) · WHEN re-set.
No real person, hospital, employer, official, publication or case appears anywhere.

⭐ **RULING C1 — HOW THE BODY STILL FEELS IT:** *"Halloran"* is placed on **beat one of the bar** in the
tannoy page. From that point every epithet's stressed syllable is written to land on **that same beat
one**, so the ear keeps arriving at a downbeat and finding a job description in the slot where a name
was. The absence is not a silence; it is **a downbeat that always resolves to a function.**

**Four epithet grammars, one per variation — no two share a shape:**

| variation | grammar | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | `The one who ___` (agent + verb) | the one who reads the pump · the one who counts the sharps · the one who signed it |
| **V2** | `Whoever ___` (applied to **both sides** — the ones who taught her and the ones who removed her) | whoever showed me · whoever taught me · whoever signed it · whoever picked the Tuesday · whoever's on nights now |
| **V3** | `The one on ___` (place and shift, because the song is climbing) | the one on nights · the one on twelve · the one on stairs · the one on fourteen · the one on the phone |
| **V4** | **bare definite job-titles**, no relative clause | the night sister · the porter on the early · the registrar who answers · the cleaner · **the one this belongs to** |

⭐ **V2's grammar is the sharpest instrument in the pair**: the same word — *whoever* — is used for the
person who taught her to hold a line and for the person who signed the removal. One of them earned
anonymity and one of them is hiding in it, and the song never says which is which.

## 5. FACET FIVE — THE HOOK'S **FUNCTION** (⛔ its words are not written here)

⭐ **Sunna's law L — LOAD THE FLOOR.** 4–8 syllables, no interval a throat can't reach at volume while
out of breath, **the room owns it by the second chorus.** Her voice is not the product; the room is.

⛔ **FORBIDDEN SECTION: the chorus.** The thesis — *the function IS the name; refusing it is the only
sanction left* — may **not** land there. So each hook is given a **job that is not the thesis**:

| variation | the hook's FUNCTION (not its words) |
|---|---|
| **V1** | **an instruction the room can obey** — the read-back, the single operational habit that makes a ward safe. It praises a *practice*, so the room chanting it is doing the practice. |
| **V2** | **the opening of the sentence that got somebody removed**, chanted flat by a crowd, so that the thing the institution treated as dangerous becomes the least dangerous object in the room. |
| **V3** | **a counting instruction** — physical, breath-paced, climbable. It is the only hook of the four that a body performs rather than states. |
| **V4** | **a declaration of the small, deliberate refusal** — what she does instead. It must read as **a practice she was taught, never as a protest**, or it becomes adolescent affect and the slot fails. |

⭐ **Sound axis (mandatory at Phase 0, `lofn-music` L21):** all four hooks are **stressed monosyllables
over a syncopated riff**, so the room's chant lands on the guitar's offbeat and the kick answers it.
Return is spent, not saved: **a byte-identical eight-line chorus, three times, plus the opening quatrain
returning byte-identical late in the song.** Removal of rhyme is not on the table anywhere in this pair.

## 6. FACET SIX — THE WOLF: AN EVENT, AND IT RESOLVES

**Somebody was removed for describing a hypothetical** — for saying out loud what would happen *if*.
Invented, non-mappable, and stated in the plainest available English: *if the nights go down to one pair
of hands, the first thing that goes missing isn't people — it's the second time somebody checks.*

⛔ **How it is delivered:** in a **sung** verse, full band, mid-list, **no drop-out, no hush, no breath,
no reverence, no tempo change.** Same voice as the joke. It resolves inside its own quatrain — she says
what happened, she says what she did, the list continues with the removal now inside it.

⛔ **Where the wolf may NOT go:** the chorus (forbidden section), the spoken tercets (that register is the
flat-threat colour and putting the wolf there would be marking it), or the last line of the song.

## 7. FACET SEVEN — THE SUNG FACT, AND HOW IT IS ANSWERED

**Exactly one, in words, never digits, responded to and never recited:** ***the thirteenth floor.***

⭐ **The joke that makes it a fact and not a factoid:** the building counts **twelve, fourteen** — and she
is standing on the landing in between. The floor was never removed. **The number was.** So when the
removed colleague said *the thirteenth floor*, and she said *there isn't one*, the answer was: *you are
standing on it.* The fact is **answered by the setting**, which is why it is sung once and never explained.

⭐ Structurally it is the same operation as the whole song at a different scale: **an institution deletes a
label and believes it has deleted the thing.** That is available to a listener who wants it and required
of nobody.

## 9. FACET NINE — SOURCE 2, IMPORTED AS PRODUCTION BEHAVIOUR ONLY

⛔ **Never in a lyric.** Both assigned phrases are **capped as COLOUR, never engine.**

- **`howling guitars`** → enters as **one four-bar swell**, mixed **under** the bass, panned wide, and gone
  before the next section. In V2 and V4 it is **named in the exclude field instead** — the excess is the
  failure mode, and naming it there is the honest use of the phrase.
- **`Olympian guitar feats`** → **negated in all four exclude fields.** There is no solo. There is no
  virtuosity lane. The most technically demanding thing on the record is the bass moving between chords
  while the kick stays straight, and it is mixed so it does not sound demanding.

⭐ **Why both are capped:** THE GRAIN LAW. A spec that fights the generator gets smoothed; a spec that
asks a rap-rock engine for guitars gets guitars **carrying the song**, which is the one outcome this slot
is forbidden. The cap therefore lives in the **field that reaches the model**, not in prose about the field.

## 10. FACET TEN — WHAT THE BASS IS DOING (the reason this is not a distortion record)

⭐ **If you can hum the bass, you have swagger.** In all four variations the bass is the **second lead
vocal**: front of mix, **−2 to 0 dB against the lead**, moving between chords rather than sitting on roots,
and carrying a figure that exists **before the vocal enters and after it leaves** (THE HUM TEST).

⛔ **The division of labour, fixed for the pair:**
- **kick** — straight, one and three, unmoved, all song
- **guitar** — a three-note figure on the **and of two** and the **and of four**; percussion, not harmony
- **bass** — the tune; it is the thing a listener leaves with
- **drive** — four bars, once, under everything, then gone

**The transplant test (RULING C2):** strip the distortion entirely and play this at 100 BPM on a clean
guitar, a Rhodes and an upright bass and **the song is unchanged**, because its absence is a lyric absence
and its engine is a rhythm section. That is the honest reason this slot could be given the run's worst-
performing bed exactly once: **its identity is not its bed.**
