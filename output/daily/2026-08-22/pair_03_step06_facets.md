## 0. THE ROOM, FIXED BEFORE ANY FACET

A launderette at two in the morning, the only lit shop on a road of shut ones. Nine machines, one of
them hers and finished an hour ago. She is wearing somebody else's coat. **Nothing is wrong.**

⭐ **THE SLOT'S REAL DANGER, NAMED FIRST.** *Steady + warm + cohesive* is one bad decision from wallpaper.
So the governing test for every facet below is: **APPETITE IS A VERB.** In each variation she **wants
something and takes it**, and the taking is an event with a hand in it — not a mood, not an atmosphere,
not "she enjoys the room." A facet that cannot name the verb does not ship.

| variation | **THE VERB** — what she actually does | what it costs her |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **EATS.** Finds mints in a borrowed pocket and takes two, then a third. | she will have to admit it, and she won't |
| **V2** | **SPENDS.** Feeds a pound into an empty machine to buy heat she does not need. | a pound, on nothing, deliberately |
| **V3** | **PULLS A STRANGER UP.** Hand out, one song, on the lino, at two in the morning. | the risk of being told no in a room with one exit |
| **V4** | **TAKES THE LONG WAY.** Chooses the hill road home because it adds a mile under the sky. | sleep, and a mile, and she is not tired |

⛔ Four different verbs, four different costs. **If two variations share a verb, one of them is furniture.**

## 1. THE TEN FACETS

### FACET 1 — ⭐ THE ABSENCE, TIMBRALLY: **A MIC'D INSTRUMENT THAT LEADS THE WHOLE FIRST SECTION AND WALKS OUT**

**The instrument is NAMED per variation, and it is a different instrument each time.** This is the one
thing that makes four takes into four records rather than one record mixed four ways.

| variation | **the instrument that leaves** | why this instrument, in this room |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **MELODICA** — close-mic'd, breathy, one player, the blow audible | a toy made into a lead voice; it sounds like somebody's mouth, which is why losing it is a person leaving |
| **V2** | **TENOR SAXOPHONE** — mic'd in the room, not a section, one horn | the room's own machinery is brass-loud; a single tenor is the only thing in the arrangement that is warmer than the dryer |
| **V3** | **TROMBONE** — slide audible, played conversationally, phrases that answer | the angle is two strangers talking without talking; a trombone is the instrument that most sounds like a second person in the room |
| **V4** | **FLUTE** — wooden, breath on the edge, close | she is walking out under an open sky; the flute is the only one of the four that sounds like air moving, and it goes out into it |

⛔ **RULING B1 — STATE IT FIRST.** In all four the instrument **leads the entire first section**: it
states the four-bar answer over the bass figure in the intro, holds the lead through Verse 1, and is
still there through the first Refrain. **That is roughly seventy seconds and at least twelve statements
of the figure.** ⛔ **No hole in the first thirty seconds** — the answer slot is *occupied* for the whole
of section one, which is the only reason its emptiness can later be heard as a removal rather than a
silence.

⭐ **RULING C1 — HOW THE BODY STILL FEELS IT.** The bass figure is a call whose answer lands at the top
of the next cycle. When the instrument goes, **the bass keeps playing the call and keeps leaving the
answer slot open at the top of every cycle**, with nothing in it but the spring-reverb tail of the bar
before. The listener's foot arrives at the top of the bar expecting the answer; the answer does not
come; the foot arrives anyway. **The hole is in tempo and it is on the strong position, which is where
it is most inconvenient.**

⛔ **THE HOLE IS INCONVENIENT, NOT DECORATIVE.** The departing instrument is carrying the *tune* — it is
the thing you would hum on the way in. Removing it costs the song its melodic lead and hands the job to
the bass, which then plays a tune with a bite out of it for two-thirds of the record.

⭐ **DUB'S OWN LAW, AND IT IS THE SLOT'S:** *you cannot drop what was never established.* The removal is
loud only because it was there ten seconds ago.

### FACET 2 — THE ABSENCE IS NAMED IN THE LYRIC, IN WORDS, **ONCE** (RULING X)

Once per song, in the same voice as the joke, and **the band does not slow down.** It lands in **Verse 2
in all four** — directly after the instrument goes, because that is when a person would actually mention
it. ⛔ It is never mentioned again: naming it twice would make the song *about* its own device.

⛔ **It may not land in the FORBIDDEN SECTION (the last verse).** It does not.

### FACET 3 — WHAT SPECIFICALLY GETS PRAISED — **FOUR DIFFERENT INVENTORIES, NO SHARED OBJECTS**

⛔ **Hard rule for this pair: no object appears in two variations.** The coat is the slot's constant
(it is in her hands in all four by assignment) but *what is praised about it* differs every time.

**V1 — THE POCKET (a borrowed inventory).** A bus ticket bitten at the corner · a bottle top gone soft
at the edge · a hairgrip · a receipt folded over twice and gone soft · half a packet of strong mints
with the foil still shut · the third button sewn back on in a colour that does not match · the lining
smelling of somebody else's washing powder · sand in a seam · a key that opens nothing she knows · a
thread hanging off the left cuff.

**V2 — THE MACHINERY (the room's mechanical facts, loved out loud).** Machine seven, the big loud one ·
the door that needs a hip · the slot that takes old pounds and refuses new ones · the enamel worn off
the dial right down at *cottons* · the change machine that drops coins like a win · the lint tray, grey
and warm · the tube light humming about a semitone flat · a coin loose in a drum going *tick* every
turn for months that nobody has taken out · the spin the floor picks up and puts in your back.

**V3 — THE OTHER PERSON'S METHOD (praise for a stranger's competence, said out loud).** A bike folded
against the wall · a wash that is all one colour · the shake-out before each thing goes in · the quick
crossword done in pen and filling · boots put on a chair and then thought about and taken off · a sleeve
folded in, then the other, then in half, then in half · the radio on the shelf · the knee that clicks
the bike open · a goodnight that is only a goodnight.

**V4 — THE ROAD (what is out there at twenty to three).** A shut chip shop with the extractor still
running · bins out in a line with one gone over · a cat that decides she is not the problem · the
streetlight by the postbox out since spring, making a good dark patch · a taxi at the top with its light
off and the driver eating · her own breath standing up under the lamp · allotment sheds leaning into each
other · somebody's runner beans having the time of their lives · one lit kitchen with a radio in it.

### FACET 4 — THE HOOK'S **FUNCTION** (4–8 syllables, room-ownable, ⛔ words not written here)

⭐ **Sunna's law L — LOAD THE FLOOR:** the hook belongs to the room, not to her. All four are inside a
fifth, chantable at volume by somebody out of breath, and **none of them is a sentence you have to parse.**

| variation | **FUNCTION** — what the hook DOES | grammatical mood (⛔ all four different) |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **A CLAIM.** She declares a borrowed thing hers for the length of the hour, and dares the room to want it back. | declarative — possession |
| **V2** | **A CHEER FOR A TRANSACTION.** The sound of money going into a machine, celebrated as an event, repeatedly. | declarative — inversion, the verb first |
| **V3** | **AN INVITATION WITH THE RELEASE ALREADY IN IT.** It asks and un-asks in one breath, so nobody owes anybody anything for saying yes. | imperative — two clauses, second one frees you |
| **V4** | **A REFUSAL TO END, STATED AS A PLAN.** Not a wish, not a hope: a route decision she has already made. | first person future — a plan |

⛔ **Four different moods, four different jobs.** No hook is a summary of the song; each is a thing a
person would actually say in that room.

### FACET 5 — THE SUNG FACT: *one thousand seven hundred and six* — **RESPONDED TO, NEVER RECITED**

One per song, spelled in words, on one line, and **the line after it is the point.** ⛔ It is not a
statistic she admires; it is a thing somebody else did that she declines to be impressed into duty by.

| variation | **how the number arrives** | ⭐ **her response — four different responses** |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | folded into a newspaper in the borrowed pocket, part of the inventory | she takes their word for it, because she was not out there — and she was somewhere better |
| **V2** | the radio on the shelf, between songs | *good for them* — she has not looked up once and is not going to; the drum is better |
| **V3** | the stranger reads it out from his paper | she asks whether he went out to look; he did not; neither did she; the warm room won |
| **V4** | she remembers it herself, on the hill road | she is not owed one, and she is looking anyway — the only variation where she goes out to meet it |

⛔ **Exactly one numeric fact is sung per song.** No digits anywhere in a sung line.

### FACET 7 — ⭐ **NO WOLF. NONE. IN ANY VARIATION.** — and the audit that proves it

⛔ **No dark line, on any schedule, in any variation. No late turn into sadness. No redemption. No "but".**

**The three places a wolf tries to get in here, and where each is blocked:**
1. **The borrowed coat implying a loss.** ⛔ Blocked at the concept: the coat belongs to **Marnie**, who
   is alive, asleep, and going to want it back tomorrow, and this is said out loud. It is a *borrowing*,
   with a return date, and the return date is funny, not sad.
2. **"Alone at two in the morning" tilting to loneliness.** ⛔ Blocked by making solitude the **luxury**:
   nobody wants anything from her, and that is the good news, and it is stated as good news.
3. **The deadline (the dryer stopping, the light going off, dawn).** ⛔ This is the run's *"delight with
   a deadline"* and it must be **fuel, not grief** — her law **O — ONE MORE**. The machine has minutes
   left, so she is having all of them. **Finitude buys appetite here; it never buys regret.**

⭐ The reveal engine — **nothing is wrong, and that is the whole event** — is stated plainly **once** per
song, in Verse 2 or the bridge, ⛔ **never in the last verse (the forbidden section).**

### FACET 8 — THE VERSE ARCHITECTURE, AND THE **RETURN DEBT** IT INCURS

**Long lines, one breath each, NO end-rhyme.** ⛔ **Removal is a debt** (`lofn-music` L21). Declared here,
paid in Step 07 and measurable in Step 10:

- **PAYMENT (a): a byte-identical refrain**, three times, unaltered to the character. It needs no defence.
- **PAYMENT (b): a vowel-chain**, one per variation, carried through every long line — in the interior
  stresses and landing on the final stressed syllable of most lines — with **the codas deliberately
  varied so no two line-ends rhyme.** The chain is what the ear holds onto where the rhyme used to be.

| variation | **the chain** | sample line-end colours (⛔ no two rhyme) |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | the round **"o"** | door · off · corner · folded · open · go · home · lot · slow · closed |
| **V2** | **"ow"** | pound · out · down · round · house · hour · loud · mouth · now |
| **V3** | **"ee / ih"** | seat · sleeve · machine · knee · easy · in · still · pleased · deal |
| **V4** | **"eye"** | night · sky · mile · side · quiet · time · fine · light · mine |

⛔ Four different chains. **A vowel-chain that two variations share is one variation.**

### FACET 9 — SOURCE 2, TRANSLATED INTO PRODUCTION BEHAVIOUR (⛔ never into a lyric)

- ***"lazily-played synth pads"*** → the pad is **hand-played and behind the beat**: chord changes arrive
  a half-beat after the bass gets there, never quantised, never swelling. It is the one element allowed
  to be late, and its lateness is what makes the groove feel unhurried rather than slow.
- ***"detuned bliss"*** → **the happiness is a few cents out on purpose.** Pads sit six cents off the
  bass; the vocal doubles are ±6 cents and 15–35 ms misaligned. ⛔ No transparent pitch correction. The
  record is warm and it is **not in tune with itself**, and that is the emotional thesis of the run
  arriving as a tuning decision rather than as a lyric.

Both phrases appear **verbatim in the MUSIC PROMPT of all four variations**, and in no lyric.

### FACET 10 — THE PROHIBITIONS, CHECKED AS A LIST

⛔ **No gear-change** — one bed, one tempo, 108 BPM end to end; every event is a **timbre entering or
leaving**, never a dynamic swell and never a genre shift.
⛔ **No period pastiche** — a **rocksteady BODY in a 2026 record**: modern sub weight under the
upright-style bass tone, a close modern vocal, clean digital top. Spring reverb and tape echo are **dub
tools in use**, not a costume. ⛔ No vintage-mono, no crackle, no lo-fi hiss standing in for arrangement.
⛔ **No fuzz as the swagger carrier** — zero drive in this slot. The strut is the rhythm section.
⛔ **No thesis in the last verse.** ⛔ **No digits in a sung line.** ⛔ **No Bandcamp phrase in a lyric.**
⛔ **No real person, band, company, route or case.** ⛔ **No child.** All credits → the DEDICATION block.
⛔ **No house-lexicon phrase.** ⛔ **No standing tension lane** — no riser, no filter that only opens.

## THE GENRE-TRANSPLANT PREDICTION (RULING C2)

**Survives.** The absence here is **timbral** — a named lead instrument leaving — which is not made of
reggae. Re-record any of the four as a slow country shuffle or a New Orleans second line and the event
is identical: *the thing that was carrying the tune walks out and the bass keeps holding its place
open.* ⛔ What would die in transplant is the *pleasure* of the one-drop's missing beat one — but that is
the bed's contribution to the body, not the song's identity.

## THE HOLE-SWAP PREDICTION (Test 1)

**Breaks against all five other slots.** P03's hole is a **timbre with a name and a mouth on it**; drop
P01's missing five-chord, P02's missing downbeat, P04's missing second voice, P05's missing name or
P06's vacated centre into this song and none of them can be *named in a lyric as an instrument that
left*, which is this song's single lyric event. **The exposure is the reverse direction:** P03's hole
dropped into P04 could read as "a voice that stopped," so P03 keeps the *instrument*, P04 keeps the
*performer*, and the two are distinguished by whether the room fills the gap. **Here nobody fills it.**
