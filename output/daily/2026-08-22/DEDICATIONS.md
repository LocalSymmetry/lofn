## `daily-music-2026-08-22-sunna-offbeat`

*`vault/DEDICATION_STANDARD.md`: every shipped piece carries a ≤500-char dedication naming what it borrowed and who it is owed to — **measured in code, never eyeballed**. All 24 measured; longest 494. Full set in `CREDITS.md`.*

⛔ **These names appear here and in the per-song `## DEDICATION` block ONLY** — never in a music prompt, exclude field, lyric or title. Proved by `credit_sweep.py`: **0 leaks across 4 generator-facing fields × 24 songs.** A name in a generator-facing field is an instruction to imitate; a name here is a debt paid.

⭐ **The credit this system is built to forget, paid.** The run's seed is a *deleted beat*. Both players who authored the deletions are named: **Carlton Barrett** for the one drop — the pattern defined by the kick that does not land on beat one — and **Lloyd Knibb** for the ska stroke, the upstroke that became an entire music. Living-scene credit is easy and already gated; the session player who made the figure is invisible, usually dead, and asks for nothing.

**Honesty clause, both ways:** these are generated tracks, **not samples**. We asked for these figures by name, so we name the players — never *"contains a sample of."*

## P01V1 — I Did Not Do This In A Hurry

*470 chars*

The body of this record is the American shuffle — the arrangement Jamaican sound systems were importing through the nineteen-fifties, where the offbeat guitar chop was still incidental. We asked for that figure by name, so we name the sides it was riding on: Fats Domino, "Be My Guest"; Barbie Gaye, "My Boy Lollypop". Players heard the decoration and made it the engine. Generated, not sampled — no recording of theirs is in this. Lofn (Claude) with Dr. Local Symmetry.

## P02V4 — Louder Than The Rain

*462 chars*

The horn line in unison is Roland Alphonso and Tommy McCook's habit: a front line that answered the singer and got out inside the bar. Lloyd Knibb's drum pattern is under all of it, Lloyd Brevett walked the bass, Jah Jerry Haynes chopped the and, and the punk tempo we play it at is John Bradbury's. Generated, not sampled: no recording of theirs is in this song. We asked for the figures by name, so the players get named. Lofn (Claude) with Dr. Local Symmetry.

## P05V2 — Whoever Signed It

*472 chars*

The clean chiming figure over a heavy floor is Brad Delson's move: a three-note guitar part that behaves like a bell and refuses to become a riff. It is the least celebrated thing in a loud genre and it is the reason the loud parts land at all. We asked for it by name, so we name him. Generated, not sampled — no recording of his is in this. The meeting, the minute-taker and everyone in the song are invented; the mechanism is not. Lofn (Claude) with Dr. Local Symmetry.

## P06V4 — All The Way Up

*477 chars*

The stab that answers and leaves is the Memphis Horns' figure — Wayne Jackson, trumpet; Andrew Love, tenor saxophone — and we asked for it by name, so we name them. The rest of the percussion is a stapler, a chair leg and a crate lid, played by nobody famous, which is the point. Art-pop frame after Noga Erez and Ori Rousso: dry stabs, hard programmed low end, a voice that turns without becoming somebody else. Generated, never sampled. Lofn (Claude) with Dr. Local Symmetry.
