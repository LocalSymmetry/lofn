## 2. THEME RESEARCH — the offbeat lineage (5 obscure facts, fetched, not recalled)

**T1 · THE OFFBEAT IS A MISHEARING THAT GOT PROMOTED.** The ska stroke *derives from the shuffle* — the
American R&B arrangement Jamaican sound systems were importing through the 1950s. The off-beat guitar
chop was **already there, incidental**, in late-'50s US R&B sides such as Fats Domino's *"Be My Guest"*
and Barbie Gaye's *"My Boy Lollypop"*, both sound-system staples. Jamaican players heard the thing that
was decoration and **made it the engine.** Within two or three years it had stopped being a shuffle.
*(Sources: Ska stroke / Ska, Wikipedia + Grokipedia.)*

**T2 · THE ONE DROP DELETES BEAT ONE.** Reggae's signature pattern is defined by **the absence of the
kick on beat 1** — it drops the "one" entirely and puts kick and snare together on **3**. Popularised by
Carlton "Carly" Barrett with the Wailers, disputed in origin (Aston Barrett, Winston Grennan), and
Barrett himself was **heavily influenced by Lloyd Knibb of the Skatalites** — so the lineage runs back
into T1. *(Sources: One drop rhythm / Carlton Barrett, Wikipedia; DRUM! Magazine.)*

**T3 · SWING IS A RATIO, AND IT MOVES.** Swung eighths run roughly **2:1 to 3:1**, first note long. The
ratio is **tempo-dependent in the opposite direction from intuition**: slower tempos swing *harder*
(2.5:1+), faster tempos straighten toward 1.5:1. It is measurable — researchers call it the
**Beat/Upbeat Ratio** and read it off waveforms. Many players reject the clean triplet 2:1 as wrong.
**The grid you hear is not the grid that is there.** *(Sources: Bret Pimentel; Open Music Theory.)*

**T4 · 2 TONE PUT THE GAP IN THE LOGO.** Jerry Dammers founded 2 Tone Records in **Coventry, 1979**;
The Specials were Black and white musicians **playing ska at punk tempos**, debut single *"Gangsters"*
cut in January 1979 at Horizon Studios, No. 6 UK. The label's name and its checkerboard are not about
two things — they are about **the edge between them.** *(Sources: 2 Tone Records / Gangsters, Wikipedia.)*

**T5 · THE MOST-SUNG LINE ON *SUBLIME* IS SUNG BY SOMEBODY ELSE.** *"Doin' Time"* (1996) samples Herbie
Mann's live bossa-nova *"Summertime"* from *At the Village Gate*. To clear the Gershwin, the band had to
sing **"summertime"** where they had recorded **"doin' time"** — but Bradley Nowell had died that May,
so the substitute line was re-cut by their friend and producer **Michael Happoldt**. Millions of people
sing along to a hook **in a voice that is not the singer's**, and the record is titled after the words
that were taken out. *(Sources: Doin' Time / Sublime (album), Wikipedia; Far Out.)*
