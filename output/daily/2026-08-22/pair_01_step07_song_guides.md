## ⛔ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — ANSWERED FIRST, CONCRETELY

**Where is the body standing?**
On a patterned carpet in the middle of the Marigold Suite, a first-floor function room in an invented
town. Her weight is on her back foot because the clipboard is against her hip and she is writing on it
with the pen held sideways. Half the round tables are dressed and half are bare chipboard. The chair
trolley is behind her, stacked taller than she is, with one bad castor. The double doors are shut. The
window is behind her left shoulder and the four o'clock light is going down the room onto the far wall.
The air is hot bulbs, ironed cotton and the vinegar in the window cleaner. On the little stage a trumpet
is playing the same bar over and over. **She is not remembering this. It is happening and she is in it.**

**What could hurt it here?**
Not the empty chair. The empty chair is a fact and she has already dealt with it by writing the card out
in the same hand as all the others. **What could hurt it is REVERENCE** — the entirely available,
entirely respectable move where the band gets quiet at the right moment and everybody agrees to feel
something on cue. That move would delete this song, because the whole claim of this song is that
**the party is going to be good anyway and that is not a betrayal of anybody.** The danger is not that
she will break down. The danger is that the record will offer to break down on her behalf.

> Both answers are a place and a stake, not a mood. All four guides are written from that carpet.

## THE HOUSE RULES ALL FOUR GUIDES OBEY
*(Written once so no guide repeats them, and so a breach is visible as a diff.)*

**BED.** Jump blues / swing shuffle, **102 BPM in every variation, no exceptions, no ramp, no drop.**
Swung eighths at roughly **two and a half to one**, first note long — slow tempos swing harder, and this
is a slow tempo on purpose. Drums **eight to fourteen milliseconds late**; upright bass **on** the grid.
That gap is the strut and it is the only place tension is permitted to live.

**FORM.** Every section is **one twelve-bar cycle** — verses, choruses, bridges, all of them — so the
form never changes and the ear can count the whole record on one hand. Intro and tag sections are eight
bars. ⛔ **No gear change, no genre shift, no key change, no tempo change, no dynamic swell doing a
timbre's job.** The change is **timbral**: named instruments arriving and leaving, one at a time.

**VERSE ARCHITECTURE.** Four tercets per verse, three bars each. A rhyming couplet on bars one and two,
then **a hanging third line on bar three which the horns answer** with one short dry stab, out inside the
bar. The hanging line is short — three to seven words — and it is always a plain statement of fact.
⭐ **In every variation the horns fail to answer exactly one hanging line, and that line is the wolf.**

**THE ABSENCE.** Bars nine to twelve are the turnaround, **V–IV–I–V**. Stated in full **twice** in the
eight-bar intro. From the first sung cycle on, **bar nine goes to the four chord and bar twelve stays on
the tonic** — the five chord never returns. The **upright bass keeps walking up to the fifth degree at
the top of every turnaround** under a chord that never comes; that is how the body still feels it.

**VOCAL.** One woman, warm northern-English alto, full chest, close on a dynamic mic, **breaths left in,
no de-esser on them, no transparent pitch correction.** Doubles misaligned fifteen to thirty-five
milliseconds, six cents flat. **Stacked girl-group thirds are a required instrument**, not a garnish, and
each variation has **one moment where two voices split and the room hears the gap open.**
⛔ **Same voice for the joke and the knife.** No breath-drop, no hush, no held note, no rubato at the
card. She says it and gets back to the list.

**SOURCE-2 IMPORTS.** *"twirling acoustic guitars"* → a flat-top and a small archtop circling the root,
third and sixth of the chord the turnaround is missing, dry, a hand's width either side of centre.
*"an overall mood of quiet reverence"* → **inverted, and it lives in the EXCLUDE field** as the thing the
record refuses. ⛔ Neither phrase may appear in a lyric.

**MIX.** Bass **minus two to zero dB** against the lead and never buried · horn stabs short and
**unreverbed** · handclaps dry and dead centre · **minus fourteen LUFS with at least ten dB of crest** ·
⛔ no standing tension lane, no riser, no filter that only opens, no supersaw, no white noise, no airhorn.

# GUIDE 1 · **V1 — THE PLAN**
### *facet 1 — Praise as an act with a deadline* · **key B-flat** · 102 BPM

**The angle.** Four in the afternoon, mid-set-up, clipboard against her hip. She reads the chart out
loud, table by table, and every entry is a reason: this person here, because they are good at that.

**Storytelling technique.** *The chart read as a roll of honour.* Each tercet is one seat and one
competence. The reveal engine — **the arrangement outlives its reason** — is never stated as a thought;
it arrives twice as a plain fact about a switch and a wall (*he learned it off the man before him* /
*the brass by the door says nineteen sixty-four*) and lands in the **Tag** as a prediction about next
Sunday. ⛔ **Thesis forbidden in the final chorus.**

**Mood arc.** Open in flat working cheer — a woman enjoying being good at something nobody watches.
Hold that exact register through the card. End in something that is not sadness and is not triumph:
the plain pleasure of knowing the room will do this again without her.

**FORM MAP**
| # | section | bars | sung lines | what enters or leaves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intro** | 8 | 0 | bars 1–4 **upright bass + wire brushes + piano** state the turnaround in full, five chord and all · bars 5–8 **three-piece horn section + twirling acoustic guitars** enter and state it a second time |
| 2 | **Verse 1** | 12 | 12 | vocal enters. **The turnaround at bars 17–20 arrives without the five chord — 0:38. The absence is taken here.** |
| 3 | **Chorus** | 12 | 8 | **handclaps** enter, dry and dead centre |
| 4 | **Verse 2** | 12 | 12 | **baritone sax** doubles the walking bass an octave down |
| 5 | **Chorus** | 12 | 8 | **stacked girl-group thirds** enter — the split moment is on the second *"in a hurry"* |
| 6 | **Verse 3 — WOLF** | 12 | 12 | nothing enters. **Tercet 2's hanging line gets no horn answer.** Bass, brushes, piano, guitar unchanged |
| 7 | **Verse 4** | 12 | 12 | **tambourine** enters on the offbeat; horns back to answering every hanging line |
| 8 | **Chorus** | 12 | 8 | everything, plus **piano block chords** |
| 9 | **Tag** | 8 | 8 | horns out, then guitars out, then piano out — **bass and brushes are last**, still walking the turnaround |

**Where the absence is STATED:** intro bars 1–8, twice, in full.
**Where it is REMOVED:** the turnaround of Verse 1, bars 17–20, **0:38**.
**Where it keeps being pointed at:** every twelve bars for the rest of the record, by the bass landing on
the fifth degree with nothing above it.
**Where the wolf lands:** Verse 3, tercet 2, hanging line — **unanswered by the horns**. It resolves in
tercet 3: she sets the card at its place, squares it to the fork, turns it to face out, and reads the
next table.
**Where the sung fact lands:** Verse 2, tercet 4 — *nineteen sixty-four* read off the brass by the door
and **answered** (*so this room has done all this before*), never recited.

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS** *(three appearances, unchanged, no ad-lib, no octave lift, no added run)*
```
Here's where everybody sits.
Here's the glass. Here's the name.
I did not do this in a hurry.
Here's where everybody sits.
Here's the door. Here's the band.
Here's the coats. Here's the cake.
I did not do this in a hurry.
Here's where everybody sits.
```
**Hook function check:** *"Here's where everybody sits"* — six syllables, an act of placement not a
feeling, sung on three notes inside a fifth, chantable out of breath. **It does not carry the thesis.**

**THE HUM TEST.** The horn shape is a **three-note rising answer figure** stated in the intro's second
turnaround, played after every hanging line, and played alone in the last eight bars. It exists before
the vocal enters and after it leaves.

# GUIDE 2 · **V2 — THE ROOM AT FOUR**
### *facet 5 — Cohesion by timbre, not by gear* · **key F** · 102 BPM

**The angle.** The hour before. Doors shut, nobody in yet, the shoebox of cards on a chair. The band is
soundchecking one line over and over. Nothing has happened. **That is the best hour of the day and she
says so.**

**Storytelling technique.** *The room described as equipment.* Objects first, people second — the stack,
the carpet, the brass rail, the shutter — then the four people who are in the room and what each of them
is doing to it. The reveal engine arrives as **Gwen bowing to an empty room** and lands in the **Outro**:
somebody took these chairs down before any of us. ⛔ **Thesis forbidden in the final chorus.**

**Mood arc.** Private, unhurried, faintly greedy — a woman with a room to herself for one more hour.
The card interrupts nothing. The doors open at the end and she calls that the win.

**FORM MAP**
| # | section | bars | sung lines | what enters or leaves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intro** | 8 | 0 | bars 1–4 **piano alone** states the turnaround in full · bars 5–8 **upright bass** joins and states it a second time. Brushes are not in yet |
| 2 | **Verse 1** | 12 | 12 | **wire brushes** enter on the vocal. **One trumpet alone answers the hanging lines** — the section does not exist yet. **The turnaround at bars 17–20 has no five chord — 0:38** |
| 3 | **Verse 2** | 12 | 12 | **twirling acoustic guitars** enter |
| 4 | **Chorus** | 12 | 8 | **the full horn section** enters for the first time in the record |
| 5 | **Verse 3 — WOLF** | 12 | 12 | nothing enters. **Tercet 2's hanging line gets no answer.** Rhythm section unchanged |
| 6 | **Chorus** | 12 | 8 | **handclaps** + **stacked thirds**; the split lands on *"in here yet"* |
| 7 | **Horn Break** | 12 | 0 | horns and guitars trade four bars each over the unchanged groove |
| 8 | **Verse 4** | 12 | 12 | **tambourine** enters; horns answering every hanging line again |
| 9 | **Chorus** | 12 | 8 | everything |
| 10 | **Outro** | 8 | 6 | section out, guitars out, **one trumpet plays the soundcheck line alone over the bass**, to nobody |

**Where the absence is STATED:** intro bars 1–8, piano then piano-and-bass, twice, in full.
**Where it is REMOVED:** the turnaround of Verse 1, bars 17–20, **0:38**.
**Where it keeps being pointed at:** the bass climbs to the fifth degree at every turnaround for the rest
of the song; in the Horn Break it does it four times in a row with nothing over it at all.
**Where the wolf lands:** Verse 3, tercet 2 — of every card in the box she does **that one first**. The
hanging line *"So I do it first."* gets **no horn answer**. It resolves in tercet 3: the card stands on
the cloth by the door, glass set beside it, and she does the rest of the box.
**Where the sung fact lands:** Verse 2, tercet 4 — *nineteen sixty-four* under the worn brass rail,
**answered** (*so it was worn like that before any of us*).

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS** *(three appearances, unchanged)*
```
Take the chairs down. Take them down.
The light comes in low and gold.
Nothing has happened in here yet.
Take the chairs down. Take them down.
It smells of carpet and hot bulbs.
The bar is cold. The stage is bare.
Nothing has happened in here yet.
Take the chairs down. Take them down.
```
**Hook function check:** *"Take the chairs down"* — four syllables, an imperative about furniture, the
flattest possible sentence, and the room can shout it back the first time it hears it.

**THE HUM TEST.** ⭐ The instrumental hook of this variation is **Gwen's soundcheck line** — a short
descending trumpet phrase heard first behind Verse 1, developed in the Horn Break, and played **alone,
last, into an empty room** in the Outro. It is the tune of the song and the voice never sings it.

# GUIDE 3 · **V3 — THE FIRST DANCE**
### *facet 4 — The card, and what she does with it* · **key E-flat** · 102 BPM

**The angle.** Nine o'clock, plates cleared, floor emptied on purpose. A record goes on that somebody
chose months ago in a kitchen with a pen — and that somebody is the name on the card.

**Storytelling technique.** *Hook first.* The chorus opens the song, because at a first dance the
instruction is the first thing anybody hears. Verses are the craft that made the moment possible: how the
records were carried, how the speakers were placed, how the needle was dropped early so the entry lands
straight. The reveal engine lands in the **Bridge**: the choice outlives the chooser and the floor is
still full. ⛔ **Thesis forbidden in the final chorus.**

**Mood arc.** Communal, warm, slightly bossy. The bridge is the only place the room goes quiet — **and it
is the ROOM that goes quiet, not the band.** The band plays straight through it, and a laugh puts it back.

**FORM MAP**
| # | section | bars | sung lines | what enters or leaves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intro** | 8 | 0 | **horns state the turnaround twice, in unison, dry**, over bass and brushes — the loudest the horns get all record |
| 2 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | vocal enters straight onto the hook. **The turnaround at bars 17–20 has no five chord — 0:38** |
| 3 | **Verse 1** | 12 | 12 | **twirling acoustic guitars** enter |
| 4 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | **handclaps** enter |
| 5 | **Verse 2** | 12 | 12 | **electric piano** enters — the sound of the record being played inside the record |
| 6 | **Bridge — WOLF** | 12 | 12 | nothing enters. **Tercet 1's hanging line gets no horn answer.** Groove unchanged |
| 7 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | **stacked thirds**; the split opens on *"off the floor"* |
| 8 | **Verse 3** | 12 | 12 | **baritone sax** enters under the bass |
| 9 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | everything plus **tambourine** |
| 10 | **Tag** | 8 | 6 | horns out, then everything but the **twirling guitars**, still circling the chord nobody plays |

**Where the absence is STATED:** intro bars 1–8, horns in unison, twice, in full.
**Where it is REMOVED:** the turnaround of the first chorus, bars 17–20, **0:38**.
**Where it keeps being pointed at:** the bass at every turnaround; and in the Tag the guitars are left
alone circling the missing chord's notes with nothing under them.
**Where the wolf lands:** Bridge, tercet 1 — the hanging line **"That was Nell."** gets **no horn
answer**. It resolves in tercet 2: she takes the card off the cloth, holds it while she stands, puts it
in her shirt pocket **and dances.**
**Where the sung fact lands:** Verse 2, tercet 1 — the record they picked was cut in *nineteen sixty-four*
and it is **answered** (*nobody dancing to it now was born before*).

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS** *(four appearances, unchanged)*
```
Everybody off the floor.
All of us around the edge.
Just them, and nobody else.
Everybody off the floor.
Somebody get the lights.
Everybody off the floor.
```
**Hook function check:** *"Everybody off the floor"* — seven syllables, an instruction shouted in real
function rooms, and ⭐ **it is the seed's own absence performed socially**: a hole made deliberately in
the middle of a crowd. It states no thesis.

**THE HUM TEST.** The unison horn figure from the intro is the instrumental hook; it answers every hanging
line and it is the last thing the horns play before the Tag hands the tune to the guitars.

# GUIDE 4 · **V4 — THE CLEAR-UP**
### *facet 3 — The answer that does not come* · **key C** · 102 BPM

**The angle.** One in the morning. The party is over and the room is being turned back into a room. The
list is now a list of **what is left on the tables**, and it is still praise.

**Storytelling technique.** *Inventory in reverse.* Verse 1 is objects with no owner in the room; Verse 2
is the people clearing them; Verse 3 is the band packing; Verse 4 is the furniture going back and the
switch plate. ⭐ **The wolf lands early — Verse 2 — so that two thirds of the song happens after it**,
which is the seed's *the list continues, with the missing thing now inside it* made structural. The
reveal engine lands in **Verse 4**: the room is booked again on Thursday and somebody else will do the
cards. ⛔ **Thesis forbidden in the final chorus.**

**Mood arc.** Tired, funny, entirely unsentimental. ⭐ **The record's loudest, fullest moment is the
INTRO** — a full band statement of a turnaround for a party that has already finished. Everything after
it is subtraction, and the subtraction is timbral, one instrument at a time, never a fade.

**FORM MAP**
| # | section | bars | sung lines | what enters or leaves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intro** | 8 | 0 | **full band** — bass, brushes, piano, guitars, three horns — states the turnaround **twice, in full**, at the record's loudest |
| 2 | **Verse 1** | 12 | 12 | horns thin to **one trombone** answering the hanging lines. **The turnaround at bars 17–20 has no five chord — 0:38** |
| 3 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | **handclaps** enter |
| 4 | **Verse 2 — WOLF** | 12 | 12 | nothing enters. **Tercet 3's hanging line gets no trombone answer.** Bass, brushes, piano, guitars unchanged |
| 5 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | **stacked thirds**; the split opens on *"out you go"* |
| 6 | **Verse 3** | 12 | 12 | **the other two horns rejoin** — the section is whole again for the first time since the intro |
| 7 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | **tambourine** enters |
| 8 | **Verse 4** | 12 | 12 | **piano goes to block chords**; guitars drop to single notes |
| 9 | **Chorus** | 12 | 6 | everything, last time |
| 10 | **Coda** | 8 | 6 | ⭐ instruments leave **one at a time and audibly**: horns, tambourine, handclaps, guitars, piano, brushes — **the upright bass is the last thing in the record, still walking the turnaround under nothing** |

**Where the absence is STATED:** intro bars 1–8, full band, twice, in full — the only time this record is
at full strength.
**Where it is REMOVED:** the turnaround of Verse 1, bars 17–20, **0:38**.
**Where it keeps being pointed at:** every turnaround; and ⭐ **in the Coda the bass walks it alone**, so
the last four bars of the record are the root motion of a chord nobody plays. That is C1 made audible with
nothing to hide behind.
**Where the wolf lands:** Verse 2, tercet 3 — the hanging line **"It says Nell."** gets **no trombone
answer**. It resolves in tercet 4: she stands the card back up on the bare table where the cloth has been
pulled off, nothing either side of it, and goes to get the chairs.
**Where the sung fact lands:** Verse 4, tercet 2 — *nineteen sixty-four* on the switch plate by the door,
**answered** (*and it's booked again on Thursday*).

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS** *(four appearances, unchanged)*
```
Lights on, and out you go.
Coats off the rail. Cake in a box.
Say it to me at the door.
Lights on, and out you go.
Somebody's still dancing.
Lights on, and out you go.
```
**Hook function check:** *"Lights on, and out you go"* — six syllables, the flattest thing anybody says
in a function room at one in the morning, and it is affectionate because of how it is sung, not because
of what it says. No thesis.

**THE HUM TEST.** The instrumental hook is the **full-band turnaround itself**, stated at the top at full
strength and then dismantled instrument by instrument across the Coda — so the tune is present before the
first word and is the very last thing heard.

in all four variations of a heavily-specified block; the sweep exists because that failure is invisible
to a reader who wrote all four.
