## 1 — Package B — 95/100 — SHIP

The return is earned by action. Before the second exact chorus, the singer swings the inside wheel wide, flashes the shoulder cue, the addressee crosses the stripe, takes her line, and moves one wheel-width closer. Consequently, the unchanged chorus lines “One step to me / One step, just right” change truth-value: first they solicit; then they describe an accepted approach. The stopped tail pays that movement off with the other skater's wheels beside hers. This is causal dramaturgy, not a relabeled EMO header.

The short, stress-clean lines, hard consonants, internal returns, and recurring “eyes / nice” make the surface easy to learn after one hearing. “Look with your eyes” is semantically obvious on paper, but in context its clipped repetition becomes a teasing command and an exchange the listener can inhabit. Minor phrases such as “Your mouth shapes it nice” are less natural than the surrounding lyric, but they do not break the vocal run.

Production has a single legible seam: the bell clips, the third guitar chop disappears, and four dry brass notes accompany the decisive crossing. Those jobs reinforce the event and should survive generation better than an abstract timbral wish-list. The roller-rink world is exact—wet wristband, lacquer, blue wheels, red stripe, shoulder cue—and the light emotional scale is honest: flirtation becomes chosen proximity rather than inflated transcendence. This is the only package here that clears the publish bar without needing the listener to forgive its mechanism.

## 2 — Package C — 81/100 — REPAIR

The song has the strongest large emotional consequence: perceived insignificance becomes relief and inclusion. The final return mutates “Make my little fear a weather pattern” into “I am not the center, I am included,” and the outro compresses that insight into a clean final state. The alpine specifics—blue snow, boot crunch, pines, zodiacal light, triple arch—give the feeling a real place rather than a generic starfield.

Its surface is less reliable. “Triple arch over me” is a strong chant, but “Turn my mouth to light and meter” and “I am smaller than serene” feel written rather than naturally sung. Several verse lines are essay sentences, especially the full 510-kilometers-per-second fact and the explanatory “It gives you beauty with numbers attached.” The lyric repeatedly interprets its own image instead of letting melody and return carry the understanding.

The production arc is coherent, but near-silence, alpine ambience, polyrhythmic pulse, glass sheen, cello undertow, starfield spread, and panoramic widening create more smoothing risk than B's one event-bearing seam. Separately, the lyrics omit the mandatory `[SONG FORM: ...]` line, so the package cannot pass as delivered. Repair should preserve the triple-arch return and final inclusion while recasting the longest explanatory lines into breath-sized images and adding the required form declaration.

## 3 — Package A — 74/100 — REPAIR

The kitchen scale is attractive and often specific: wet spoon, copper pipe, steam, tile dust, morning cup, and sink water are usable sensory anchors. “Look again, the world is lit” is concise, melodic, and clip-ready. The lyric also contains a few excellent turns, notably growing “less armored” and writing thank-you “on the back of panic.”

The decisive weakness is that the return does not materially change. The opening has already put dread down and discovered the lit world; verses, bridge, and final chorus then accumulate examples of the same conclusion. Opening the laboratory window is an action, but it does not create a new fact that alters the hook. The final expansion to “Every tired human listener / Gets written back in” asserts inclusion without dramatizing who was excluded or what happened to restore them.

Prosody is uneven: the hook and short breakdown sing easily, while the eight-minute sunlight sentence, “certainty has bad audio,” and self-description as a “disappointed idealist heart” expose authorial machinery. “Awe is everywhere you look,” “Every corner has a choir,” and “Every threshold has a hymn” flatten distinct sink-water perception into generalized inspiration. The prompt is renderable, but its many ornamental technologies—432 Hz, quantum bit-depth swells, noise-gate silence, blossom pads, code scratches—compete with the one material that matters. Repair needs a bodily action with a cost or risk immediately before the final return, so “look again” discovers a changed relationship rather than another example of ambient wonder.
