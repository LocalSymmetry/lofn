# SUNNA R4 Daily — Blind Music QA, Stage 1

## Blindness statement

This ranking judges the 26 anonymous packages only for publish-level song force: first-listen hook and pleasure, vocal singability/prosody, returns, formal consequence, specificity, production survivability, distinctiveness, emotional afterimage, and whether the prompt/lyric relationship is causal rather than decorative. Package roles remain unknown. No candidate/golden/decoy identity is assumed, and calibration is pending reveal.

Scores use a 100-point publish bar. `SHIP` means unambiguously publishable on this textual package; `HOLD` means strong but not yet an unqualified yes; `REPAIR` means a concrete revision could cross the bar; `FAIL` means the song's governing idea or surface needs replacement rather than a local polish.

## Strongest and weakest

- **Strongest: G.** Its hook, comic scene, bodily actions, production cues, and character decision are the same mechanism. Removing the production still leaves a sharp song; adding the production makes the giant-prize turn hit harder.
- **Weakest: P.** It has a renderable groove but no sufficiently singular human event, transformation, or natural title phrase. Its competence is the problem: almost every line could move to another glossy flirt package unchanged.

## Substantive flags and required repairs

1. **Pool-level distinctiveness collision.** Six anchor systems recur across four labels each: `SCAN ME TWICE` (D/H/I/V), `PRETTY ON PURPOSE` (G/M/N/W), `High Tide, High Gloss` (B/E/O/Q), `Kiss the receipt` (C/S/T/Z), `Look with your eyes` (F/J/P/U), and `Too bright to keep` (K/R/X/Y). Several individual songs are strong, but publishing multiple same-anchor packages would make the set feel templated. The reveal must not erase this blind first-listen fact.
2. **Title/hook split.** E, O, and Q require a single listener-facing identity; their printed titles are not the repeated melodic anchor. Z also has a milder two-hook conflict. Retitle or rewrite the detonations, then re-judge sing-back.
3. **Static exact-return risk.** J, U, F, and P repeat long choruses without enough semantic change. Preserve the anchor, but let one or two surrounding actions mutate after the seam so repetition becomes return rather than copy.
4. **Artificial antagonist risk.** C, S, T, and Z rely on an unexplained square/bar/line. T survives because the wash physically removes it and changes “fold” to “keep”; C/S/Z need clearer first-listen stakes or a less pseudo-mysterious obstruction.
5. **Sung production/procedure leakage.** X must remove “My lead stays one woman / My bass keeps its route.” Y should replace “The horn drops three / I sing that note” with bodily or relational action. D, I, and V should reduce access-level/body-inventory scaffolding where it calls attention to design rather than desire.
6. **Package/prosody repair.** L needs the mandatory song-form header and a singable recut of the long scientific/expository lines. The numerical solar-wind sentence should become image or rhythm, not a fact recital.
7. **Fundamental rewrites.** A needs to stop announcing awe and let one ordinary action transform; P needs a new natural-language hook and a scene with an irreversible or revealing choice. Both are below the publish bar at the concept/surface level.
