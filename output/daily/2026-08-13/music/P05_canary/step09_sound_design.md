# STEP 09 — SOUND DESIGN · P05 · **CANARY**
**Bed (assigned, heavy 2 of 2):** **GLITCH-BAROQUE × BREAKCORE** — AWE and INDIGNATION as **one bed**,
in the same bar, ⛔ never alternating.

---

## 1. THE COLLISION RULE — how the two modes share a bar

Most of the archive's schism songs put AWE in the verse and INDIGNATION in the chorus and *cut between
them*. That is alternation, and this pair was assigned the other thing.

> **AWE owns the pitched material and the voice. INDIGNATION owns the transient material and the floor.
> They run simultaneously and neither one is ever acknowledged by the other.**

- The **harpsichord ground bass** is continuous from bar one to the last bar. It never stops for a drop
  and it never gets filtered for a build. It is the room.
- The **breakcore** enters *across* it — ⛔ never at a phrase boundary. Every break edit lands **mid-bar,
  on the second sixteenth of beat 2 or beat 4**, so it always sounds like an interruption rather than an
  arrangement.
- ⭐ **The singer never reacts.** No line changes delivery because the floor got violent. That
  unacknowledged coexistence *is* the collision; a vocal that snarled back would collapse it into
  alternation again.

## 2. ⭐ SIGNATURE 1 — **THE TICKET ENGINE** *(and it is the hi-hat)*

The entire high percussion layer is **paperwork**. A clipboard spring click, a ledger page turning, a
rubber date-stamp hitting an ink pad and then hitting paper, and the metal snap of a bulldog clip —
recorded close, chopped to thirty-second notes, gated hard, **panned narrow (±15°) and kept dry.**

- The **stamp** is the hat. The **clip snap** is the ghost note. The **page turn** is the open hat.
- ⚠️ Deliberately **narrow, not hard-panned** — L22 THE GRAIN LAW: hard-panned non-musical elements get
  smoothed away by the generator. Narrow and dry survives; wide and clever does not.
- The reason it is not decoration: **the certainty in this song is administrative.** The thing that keeps
  time is the thing that got the name wrong.

## 3. ⭐ SIGNATURE 2 — **THE VAT SUB**

A **superhumanly funky bass, furiously slapping and popping** against a baroque ground bass — a slapped
thumb and a popped octave sitting *inside* a walking figure that has no business being funked.

- **Mono below sixty hertz**, chest-frequency, the only element allowed under the harpsichord.
- ⭐ It **ducks on her inhale, not on the kick.** Sidechain trigger is the vocal breath. The floor gets
  out of the way for the lungs — which is the song.
- Depth of the vat is written into the low end: the sub is **wide-open in Chorus A** (the room you could
  fall into) and **shortened to a two-hundred-millisecond thud in Chorus B** (two inches of water on
  concrete). Same note. Different volume of air.

## 4. THE SECOND EYE — the sonic half *(the lyric half is in Step 07 §2)*

⛔ L22: a Somatic objection answered in the production spec is **not answered**. THE SECOND EYE is
carried by the lyric and the form first — this is only its reinforcement.

| | Chorus A — **direct** | Chorus B — **reflected** |
|---|---|---|
| register | vocal at pitch | ⭐ **the same melody an octave down** (the HINGE) |
| reverb | a small dark plate, pre-delay 8 ms — a room with a lid | a long bright slap off wet concrete, single repeat |
| sub | open, sustained | short, thudded |
| breaks | dense | thinned to a rim and the valve |
| air above 8 kHz | rolled off — nothing to see | opened — daylight |

**Chorus C:** both at once, **the low voice one beat late for the entire section**, so the two never lock.
⛔ Not a merge. The stagger is the non-resolution, and it is the reason it must be performed rather than
copy-pasted.

## 5. ⭐ THE BOLD CHOICE (not a glitch effect) — **THE RESPIRATOR**

The last chorus is sung **through a rubber half-mask respirator**, close-mic'd, **with no processing at
all.**

- Consonants get eaten; the vowels stay perfectly in tune. It sounds like someone singing beautifully
  from inside a small rubber room.
- ⭐ **The exhale valve is left in the mix and becomes the hi-hat** for that section — the Ticket Engine
  hands off to a lung.
- It puts a mask in the room without one line mentioning one, and it is a **physical object between mouth
  and microphone**, not a plugin. In a glitch lane, that is the only kind of bold that still counts.

## 6. VOCAL

Female, late twenties, mezzo. **Crystalline and breathy with audible inhales**, devotional, unhurried,
sung slightly behind the beat all song. The Indignation flip (bratty, compressed snarl) is held back to
**a single word** — the gang answer *"hard"* at the end of the pre-chorus rebound — and never returns.
⭐ Restraint is the point: the indignation lives in the floor, so the voice keeps the awe.

- Gang answers: adult female stack, four voices, close and unglamorous — the floor saying the name back.
- ⛔ No autotune. ⛔ No children. ⛔ No male lead.

## 8. WHAT RUNS **WITH** THE GENERATOR (L22 THE GRAIN LAW, render-measured)

**Specs that survive — kept:**
- open quiet (intro is ground bass alone), build the sub, **thicken the last chorus** (the stack is three
  choruses deep and the last is the densest)
- narrow-and-dry percussion, mono sub, a continuous pitched bed under everything
- a vocal instruction stated as a *performance* ("sung through a respirator"), not as an effect chain

**Specs that get smoothed — refused:**
- ⛔ no long full stops (the bridge drops the drums but the consort keeps playing — the room never dies)
- ⛔ no untuned drones · ⛔ no hard-panned non-musical elements
- ⛔ the octave drop is written **in the lyric field as a section instruction**, not left to the mix

⭐ **And if the generator deviates, the deviation is raw material.** If the respirator reads as a lo-fi
filter, that is still a mask; if the consort locks to the grid, that is a different and possibly better
seam. Judge the result, not the distance from intent.

## 9. NEGATIVE LIST (ships in the exclude field)
`trap hi-hats, EDM risers, autotune, male lead vocal, children's voices, orchestral swell endings,
fade-out, hard-panned percussion, untuned drone, sub-bass sidechained to the kick`
