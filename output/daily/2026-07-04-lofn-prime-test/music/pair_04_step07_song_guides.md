### The shared spine (identical across all four; the DIVERGENCE is scene + fuse + structure)

- **Emotion path:** contempt/indignation at the *counting* → the sub-fuse converts heat to weight → lament (the voice de-glitches into plainness, says a name it doesn't have).
- **Source-3 mandatory:** a dry surface SNAP (small, too-close) answered ~3 seconds later by a DEEP sub-fuse (felt before heard); the drop is the fuse detonating, never the snap.

### VARIANT 1 — "The Weather App Said Feels Like" — the kitchen phone
**Angle:** the most banal possible interface for a death: a weather notification. A person stands at a hot kitchen counter reading the app's "feels like" number, half-annoyed. The fact arrives *where a temperature should be* — the app, absurdly, is also the place a count of the dead is scrolling past. INDIGNATION at the interface that treats a body like a high of the day.
- **Ordinary scene / object-as-fuse:** phone face-up on a counter; a glass of water going warm. SNAP = the notification chime (dry, bright, too-close). Sub-fuse = the silence under the chime where the meaning drops 3 seconds late.
- **Micro-structure:** verse (snarl, reading the app) → pre (glitch swells, the banner won't stop) → the chime-snap → 3-sec fuse → drop (the number, once) → recontextualized verse (the same glass, now unbearable) → lament outro (spoken to the person the notification was actually about).
- **Genres:** HyperRaaga glitchcore (160) + digital-hardcore glitch-pop + ambient drone. **Signatures:** (1) *Notification-Gamaka* — the OS chime pitch-bent down a microtone and looped until it sours; (2) *Feels-Like Sub-Fuse* — a mono 40Hz that arrives 3s after each chime, sidechained to the kitchen room-tone, not the kick.
- **Vocal:** bratty-glitched reading the app; clears to crystalline breath at the lament.
- **Hook (15–30s):** "It said *feels like* — feels like nothing at all." (adoptable, survives a 9:16 clip).
- **Emotion tags (from taxonomy):** Contempt / Disillusionment → Sorrow (Mourning).
- **One number:** at the drop only — the count read once, then answered "that's someone's whole warm morning."

### VARIANT 2 — "Cooling Center, Row F" — the empty folding chair
**Angle:** institutional relief that arrived, but not for everyone. A public cooling center: rows of folding chairs, fluorescent hum, free water. One chair in Row F stays empty. The fact is the count of the people the rows didn't reach in time. INDIGNATION at a mercy that was *rationed*, then lament for the seat that stayed cold.
- **Ordinary scene / object-as-fuse:** a grid of metal folding chairs; a cup of water sweating on one. SNAP = a single chair leg scraping tile (dry, echoing). Sub-fuse = the room's HVAC drone dropping an octave 3s later.
- **Micro-structure:** through-ish MEASUREMENT with a counted refrain ("row A, row B…") that *should* reach everyone → the scrape-snap on Row F → fuse → drop (the number, once) → the rows re-counted, now as absences → lament (address to whoever should have sat in F).
- **Genres:** HyperRaaga glitchcore (150) + post-punk negative space + gospel-adjacent choir-pad. **Signatures:** (1) *Fluorescent-Raaga Drone* — a sargam fragment fed through a fluorescent-ballast buzz, microtonally detuned; (2) *Row-F Sub-Scrape* — the chair-scrape resampled into the sub, a 3s tail that becomes the drop's floor.
- **Vocal:** counting-clerk snarl on the roll-call; breaks to crystalline on the empty seat.
- **Hook:** "Row F, Row F — nobody came to Row F."
- **Emotion tags:** Indignation (Moral Outrage) / Frustration → Loneliness (Abandonment) → Sorrow.
- **One number:** at the drop — the count, answered "and one of them was Row F."

### VARIANT 3 — "The Cell Named After No One" — the spreadsheet clerk
**Angle:** the actual bureaucratic act — a data clerk enters the excess-death figure into a cell. The most literal MEASUREMENT: a life *becoming a number in a box*, live, on screen, and the system says "saved." INDIGNATION at the ease of the keystroke; lament that the cell will never know the name it stands for.
- **Ordinary scene / object-as-fuse:** an office at end of shift; a cursor blinking in an empty cell; the AC failing. SNAP = the Enter key (a single hard click). Sub-fuse = the workstation fan spinning up 3s late into a sub-rumble.
- **Micro-structure:** verse (AI code-scratch intro — the clerk's own logs as vinyl scratch; snarl narrates the data entry) → pre (the cursor won't stop blinking) → Enter-snap → fuse → drop (the number entered, once, "saved") → the same cell re-read as a person → lament (the clerk speaks TO the cell / the remainder). **Bit-depth swell used HERE**: hi-fi certainty of the saved figure collapses to 2-bit at the turn — certainty→doubt, serving count→prayer (NOT a genre hand-off).
- **Genres:** HyperRaaga glitchcore (155) + IDM/glitch + drone-ambient. **Signatures:** (1) *Sargam-Keystroke Lattice* — auto-tuned sargam syllables triggered by typing sounds, microtonal; (2) *Saved-State Collapse* — a quantum bit-depth swell keyed to the word "saved," hi-fi→2-bit→one beat of silence.
- **Vocal:** flat bureaucratic snarl entering data; de-glitches into breath at "the cell named after no one."
- **Hook:** "One row down, and the sheet said *saved*."
- **Emotion tags:** Numbness / Contempt → Regret → Sorrow (Grief).
- **One number:** at the drop — typed/sung once, answered "a cell that should have had a name."

### VARIANT 4 — "The Degree You Couldn't Turn" — the capped thermostat at 3am
**Angle:** the coldest possible cause: a thermostat locked by someone else's rule, at night, in a rented room, over a sleeping body. The fact is the count of the people whose one degree stayed where a rule set it. INDIGNATION at the *cap* — a decision that a life was worth less than a setting; lament for the body that didn't wake.
- **Ordinary scene / object-as-fuse:** a dark apartment, 3am; a thermostat with a locked cover reading one number; a fan pushing warm air. SNAP = the thermostat relay clicking (dry, mechanical, and it does nothing). Sub-fuse = the wall itself seeming to breathe out 3s later, a sub that never resolves.
- **Micro-structure:** slow verse (crystalline-tense, near the sleeping body) → snarl rises at the locked cover → the relay-snap that changes nothing → fuse → drop (the number, once, as the degree that couldn't move) → the room re-seen at dawn → lament (a prayer said to the one who slept, no name). The sub-fuse here is the LONGEST/most unresolving — Source-3 taken to its melancholy extreme.
- **Genres:** HyperRaaga glitchcore (150, half-time feel) + doom-adjacent sub-bass + Old-Norse phoneme choir (myth/memory sampling — a dead tongue as the prayer's texture, credited). **Signatures:** (1) *Locked-Cover Gamaka* — a microtonal bend that keeps trying to rise a semitone and is held down, mirroring the capped degree; (2) *3am Sub-Fuse* — a 3s-delayed 35Hz that swells under the sleeping-room tone and never lands a downbeat.
- **Vocal:** breath-tense → snarl at the lock → plain, cracked lament; a single Old-Norse phoneme figure as the wordless prayer.
- **Hook:** "One degree — one degree they wouldn't move."
- **Emotion tags:** Indignation → Helplessness (Powerlessness) → Sorrow (Heartache) → Compassion.
- **One number:** at the drop — the count, answered "one degree they wouldn't move, and one of them was you."
