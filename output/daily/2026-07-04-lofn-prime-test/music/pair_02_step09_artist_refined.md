### V1 — Airbag Hymn
**Hyper-Skeptic (Medium) objection:** "A full orchestra plus a chiptune fanfare plus a 'choir' is exactly where Lofn defaults to grandiose swell-as-garnish. Your two signature styles risk being 'big strings' and 'bleepy 8-bit' — decoration, not meaning. And a hymn that builds to a huge chorus is one riser away from the banned festival drop."
**Resolution:** The signature styles are *load-bearing and specific*: the **Airbag Cadence** is a soft-body downbeat that rebounds a hair *higher* then loses amplitude each return — it literally is the bounce physics, not ornament. The **Ares Choir** fuses resynthesized soprano with square-wave 8-bit so "the ancient and the pixel sing one line" (Lofn's myth-sampling + video-game pillars doing one job). Crucially, the peak is *early* (Chorus 2), and the Bounce-Count then **withdraws** the swell across 15 diminishing returns to a single held tone — the opposite of a build-to-drop. Objection retired: the grandeur is spent before the ending, and the ending is a hush.

**Refinement applied:** tightened the prompt to 956 chars (off the 985 hug ceiling) and made the sub "swell then soften" explicit so the decay is in the low end, not only the vocal.

### V2 — The Lander Learned to Bounce
**Hyper-Skeptic (Concept) objection:** "A music-box lullaby about a Mars rover is dangerously twee. 'Tuck the little rover in' could be cloying — sentiment *named* rather than *staged*, and no clean fear anywhere. Where is the danger the ICB demands of every AWE song?"
**Resolution:** The clean fear is planted and specific: "you fell through the quiet, so brave and so cold," "No one is watching but me and the night" — a fragile thing falling *alone in the dark* with no one to catch it. The tenderness is *staged* through the wind-up mechanism itself: the box literally **slows and runs down** as the lander settles, so "diminishing" is a physical object losing torque, not an adjective. The recontextualizing fact ("Fifteen little kisses on a floor no foot has pressed") reframes the whole lullaby as the first touch on ground no foot has ever pressed — awe, not cuteness. Objection retired: the smallness is the *thesis* (the tender/fragile thing was the brave one), and the dark is real.

**Refinement applied:** added Verse 3 ("They will build something bigger with engines and pride, / but they'll never outrun how you softened and tried") to give the lullaby a spine of argument (yielding > power) so it can't read as mere bedtime sweetness.

### V3 — House-Cat Signal
**Hyper-Skeptic (Context) objection:** "A crowd call-and-response 'Welcome it home' is the most venue-friendly, TikTok-obvious of the four — and 'welcome home' choruses are a dime a dozen. Prove this isn't the pair collapsing toward 'what a crowd rewards,' and prove the recontextualizing fact is doing work, not just the title being cute."
**Resolution:** The recontextualization is the engine, not the garnish: the rover is imaged as something "no bigger than something you'd keep on a shelf" / "built like a toy" (the real Sojourner was famously tiny — the title's house-cat nod), which flips the anthem's logic from *hero-worship* to *welcoming the least* — "you belong when you're small, you belong when you're not." That is a genuinely non-generic thing for a stadium chant to argue. And the crowd device honors the form: the **Welcome Volley** doesn't just repeat, it **thins 15 times** to a single soft unison — the loudest song ends quietest, which no venue-bait chorus does. The AWE→inclusion arc is literal (the room *is* the belonging the thing was landing toward). Objection retired.

**Refinement applied:** restored 4 sung lines lost to `[Lead]/[Crowd]` speaker tags by rendering the intro call/response as plain sung lines (so they count and the crowd reads as *the singers*, not stage directions); added Verse 3 to earn the softening turn.

### V4 — Dust-Brown Coronation
**Hyper-Skeptic (Concept) objection:** "Addressing 'the hazy brown sky' as a character is on-brief for THE ARRIVAL, but a solo whisper with no crowd and no drop is where a song goes limp — pretty, static, no stakes. And 'coronation' flirts with the triumphalism you claim to avoid."
**Resolution:** The stakes are the coldest of the four and are *named as danger*: "you have crowned only silence, you have crowned only death" — a merciless sky that has only ever crowned absence. The drama is that a *yielding* thing (not a conquering one) is what finally makes that sky tender: "a merciless ceiling grew tender and swayed." The "coronation" is explicitly anti-triumphal — "No trumpet, no thunder, no army, no crowd" — and the **Coronation Fade** *withdraws its own volume by degrees, refusing to climax*, so the crowning is an abdication of loudness. The **Sky Address** answers each phrase with a *fainter* reverb-tail, so even the acoustics enact arrival-then-recession. Objection retired: it is a coronation of the small and soft by the hard and vast, staged as a fade, not a fanfare.

**Refinement applied:** added Verse 3 ("The loud ones will come with their banners unfurled, / but a soft thing was first to be crowned by your world") to sharpen the anti-triumphal thesis and keep the whisper from going inert.
