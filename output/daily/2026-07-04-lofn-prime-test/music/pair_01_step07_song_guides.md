### VARIANT 1 — "Switchboard for the Fourth" (THE OPERATOR)
**Object holding the switchboard:** a human operator — one person patching a whole small town's Fourth together by hand (porch to street to field), call by call, until the sky pulls the last cord.
**Angle:** the switchboard is *labor and love* — someone is connecting the ritual by hand; the rupture is that person's cord being yanked from the jack.
**Verse architecture:** operator's-log couplets — each Section-A couplet patches ONE more connection ("porch three to the corner… the corner to the field"), the board lighting up line by line. This is the most call-and-response-dense variant (chosen as first-variant for maximal cross-pair distinctiveness).
**Rupture placement:** at the last patch — the moment the whole town is finally connected — the downbeat that would fire the celebration is withdrawn; the operator's board goes dark.
**Hook (15–30s):** "Patch me through, patch me through / the whole town's on the line" → after rupture becomes "nobody's on the line."
**Vocal:** tender operator's murmur (A) → snarl at the dead board (B) → the operator teaching the town to hum its own signal (C).
**One-fact hinge:** "a hundred degrees and the line went dead" — the temperature answers *why* the board died.
**SFX:** patch-cord click/plug; dial tone; the dial tone flatlining at rupture; a hand-clap grid in C.
**EMO path:** Anticipation/Eagerness/Wonder → Indignation/Contempt → Resolve/Solidarity/Hope.

### VARIANT 2 — "Countdown to Nothing" (THE ZERO THAT NEVER FIRES)
**Object holding the switchboard:** a crowd countdown — the switch is between the count (AWE) and the silence after zero (INDIGNATION); the downbeat is amputated exactly *at* zero.
**Angle:** the switchboard is *time itself* — the count patches expectation up toward a payoff; zero is the jack the cord is pulled from.
**Verse architecture:** descending count-structure — Section-A stanzas step down (a loose "ten… nine…" feel woven into the lines, not literally recited) tightening the pre-chorus, so the whole of A is one long lift toward a number. Distinct from V1's additive log: this is *subtractive*, a shrinking runway.
**Rupture placement:** on the beat where "zero" should detonate the fireworks — total silence, then the wrong genre (Baile Phonk indignation) floods the vacuum.
**Hook:** "Count it down, count it down / to the drop that never drops" → after rupture: "we counted down to nothing."
**Vocal:** breath-held crystalline countdown (A) → bratty "so where's the bang?" snarl (B) → the crowd counting *up* into its own making (C).
**One-fact hinge:** "zero, and a hundred degrees stood where the sky should be" — the number occupies the empty downbeat.
**SFX:** crowd inhale/hush; a single firework fuse-hiss that sputters and dies; heartbeat-metronome; clap grid in C.
**EMO path:** Suspense/Eagerness/Anticipation → Moral Outrage/Defiance → Determination/Hope.

### VARIANT 3 — "Two Stations" (THE BLOCK-PARTY PATCH-THROUGH)
**Object holding the switchboard:** a block-party sound system whose operator keeps cutting between two channels — the ritual station (music, joy) and the emergency-broadcast heat-alert station — the switchboard is the *dial* itself.
**Angle:** the switchboard is *the airwaves* — two stations fighting for the same speaker; the rupture is the ritual station being overridden by the alert.
**Verse architecture:** A/B channel-swap couplets — Section A alternates a warm music-station line with a thin, low-passed alert-station intrusion that grows each verse (the alert bleeding in), so the switch is *pre-figured* by static long before the rupture. Distinct from V1 (additive) and V2 (subtractive): this is *interleaved / two-track*.
**Rupture placement:** the alert station finally *wins the speaker* — the music cuts, the emergency tone takes the downbeat; Baile Phonk is the crowd's furious answer to the override.
**Hook:** "Turn it up, turn it up / keep it on our station" → after rupture: "they took our station."
**Vocal:** warm DJ-croon (A) with a robotic alert-voice intrusion → snarl at the override (B) → the block choosing a third frequency, humming, in C.
**One-fact hinge:** "a hundred degrees came through on every channel" — the number is what's on the station that won.
**SFX:** radio-tuning static/squelch; EAS-style alert tone (stylized, not the real signal); vinyl scratch; hand-clap + hummed-choir grid in C.
**EMO path:** Warmth/Fascination/Zeal → Indignation/Contempt/Defiance → Solidarity/Compassion/Resolve.

### VARIANT 4 — "Breaker Box" (THE GRID BROWNS OUT)
**Object holding the switchboard:** the literal electrical switchboard — a neighborhood breaker box / grid browning out as every AC unit pulls at once, so the very power that would light the celebration is spent keeping bodies alive indoors.
**Angle:** the switchboard is *the grid* — a physical panel of breakers; the rupture is a breaker tripping / the lights browning down; the cruel irony that the heat both cancels the party AND drains the power that party needed.
**Verse architecture:** rise-and-dim strophes — each Section-A strophe builds brightness (more lights, more current) then dims slightly at its tail (a flicker), the grid straining harder each time, until it can't. Distinct from the other three: an *oscillating brighten/flicker* shape, not additive/subtractive/interleaved.
**Rupture placement:** the main breaker trips at the moment of peak load — the downbeat browns out to 2-bit and gutters; Baile Phonk runs on the emergency circuit (indignation as the only thing still powered).
**Hook:** "Light it up, light it up / the whole grid's gonna sing" → after rupture: "the whole grid went down."
**Vocal:** hushed awe at the lit street (A) → snarl at the dark (B) → the block lighting hand-held sparklers / phone-lights, a human grid, in C.
**One-fact hinge:** "a hundred degrees pulled the last amp from the line" — the number is the load that tripped it.
**SFX:** electrical hum/60-cycle buzz; a breaker's hard clack; power-down whir/pitch-drop; sparkler crackle + clap grid in C.
**EMO path:** Wonder/Anticipation/Eagerness → Frustration/Indignation/Defiance → Hope/Solidarity/Determination.
