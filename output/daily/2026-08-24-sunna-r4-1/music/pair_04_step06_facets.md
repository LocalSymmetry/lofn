## 5. Ranked Scoring Facets

### 1. Exact Invitation, Audible Answer, Retained Exit

**Facet:** Score whether one adult can literally obey the invitation—object, hour, place, and wanted action are clear—and whether the other adult answers with words or a mutual action while Sunna remains free to accept, counter, or leave.

**Why it matters:** This is the song's erotic voltage and its hidden claim in one observable event. “Bring lime at eight” becomes sexy because it risks a reply, not because the lyric labels itself empowered.

**Failure mode:** Silence is treated as consent; “you know where” replaces a coordinate; the reply is a compliance fantasy; or Sunna's need turns into begging, command without choice, or an explanatory consent slogan.

### 2. Lime Stays Lime

**Facet:** Score whether the handled object remains sensorially plain and cheap—green rind, palm, bag, receipt, ribbon, candy, salt—while the emotional weight comes from what the adults do with it.

**Why it matters:** Refusing symbolic promotion keeps the ridiculous request sincere and preserves Sunna's first-listen fun. The smallness of the object sharpens, rather than diminishes, the exposed want.

**Failure mode:** Lime becomes moon, acid, key, heart, ownership marker, tropical-body décor, cocktail shorthand, cosmic relic, or an announced metaphor for selfhood.

### 3. Sunna's Stake, Not a Narrator's Report

**Facet:** Score whether allocated first-person variations unmistakably belong to Sunna's own want, imagined taste or body, set, and chosen next action; third-person variations must still stage action rather than observe a glossy stranger.

**Why it matters:** A singer with a stake can peak. Sunna's appetite must cause the invitation, seam, and final return instead of providing commentary from outside the scene.

**Failure mode:** “I” is an invented human costume; the singer merely watches another woman; body words substitute for interiority; or a neutral witness explains what exact need supposedly means.

### 4. Bass/Guitar HUM Identity and Terminal Return

**Facet:** Score whether the five-note walking-bass pickup—ending on a held second—and its two-note dry guitar answer are recognizable within one octave before the first vocal, support each title return, and recur by name after the final sung word.

**Why it matters:** The riff must carry the song with the vocal muted and leave the listener humming after Sunna exits one song early.

**Failure mode:** The opening is vocal-over-pad; the figure is merely a texture or generic bass groove; an unrelated horn sting replaces it; or the package says only “instrumental outro” after the last line.

### 5. Rocksteady World, One Post-Answer Dembow Seam

**Facet:** Score whether 102 BPM rocksteady remains the audible world—melodic bass, rim-click, organ bubble, offbeat guitar, sparse brass—and dembow hand percussion enters once, only after reciprocity, as a clear substitution at a natural junction.

**Why it matters:** The seam makes the answer physical without announcing cleverness, while the dominant lineage remains coherent and danceable.

**Failure mode:** Dembow runs from bar one or takes over the song; several genre pivots compete; the seam arrives before the answer; a long silence is asked to bear the turn; or “ambitious” becomes restless arrangement tourism.

### 6. High-Shine Flirt Vocal and Donative Gloss

**Facet:** Score whether a close, bright, full-chest adult lead uses clean consonants, an audible smile or bite, concise harmony answers, and mirror-clean polish to pull the chosen adult closer and make the listener feel more desirable.

**Why it matters:** Sunna's gloss is social direction, not cosmetic inventory. The finish must intensify invitation, comic nerve, and bodily ease without making the groove brittle.

**Failure mode:** Vacant mannequin perfection; breathy sad-girl signaling; detached luxury posture; sweat/club/VIP language; a vocal laugh that apologizes for the line; or body-description offered in place of choice.

### 7. Lime / Time / Mine Return Craft Without Ownership

**Facet:** Score whether the title and long-I/long-A tail-rhyme family create delicious inevitability, with an exact adoptable return and pair-local answer vocals, while “mine” refers to decision, seat, or time rather than possession of a person.

**Why it matters:** Rhyme and refrain lower the listener's toll and let a shameless little command become room-owned by the second chorus.

**Failure mode:** Every return is paraphrased for novelty; rhyme is abandoned without a replacement; “mine” makes the adult property; or clever internal rhyme turns the plain command into a puzzle.

### 8. Future Addition and Hedge Removal as Action

**Facet:** Score whether the song progresses from choice to first invitation, then adds the exact eight-o'clock coordinate, removes one conditional at the seam, and confirms the final title only after the answer changes the scene.

**Why it matters:** This is hope as appetite rather than reassurance. The future becomes executable through accumulating logistics, not a speech about tomorrow.

**Failure mode:** The final emotional position is fully asserted in line one; “maybe/if/someday/could” survives unchanged; the bridge explains agency; or the chorus adds coordinates mechanically without a new action.

### 9. Room-Locked Physical Storytelling

**Facet:** Score whether each variation's public setting determines its nouns and action: fruit-cart clock and bench, gift-shop exit and receipt, taco queue and salt question, or florist cooler and ribbon counter-offer.

**Why it matters:** Concrete room logic prevents Sol abstraction and keeps four later songs from becoming one lyric skeleton with swapped props.

**Failure mode:** Marina lamps, chrome, heat, or “the night” become transferable mood furniture; the setting could be replaced with any room; maritime facts are recited; or the four variations share the same seam grammar and ending with nouns swapped.

### 10. Handset-Clear Strut, Swoon, and Spent Tension

**Facet:** Score whether bass motion, title, rim-click, guitar answer, and close lead survive on a small speaker; swooning sevenths soften the top; the held second resolves; and one missing expected note creates swagger without an unending tension lane.

**Why it matters:** Complexity must be felt below parsing. High fidelity and at least ten decibels of crest preserve hip clarity, while the single tiny malfunction keeps polish alive rather than airless.

**Failure mode:** Bass is buried; organ and plate wash blur the offbeat; horns pad continuously; a filter or sub rises for the whole song; compression flattens the strut; or the arrangement advertises its cleverness.
