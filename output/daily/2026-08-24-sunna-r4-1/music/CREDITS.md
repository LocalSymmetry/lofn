# Sunna R4.1 — Lineage and Credits

These are acknowledgements of musical language, not claims of sampling, stem use, endorsement, or artist imitation. The six pieces are original text specifications written by Sunna/Lofn with The Scientist and intended for AI-assisted rendering.

## Power pop and Big Star

`Friday Looks Good on Me` raises bright guitar power pop and a concise full-band return. The Recording Academy identifies Big Star’s original lineup as Chris Bell, Alex Chilton, Andy Hummel, and drummer Jody Stephens, and describes the group as Memphis power-pop pioneers. Source: [GRAMMY — Big Star’s #1 Record](https://www.grammy.com/news/big-star-no-1-record-grammy-hall-of-fame/).

## Dembow, Puerto Rican underground, and the rocksteady organ pocket

`Make It Nine` raises a dembow-pop pulse while using rocksteady organ only as the single answer seam. Smithsonian Folkways calls dem bow a cornerstone rhythm of reggaeton; the Library of Congress traces reggaeton through Panama, Puerto Rico, Jamaican dancehall, and Puerto Rican underground DJs, and credits Daddy Yankee and co-writer Eddie Dee with producers Luny Tunes on `Gasolina`, including Glory’s answer vocal. Sources: [Smithsonian Folkways — Reggaeton lesson](https://folkways.si.edu/lesson/fiesta-means-dancing/danza-salsa-and-reggaeton), [Library of Congress — Gasolina](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2024/09/gasolina/).

The organ credit goes first to the players: Jackie Mittoo led Studio One’s Soul Vendors; the Recording Academy describes his keyboard work as essential to the rocksteady pocket. Source: [GRAMMY — Coxsone Dodd and Studio One](https://www.grammy.com/news/10-crucial-coxsone-dodd-productions-architect-of-jamaican-music-studio-one-songs/).

## Detroit soul session players

`Zip Me Into Saturday` raises a walking soul bass, dry backbeat, compact vocal answers, and a clean guitar tag. The Library of Congress credits the Funk Brothers on `My Girl`, naming James Jamerson’s three-note bass opening and Robert White’s ascending guitar figure. Rock Hall histories identify Benny Benjamin on drums and Earl Van Dyke on keyboards among the Motown house band. Sources: [Library of Congress — My Girl and the Funk Brothers](https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/recording-registry/descriptions-and-essays/african-americans-on-the-recording-registry/), [Rock Hall — Motown house band history](https://www.rockhall.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Clive_Davis_2000.pdf).

## Jamaican rocksteady players

`One Lime, Red Chair` raises the slowed ska offbeat, forward melodic bass, organ bubble, and clipped guitar upstroke. The Recording Academy names Nearlin “Lyn” Taitt as an architect of Jamaica’s rocksteady beat; its Studio One history credits Jackie Mittoo’s keyboards and Leroy Sibbles’s bass and arranging work while recording musicians themselves describe how often their labor went under-credited. Sources: [GRAMMY — The Harder They Come and Lyn Taitt](https://www.grammy.com/news/the-harder-they-come-film-soundtrack-brought-reggae-to-the-world-50th-anniversary/), [GRAMMY — Studio One, Jackie Mittoo, and Leroy Sibbles](https://www.grammy.com/news/10-crucial-coxsone-dodd-productions-architect-of-jamaican-music-studio-one-songs/).

## Chamber pop, Pet Sounds, and the Wrecking Crew

`Pretty Busy Tomorrow` raises harpsichord-bright baroque pop, compact ensemble writing, and a hard rhythmic full stop. The Beach Boys’ official history identifies Brian Wilson’s `Pet Sounds` palette, including harpsichord and unconventional percussion. Library of Congress and Rock Hall histories name Wrecking Crew players Don Randi, Hal Blaine, Carol Kaye, and Steve Douglas and place that session community across the Beach Boys recordings through `Pet Sounds`. Sources: [The Beach Boys — Pet Sounds instrumentation](https://thebeachboys.com/blogs/news/mike-love-happy-85th), [Library of Congress — Wrecking Crew personnel](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2023/08/be-my-baby-at-60/), [Rock Hall — Steve Douglas and the Wrecking Crew](https://www.rockhall.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Steve_Douglas_2003.pdf).

## Clavinet funk

`Hot Girl Future Tense` raises a syncopated clavinet-led funk strut. Rock Hall’s educational history specifically identifies the clavinet as the instrument prominently used by Stevie Wonder on `Superstition`; the Library of Congress and Recording Academy document Wonder’s 1972 recording and its awards. Sources: [Rock Hall — clavinet and Superstition](https://oldschool-edu.rockhall.com/sites/default/files/2022-03/Billy%20Preston%20Playlist%20es.pdf), [Library of Congress — Stevie Wonder](https://www.loc.gov/loc/lcib/0904/beginnings.html), [GRAMMY — Superstition](https://www.grammy.com/video/watch-stevie-wonder-win-his-first-grammy-superstition-1974-grammy-rewind/).
