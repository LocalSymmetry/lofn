## Sound and return

Before any lyric, write the pair's hummable instrumental figure. Keep it within one octave, name the instrument and contour, and place it before the first line and after the last. A texture, pad, isolated stab, generic horn hit, or “instrumental outro” does not satisfy the HUM test.

Every song owes returns. Use exact title repetitions, rhyme, consonance, answer vocals, instrumental recurrence, or a clearly named substitute. Exact chorus repetition requires no defense. Do not mutate a strong chorus merely to evade similarity. Verse rhyme may be removed only by naming what returns instead.

Use the source-side sonic vocabulary without imitation: **swooning seventh chords and lingering notes**, **delicious inevitability**, one close bright adult lead, one small malfunction that makes the performance feel chosen, and bass/drum clarity that survives a handset.

No identifiable real person. No child. No real grief. No real disaster, war scene, victim, or private biography. An invented adult may answer or occupy the world; their first-person voice does not count as Sunna-first-person.
