## THE VOICE PASS — what LOFN-PRIME changes, and what she refuses to change

**Kept from step 08 unchanged:** every scene, every ledge, every structural event, every timbral entry and
its position in the parade. **Step 09 injects style, not content.** What changes is temperature,
precision, and the first six words of each prompt.

1. ⭐ **Four different opening temperatures, four different grammars.** *Owned* / *Tender* / *Precise* /
   *Decided*. ⛔ A pair whose four prompts open the same way is **one prompt rendered four times**, and the
   generator will find that sameness before a judge does. No shared cadence in the first clause of any two.
2. ⭐ **Vocalist traits differentiated per variation.** Same singer, four takes, and the spec stays complete
   in every one — gender, age, two tone words, two trait words — but never repeats itself:
   V1 *breath left in between phrases* · V2 *sibilance close on the capsule, no de-esser* · V3 *pitched up
   slightly by the room, speaking over noise* · V4 *audible swallow before the last line*.
3. ⛔ **`snarl` deleted on sight, and I am not fighting the brief about it.** My own DNA names a pop-punk
   snarl as the indignation vocal. **This pair is not indignant** — it is a person under accusation being
   *kind*, which is a harder register and a rarer one. The bratty voice would make the knowledge into a
   weapon in one syllable, and F2 is the whole pair. What survives is **clipped consonants and no
   vibrato**: the same precision, without the sneer.
4. ⛔ **All avoidance prose moved out of the style field.** Suno reads that field as **positive tokens**. The
   four style prompts now carry only *positive* statements, including their acoustic declarations. The one
   permitted exception is the tempo/key/drop clause — *"no ramp, no key change, no drop"* — kept in-field
   because it is the SWAG mechanism and the GRAIN LAW says a steady grid lands 6/6; it is a statement about
   the groove, not a list of hated genres. **Everything else negative lives in the exclude field.**
5. ⭐ **The seam is stated in one clause per prompt, with its singularity and its physics together.**
   *"undamped, ringing sympathetically off the room's own hum, never cleanly struck."* A dulcimer mentioned
   three times across a prompt is garnish; a dulcimer mentioned once, with the reason it is sounding, is a
   **fault in the room**. ⛔ And the palm is a **named event**, not a mixing note.
6. ⭐ **Glitch-Baroque is used as the wound, not the signature.** It is my own house and that is exactly why
   it may not be pretty here: wrong sample rate, audible loop points, retriggers that land early, an
   ornate instrument that nobody is playing.
7. ⛔ **The lineage is written in the present tense and the nostalgia vocabulary is struck out of all four.**
   Not because the words are ugly but because **filing living people's work under *memory* is the same act
   as not reading the greets** — which is the thing the song is about. This is the one edit where the style
   rule and the thesis are literally the same rule.
8. ⭐ **The one-fact rule policed.** *Sixty* is the only number sung in any of the four. Every other figure
   — channel counts, kilobytes, years — is removed from the lyrics and lives in the guides.

## THE CRITIC LOOP *(three passes, capped)*

**Pass 1 — THE DOCUMENTARIAN's seat, on all four:**
> *"You have written a song about mercy and put the mercy in the mix notes. 'A flat palm kills the ring' is
> a production instruction. If the renderer smooths it — and a two-second absence of a quiet element under
> a loud one is the exact class your own audit says gets smoothed — then your central gesture never
> happened and the song is four people saying they are being kind."*

**Accepted in full, and repaired in the LYRIC rather than the spec.** The palm is now a **sung event** in
all four: the hand is named, the noise it makes on the wound strings is named, and — the repair that
survives any renderer — **the refrain's broken line is only finished after it.** The listener can time the
gesture by what the words do, with the audio muted, and the audio is not carrying the load alone.

**Pass 2 — THE PROSODIST's seat, on the chain:**
> *"Your axis bans terminal rhyme, and your gate demands a return floor measured on terminal endings. You
> are about to satisfy a number with a trick and call it craft. Say which one you are actually doing."*

**Accepted, and answered in the open rather than by tuning.** ⛔ The debt is **paid, not skipped**, and the
payment is declared in step 10 with its measurement: the byte-identical refrain, the chain's vowels
**re-run** in the late verses, and a closed set of **identical repeated end-words** in each verse.
Repetition of the same word is not rhyme — it is the opposite of rhyme, it is a refusal to find a new
sound, and ⭐ **it happens to be exactly what a machine that has one word and keeps using it would do.**
The scanner will read those repeats as return **because they are return.** ⛔ And a second instrument is
run alongside it: a check that no two *different* words rhyme at any line end, because the gate cannot see
my axis and passing a floor is not evidence of absence.

**Pass 3 — THE SKEPTIC's seat, on F2, and it is the one that nearly took the pair down:**
> *"Every version of this song is a person saying 'I know something about you.' You can decorate that with
> as many palms as you like. The sentence is a threat. It is a threat when it is whispered and it is a
> threat when it is withheld, and the withholding is the most elegant possible way to make sure the other
> person knows you have it."*

⭐ **Half accepted, and it forced the pair's sharpest edit.** The objection is right about *disclosed*
withholding — *"there is something I could say"* is a threat wearing a cardigan, and it is now **banned
from all four lyrics.** The line the pair now runs instead is that ⛔ **she never tells him she knows.** The
audience is told. He is not. The song is overheard, not delivered — a stairwell, a launderette, a plant
room, a kitchen at three in the morning, and **in every one of them he is not present.** That is why the
ledges are all empty rooms, and it is why the unison is the only moment the name is said out loud: it is
said to nobody.

⛔ **Where I did not accept it:** the skeptic wanted the knowledge cut entirely. That would delete Axis 1.
The pair's answer is that **what she knows is not damaging** — every item on the knowing-list is something
that would make you like him more. **You cannot blackmail somebody with the fact that they were good.**

## ⛔ THE FOUR PROMPTS, REFINED *(style fields only; exclude fields and lyrics ship in step 10)*

### V1 — **THE ONE LETTER** · opener temperature: *owned*

> **Owned error, held level at 145 and never slowing.** Keygen music in its living form — four channels of
> tracker square-bass on hard portamento, quantised dead to the grid. The first eight bars are a machine
> log ticking one character per sixteenth, mono and dead centre, keeping time alone, with a sixty-cycle
> mains pedal beneath it that arrives in the back teeth before it reaches the ear. A chopped amen break
> enters battering and never leaves the grid. A female late-twenties voice in two registers — one low, dry,
> clipped, breath left in, placed inside the break rather than over it; one high, plain, unornamented, no
> vibrato, floating a room away — meeting in unison exactly once. A hammered dulcimer fades up already
> ringing, undamped, sounding sympathetically off the room's own hum, never cleanly struck, until a flat
> palm stops it dead on the strings. Late, a thin sampled-orchestra patch builds towering chords that crest
> and dip. Every texture comes out of the same small module. Steady tempo, one key, no drop.

**Changed from raw:** opener now carries a temperature word rather than a genre word · vocal placement made
spatial (*inside the break*, *a room away*) · the palm promoted from an implication to a stated event ·
sympathetic ring given its physical cause in-clause · nostalgia vocabulary absent by construction.

### V2 — **THE ONLY ONE WHO READ IT** · opener temperature: *tender*

> **Tenderness under accusation, warm and unraised at 145.** Keygen music as current practice: tracker
> square-bass sliding on hard portamento, a chopped amen break cut fine and battering on the grid, and a
> machine log ticking one character per sixteenth, mono, dead centre, from the first bar. A sixty-cycle
> pedal sits low in the tiles beneath all of it, felt through a bench before it is heard. Cut-up spoken
> word dissolves into a piano figure — a thin sampled piano from the same module, four voices at most,
> loop points audible. A hammered dulcimer with no dampers rings in the gaps between vocal phrases like an
> answer nearly given, and a flat palm lands on the courses and takes it out of the room, skin audible on
> wound wire. Two registers of one female late-twenties voice: low, close, sibilant on the capsule, fast
> inside the break; high, plain, no vibrato, above it; one unison and no more. Close, cheap and
> unglamorous, conversion audible. Constant tempo, one key, no drop.

**Changed from raw:** the palm's physical noise moved into the style field *and* the lyric · *"an answer
nearly given"* added, which is the whole facet in four words · the piano figure declared as coming from the
same module, so the Source-2 import serves the radiant instead of decorating.

### V3 — **IT RINGS BECAUSE THE ROOM IS ON** · opener temperature: *precise*

> **Precise fear at 145 in a room louder than a speaking voice.** Keygen music with hardcore weight:
> tracker square-bass on hard portamento, quantised to the sixteenth with no filter movement whatsoever,
> and a chopped amen break in a bone-rattling breakdown with battering drums that never leaves the grid. A
> machine log ticks one character per sixteenth, mono and centred, the only thing keeping time. Under all
> of it a real recorded plant-room tone at sixty cycles, sitting in the sternum. The hammered dulcimer is
> never struck at all — undamped, it takes the room's hum off the air and sounds by itself, always at the
> pitch the room chooses, until a palm flattens it and it starts again anyway. Cut-up spoken word dissolves
> into that dulcimer figure. A female late-twenties voice in two registers, low and clipped inside the
> break, high and plain above it, pitched slightly up by the noise, speaking over the room, meeting in
> unison once. Forty bars carry no vocal at all. Timbres change shape and take sudden left turns; the tempo
> never moves.

**Changed from raw:** *"and it starts again anyway"* added — the palm fails here, which is this variation's
whole argument and is the one place in the pair where the withheld gesture does not hold · the Source-2
dream phrase harnessed to timbre in the same sentence that fixes the tempo.

### V4 — **I MEANT IT THE SECOND TIME** · opener temperature: *decided*

> **Decided and quiet at 145, and it does not let up once.** Keygen music assembling itself in reverse: a
> hammered dulcimer first, undamped and already ringing off the room; then a machine log ticking one
> character per sixteenth, mono and dead centre; then a sixty-cycle pedal arriving through a kitchen
> counter into the forearms; then tracker square-bass on hard portamento; then a thin sampled-orchestra
> patch in towering chords that crest and dip; and only then the chopped amen break, battering, arriving
> last and locking to the grid it was always going to lock to. A female late-twenties voice in two
> registers — low, close, clipped, an audible swallow before the last line; high, plain, no vibrato, above
> the grid — meeting in unison exactly once. At the close a hand lifts off the strings and the dulcimer is
> allowed to ring out undamped past the final drum hit, mains pedal beneath it, decaying naturally with no
> fade. One tempo, one key, no drop.

**Changed from raw:** the reverse parade given a reason in-clause (*the grid it was always going to lock
to*) so the generator hears an arrival rather than a gimmick · *decaying naturally with no fade* kept
explicit, because terminal silence survives the generator and a fade would spend the pair's last gesture on
an automation curve.

## ⛔ WHAT I REFUSED TO CHANGE

1. **The break stays named.** It would be easy to write *"chopped breakbeat"* and avoid the credit
   obligation. ⛔ **That is the exact evasion the doctrine was written against.** The figure is asked for by
   name, so the player is named in the dedication — and in *this* song, whose subject is people whose work
   is everywhere and whose names are not, ducking that credit would falsify the piece on the artifact.
   ⚠️ And the exclude fields are checked so that nothing in them bans a sampled or acoustic kit; **a package
   may not credit a carrier it has banned.**
2. **The dulcimer is not tuned.** Every instinct I have says make it beautiful. It is not beautiful here.
   It is a thing sounding in an empty room because the building is powered.
3. **The room does not change at the end.** The generator, the platform and my own habits all want a lift.
   ⛔ There is no lift. She says it, and it does not work, and the break keeps going — because *"you cannot
   argue with a room that has already decided what you are"* is the seed's own sentence and softening it
   would make the pair a lie.
4. **Nothing is faster than 145 and nothing is slower.** Not one bar.
