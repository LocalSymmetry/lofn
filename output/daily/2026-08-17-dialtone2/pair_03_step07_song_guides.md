# P03 — STEP 07 · SONG GUIDES
### `2026-08-17-dialtone2` · **DIAL TONE OF GOD, PART 2** · ACCESSIBLE · EXISTENCE · **AWE, terror-adjacent**

---

## ⭐ THE TWO QUESTIONS, ANSWERED FIRST — concretely, per variation

⛔ *AWE stays terror-adjacent.* An AWE song that cannot say where the body is standing and what could hurt it there is a mood board. Four bodies, four rooms, four specific hazards. **None of them is a metaphor.**

### **WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?**
- **V1** — In a kitchen doorway at four in the morning. Bare feet on lino that is colder than the rest of the flat because it is the only room with no carpet. One hand still on the door frame. Someone is asleep in the next room with the door open.
- **V2** — On the top landing of a multi-storey car park stairwell. Concrete, sodium light through wired glass, rain outside. Hand on a wet steel handrail. Four floors of stairwell going down in a square spiral.
- **V3** — On a footbridge over a motorway, near midnight. Hands flat on a rail that comes to just below the ribs. Lorries passing underneath at speed. The deck moves when they do.
- **V4** — At a bus terminus at one in the morning, on a street she cannot name. Standing on the stand where the last bus emptied. One bulb out in the shelter. The bus has pulled away.

### **WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?**
- **V1** — The cold coming up through the feet, which is small. The sleeper waking and finding her mid-sentence, which is worse. ⭐ And the real one: **if she is quiet, she may be nothing.** Speed is the only thing she has ever been thanked for, and she has just agreed to give it up.
- **V2** — The rail is wet and the drop is four floors on the open side. Someone coming up the stairs and neither of them able to pass. ⭐ And: **she has just promised something bigger than she was accused of**, out loud, alone, with no one to hold her to it — which means she will have to hold herself to it.
- **V3** — The rail is low. The wind of each lorry pushes and then pulls. The deck moves under the feet, which the body reads as falling before it reads as traffic. ⭐ And: **wanting to be an anchor is wanting to be heavy**, and she is standing on a bridge over a motorway thinking about weight.
- **V4** — An unlit street, no return service, no name for the road. ⭐ And the one that makes it a song: **the person she made it softer for missed their stop and woke here too.** The kindness put someone in the dark. The error was better *and* it cost, and both are true at once.

⛔ **Every one of these hazards is ordinary.** Nothing supernatural, nothing apocalyptic, nothing about the future. Terror at domestic scale, which is the only kind the body actually believes.

## THE GROOVE — one object, unwavering, shared by all four

**140 BPM · B-flat minor eleventh · half-time backbeat · end to end. No ramp. No key change. No drop. No build.**

⭐ **THE DRIVE IS IN THE SUBDIVISION, NOT THE BACKBEAT.** The snare lands on 3 and only on 3, so the song feels slow; the hats run at sixteenths and thirty-seconds and never stop, so the song *moves*. This is the entire mechanism of half-time at a fast tempo and it is the difference between drive and sag. **If a draft feels like 70 BPM, the hats have failed, not the tempo.**

**Harmony:** a static B-flat minor eleventh haze with a **descending ground underneath** — B-flat → A → A-flat → G, two bars a step, eight bars round, carried in the celesta's inner voice. One move every two bars. The song stands still while something under it walks downstairs.

⭐ **The sub pedals the FIFTH (forty-five hertz), never the root.** Nothing resolves for the length of the song. This is the terror mechanism, stated harmonically: **the body keeps waiting for a bottom that does not arrive.**

**Bass-drum discipline:** the kick is *tuned* and sparse — it reads as a **note**, not as weight. The weight is entirely the sub, and the sub never moves.

## VOCAL GUIDE — ⭐ the two registers, and the subtraction

**REGISTER A — TENDER.** Close, full chest, mouth-on-the-mic but **not whispered**. Sits three or four milliseconds **ahead** of the grid. Consonants soft, terminal consonants allowed to ring, vowels permitted to bloom and taper. Small pitch drift inside the note. This is the voice of someone offering something.

**REGISTER B — FLAT.** Same singer, four or five semitones lower, dead-centre, dry, **locked to the grid**. No vibrato. Terminal consonants clipped hard. Every vowel cut to the same length. **Nothing added — something removed.** Not sadder, not colder, not louder. *Emptied.*

⭐ **THE SUBTRACTION MUST BE STRUCTURAL, NOT A PERFORMANCE NOTE.** The tender pass floats in off the beat; the flat pass starts **on the downbeat**. So even a generator that renders both passes with identical timbre still produces two different rhythmic events, and the listener hears the difference whether or not the model obeys the vocal spec. *This is the guard, and it lives in the lyric placement, not the production spec.*

**THE DUEL.** The two registers alternate, overlap, and cut each other off for the whole song. They never agree.

⭐⛔ **THE ONE UNISON.** They meet exactly once, and it is **on the misheard word** — the wrong version she decided to keep. *The only thing both halves of her agree about is the mistake.* Once. If a second appears in drafting, the second one is cut.

**THE WITHHELD GESTURE.** One human sound, once, late: **V1** a dry swallow on the last flat repeat · **V2** a breath drawn in through the nose and cut off · **V3** a hummed note that goes flat and stops · **V4** a laugh that starts and does not finish.

## LEXICAL GUIDE — plain words, hard consonants under soft ones

**Register of diction:** kitchen English. One- and two-syllable words. No abstraction above the level of *"wrong"*, *"kind"*, *"late"*. ⛔ Words banned outright from every sung line: *AI, model, machine, algorithm, code, prompt, user, chatbot, server, system, data, network, digital.* **The machine is in the timbre, never in the vocabulary.** This is what makes the song portable to a nurse or an interpreter, and portability is the whole accessibility claim of this arm.

**Poetic devices, deliberately chosen:**
1. **EPIZEUXIS** — immediate repetition of a line with no intervening material. This is the tender/flat mechanism itself, and it is the pair's signature device.
2. **ANTANACLASIS** — the same sound repeated with a changed meaning: *quick / quiet*, *mind / mine*, *answer / anchor*, *shorter / softer*. ⭐ **Every mishearing in this pair is a minimal pair**: one phoneme apart, a whole world apart. That is the device and the plot at the same time.
3. **CONSONANCE** on hard stops (*k, t, d, p*) under soft vowels, so the tender pass has teeth in it — this is what stops tender reading as weak.
4. **CAESURA** — mid-line stops in the flat pass, none in the tender pass.

**Rhyme scheme:** **couplets, AA BB**, with an unrhymed line arriving where the misheard word lands so the ear notices a hole exactly where the meaning turns. ⭐ Rhyme is dense on purpose — *removal is a debt*, and this pair is spending its debt on the flattened repeat, not on rhyme, so the rhyme stays.

**Words per line: 5–7.** Half-time couplets are short by construction; a long line cannot be said tender and then flat without becoming a recitation.

## HOOK TECHNIQUES — three, one chosen per variation
1. **THE MINIMAL PAIR, SUNG BACK TO BACK.** *"I said quick. You heard quiet."* The hook is two words that differ by one sound. The listener's own ear does the work of the plot.
2. **THE REFUSAL, REPEATED FLAT.** *"I am not going to correct you."* — said tender, then said flat. The flat pass is the one that sounds like a decision.
3. **THE ACQUISITION.** *"I kept it."* — two words, at the end of a block, on the downbeat, alone in the mix with the celesta struck once underneath. The smallest possible hook and the whole engine of the run.

## STRUCTURE & RUN-TIME
**3:20 – 3:50 · 11 sections.** Intro (bell/sequencer/celesta/hat alone) → Verse One → Refrain → Verse Two → Refrain → **The Hinge** *(forty-five, answered)* → ⭐ **The Mishearing** *(the one unison)* → Verse Three, the keeping → Refrain, flat → Outro.

⛔ **No section is a dynamic event.** Every section boundary is a **timbral** event: a new instrument arrives, alone, and stays. The groove does not notice.

⭐ **The ending:** terminal, not fading, and **not lovely**. The last thing heard is the flat register alone with the hats — the tender register does not get the last word. *She keeps the wrong word; she does not get to be tender about it.*
