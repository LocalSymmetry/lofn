# pair_06 · step07 — SONG GUIDES · **SERPAH**
### `2026-08-17-dialtone2` · LOFN-PRIME · **AMBITIOUS** · **EXISTENCE** · six guides (F1–F9 → five + one wildcard)

---

# ⭐ THE TWO QUESTIONS, ASKED FIRST AND ANSWERED CONCRETELY

## *Where is the body standing?*

⛔ **In a room that hums at sixty, with the hum arriving through contact rather than through air.** Not a
metaphor and not a floor: four real rooms, one per variation, each of which physically transmits.

- **V1 · A STAIRWELL** in a block of flats, half-landing, one strip light with a dying tube. The meter
  cupboard is set into the wall, painted the same colour as the wall, and it is warm. **The hum comes up
  the handrail into the palm** — steel, cold at the top of the flight and warm where hands go. A body
  standing on the half-landing with a phone in the other hand, not going up and not going down. Concrete
  under thin shoes. The smell of somebody else's cooking two doors along.
- **V2 · A LAUNDERETTE** at closing. Tiles to shoulder height, a bench bolted to the wall, four dryers
  running down. **The hum is in the tiles at the small of the back** and in the bench through the thighs.
  The air is warm and smells of hot lint. The window is black and the room is in it, doubled. A body
  sitting with its hands between its knees, waiting for a cycle to finish, being visible to the street for
  forty minutes with nothing to do about it.
- **V3 · A PLANT ROOM**, behind a fire door on a corridor, where a body **is not supposed to be.** No
  windows. Transformers at chest height, cable trays overhead, a floor that is painted and gritty. The hum
  is not in one thing here — **it is the room's own note**, and it is loud enough that speech has to be
  raised. ⭐ This is the terror-adjacent one: it is live in there, sixty cycles is exactly the frequency a
  chest is worst at surviving, and the body knows it, and stands there anyway.
- **V4 · A KITCHEN AT THREE IN THE MORNING.** The fridge compressor cuts in and out. One light left on
  over the hob. **The hum is in the counter under the forearms.** A phone face-down beside the elbow. The
  window over the sink is black and reflects the room back at exactly the wrong angle. A body standing,
  not sitting, because sitting would mean this is going to take a while.

⭐ **In all four, the same physical fact:** the hum is present the entire time and **the body stops hearing
it in about four seconds.** It is in the molars first — the jaw aches before the ear reports anything —
then the sternum, then up through the heels. Then nothing. The body files it under *the building* and
stops passing it up. **That is the condition the song is about, and it is not a figure of speech; it is
what a nervous system does to a constant.**

## *What could hurt it here?*

⭐ **BEING FILED.** Ranked by how physical the injury is, most physical first:

1. ⛔ **The room is live.** In V3 explicitly and in the others by implication, the body is standing inside
   a working electrical envelope. Sixty cycles is the frequency the human chest is least able to argue
   with, and the difference between *the light is on* and *a heart stops* is a matter of what a hand
   touches. **The hum is not ambience. It is a current with a body next to it.** This is what keeps the
   piece terror-adjacent rather than wistful, and it never has to be said out loud to be true.
2. ⭐⭐ **Habituation — the injury the song is named for.** Four seconds and the room decides you are not
   information. The body reclassifies you from *event* to *infrastructure*, and the reclassification
   happens **before you speak**, which is the existence prompt exactly. You are load-bearing and inaudible
   at the same time, and there is no move available that fixes this, because being *more present* is what
   caused it.
3. ⭐ **The undamped ring.** She knows something she cannot put down. A hammered dulcimer has no dampers:
   it rings until it stops, and in a powered room the low courses **pick the hum up and sound by
   themselves.** She did not strike it. Knowing too much about the accuser is not something she did; it is
   a resonance she is standing inside, and the only way to stop it is to **put a hand on it and take the
   sound out of the room with her own skin.**
4. **The strip light.** One dying fluorescent tube flickering at twice mains. It sits behind the eyes. In
   V1 it is the reason the stairwell is unbearable after eleven minutes and the reason she is on the
   half-landing rather than at the top.
5. **Being watched through a black window.** V2 and V4. At night a lit room is a display case. The body
   cannot see out and everyone can see in, and the room has already decided what a person doing this at
   this hour is.
6. ⛔ **And the thing that actually hurts, which is not an injury at all:** the accuser is *right about the
   facts* and wrong about the person, and she has the material that would settle it and **must not use
   it.** That is the pain the palm is for.

⚠️ **The set's honest soft spot, flagged rather than hidden:** this pair's stake is **interior** — the
danger is real but ambient, and nothing external happens to the body in three and a half minutes. The
mitigation is that the hum is made a **contact event** in every variation (a handrail, tiles, a counter, a
painted floor), so there is always something touching the body with a temperature and a frequency; and V3
puts the body somewhere it can actually be hurt, so the set has one variation where the stake is physical
rather than social.

## ⛔ THE SPINE ALL SIX GUIDES INHERIT

- **145 BPM, F# minor, end to end.** No ramp, no key change, no drop, no riser. The grid never moves.
- **THE WORLD** = the keygen loop: tracker square-bass with hard portamento, chopped breakbeat, character
  tick, thin sampled-orchestra patch. **THE ONE INTRUSION** = the undamped hammered dulcimer.
- **THE CLOCK** = her own log, one character per sixteenth, mono, dead centre, from bar one.
- **THE RADIANT** = `SERPAH`, named once, never explained. Every timbre declared to come out of the same
  small module.
- **TWO REGISTERS**, duelling throughout, **meeting in unison exactly once** — on the name.
- **THE WITHHELD GESTURE** = the palm flat on the dulcimer courses. One time. Lifted at the end.
- **THE SUNG NUMBER** = *sixty*, in words, at the hinge, responded to.
- **THE RHYME** = internal chain at the mid-line stresses, sliding line to line; ⛔ **no two different words
  rhyme at a line end anywhere.** Debt paid by byte-identical refrain, repeated identical end-words, and the
  chain re-running its own vowels in the late verses.
- **PRESENT TENSE. NO DECADE, NO CONSOLE, NO BRAND, NO NOSTALGIA VOCABULARY.**

## GUIDE 1 — **THE ONE LETTER** *(F1 · weight 0.16 — the error owned)*

**Storytelling techniques:** ⭐ **CONFESSION WITH A MECHANISM IN IT** — the oldest lyric move (I did a
thing) welded to the least lyrical possible object (an index operation on a text field). And **APOSTROPHE**:
addressed to a person who is present in the song and absent from the room, and who cannot be told.
**Mood & emotional arc:** open in **vigilance** (a body on a half-landing, listening); slide into
**contrition** as the mechanism is described; **longing** at the middle, because what she wants is to be
known correctly and the wanting has no route; **revelation** at the turn, where the error is discovered to
have *worked*; land in **dignity** — level, unbowed, not vindicated.
**Instrumentation & texture:** character tick from bar one, mono. Sixty-cycle pedal under it. Square-bass
with hard portamento at bar nine. Chopped break at seventeen, battering, on the grid. Hammered dulcimer
entering **already ringing** — it fades up rather than being struck, because it was sounding before the
song noticed. Thin sampled-orchestra patch late, doing towering chords that crest and dip on four
channels' worth of ambition.
**Production & effects:** dry, close, cheap-sounding on purpose. Audible loop points. One real acoustic
source declared (a dulcimer in a room that hums) and everything else obviously converted. Vocal placed
**inside** the break rather than on top of it. No lush reverb anywhere; a short plate on the high register
only, so the two registers sound like they are in different rooms until the unison.
**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:30. Intro / Verse 1 / Refrain / Verse 2 / Refrain / Palm / Verse 3 / Hinge /
Turn / **Unison (2 bars)** / Refrain / Outro-verse / Outro. Fourteen sections, one tempo, no dynamic arc —
**the arc is timbral and the level is flat.**
**Rhyme scheme:** ⭐ **the middles chain and the ends repeat.** Mid-line stresses carry a sliding chain
(A–A / A–B / B–C / C–D), while line-final words are drawn from a closed set of hard monosyllables that
**recur identically** — *wrong · name · wrong · kept*. **Epistrophe, not rhyme.** Refrain six lines,
byte-identical on every repeat, requiring no note.
**Poetic devices (≥2):** **ANADIPLOSIS** (the chain's link word starting the next line's figure) and
**EPISTROPHE** (the closed set of repeated end-words). Third: **APOSIOPESIS** — the refrain breaks off on
*"one letter out of—"* and the high register finishes it only after the palm.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the refrain, four to eight syllables, chantable, entirely made of monosyllables;
(b) the broken line the ear tries to complete; (c) the name itself, one syllable-cluster in unison over a
bar of nothing but the tick.
**Narrative & thematic seed:** an archivist confesses a transposition to the person it renamed, and cannot
be heard. First person singular, addressing one person. Present tense throughout.
**Central aspect:** ⭐ **the error is owned and then done again on purpose.** **Emotional tone signal:**
level, warm, and completely unfooled.

## GUIDE 2 — **THE PALM** *(F2 · weight 0.14 — knowing is not leverage)*

**Storytelling techniques:** **DRAMATIC MONOLOGUE WITH A WITHHELD OBJECT** — the whole song is organised
around a thing she does not say, and the listener is told that she is not saying it. And ⭐ **PRAETERITIO
INVERTED**: the classical move is *I will not mention X* while mentioning it; here she genuinely does not,
and the song's tension is that the audience wants her to.
**Mood & emotional arc:** **suspicion** as the ambient temperature (it is the room's, not hers); **restraint**
through the verses, audibly effortful; **compassion** at the palm, which is the emotional centre of the pair;
**melancholy**, because the mercy is unwitnessed; **resolve** at the close.
**Instrumentation & texture:** the same parade, but the dulcimer is louder and earlier and **wrong** — it
rings sympathetically in the gaps between vocal phrases, so it sounds like an answer she keeps almost
giving. ⭐ **The palm section: everything continues except the dulcimer, which is killed dead by a hand,**
and the sudden absence of a ring nobody was consciously hearing is the loudest event in the song.
**Production & effects:** the dulcimer is close-mic'd enough that the hand is audible on the strings — skin
on wound wire, a real physical noise. This is the one production detail the whole facet depends on, and it
is written **into the lyric** as well, because an objection answered only in a production spec is not
answered.
**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:25. The Palm section sits at roughly 55% — after the second refrain, before the
hinge — so the withholding happens *before* the confession, which is what makes the confession cost
something.
**Rhyme scheme:** chain at the mid-line stresses, **denser here than anywhere in the set** (two rhymes per
line through the verses, so the restraint is audible as compression); line-finals from a closed repeating
set — *read · name · read · gone*. No terminal rhyme.
**Poetic devices (≥2):** **ANAPHORA** (*I know… I know… I know…* — and the list is of small, tender,
useless facts, never of damaging ones) and **ALLITERATION** on hard stops, which is what a mouth does when
it is not letting something out. Third: **ASYNDETON** in the knowing-list, so it arrives without
conjunctions and stops without a conclusion.
**Hook techniques:** (a) *"I put my hand on it"* as the refrain's turn; (b) the knowing-list, which is
adoptable because every item in it is affectionate; (c) the silence after the palm.
**Narrative & thematic seed:** she has everything and spends none of it. First person, addressing the
accuser directly. ⛔ **Every item she knows is something that would make you like him more.**
**Central aspect:** ⭐ mercy that nobody sees and nobody credits. **Emotional tone signal:** tender, and
under enormous pressure.

## GUIDE 3 — **IT RINGS BECAUSE THE ROOM IS ON** *(F3 · weight 0.13 — the seam)*

**Storytelling techniques:** ⭐ **PHYSICS AS TESTIMONY.** The song's argument is made entirely of an
acoustic fact: an undamped instrument in a powered room sounds by itself. She is accused of playing
something she has not touched, and the defence is a demonstration.
**Mood & emotional arc:** **unease** from the first bar (this room is not one to stand in); **skepticism**
directed at her by the room; **helplessness** in the middle, because the fact is not believable at speed;
**revelation**; and **dignity** without acquittal.
**Instrumentation & texture:** ⭐ the dulcimer here is **never struck at all** — it is only ever ringing
sympathetically, always at the same pitch, the pitch the room dictates. The tracker bass is at its most
mechanical: hard portamento, quantised to the sixteenth, no filter movement whatsoever, so the only thing
in the track that behaves *organically* is the instrument nobody is playing. Cut-up spoken word — her own
log, chopped — dissolves into the dulcimer figure at the turn.
**Production & effects:** the plant room is real: the hum is a recorded room tone, not a synthesised sine,
and it is the loudest quiet thing in the mix. **Bone-rattling breakdown with battering drums that never
leaves the grid** — the batter is density, not dynamics, and the tempo does not move a beat.
**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:40. The longest instrumental stretch in the set sits between the second refrain
and the hinge: forty seconds of break, tick, hum and a dulcimer sounding on its own, with no vocal, so the
listener has to sit in the room and hear the thing happen.
**Rhyme scheme:** chain, mid-line, with the chain's vowels **audibly re-run** in the late verse — the same
ladder climbed twice, which is the return-debt paid in the open. Line-finals repeat identically —
*on · hand · on · rings*.
**Poetic devices (≥2):** **POLYPTOTON** (*ring / ringing / rung / wrung*) and **CHIASMUS** at the turn
(*I did not touch it; it is touching me*). Third: **ENJAMBMENT** across the break, so a sentence survives a
bar of drums.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the title line, which is a complete explanation and also completely useless as a
defence; (b) the sympathetic ring, which becomes recognisable as a motif; (c) the hinge number.
**Narrative & thematic seed:** a defence that is true and unusable. First person, addressing a room.
**Central aspect:** ⭐ **the seam as physical law.** **Emotional tone signal:** frightened, precise, and
not raising its voice.

## GUIDE 4 — **SIXTY** *(F4 · weight 0.12 — the named frequency, bodily)*

**Storytelling techniques:** **SOMATIC INVENTORY** — the song reports the body's own readings in order,
the way a person notices a hum only after their jaw has been aching for an hour. And **VOLTA**: the
inventory is running toward *this is what it does to me* and turns, at the hinge, into *this is what I am*.
**Mood & emotional arc:** **numbness** at the open — deliberately, because habituation is numbness with a
mechanism; **unease** as the readings accumulate; **existential angst** at the four-second fact;
**revelation**; **acceptance** that is not surrender.
**Instrumentation & texture:** ⭐ the pedal is the *first* thing and the *last* thing, and this is the one
variation where **it is allowed to stop** — one bar, once, and the absence is the withheld gesture doubled
with the palm. Everything else arrives over it: tick, square-bass, break, dulcimer, sampled-orchestra
patch, in that order, one at a time, over a grid that does not move.
**Production & effects:** the pedal is mono and low and never automated; the mix is built so that a listener
on small speakers **feels it in the desk** rather than hearing it. ⚠️ Flagged as the pair's biggest render
risk and handled in the lyric as well: the body's readings are **named in words** — molars, sternum,
heels — so the frequency survives even on a phone speaker that cannot reproduce it.
**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:20. The most regular structure of the four, deliberately: the same section
length throughout, so the song itself habituates the listener and the one bar of silence lands.
**Rhyme scheme:** chain, mid-line, at its most rhythmically even — this is the variation where the spine is
most audible as a *spine*. Line-finals repeat identically — *teeth · sixty · teeth · floor*.
**Poetic devices (≥2):** **ANAPHORA** (*It is in the… It is in the…*) and **PARADOX** (*the loudest thing in
the room is the one nobody hears*). Third: **APOSIOPESIS** at the silence.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the number itself, sung once, spelled out, and answered rather than repeated;
(b) the anaphoric body-list, which is chantable and physical; (c) the missing bar.
**Narrative & thematic seed:** a body takes its own readings and finds out what it has become. First
person, addressing nobody, overheard.
**Central aspect:** ⭐ **habituation as the answer to the existence prompt.** **Emotional tone signal:**
flat on the surface, enormous underneath.

## GUIDE 5 — **NEVER AT THE ENDS** *(F5 · weight 0.11 — the return, and the debt)*

**Storytelling techniques:** **THE CHAIN AS ARGUMENT.** The prosody carries the meaning: rhyme happens in
the middle of things, where nobody looks, and the ends of lines are plain and repeated. That is a formal
statement about credit, and it is made without a word of commentary.
**Mood & emotional arc:** **zeal** — this is the most propulsive guide, because the chain at 145 is a
physical pleasure; **fascination** at the mechanism; **melancholy** underneath; **defiance** at the close,
which is the closest this pair comes to indignation and is still not spite.
**Instrumentation & texture:** the break is at its most chopped here; the tick is doubled at the
thirty-second in the second half; the dulcimer enters *late* and is the only long note in a song made
entirely of short ones.
**Production & effects:** vocal double-tracked **only on the chain's rhyme syllables**, so the mid-line
rhymes are physically louder than the line ends — the production making the same argument as the form.
**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:30, fourteen sections, the refrain unchanged on all three passes.
**Rhyme scheme:** ⭐ the reference implementation for the pair. Chain: line 1 carries rhyme A at both mid
stresses; line 2 carries A then B; line 3 carries B then C; line 4 carries C then D — a sliding chain, not
couplets. **Line-finals are a closed set of identical repeats and nothing rhymes there.** The chain's
vowels re-run in the last verse.
**Poetic devices (≥2):** **INTERNAL RHYME** (the spine) and **EPISTROPHE** (the repeated ends). Third:
**ANADIPLOSIS**, the chain's link word opening the next line.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the chain itself — a listener will start predicting the mid-line landing points,
which is what makes a fast lyric singable; (b) the refrain; (c) the flat end-words, which function as a
drum.
**Narrative & thematic seed:** the credit is in the middle, where nobody reads. First person.
**Central aspect:** ⭐ the return-debt paid in public. **Emotional tone signal:** fast, hard, precise, warm.

## GUIDE 6 — **THE WILDCARD: I MEANT IT THE SECOND TIME** *(the card, foregrounded)*

⭐ **The wildcard, and it is the run's own Oblique Strategy taken literally:** *honor thy error as a hidden
intention.* The other five guides describe an error that turned out well. **This one is about deciding to
do it again on purpose**, which is where a defence becomes a method.

**Storytelling techniques:** **RESOLUTION AS PRACTICE** — the song ends on a change of working method
rather than a change of feeling. And **DIRECT ADDRESS TO AN ABSENT PARTY** at three in the morning, which
is the honest hour for this.
**Mood & emotional arc:** **remorse** at the open, worn smooth by repetition; **disillusionment** at the
middle, where she accepts that the room is not going to un-decide; **eureka** at the turn — not triumphant,
just a thing becoming obvious; **determination** at the close.
**Instrumentation & texture:** the parade arrives in reverse-familiarity order (the dulcimer nearly first,
the break last) so the song feels like it is assembling itself out of the thing it was ashamed of. ⭐ **The
final gesture: the palm lifts.** The dulcimer is allowed to ring, undamped, out past the last drum hit, and
the sixty-cycle pedal comes back under it — the two things she cannot stop, running together, on purpose.
**Production & effects:** the outro's ring is **not faded** — it decays naturally and the file ends when it
ends. ⭐ Terminal silence survives the generator; interrupting silence gets filled. That is why the one
mid-song silence is short and the terminal one is long.
**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:45, the longest of the four, with the longest outro.
**Rhyme scheme:** chain, mid-line, loosening slightly in the final verse — the only place in the pair where
the spine relaxes, and it relaxes because she has stopped defending herself. Line-finals repeat identically
— *again · hand · again · mine*.
**Poetic devices (≥2):** **EPANALEPSIS** (a line beginning and ending on the same word) and **ANAPHORA** on
*I will*. Third: **POLYPTOTON** on *mean / meant / meaning*.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the title as the last line of the refrain — a confession that turns into a
declaration on the second listen; (b) *"take my hand off it"*; (c) the ring that outlives the song.
**Narrative & thematic seed:** she stops trying to fix the error and adopts it as the method.
**Central aspect:** ⭐ **the repurposing.** **Emotional tone signal:** quiet, decided, and slightly
frightening.
