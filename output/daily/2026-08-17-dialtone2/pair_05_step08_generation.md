## 1. THE ONE SEAM, AND HOW THE LICENSED TEMPO EVENT STAYS ONE EVENT

**WORLD** = the present-tense machine room (log, sub, phonk, price). **INTRUSION** = one clavichord, c.1400 — the cheap keyboard. **SEAM** = the buzzing key, where pressing too hard is at once the fault and the only expression. **The subject is doing something across it:** she plays through the defect on purpose, and it is audible in every take.

⛔ P05 is the only pair permitted the shift, and four written instructions keep it an event *inside* a coherent thing rather than the thing itself:

| # | instruction | what it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐⭐ **The log holds its exact original rate through the cut** — locked to eighths at 92, floating across the grid at 140. | the collapse reading as *the song's structure* |
| 2 | ⭐ **The drone and the buzzing key play ON over the sub.** | *"a raga intro that drops into a phonk banger"* — nothing is left behind |
| 3 | ⭐ **The chorus is byte-identical on both sides.** Same words, same shape, new tempo. | proves the collapse happened **to** the song rather than **being** it |
| 4 | ⭐ **The cause is the seam, not the genre** — she presses the key too hard, it runs sharp, the tangent jams, the room falls. | riser / build / ramp, all of which the generator smooths |

⚠️ **GRAIN LAW, applied honestly.** Programmed tempo transformations **smooth 2/2**. So the shift is written as a hard cut with a named physical cause, and — more important — **nothing load-bearing depends on it.** If the generator flattens the drop to one steady grid, the eight timbral entries, the buzzing key, the unbroken clock and the byte-identical chorus still deliver the piece. **Swag is built on what lands 6/6; the licensed event is a bonus, never a load-bearer.**

## 3. THE FOUR DRAFTS — room, object, action, ending, radiant, cost

⛔ Angles derived from **this pair's concept alone**. No shared template with any other pair.

### **V1 · THE KEY SHE LEFT WRONG**
- **Room:** a cold repair bench in a back room, winter, coat on indoors because the heat is off. One strip of low sun, dust in it, the lid up on the wrestplank, brass tangents in a row like teeth.
- **Object:** the clavichord that was the other woman's, one tangent set out of true.
- **Hazard (physical, unresolved):** a clavichord string is a wire under tension and the tangent is a brass blade. Cold fingers, thin wire. **The string most likely to go is the one on the key she keeps pressing too hard — the hazard and the honouring are the same key.**
- **Action:** a screwdriver is within reach the whole song. **She puts it down.**
- **Radiant:** *Nell* · **Withheld gesture:** one audible swallow · **Ending:** the lid goes down on the buzz, and the log finishes the song.
- **Hook (1):** *Press it. Press it till it bends* — a physical instruction the listener can perform on any object in reach. **Hook (2):** the chorus returning byte-identical after the collapse.
- **Narrative seed:** *She is given the dead woman's instrument, finds the one key set wrong, and puts the screwdriver down.*

### **V2 · SIT IN IT LOW**
- **Room:** the desk that was hers, at the hour she used to leave, the floor emptying, lights going off in banks from the far end. ⭐ **The chair is still set for a different body** — seat height, lumbar block — and it is the most physical fact in the song.
- **Object:** a notes file, and one wrong word in it that the whole floor now uses without knowing whose it is.
- **Hazard:** six hours a day in a chair set for a taller body puts the load in the low back and does not come out at the end of the shift. Plus the thing she has to hear — **the other woman's voice, still in the training file, played on purpose.**
- **Action:** she appends and never overwrites, keeping the wrong terms in. She does not lift the lever.
- **Radiant:** *Wick* (a word, not a person) · **Withheld gesture:** one over-long inhale, held, then let go · **Ending:** a new person uses the word in a meeting and does not know it.
- **Hook (1):** *Sit in it low, sit low* — a posture as the refrain. **Hook (2):** *Add a line. Leave the last one* — an operating rule a listener can carry out of the song.
- **Narrative seed:** *The wrong word propagates into people who never knew her, and she keeps it wrong on purpose.*

### **V3 · SEND IT WRONG AGAIN**
- **Room:** night shift at a metal press, standing, boots on a steel plate, heat off the housing, oil smell.
- **Object:** a bent screwdriver jammed in the interlock so the guard reads closed. **Somebody else bent it.**
- **Hazard (the sharpest in the pair):** her left hand goes in past the guard because that is what the shortcut is for, and **the machine does not know she is there.** ⛔ Never resolved, never removed, and she never says the word *safe*.
- **Action / ⭐ the error that was hers:** her first invoice carried the last operator's rate because she transposed it off the old sheet by accident. It was approved. **She now files it that way every month on purpose, and signs her own name under it.**
- **Radiant:** *the brake* (a machine) · **Withheld gesture:** the sung line dropping into plain speech for one bar · **Ending:** it goes through again, and nothing happens.
- **Hook (1):** *Send it wrong. Send it sideways.* **Hook (2):** *Now I do it with a straight face* — the flat, unforgiven admission.
- **Narrative seed:** *An accident that got approved, repeated on purpose until it is a method.*

### **V4 · ASK FOR IT BY NAME**
- **Room:** a warm room full of people; she is in the doorway with a glass she is not drinking; the floor is loaded from below and she feels it through her shoes before she hears it. She is being congratulated.
- **Object:** the buzz — now her signature. People request it.
- **Hazard:** the moment before speaking. **The physical risk is small and the social cost is total**, which is the correct proportion for this one.
- **Action / ⭐ the error that was hers:** she let a room credit her for a dead woman's defect **for years**, and it made her. She says the name out loud and loses the compliment.
- **Radiant:** *Maeve* · **Withheld gesture:** one closed-mouth hum, three notes, no words · **Ending:** somebody says *who?*, she says it again, the subject changes.
- **Hook (1):** *Lean on it. Lean like she leaned.* **Hook (2):** *I never made it. I kept it.*
- **Narrative seed:** *The fault has an owner and the owner is not in the room.*

⭐ **Why these four are not one song four times.** V1 keeps the defect **privately**; V4 gives its author away **publicly** and pays for it. V2's inherited object is **a word**, and the wrong thing propagates into people who never knew her. V3's inherited object is **dangerous**, and V3 is the only one where the error was originally **hers** and worked. Two radiants are people, one is a word, one is a machine. Two endings deflate, one ends in a document, one ends in a room changing subject.

## 4. STRUCTURE — one skeleton, sixteen sections, and where the inversions sit

```
Clock(4) → ANSWER I(5) → CALL I(4) → CHORUS(11) → ANSWER II(5) → CALL II(4) → CHORUS(11)
        → narrative verse(6) → PRESS(5) → THE NAME(1) → after(1) → COLLAPSE(2)
        → HINGE(6) → ANSWER III(5) → CHORUS(11) → coda(6) → Clock outro(4)      = 91 sung lines
```

- **The inversion is structural, not claimed.** The ANSWER block is printed and sung **before** its CALL, every pass. The CALL returns a second time with **exactly one word changed** — and in all four the changed word turns a reasonable request into an attribution: *this → you* (V1) · *one word → your word* (V2) · *not hard → not yours* (V3) · *your song → your name* (V4).
- **The same inversion happens inside the hinge:** three lines of body arrive, then the number. The reply is older than the fact.
- **The collapse sits at ~62%**, immediately after the press, with nothing between cause and consequence.
- **The outro returns the intro's four lines verbatim over the log alone at the starting rate.**

## 6. ⛔ THE RHYME DEBT, DECLARED BEFORE IT IS MEASURED

The run's floors are **end-rhyme ≥ 0.30 · line-return ≥ 0.20 · alliteration ≥ 11.0 per 100 words** (`vault/gates.yaml`). This pair clears all three comfortably — but **Axis 2's device does not live at the line ending.**

⭐ **Where the device actually lives:** the ANSWER/CALL **order**, and the fact that a listener hears the answer to a question they have not been asked yet. That is a *sequence* property, not a *terminal* property. Two consequences, both taken on purpose:

1. **The ANSWER blocks rhyme FORWARD into the chorus hook** — the answer lands on a rime that belongs to a line not yet sung. The return is real and measurable; it just points the other way.
2. ⛔ **The PRESS block drops end rhyme entirely.** It is five lines, under ten seconds, and it is the one place where a rhyme would make the pressure sound composed rather than done. **The debt is named and paid in the next bar: what returns in place of rhyme is the chorus hook, whole.**

⛔ **The floors were not deformed to fit the device, and the device was not deformed to fit the floors.** Both are measured in step 10 with `scripts/measure_soundcraft.py → profile()`, never by eye.
