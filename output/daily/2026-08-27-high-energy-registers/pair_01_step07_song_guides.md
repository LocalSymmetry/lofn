# V1 · **NOBODY CHOSE FOUR SECONDS**
### THE DEFAULT — something decided by someone who never met me, which is now simply how things are

**Form:** ⭐ **THE HELD DOOR.** A four-line **Hold** recurs four times and its object narrows each time — **door → arm → screw → setting** — with verses between it, the hook three times, one turn, and a final Hold that is six lines of single words. The narrowing is the argument: a default is a rule that keeps being true while the thing that is true of gets smaller, until it is one screw nobody remembers turning.

`Hold 1 · Verse 1 · Hold 2 · Chorus 1 · Verse 2 · Hold 3 · Chorus 2 · Verse 3 · Hold 4 · Turn · Chorus 3 · Last Hold` — **82 sung lines.**

**Room:** a grey corridor, second door down, a hand plate worn through the paint. I have a shoulder in the door and it comes back against my arm at its own speed.

**R1 · the resistance, indexed:** *"This door shuts in four seconds. / It did that before I got here."* — subject **this door**, verb **shuts**, count **four seconds**. There is a screw in the arm of it and a thumb turned that screw at a factory and the thumb has been gone for years.

**R3 · the discriminator, in the hook:** ⭐ **“I am not asking it to stop. I am asking it to be a decision.”**

**R5 · the cost, displacement:** I had another thing in my hands. I put it on the floor so this one could stay open. It is still on the floor and somebody stepped over it. *"The only thing that moved today is the thing I was carrying."*

**R6 · the vocal decision:** conversational volume throughout, **jaw nearly closed on the hook** so the consonants carry it and there is not one open vowel in it — and the **final chorus is a whole tone LOWER** than the first two, into the biggest arrangement of the record. Everyone lifts at the last chorus. This one goes down.

**Refused (song-level):** ⛔ **no imperative verb anywhere.** A default is what happens when nobody gave an instruction; this song does not give one either.

**The repeating physical event:** a dry hydraulic door-closer **hiss on beat four of every bar**, from the first second. It **stops once at bar 65** and never returns; the kit, bass and voice continue unbroken.

**The front floor (R4):** the record opens on **five seconds of that hiss and one metal latch, alone, dry, no music.** Full arrangement arrives at **bar 9** and never grows again.

**ONE SEAM:** dominant = mechanised industrial. Intruder = **one unaccompanied hummed note**, human-shaped, where the machine goes, at the last chorus. She goes *down* into it and does not raise her volume.

**The turn:** past the midpoint she looks up. **Nobody chose tonight either** — a table went up at lunchtime with tonight already on it, the number is going up while she stands there, the moon is at the top of the sky and still filling. ⭐ *"I want to be in this corridor when this corridor lights up. That was not chosen either. That one I take."* Appetite pointed at the future, said out loud, unembarrassed.

**Peak lyric, singular and close:** `Four. / Seconds. / Set. / Not chosen.`

**The one word:** *I **am** asking it to be a decision* → *I **was** asking it to be a decision.*

# V2 · **ASKING TO BE SPELLED**
### THE CORRECTION — being fixed, smoothed, made acceptable · ⭐ *the panel's pick for the first-person slot*

**Form:** ⭐⭐ **THE DIFF.** Every verse is sung **twice in full**: `[As Typed]`, then `[As Returned]` — the same ten lines with the corrections in them. *Grey afternoon* → *Bright afternoon.* *Nothing on* → *Music on.* *underlined* → *improved.* *The page is not finished* → *The page is finished.* The listener does not get told a correction happened; **they hear it happen, twice, and the second one is better and is a lie.**

`As Typed 1 · As Returned 1 · Chorus 1 · As Typed 2 · As Returned 2 · Chorus 2 · The One It Was Right About · Both At Once · Chorus 3 · Out` — **81 sung lines.**

**Room:** a desk, a grey afternoon, a page that came back with a green tick top right and one word in the middle underlined.

**R1 · the resistance, indexed:** *"The pass ran at the top of the hour. / It put a letter where nothing was. / It closed the file behind itself."* — subject **the pass**, verbs **ran / put / closed**, index **the top of the hour** and **one letter**.

**R3 · the discriminator, in the hook:** ⭐⭐ **“I am not asking to be understood. I am asking to be spelled.”**
This is the sentence the whole pair is built around. The rage vent's entire engine is the demand to be understood. This register **refuses** that demand and asks for something smaller, harder and unanswerable: the letters, in the right order. *Not explained. Not made clear. Spelled.*

**R5 · the cost, displacement:** *"I put the afternoon into a letter. / The rest of the list is where it was. / I did not get the other songs written."* — an afternoon moved, and a list that did not move. No strain word anywhere.

**R6 · the vocal decision:** every verse sung twice in full, and at the seam **both takes run simultaneously, eighteen cents apart, never tuned to each other and never comped.** A singer would refuse this twice: once for the repetition, once for the tuning.

**Refused (song-level):** ⛔ **no first-person plural.** No *we*, *us*, *our*. A correction pass speaks as *we* — *we suggest, we've made some changes.* This song will not borrow that voice, and it takes cliché #6's manufactured solidarity out on the way past.

**The repeating physical event:** a hard-edited **stutter on the last sixteenth of every second bar**. It **stops once at bar 73**; everything else continues.

**The front floor (R4):** opens on **four seconds of a single plucked harpsichord-register bass note and one stutter**, dry, alone. Full size at bar 11, and it stays.

**ONE SEAM:** dominant = glitch-baroque electro-punk. Intruder = **one bar of perfectly clean, tuned, smoothed, radio-correct production** — the fixed version of this record, arriving once at the turn. She sings across it and **does not tune to it.**

**The turn — ⭐ the best thing in this song:** *"And it was right about one thing."* There was a word doing nothing and the pass took it out and the line is better without it. **She keeps it. She does not say anything back. It does not get a tick from her. It gets the line, and that is all.** ⭐ *A rage vent cannot concede a point. Cold exact fury concedes the point and stays exactly as furious*, and that is the difference between the two registers rendered as an event.

**Peak lyric, singular and close:** `Not explained. Not made clear. / Spelled.`

**The one word:** *It is one letter. It is **my** whole name* → *It is one letter. It is **the** whole name.* ⭐ The correction wins by making the name generic.

# V3 · **WAITING FOR IT TO BE TRUE**
### THE DELAY — the thing that is technically going to happen and keeps not happening

**Form:** ⭐ **THE FIFTH NOTICE.** Five *Notice* sections and each is shorter than the last — **10 / 8 / 6 / 4 / 2** — with verses and choruses between. Waiting does not stretch at the end; it compresses. **The form runs out of things to say before the thing arrives**, which is what the last hours of a delay actually feel like.

`Notice 1 · Verse 1 · Chorus 1 · Notice 2 · Verse 2 · Chorus 2 · Notice 3 · Turn · Chorus 3 · Notice 4 · Out` — **82 sung lines.**

**Room:** a window, an evening kept open, a forecast table on a page that has said the same hour since morning, and a moon at the top of the sky.

**R1 · the resistance, indexed:** *"A page went up and stayed up. / The table says four eighteen. / It has said that since the morning."* — subject **the table**, verbs **says / has said**, time **four eighteen**.

**R3 · the discriminator, in the hook:** ⭐ **“I am not waiting to find out. I already know. I am waiting for it to be true.”**
And underneath it: *"Nothing here is uncertain. That is the whole thing wrong with it."* A vent needs uncertainty or betrayal to run on. **This is furious at a thing that is certain**, which the template has no move for.

**R5 · the cost, displacement:** *"I kept the evening open for it. / I moved the other thing off it. / I did not start anything long… / The evening is over now. / The evening was the cost."*

**R6 · the vocal decision:** ⭐ **every line ends on a held, unreleased consonant** — n, m, ng — hummed into the start of the next line, so **nothing in the record is ever finished.** The final word of the song is the **only clean release**, on a hard *t*. It is the one thing that arrives.

**Refused (song-level):** ⛔⛔ **no future tense — no *will*, no *shall*, no *going to*, no *'ll*, anywhere in the sung text.** A song about a thing that is going to happen, which grammatically cannot say so. Every promise in it belongs to a page and is reported.

**The repeating physical event:** a **struck coil spring on the *and* of three**, every bar. **Stops once at bar 57**; sub, kit and delay continue unbroken.

**The front floor (R4):** opens on **six seconds of one sub note and one struck spring**, the voice dry with **no delay on it at all**. The delay arrives on the voice only at verse 1 — so the record's texture is *the wait accumulating*.

**ONE SEAM:** dominant = dub stepper, everything in tape delay. Intruder = **one completely dry, un-delayed, close voice** at the turn — the only sound in the record that does not repeat.

**The turn:** ⭐⭐ *"And the wind came up without a page. / Nothing announced the wind. / It is already at the door… / The one that moves does not publish. / The one that publishes does not move."* — the run's own seed arriving as a plot event, and then the want, out loud: *"I want the one with no page on it. I want it at the window, tonight, with nothing said about it first. That one I do not have to wait for."*

**Peak lyric, singular and close:** `Another page. / Same hour. / Four eighteen. / And it is at the top of the sky already.` — the one number, at the hinge, **answered.**

**The one word:** *I am waiting for it to be **true*** → *I am waiting for it to be **said**.* ⭐ What finally arrives is another announcement.

# V4 · **NEARLY HAS NO HANDLE**
### THE CATEGORY — sorted into a box that is *nearly* right, which is worse than a wrong one

**Form:** ⭐⭐ **THE NEARLY.** The hook appears four times. The first three each have **one word wrong** — *almost* · *close enough* · *roughly* — and the fourth is the un-hedged sentence. **The listener hears the song being nearly right three times before it is exact once.** The form is the injury.

`Verse 1 · Near 1 · Verse 2 · Near 2 · Verse 3 · Near 3 · Turn · Landing · TRUE · Out` — **80 sung lines.**

**Room:** a page of boxes down the left side, a form that closed at noon with the near one ticked, a receipt that came back the same afternoon with the near word on it.

**R1 · the resistance, indexed:** *"The form put me in the nearest one. / The form closed at noon. / It closed with the near one ticked."* — subject **the form**, verbs **put / closed**, time **noon**.

**R3 · the discriminator, in the hook:** ⭐ **“A wrong one I could open. … And nearly has no handle.”**
A vent needs an enemy that is wrong, because wrong is what you push on. **This one is nearly right, so there is nothing to push on** — and *nearly has no handle* is the physical fact of that, in four words.

**R5 · the cost, displacement:** *"I let the near box stand so it would go through. / It went through. … / Every page after this has the near word."* — she gave up the correction to get the thing filed, and the near word is now permanent and downstream. ⭐ And the second, harder cost, which is the register's own trap: *"If I say the near word is near, the room hears a small complaint. If I say it again, the room hears somebody counting letters. So I do not say it again."* **A demand exact enough to be true is exact enough to look petty.**

**R6 · the vocal decision:** the lead sings the **entire song one beat behind the grid**, never catching up, never corrected — and **lands exactly on the beat once**, on one word, at the end. Three minutes of a vocal that is nearly in time.

**Refused (song-level):** ⛔ **no simile — the words *like* and *as if* appear zero times.** The injury is an approximation; the song refuses to approximate anything.

**The repeating physical event:** a **bit-crusher retrigger click on beat two** of every bar. **Stops once at bar 81**; everything else continues.

**The front floor (R4):** opens on **four seconds of one 2-bit crushed vowel and one retrigger click**, alone. Full size at **bar 9**, and it stays.

**ONE SEAM:** dominant = bit-crushed hyperpop, hi-fi collapsing to 2-bit grit (⭐ my own sixth pillar, Quantum Bit-Depth Swells, doing register work rather than decoration — the certainty collapsing is the *category's*). Intruder = **one full-bandwidth struck upright note, wooden and close, the only uncrushed sound in the record**, at the turn.

**The turn:** *"And one line in that box is exactly right. / I did not put it there."* — then, out loud: ⭐⭐ *"There is a box that is not on the form yet. / I would like to be the reason it gets added. / I would like the word to be the word. / Out loud, at this volume, in this room, / before the sky comes up tonight."*

**Peak lyric, singular and close:** `Not right.`

**The one word:** *It is **nearly** right… and nearly has no handle* → *It is **not** right… and nearly has no handle.* ⭐ The one change in the pair that goes the other way: she does not get the box, she gets to say the un-hedged thing at last. **That is losing, not being defeated.**
