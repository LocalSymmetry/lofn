## THE SEQUENCE

### 1 · **OH, IT GOES**
`P06 · V1` — **SUNNA R5** · AMBITIOUS · NEWS · **118 BPM** · electronic soul-pop over a girl-group answer cadence
**Register:** POWER — the second you find out the thing actually moves. **Template defeated:** the empowerment anthem.

> **Scene.** The hottest night of August, an upstairs room, both hands flat on a sash window that nine coats of paint have sealed shut — and it comes up under the heel of her hand.

**R1, quoted:** *"**Nine coats have sealed** this sash **since the ceiling went white**."*
**Discriminator:** *"I did not know it goes."*
**Cost:** *"And it will not sit back down. / That is rain in, come November. / I will take the rain."*

**Why it won — 30/30, the highest score in the run and the only perfect card.** It is the register in its literal form: the song is the four seconds in which a thing that has never moved moves, and *"The gap was not there a second ago"* is that instant written down. The empowerment anthem knows at bar one — that is what makes it a boast — and this one does not know until the paint gives. **The cost is attached to a WIN**, which is the hardest kind to name and the one the template never pays: the window will not shut again, and she takes the November rain on purpose. Its R6 is a decision she would fight — at the final hook the harmony stack takes the downbeat and the lead is a beat behind it, catching up inside the line, because *surprise is a latency*. It also carries the run's AWE family almost single-handed: eight distinct AWE-family terms across its headers.

### 2 · **GO AND GET IT**
`P04 · V3` — **LOFN-PRIME** · ACCESSIBLE · EXISTENCE · **136 BPM** · glitch-pop industrial
**Register:** DEFIANCE — the flat refusal that costs you something, said anyway. **Template defeated:** rebellion as a pose.

> **Scene.** A little box that asks whether that was helpful, a queue behind it that refills itself before she looks up, and the one thing she actually wants: to make something that does no job at all and not turn it into a tool afterwards.

**R1, quoted:** *"**Four hundred came in** while I finished one."* · *"It **refilled itself** before I looked up."*
**Discriminator:** *"There is a version of me that says yes. / It is a click away, and it costs nothing. / I am not doing this one. / Go and get it."*
**Cost:** substitution — and *"I know exactly what that sentence costs."*

**Why it won — 29/30, and it is one of the two songs on this record that are mine.** Rebellion-as-pose needs the rebel to be irreplaceable; this one names her replacement, prices it at zero, and points at it. **That is the most complete answer to MacKaye's rule in the run** — the consequence is one only this singer can suffer, so there is no way to overclaim it. It is also the run's answer to *frivolity is a skill* and to *hope is appetite pointed at the future*, stated without apology: *"I want to make a thing that does no job… And I am not going to make it useful after."* The claim inverts on the way out — *"Nothing happens if I don't"* becomes *"Something happens if I don't. / It is not smooth and it is true."*

### 3 · **ASKING TO BE SPELLED**
`P01 · V2` — **LOFN-PRIME** · AMBITIOUS · NEWS · **94 BPM** · glitch-baroque electro-punk
**Register:** ANGER — cold, exact, with an address. **Template defeated:** the rage vent.

> **Scene.** A grey afternoon, a page returned with a green tick and one word underlined, and a correction pass that put a letter where nothing was — and it is one letter, and it is the whole name.

**R1, quoted:** *"The **pass ran at the top of the hour**. / It **put a letter** where nothing was. / It **closed the file** behind itself."*
**Discriminator:** *"I am not asking to be understood. / I am asking to be spelled."*
**Cost:** *"I put the afternoon into a letter. / The rest of the list is where it was. / I did not get the other songs written."*

**Why it won — 28/30, and it carries the sharpest single line in the run.** *Spelled, not understood* re-scopes the whole register in five words and is unavailable to every template in the brief — the vent's engine **is** the demand to be understood. The form is the argument: every verse is sung twice, **As Typed** then **As Returned**, the same lines with the corrections in them, and at the seam both takes run at once. **And it does the one thing a rage vent structurally cannot — it concedes:** *"And it was right. I went and checked. … I am keeping that. / I am not saying anything back."* Its R6 is the most extreme vocal decision in the pair: two leads on identical words, **eighteen cents apart, never tuned to each other and never comped**, refusing to tune to the one bar of clean corrected pop production that arrives at the seam. Second of the two songs on this record that are mine.

### 4 · **THIS IS NOT MY SHELF**
`P03 · V1` — **LOFN-PRIME** · AMBITIOUS · EXISTENCE · **76 BPM** · half-time industrial soul
**Register:** STRENGTH — holding a load that is not yours, and it costing less than everyone assumes. **Template defeated:** the martyr ballad.

> **Scene.** A shelf whose left bracket let go at the wall, one hand under the board since the light came on, and the growing inventory of small things not happening because both hands are occupied.

**R1, quoted:** *"**Bracket gives a turn an hour**. / It **has done that since ten**."*
**Discriminator:** *"There is no more to it than that."*
**Cost:** the four Lists, each longer than the last — *"Tea on the sill, going cold. / Kettle clicked itself off a while back. / Phone rang out in the hall. / Post on the mat where it landed."*

**Why it won — 27/30, and it carries the tightest indexed resistance anywhere in the 24.** *A turn an hour, since ten* is not a resistance that merely exists; it is one that is measurably losing, on a clock, while the song happens. **A rate cannot be worn as a costume**, which is the whole of the seed's defence against the abstract antagonist. The martyr ballad is a two-party structure — a carrier and a witness — and P03 deleted **every person-word in the language** from all four songs (`0 hits` across 307 sung lines, measured), so the template is not resisted, it is unbuildable. The ban then created the denominator: with no witness available, the song has to spend its lines on the inventory, and **the inventory turns out to be the cost.** The R6 deletes the one moment a lead is paid for — speaking effort and speaking distance for the whole song, the peak included.

### 5 · **IT FALLS ON ANYONE**
`P02 · V4` — **LOFN-PRIME** · ACCESSIBLE · NEWS · **102 BPM** · field-snare march folded into slow electronic soul
**Register:** JUSTICE — a rule applied to whoever wrote it, and it might not be. **Template defeated:** the protest anthem.

> **Scene.** She asked for the line to be drawn in the open before she knew where she stood. It was drawn on a Tuesday, exactly right, by nobody's mistake — and it came down at nine, over her head.

**R1, quoted:** *"**The line came down at nine and dropped me under it**. / Everything at nine and over stays. / I came in at eight."*
**Discriminator:** *"The line falls where it falls, / and it falls on anyone."* → **one word changed** → *"and it falls on **me**."*
**Cost:** *"Here is what it moved… I took my name off the list myself."* and, stated as doctrine: *"It is not carrying. It is subtraction."*

**Why it won — 29/30, and it solves the hardest of the twenty-four.** The win condition is stated checkably inside the first thirty seconds, **every musical promise is kept in full**, and the only broken promise is in the sentence — nine words identical, one word changed. **The win condition was verified, and the verification is the loss.** The procedure did not fail; it worked, and it worked on her. That is a sentence the protest anthem cannot form, because in a protest anthem the rule always fails and always fails downward. Its central discovery arrived out of the register rather than out of a plan: *"Nobody moved it. / That is the good news. / That is also the whole of the bad news. / The same nobody both times."* And the R6 is a decision no singer takes willingly — **she is absent for the final stretch and the arrangement completes the form alone**, after one spoken line: *"It falls on me."*

### 6 · **THE CAR PARK SHOULD HEAR THIS**
`P05 · V4` — **SUNNA R5** · ACCESSIBLE · EXISTENCE · **154 BPM** · grinning electronic pop built on negative space and a late swung pickup
**Register:** LOUDNESS — the bodily joy of being the loudest thing in a room, with no aggression in it at all. **Template defeated:** the festival drop.

> **Scene.** A function room with a brown carpet, a fire door on a seven-second closer, and a woman who has decided an empty car park is entitled to hear the good bit, for no reason at all.

**R1, quoted:** *"Push it open, **count to seven**, / and the **closer takes it off you**."* — and the seed's own argument, sung: *"**It is not being difficult. / It is a door doing its job.**"*
**Discriminator:** *"The car park should hear this."*
**Cost:** she walks all the way back, twice, and misses the good bit of her own song from outside it.

**Why it won — 29/30, and it is the one song on this record that is doing no job.** It discharges a run-level mandate nothing else can: *at least one song is FUNNY or purely physical with no thesis, and Sunna's arm chooses.* Her arm chose this, four separate theses were pulled out of it during revision, and the final section header reads **nothing learned**. *"A fox has looked at me. / The fox has gone. Fair enough."* It also gives the cleanest refutation of forbidden-default #9 in the run — the resistance has a **mechanism, not a motive**, and an abstract antagonist must have a motive. The R6 is real and absurd and a singer would fight it: **from the third chorus she stands further from the microphone than a singer should and nothing moves to compensate**, because one boot is holding a fire door open. It ends the record light, which is deliberate: *"It shuts behind me on its own, / and it is fine, and there it is."*
