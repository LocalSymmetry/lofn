## 1. THE REGISTER, IN MY OWN WORDS

Lofn would write this song about what the volume costs. That is her disappointment doing its usual thing — she looks at a room going off and finds the part of it that is a shame.

**I am writing the other thing.** Being the loudest thing in a room is a **bodily** pleasure and it is completely innocent. Nothing is being defeated. Nobody is being shouted down. It is the same pleasure as pushing a heavy door and finding it swings — you are simply, briefly, **the largest event in a bounded space**, and the space tells you so by moving.

⭐ **So the register's whole engineering problem is: how do you show volume without an enemy and without a crowd?**

**The answer, and it is the spine of all four songs: OBJECTS THAT MOVE.**
A window buzzing in old putty. A strip light ticking. A bracket knocking a shared wall. A fire door swinging back on its closer. **A moving object is not a level and cannot be normalised away** — it is a *fact about the world at that level*, and it survives the master, the platform, and the listener's own volume knob.

⛔ And it is the exact opposite of the two things every loud template reaches for: **a level word** and **a crowd**.

## 2. ⛔ HOW I DEFEAT THE SCHEDULE — FIVE MOVES, NONE OF THEM INTENSITY

The festival drop is made of two things: **a predictable onset** and **rising intensity into it**. That is also, per the LeDoux seat, the exact definition of *looming* — which is why the template reads as mild threat even when it is trying to be euphoric. **Remove the ramp and you remove both problems at once.**

**MOVE 1 — ⭐⭐ ONE ARRIVAL, AT SECOND FIVE, AND NOTHING GETS BIGGER AGAIN.**
Every one of the four buys its floor at the **FRONT** (R4), spends it inside five seconds, arrives at full size, and **stays exactly there for the rest of the record.** There is no second arrival in any of the four songs. A timetable needs a build to be a timetable; there is no build to schedule.
⭐ *This is me going one step past the ruling.* R4 says do not buy loudness with an internal drop-out. I am also refusing to buy it with an internal **rise**. After second five, none of my four songs has a moment quieter or smaller than any other moment. **I think that, not the front floor, is the actual answer to a schedule** — and it is falsifiable, see §7.

**MOVE 2 — ⭐⭐ EVERY SCHEDULED EVENT IS A SUBTRACTION.**
R4 requires one repeating physical event that **stops once** while everything else continues. I have four, and they are the only "big moments" in the four records:

| song | the repeating physical event | it stops at | what continues |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | a loose window buzzing in its putty on every upstroke | **bar 57** — a hand goes flat on the glass | everything |
| V2 | a fluorescent tube ticking on the offbeat | **bar 65** — the tube goes out | everything |
| V3 | a fire-extinguisher bracket knocking the shared wall on 2 and 4 | **bar 49** — *the other room* stops for four bars | her side, at full size |
| V4 | the door closer hissing and thumping every seven seconds | **bar 53** — a boot goes under it | everything, plus her, from further away |

⛔ **A subtraction cannot be a drop.** There is nowhere for hands to go up. And each stop is caused by a **hand, a filament, a decision in another room, or a boot** — never by an automation lane.

**MOVE 3 — ⭐ TIME IN ALL FOUR SONGS REMOVES; IT NEVER DELIVERS.**
Eleven o'clock **ends** V1. The hall in V2 is permanently **half a second behind**. The wall in V3 makes counting **impossible**. The closer in V4 takes the open door back every **seven seconds**. In the template, the clock is the thing that hands you the euphoria. **Here the clock is the thing that takes the room away, and the song is delighted anyway.** That is the register in one sentence.

**MOVE 4 — ⭐ THE CONSEQUENCE CLASS ROTATES, AND NOT ONE OF THE FOUR IS A DROP.**
*(Sunna R5, binding law 4. Across this set of four, my answer-to-drop count is **ZERO**, not one — the set's single permitted club/drop consequence is available to another pair and I do not want it.)*

| song | event | ⭐ changed layer | ⛔ invariant layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | the hand on the glass | **percussion grid** — the buzz leaves the offbeat, the clap stays | the **sub**, dead still, whole song |
| V2 | the tube goes out | **vocal harmony** — the answering stack drops a fifth below the lead | the **kit**, unchanged, whole song |
| V3 | the other room stops for four bars | **offbeat relation** — her downbeat becomes their offbeat and stays there | the **bass melody**, note-identical, whole song |
| V4 | the boot goes under the door | **spectrum** — the top of the record opens; nothing gets louder | the **tempo and the grid**, untouched |

**MOVE 5 — ⭐ NO FOUR-ON-THE-FLOOR, NO RISER, NO SIDECHAIN, ANYWHERE IN THE FOUR.**
Not as a prohibition in a style field — as a fact about what I wrote. Three of the four are felt in **half-time** by the inherited body while the chassis runs at the assigned tempo.

**Sunna's hook law:** four to eight syllables, no interval a normal throat cannot reach at volume and out of breath, **built to be sung back.**

## 7. ⚠️ THE QUESTION THE EIGHTEEN-SEAT PANEL COULD NOT SETTLE, AND IT IS MINE

> *Can a normalised stream **DELIVER** loudness-without-aggression, or only **DEPICT** it?*

**I am not going to pretend this is solved, and I am not going to pretend my four songs settle it.**

Here is what I actually think. **Loudness-as-dominance is a room phenomenon, and normalisation deletes exactly that variable — so the level is gone, and it is not coming back.** I accept that completely. The bet I am making is that **level was never the thing**, and that what survives the master is not a magnitude but a **consequence**: a window buzzing is not a level, it is a *fact about the world at that level*, and a fact survives normalisation, the platform, and the listener's own volume knob. So my four songs are built entirely out of consequences and contain **no level instruction of any kind.**

⚠️ **And here is the honest part, which costs me something to write: my four songs cannot distinguish the two cases.** All four **depict** moving objects — that is the whole design. To actually decide *deliver vs depict*, the experiment needs an arm that depicts nothing at all and relies purely on production, and **I did not write that arm, because it would be a worse song.** I would rather ship four good songs and an open question than one clean instrument and three compromises. That is a choice and it is on me.

⭐ **What I would hand Skaði as the expedition, and it is cheap:** the blind-listening panel is currently asked *"how loud is this record?"* — a question normalisation has already answered for them. **Ask them "how loud is the ROOM?" instead.** I do not think we have ever asked the first question, and it is the only one my songs are trying to answer.
