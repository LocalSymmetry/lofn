# V1 · **THIS IS NOT MY SHELF** — 76 BPM

**Angle:** NO CEREMONY — picked up without announcement, put down without mention.
⛔ **Collapse template:** THE QUIET MARTYR (*nobody saw, and I am telling you nobody saw*).

## The scene, in one sentence a stranger could retell
A shelf's left bracket let go at the wall; a hand went under the board; the board is still up, and everything that needs two hands has stopped.

## Form — **THE LIST THAT GROWS**
`OPEN(5) → LIST 1(9) → HOLD(5) → LIST 2(10) → HOLD(5) → LIST 3(11) → HOLD(5) → SEAM(7) → LIST 4(9) → HOLD-FINAL(5) → COOLING LIST(6) → CLOSE(5)`

⭐ **The mechanic:** the lists are not verses. They are inventories of things that are not happening, and **each list is longer than the last** — the denominator, written. Nothing in a list has a pronoun. **The HOLD returns byte-identical three times** (L59: in a three-pass structure, *any* variation reads as direction; the only non-directional set is one that returns identical) and moves exactly one word at the fourth.

## Sound
Half-time industrial soul, 76 BPM, **one guitar voicing held for the entire song — no chord change anywhere.** Opens on a screw ticking against plaster in a dry room and one contralto at speaking distance, nothing else, for five seconds. A synthetic sub enters at bar 5 and **never releases** — SCR-01 UNBROKEN BUREAUCRACY: the dead-level continuity is more relentless than a break would have been, and the record therefore **cannot get quiet**, which is L60's *make it necessary, not forbidden.* Brushed metal on the offbeat only, no acoustic kit. Micro-edited digital debris (glitch pillar).
⭐ **Signature device, and it is permanent rather than a rupture:** at the seam the guitar drops to 2-bit grit **and stays there for the rest of the record** — a *Quantum Bit-Depth Swell* that never swells back. A requested one-bar rupture disappears; a permanent state change cannot be averaged away.

## R6 — the vocal decision
**Speaking effort and speaking distance for the entire song, the peak included. No projected consonants anywhere.** The peak is carried by everything except the voice. A singer would fight this: it removes the one moment she is paid for.

## The obligations, placed
- **R1 (indexed):** *"Bracket gives a quarter turn an hour. / It has done that since ten."*
- **R5 (displacement):** the tea, the kettle, the post, the phone, the drill on the floor, **the room across the room.**
- **R3 (hook):** *"There is no more to it than that."*
- **R4 (stops once):** the screw tick, one per bar, **stops at the seam** while sub, guitar and brushed metal continue.
- **Peak, singular and close:** *"My hand. The board. That is the whole of it."*
- **Checkable line:** *"Screw goes back in and the board sits flat."* → returns three times verbatim inside the HOLD → final: **flat → empty.** ⭐ The win condition technically arrives and is worthless: the board is flat because I took everything off it. **Every musical promise kept; the only broken promise is in the sentence.**

# V2 · **A MEASUREMENT, NOT A PROMISE** — 80 BPM

**Angle:** BUILT FOR IT — someone else's weight, and the discovery that you are shaped to carry exactly that.
⛔ **Collapse template:** THE VOCATION SONG (*I was made for this*) — the empowerment anthem wearing humility.

## The scene
A load on a rope, held by a ratchet whose pawl is slipping; a hand happens to be the exact span of the handle; the hand is on the handle.

## Form — **CONGRUENT / NOT CONGRUENT**
`OPEN(4) → FIGURE 1(8) → FIT(6) → FIGURE 2(9) → FIT(6) → FIGURE 3(10) → FIT(6) → SEAM(7) → FIGURE 4(8) → FIT-FINAL(6) → CLOSE(5)`

⭐ **The mechanic, and it is the angle made audible:** in every FIGURE section the lead's phrasing is **note-for-note congruent with the bass figure** — same rhythm, same length, same landing, all song. *Built for it* is not asserted, it is heard. **A high answering voice sits above and is never congruent with anything**, and never joins the lead. ⛔ The song never says *made for*, *meant to*, *chosen* or *born to*; the fit is a geometry, never a merit.

## Sound
80 BPM, slow wide-stride bass ballad with mechanical percussion. Opens on one bass note and a pawl click, alone, four seconds. Tuned wooden percussion; a kick dragged fifteen milliseconds behind a grid-locked bass so the pair limps (the kinetic defect); synthetic sub; no acoustic kit until the tuned wood, which is struck and dry. Answering voice staged above and behind, never doubled with the lead.
⭐ **Signature device:** at the seam the **pawl stops and the bass figure keeps going** — the thing that was holding the rope stops, and the rope does not fall, because the hand is what was holding it. The arrangement arrives at full size at the seam and **stays** rather than growing (aggression is a contour: arrive and stay, never loom).

## R6 — the vocal decision
**The lead never takes the top note of her own melody.** Every high line belongs to the answering voice; the lead stays underneath for the entire song and never once goes above it. A singer would have to be talked into surrendering her own ceiling.

## The obligations, placed
- **R1 (indexed):** *"Pawl has dropped one tooth since the yard lights came on."*
- **R5 (displacement):** not on the other side of the yard; the tools set down stay down; the gate stays open because the hand that shuts it is on the handle.
- **R3 (hook):** *"It fits the way I am made. / That is a measurement, not a promise."*
- **R4 (stops once):** the pawl click, one per bar, stops at the seam; bass, sub and wood continue.
- **Peak, singular and close:** *"One hand. One rope. It fits."*
- **Checkable line:** *"Pawl catches on the next tooth and the rope holds itself."* → final: **itself → me.**

# V3 · **I AM NOT SPENDING IT** — 74 BPM *(slowest song in the run)*

**Angle:** HOLDING STILL — the strength is in *not* doing the thing, under provocation.
⛔ **Collapse template:** THE RESTRAINT FLEX (*look how much I did not say*).

## The scene
A screen face-up on a table comes up every four minutes and goes back down. A sentence is finished and ready. A hand is on the wood. Neither the hand nor the sentence moves.

## Form — **THE SENTENCE, UNSPENT**
`OPEN(4) → NEAR 1(8) → HELD(5) → NEAR 2(9) → HELD(5) → NEAR 3(10) → HELD(5) → SEAM(6) → FURTHEST(9) → HELD-FINAL(5) → CLOSE(6)`

⭐ **The mechanic:** the sentence is present as a **shape** from bar one and is never said. Each NEAR section is one degree closer to saying it — more of its rhythm surfaces in the phrasing, more of its consonants land — and then ⭐ **the last section is the FURTHEST away of the four**, not the closest. The form does not resolve toward the release; it walks back. ⛔ And the song never reports how large the withheld thing is; the flex is exactly the tell I filed.

## Sound
74 BPM, skeletal cold-wave soul. Opens on a knuckle on wood on the *and* of 3 and one low alto, alone, four seconds.
⭐ **The harmonic engine is a physical impossibility, not a prohibition (L60):** a **suspended fourth that never resolves to the third — the third is not present in the record at any point.** The song is built so it *cannot* release. Plucked low string doubling the root; flat palm on wood where a clap would be; synthetic sub; no cymbals at all, so nothing can wash. Micro-edited stutter on one word per section (SCR-06: an ordinary pop stutter becomes the one production event that enacts the word).

## R6 — the vocal decision
⛔ **No held vowels anywhere in the song. Every line stopped on a consonant, cut, no decay, for three minutes.** A singer's whole instinct is to hold; denying every hold is the ask, and it *is* the angle — the song holds nothing, which is why it is still holding.

## The obligations, placed
- **R1 (indexed):** *"Screen comes up every four minutes and goes back down."*
- **R5 (displacement):** ⭐ **the cost is the sentence — built, kept, unspent.** Not effort withheld; a made object not handed over. That is the register's cleanest instance: **displacement of a thing that exists**, not a report of restraint.
- **R3 (hook):** *"I have the sentence. I am not spending it."*
- **R4 (stops once):** the knuckle on the *and* of 3 stops once at the seam and the hand goes flat; string, palm and sub continue.
- **Peak, singular and close:** *"Flat hand. Wood. Nothing said."*
- **Checkable line:** *"Screen stays down and the sentence gets said."* → final: **said → kept.**

# V4 · **SIDEWAYS AT DOORS** — 78 BPM

**Angle:** THE SHAPE IT LEFT — carried so long the body changed around it, and that is not a complaint.
⛔ **Collapse template:** THE SCAR SONG (*here is what it did to me*).

## The scene
A strap and a case that are not mine, carried daily; the strap takes a notch back twice a day and gives none of it up; and the hand now closes to the size of the handle when it is empty, and the body turns sideways at doorways that are wide open.

## Form — **A LOOP WITH A VISIBLE SPLICE**
`OPEN(5) → PASS 1(9) → SHAPE(6) → PASS 2(10) → SHAPE(6) → SEAM(8) → PASS 3(7) → SHAPE-WIDE(9) → SHAPE-FINAL(6) → CLOSE(6)`

⭐ **The mechanic:** a two-bar loop whose edit point is **audible and never repaired.** The song is built on the flaw the way the body is built on the load. ⭐ **And the SHAPE section widens by one line each time while the melody's range narrows** — the words get more and the reach gets less, which is the finding stated without stating it. ⛔ Nothing is ever named as damage; the changed body is described only in the **present tense of shape**, never the past tense of loss.

## Sound
78 BPM, slow future-nostalgic soul with 8-bit residue (video-game pillar). Opens on the loop alone with its splice audible, four seconds, then one warm alto. **A tape-replay keyboard with audible flutter and a short note ceiling** — mechanism, never a trade name. A buckle tongue ticking against a frame once a bar. Synthetic sub. Kick placed slightly early against the loop so the two never quite agree — the kinetic defect, and it never gets fixed either.
⭐ **Signature device:** the loop's splice is left in the final mix at every repetition and is **louder in relation to everything else at the end than at the start**, because the arrangement thins around it rather than the splice growing.

## R6 — the vocal decision
**One audible breath in the same place in every chorus, never edited out — and the phrase is written around the gap.** The melody makes room for it. A singer would want that breath gone; here the breath is the shape the load left, and removing it removes the subject.

## The obligations, placed
- **R1 (indexed):** *"Strap takes a notch back twice a day and gives none of it up."*
- **R5 (displacement):** the hand's rest shape; the line taken through every doorway; the width now unavailable.
- **R3 (hook):** *"I turn sideways at doors that are wide open. / That is where it went."*
- **R4 (stops once):** the buckle tongue stops once at the seam; loop, keyboard and sub continue.
- **Peak, singular and close:** *"This hand. This width. Here."*
- **Checkable line:** *"Strap goes back to the notch it started at."* → final: **the → my.**
- ⭐ **The sky, and it is the only world material in the four:** *at maximum and still filling* — the moon 100% illuminated and not exactly full until morning — placed as an image at the seam, unattributed, no number, no instrument named. **Force and volume are different measurements today.**
