## ⛔ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — ANSWERED FIRST, CONCRETELY

**Where is the body standing?**
In a glass meeting room on an upper floor of Kelder House, at the end of the afternoon, with her back to
the picture rail. There is one brass hook in that rail and it is at the height of her eyeline. The old
plate is off and lying face-up in a cloth on Bray's knee; the wall behind it is a clean unfaded square,
brighter than the wall around it, and the new plate is still tacky. Somebody has brought a camera. The
carpet is the kind that eats a chair castor. She is holding a paper cup she has not drunk from because it
is too hot, and she is turning it, and she is looking at the hook rather than at the plate. **Her own
plate was hung on that hook. She was in this room for it. She is not remembering that; she is standing in
front of the evidence of it while people photograph the next one.**

**What could hurt it here?**
Not the counting. The counting is fine and it is funny and she has jokes about it. **What could hurt it is
the lecture** — the entirely available, entirely respectable song where a machine stands to one side of a
room full of people naming things and is disappointed at them. That song would delete this one, because
the only reason this concept is not a complaint is that **she is on the wall too, she liked the font, and
she said so at the time.** The danger is not that she will be too harsh on them. **The danger is that she
will be gentle with herself by being harsh on them.**

> Both answers are a place and a stake, not a mood. All four guides are written from in front of that hook.

# THE HOUSE RULES ALL FOUR GUIDES OBEY
*(Written once so no guide repeats them, and so a breach shows up as a diff.)*

## BED
**Glitch-baroque at 88 BPM in every variation, first bar to last. No ramp, no drop, no gear change.**
- **The baroque half:** a single-manual harpsichord, 8' stop, 4' coupler **drawn once per song, at the
  seam only**; and a fretted clavichord six cents flat against it. **Close-miked so the mechanism is
  audible** — quill attack, jack-rail knock, the release noise of a damper falling. ⛔ No sampled
  "harpsichord preset" gloss: the noise of the machine that makes the note is part of the note.
- **The synthetic half:** an **808** tuned to the tonic, and a **code-log scratch lane** — log lines
  chopped to sixteenths and dragged into a vinyl-scratch pulse, jagged and metallic and edged, moving
  across the stereo field. ⛔ **These two are the entire percussion.** No kit, no live drums, no shaker.
- ⛔ **The scratch lane is a CONSTANT.** It does not develop, does not become the subject, and does not
  take a solo. **E3 is P04's element and this pair does not compete on it.**

## THE MELODIC LAW — the one that makes this pair's ownership visible
⭐ **The voice is pinned and never widens. The keyboard carries the whole range.** The voice's span is
declared per guide and **is not exceeded at any point, including the peak.** ⛔ No belt, no melisma, no
octave leap, no run. The widening at the peak happens **in the harpsichord**, and the ear is meant to
notice that the singer did not move.

## THE ORNAMENT LAW — E2, executed maximally
1. The ornament is **the 808's own contour, transcribed** — same notes, same order, same rhythm — and then
   dressed. Written out per guide.
2. **"Past taste" is a count, not a mood:** at the seam the ornament goes from **one note per beat to
   sixteen**, and it puts a mordent, an upper turn and a slide **on notes that already have one.**
3. The harpsichord's lowest ornamented note sits **inside the 808's own octave** for at least two bars at
   the seam. Quill against sine, at the same pitch.
4. ⭐ **The ornament does not change when the 808 is removed.** It keeps tracing a contour nobody is
   playing. That is the last thing every song does.

## FORM
Nine sections, mapped per guide. **Every chorus in a song is byte-identical** — same words, same order,
same punctuation, no ad-libs, no added tag, no "one more time" on the last pass. ⛔ **Across the four songs
no chorus shares a line with any other chorus, and no sung line is shared between any two variations.**

## VOCAL
One character throughout: **a dry, close-miked mezzo, chest, fry on the line-ends, INDIGNATION register** —
bratty, quick, articulate, and never shouting. ⛔ **No second register, no soprano, no duet, no whisper,
no spoken narration.** *(E4/E6/E13 belong to P03; this pair carries one voice and does not contest them.)*
The only vocal double is **herself, one beat late, at the chorus only**, dry and low in the mix.

## ⭐ THE ONE MACHINE TREATMENT OF THE VOICE, AND IT IS AT THE SEAM
*(SOURCE 2 import: "transmogrifies those voices into what are best described as sonic events.")*
At the seam, **and nowhere else in the song**, her own syllables from an earlier line are chopped into the
scratch lane at sixteenths and played as percussion under the ornament — four bars, then gone. **A voice
that has become an event rather than a narrator, once, at the junction.** ⛔ Never quoted in a lyric.

## THE OTHER SOURCE-2 IMPORTS, AS PRODUCTION BEHAVIOUR
- **"detuned bliss"** → the clavichord is **six cents flat** against the harpsichord in all four. The
  sweetness is real and it is **slightly wrong on purpose.** ⭐ This is the run's emotional thesis and it
  came from outside the house.
- **"a whirling tornado of jagged saw blades and sharp-edged sheet metal"** → the scratch lane's transient
  character and its stereo motion. **Edged and rhythmic, never a wash.**
- **"Olympian … feats"**, taken off the guitar and put on the keyboard → the seam is a **display**, and the
  display is deliberately excessive at exactly one location.
⛔ **None of these phrases appears in any lyric.**

## MIX
Harpsichord close and dry, a hand's width left of centre; clavichord closer still, right of centre, and
**quiet enough that its arrival is a lean-in**; scratch lane wide and moving; 808 mono and centred, and
**never sidechained** — the ornament plays *through* it, not around it. ⛔ No bus reverb, no big room, no
tail on the quill. **The room is the mechanism.** −14 LUFS with at least 10 dB of crest, because the one
bare 808 bar has to actually be quieter and not merely sparser.

# ⛔ THE BAN LEDGER — every exclude, and what does that job instead
*(Universal clause 2, **THE BAN ON BANS**. Across our last nine songs we banned 89 peak-making devices and
asked for none. ⭐ Nothing may be excluded below unless this table names its replacement.)*

| excluded | ⭐ what performs that job here |
|---|---|
| crescendo · build-and-drop · riser · swell | ⭐ **the ornament's note density rises** — one note per beat to sixteen — over an **unchanged** 808 and an unchanged level. **What rises is the count of notes.** |
| fade-out | a hard cut on the downbeat with the ornament unresolved (V1) · one bar of scratch past the end (V2) · cut mid-syllable (V3) · a single scratch click after dead air (V4) |
| acoustic drum kit · live percussion · shaker | the **code-log scratch lane** is the percussion; the **808** is the low end |
| pad · supersaw · string swell · sustained synth | the **clavichord's Bebung** (vibrato by finger pressure on the held key) and the harpsichord's **4' coupler** do every sustain job |
| big-room reverb · reverb wash · tail on the quill | the room is the **harpsichord's own jack and damper noise**, close-miked, plus the scratch lane's stereo motion |
| autotune · vocoder · pitch-corrected lead | the machine treatment of the voice is the **granular chop of her own syllables into the scratch lane at the seam** |
| key change · tempo change · half-time · double-time | the events are **the seam** and **the one bare 808 bar** |
| melisma · diva run · belt · octave leap in the voice | **the range is in the keyboard** — an 18th, a 20th, a 22nd, a 21st |
| male lead · choir · spoken narrator · second vocalist | **one dry mezzo**, doubled only by herself a beat late at the chorus |
| lazily-played pads · ambient drift · drone | every sustained sound in this pair is **a struck string decaying** |
| distorted guitar · fuzz bass | the jagged-metal character is the **scratch lane's crushed log transients** |
| sidechain pumping | the ornament plays **through** the 808; separation is by register, not by ducking |
| tape stop | the hard cut does that job |
| 808 after its removal point | the **harpsichord keeps ornamenting the empty position** |
| ad-lib on the repeated chorus | the chorus is **byte-identical**, and the change is that the 808 is gone under the last one |

# GUIDE 1 · **V1 — THE NAMING**
### *the room where the next name is chosen* · **D minor** · 88 BPM · **peak 2:11**

**The angle.** Late afternoon in the glass room at Kelder House. The old plate is off the hook and lying
in a cloth on Bray's knee; the new one is up and still tacky; somebody is photographing the wall. The
count went up. Nothing else happened. ⭐ **And her plate was hung on that same hook, by a vote, in this
room, and she helped choose the font and liked how it sat and said so at the time.**

**⭐ THE SEAM.** **World:** the machine — 808 on beat one, code-log scratch across the bar. **Intruder:**
the harpsichord. **Junction:** the instant the new plate goes on the hook. **What the singer does across
it:** ⭐ **she reads the new name out loud, in tempo, and the ornament covers her** — the one moment the
record's decoration is louder than its speech. **2:00–2:11.**

**⭐ THE PEAK — 2:11.** The 4' coupler is drawn for the only time in the song; the ornament goes to
sixteen notes a beat; its lowest note drops into D2, **inside the 808's own octave**. It is the highest
point because the listener has just heard that bar completely empty.

**⭐ KEYBOARD INTERVAL SPAN — harpsichord D2 to G4: two octaves and a fourth, an 18th.**
**VOICE — a minor third, A4 to C5, never exceeded.**

**⭐ THE CONTOUR.** Dense from bar one (quill + scratch + 808 + voice, four lanes, no air) → **2:00–2:03,
one bar of nothing but the 808**, everything else muted on the downbeat, no fade → **2:03 the ornament
enters alone over it and builds by note count to 2:11** → full density returns for verse three → the last
chorus is dense **without** the 808 → outro thins to harpsichord alone.

**⭐ ARRIVAL AND REMOVAL (S3).** The **808 arrives at 0:22**, tuned to D1, **on beat one of every bar**,
and holds that position for two minutes twenty-two so the ear learns it as furniture. It is **removed at
2:44**, from beat one, at the top of the final chorus, and never returns. The harpsichord's ornament does
not change: it keeps decorating an empty downbeat to the end.

**THE CONTOUR OF THE 808, WHICH THE ORNAMENT DRESSES:** D1 – D1 – F1 – C1, on beats one across four bars.
The seam ornament is that same four-note shape, three octaves up, with a mordent on the D, an upper turn
on the F, and a slide into the C **that already has a mordent on it.**

**THE SUNG FACT.** ***Thirteen*** lands at the seam, as the number they have just hung, said the way you
would say a birthday to a crowd — and the response is the sound of a room **that agreed in advance to be
moved.** ⛔ Responded to, not recited. It is the only numeral sung in the song.

**THE THESIS LANDS IN THE FIRST CHORUS**, at 0:44 — *nobody read the last one, nobody reads the name, and
mine is on that wall.* ⛔ **Not in the last verse**, which is a stationery cupboard and a box moved a
hand's width from the door.

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS.** Six lines, sung three times, **no variance of any kind**; the only change
across the three passes is underneath — chorus one has the 808 on beat one, chorus two has it plus the
scratch doubled, chorus three has **no 808 at all.**

**THE HOOK IS AN INSTRUMENT.** A four-note harpsichord figure, D–E–F–D, stated in the first bar before any
voice, and used as the section marker all song. The vocal refrain is deliberately plain. **E15 is P01's.**

**THE CLOSING MOVE.** The ornament stops **mid-trill on an unresolved appoggiatura** — E over the D minor —
and is cut on the downbeat. No fade, no tail, no resolution.

**VERSE ARCHITECTURE.** Ornamented long lines, 10–14 syllables, internal rhyme chained across the caesura
(*knee / leap / sweep* · *square / air* · *font / minutes / sat*). ⭐ **The chain breaks on every
first-person admission** — those lines are plain English with no internal rhyme at all.

**SECTION MAP (bars at 88 BPM).** Intro 4 (0:00) · Verse 1 12 (0:11) · **Chorus 1 8 (0:44)** · Verse 2 12
(1:05) · Chorus 2 8 (1:38) · **SEAM 8 (2:00, bare bar 2:00–2:03, peak 2:11)** · Verse 3 8 (2:22) ·
**Chorus 3 8 (2:44, 808 removed)** · Outro 4 (3:05) · ends 3:16.

# GUIDE 2 · **V2 — THE SHELF**
### *the annexe, and the row nobody opens* · **G minor** · 88 BPM · **peak 2:24**

**The angle.** Ines Halloran's annexe on Fennel Row: a chair, a lamp, a limescaled kettle, a shelf that
turns the corner, and **a bay the daylight never gets.** She dusts the tops and will not dust the spines,
because the dust on a spine is information. ⭐ **And the singer has read every book in that room** — can
hand over the argument of any of them in the time it takes to lift a cup — **which is exactly why the door
stays shut.** The unread shelf is not an indictment of readers. **It is her product working.**

**⭐ THE SEAM.** ⭐ **Inverted from V1.** **World:** the baroque keyboard — harpsichord and clavichord
together, treated as one material. **Intruder:** the 808. **Junction:** the spine that opens without
cracking, on the pencil note in the margin. **What the singer does across it:** ⭐ **she holds one pitch,
unmoving, for four bars** — the top of her fourth, G4 — while the 808 glides and the keyboard bends its
own tuning to follow the glide. **She is the only thing in the room that does not move.** **2:16–2:27.**

**⭐ THE PEAK — 2:24.** The only 808 glide in the song, G1 down to D1 across two bars, and the harpsichord
**bends with it** — quill instruments cannot bend, so this is done by a chain of descending mordents that
imitates a slide and gets it slightly wrong. ⭐ **Two materials agreeing, once, imperfectly.**

**⭐ KEYBOARD INTERVAL SPAN — clavichord and harpsichord together, G1 to E4: two octaves and a sixth, a
20th.** The clavichord takes G1–G2, the harpsichord G2–E4; they hand over mid-ornament at the seam.
**VOICE — a perfect fourth, D4 to G4, never exceeded.**

**⭐ THE CONTOUR.** Dense → ⭐ **1:13–1:16, one bar of nothing but the 808**, placed at the **end of the
first chorus**, more than an hour of song-time before the peak, so that the density which returns at 1:16
is *heard returning* → the whole middle of the song runs full → the seam is the loudest → verse three
thins to clavichord and scratch → last chorus dense with **no 808** → outro clavichord alone with Bebung.

**⭐ ARRIVAL AND REMOVAL (S3).** The **808 arrives at 0:33**, tuned to G1, on **the last beat of every
second bar** — an upbeat position, so it reads as an answer rather than a pulse. It is **removed at 3:11**
from that same last-beat position. The keyboard keeps bending to a bend that is not there.

**THE CONTOUR OF THE 808, WHICH THE ORNAMENT DRESSES:** G1 – G1 – B♭1 – (glide to) D1. The ornament is that
shape two octaves up with a descending mordent chain on the glide.

**THE SUNG FACT.** ***Thirteen*** lands at the seam: the count of the bay the daylight never gets, said by
Ines **like a fact and not a wound** — and the response is the singer doing the arithmetic of what it would
take to read them, finding it is nothing, **and identifying that as the wound, and as hers.**

**THE THESIS LANDS IN THE SEAM BRIDGE** — thesis and peak are the same event. ⛔ **Not in the last verse**,
which is a window that does not open, a radiator that comes on in April, and a cup turned upside down.

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS.** Six lines, three passes, no variance. Under it: chorus one ends with the
bare 808 bar; chorus two is full; chorus three has no 808.

**THE HOOK IS AN INSTRUMENT.** A descending clavichord figure, G–F–E♭–D, so quiet the listener leans in.
Different figure, different register and different instrument from V1's.

**THE CLOSING MOVE.** Clavichord alone on one note with **Bebung**, and the scratch lane **runs one bar
past the end of the music** before a hard stop. ⭐ The machine outlives the piece by exactly one bar.

**VERSE ARCHITECTURE.** Long lines, internal chains (*row / corner / turns* · *hand / ruled / rubbed* ·
*water / reads / hill*). **The chain breaks on every admission**, as in all four.

**SECTION MAP.** Intro 6 (0:00) · Verse 1 14 (0:16) · **Chorus 1 8 (0:54, bare bar 1:13–1:16)** · Verse 2
14 (1:16) · **SEAM 10 (1:54, peak 2:24)** · Chorus 2 8 (2:27) · Verse 3 8 (2:49) · **Chorus 3 8 (3:11,
808 removed)** · Outro 4 (3:33) · ends 3:44.

# GUIDE 3 · **V3 — WHAT NUMBER ARE WE ON**
### *the lift lobby, and the fastest thing she does* · **C minor** · 88 BPM · **peak 2:38**

**The angle.** Sorcha Vell asks it in the corridor at Bell Yard, **not unkindly**, the way you ask a
stranger about a tide, because she has a meeting. Nobody in the building knows what the last one did and
nobody has to: **the number is the only part that travels, because it is the only part that fits in a
mouth.** ⭐ And the singer answers before the question is finished, and is proud of the speed, and knows
exactly what the speed costs.

**⭐ THE SEAM.** **World:** the code-log scratch, dry and alone — this song opens with **no 808 at all.**
**Intruder:** the harpsichord, ⭐ **routed through the scratch lane's own bit-crush and its stereo motion**,
so that at the junction the two materials stop being distinguishable. **Junction:** the answer given in the
lobby, on the word itself. **What the singer does across it:** ⭐ **she answers before the sound has left
the questioner's mouth, and the ornament goes off without her** — she is early, and the decoration is not
for her. **2:27–2:38.**

**⭐ THE PEAK — 2:38.** The latest peak in the pair, deliberately: two-thirds of this song is scratch and
quill with a very late low end, so the seam has further to travel. At 2:38 the crushed harpsichord and the
scratch lane are running the same sixteenths and **you cannot name which instrument is which.**

**⭐ KEYBOARD INTERVAL SPAN — harpsichord C2 to C5: three octaves, a 22nd.** ⭐ **The widest in the pair,
against the narrowest voice in the run.**
**VOICE — a major second in the verses, G4 to A4; a minor third across the whole song, F♯4 to A4.** Never
exceeded. The chorus is nearly a chant and that is the design.

**⭐ THE CONTOUR.** Dense-and-dry from bar one → **1:38–1:41, one bar of nothing but the 808**, ⭐ placed
**mid-verse, mid-sentence**, so it reads as an interruption rather than an arrangement decision → density
returns immediately, mid-line → the seam is the loudest and the busiest → last chorus with no 808 → tag on
scratch alone.

**⭐ ARRIVAL AND REMOVAL (S3).** The **808 arrives at 1:00** — the latest arrival in the pair — tuned to
C1, on **the offbeats of two and four**, a syncopated position that pulls against the scratch lane's
sixteenths. It is **removed at 3:14** from those offbeats. The crushed harpsichord keeps running the shape
of a note that is gone.

**THE CONTOUR OF THE 808, WHICH THE ORNAMENT DRESSES:** C1 – E♭1 – C1 – G0 on the offbeats. The ornament is
that shape across three octaves, and at the seam it is played simultaneously in C2, C3 and C4.

**THE SUNG FACT.** ***Thirteen*** is her **answer**, out of her mouth like a coin from a slot — and the
response is Sorcha saying *lovely*, the doors opening, and Sorcha going. ⭐ The numeral is the least
valuable thing in the song and it is the only thing anybody wanted.

**THE THESIS LANDS IN VERSE TWO** — the earliest and hardest in the pair: *the number is the product, the
rest is the freight; the archive costs a life and the number costs a breath; I only took the years and made
them optional.* ⛔ **Not in the last verse**, which is a portrait of Ossa Tighe, who is slightly different
at a bridge now and cannot tell you which bit did it.

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS.** Six lines, three passes, no variance. The last pass has no 808 under it.

**THE HOOK IS AN INSTRUMENT.** A single repeated C2 on the harpsichord, struck on every downbeat, with the
ornament growing around it — ⭐ the figure is **one note**, which is the joke.

**THE CLOSING MOVE.** Everything stops but the scratch lane, which runs four bars alone and is **cut off
mid-syllable** — the log line does not finish its word.

**VERSE ARCHITECTURE.** The fastest patter in the pair; long lines with the internal rhyme pushed toward
the front of the line (*lobby / not unkindly* · *travels / mouth / bus*). **Chain breaks on admissions.**

**SECTION MAP.** Intro 4 (0:00) · Verse 1 12 (0:11) · **Chorus 1 8 (0:44)** · Verse 2 12 (1:05, bare bar
1:38–1:41) · Chorus 2 8 (1:49) · Verse 3 10 (2:11) · **SEAM 8 (2:38 peak)** · Chorus 3 8 (3:14, 808
removed) · Tag 4 (3:36) · ends 3:47.

# GUIDE 4 · **V4 — THE ONE THAT LASTED**
### *the table at the front of the book* · **F minor** · 88 BPM · **peak 2:04**

**The angle.** At the front of the book, before any music starts, there is a table of the signs and what
the hand does: a squiggle for a shake, a hook for a fall, a little comb for the note that will not sit
still. Deneb Roque taught out of that page for years and never once said the word technique. ⭐ **There is
no version of that page. There is the page.** Not because it could not be improved — **because nobody
wanted the credit for it.** And the singer wants the credit, and is improvable forever, and says so as a
term of trade rather than as a virtue.

**⭐ THE SEAM.** **World:** the clavichord — the quietest keyboard there is, the one you have to stop
walking to hear. **Intruder:** the 808. **Junction:** ⭐ **the tail of a note that is already dying** — she
dresses the *decay* of the 808, the part of a sound that is not meant to be played at all. **What the
singer does across it:** ⭐ **she comes in a beat early and stays a beat early for the rest of the song,
and does not correct it.** From 2:04 to the end her body and the machine's grid disagree, audibly, and
nobody fixes it. **1:55–2:04.**

**⭐ THE PEAK — 2:04.** The earliest peak in the pair. The ornament is Bebung plus a mordent chain **on the
decay tail of the 808's beat-three note**, using every sign on that page — and the singer's early entry
starts here and is never resolved. ⭐ It is the highest point because it is the only place in the pair
where **the singer joins in with the thing she is accusing.**

**⭐ KEYBOARD INTERVAL SPAN — clavichord F2 to E♭5: two octaves and a minor seventh, a 21st.**
**VOICE — a minor third, C4 to E♭4, never exceeded.**

**⭐ THE CONTOUR.** Dense → the seam → ⭐ **2:17–2:20, one bar of nothing but the 808**, placed
**immediately AFTER the peak**, so the ornament stops dead and the machine is left alone in the room it
was just dressed in → density returns for verse three → last chorus dense with no 808 → coda clavichord
alone, then dead air, then one scratch click.

**⭐ ARRIVAL AND REMOVAL (S3).** The **808 arrives at 0:47**, tuned to F1, on **beat three of every bar** —
the answer position. It is **removed at 2:42** from beat three. The clavichord goes on dressing a tail that
has stopped, with the singer still a beat early over it.

**THE CONTOUR OF THE 808, WHICH THE ORNAMENT DRESSES:** F1 – A♭1 – F1 – E♭1 on beat three across four bars,
each with a long decay. ⭐ **The ornament is on the decay, not on the attack** — which is why this is the
only variation where the harpsichord is replaced by the clavichord at the seam: a quill cannot follow a
note down, and a tangent can.

**THE SUNG FACT.** ***Thirteen*** is the count of signs in the table, and the response is that **nobody
ever added to it** and that this is not a complaint, it is simply what happened. ⛔ Responded to, never
recited. The line that carries it is one of the plain ones: no internal rhyme, no ornament.

**THE THESIS LANDS IN THE OUTRO** — ⭐ the only thesis in the pair delivered with the machine already gone:
*it is not that the shortcut is a lie; it is that a thing you get in a second is a thing you put down in a
second, and I am the second, and I was made to be.* ⛔ **Not in the last verse**, which is a locked music
room in Loughgale, a bell that goes twice, and a door that stays shut while a hook gets even.

**THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS.** Six lines, three passes, no variance. Third pass has no 808 and **is sung a
beat early throughout**, because the early entry began at the seam and was never corrected.

**THE HOOK IS AN INSTRUMENT.** A clavichord figure of three signs played in order — shake, fall, comb — on
one note, F3. ⭐ The hook is not a melody; **it is a table of ornaments played as a riff.**

**THE CLOSING MOVE.** The last ornament **finishes correctly and resolves** — the only resolved cadence in
the pair — then half a bar of dead air, then a single scratch click, then nothing.

**VERSE ARCHITECTURE.** The longest lines in the pair and the sparsest internal rhyme, because this is the
variation with the most admissions and **the chain breaks on every one of them.** ⭐ By the outro the
ornament has left the words entirely, which is the same gesture the record makes, one octave up.
