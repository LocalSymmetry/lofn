## 0. THE SLOT, RESTATED IN MY OWN WORDS BEFORE ANY FACET EXISTS

Ash gave three renders one unqualified compliment: **"a beautiful melody."** She gave the same renders
one unqualified complaint: **"the melody is in a tight range, and not much [to] keep me invested."**

⭐ **Those are the same sentence.** She liked the tune and then ran out of tune to like. The repair is
not *more* melody. It is **melody with somewhere to go** — an interval big enough to be an event, put
in a fixed place, learned, enlarged once, and then taken away.

> **This slot exists to do the compliment on purpose, and the only way to do a melody on purpose is to
> specify it as an INTERVAL and a BAR NUMBER, not as an adjective.**

⭐ **THE ONE RULE THIS SLOT LIVES OR DIES BY:** if the brief anywhere says *"soaring"*, *"beautiful"*,
*"memorable"* or *"a real tune", I have failed*, because none of those are instructions. **A rising
major sixth from D to B, on the downbeat of bar three of every chorus** is an instruction.

## 1. FACET — THE INTERVAL, WHICH IS THE HOOK (E9, owned)

⭐ **THE LEAP IS THE HOOK.** Not a slogan; an interval. The lyric hook (adoptable, 4–8 syllables,
ownable by a room) is **E15 and it belongs to P01** — I carry it at baseline and I do not compete on
it. What I own is the shape of the tune.

**The architecture of the range, derived rather than picked:**

| quantity | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| total span | **a 10th** | assigned |
| the leap | **a rising 6th**, chorus, fixed bar | assigned; it IS the hook |
| the peak | the leap **opens to an octave**, third chorus only | assigned |
| ⇒ top of range | launch note **+ an octave** | forced by the peak |
| ⇒ bottom of range | launch note **− a 3rd** | forced: octave + 3rd = a 10th |

⭐ So the whole range is **a single note's neighbourhood**: a third below the launch note, an octave
above it. **The verses live under the launch note. The choruses live above it. The peak is the only
time the voice touches the ceiling.** That is a melodic design that can be verified by counting, and it
is the reason a listener can *feel* the top arrive rather than be told it has.

**Per variation** — four keys, four launch notes, four different bars, and **one of the four leaps is
minor**, because the song about the failure should not leap the same way as the song about the joy:

| | key | range (a 10th) | launch | the leap | opens at | peak = octave |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | D major | **B3 → D5** (minor 10th) | **D4** | **D4 → B4**, rising **major 6th** | **bar 3** of every chorus | **D4 → D5** |
| **V2** | E-flat major | **E♭4 → G5** (major 10th) | **G4** | **G4 → E♭5**, rising **minor 6th** | **bar 1** — the chorus *begins* on it | **G4 → G5** |
| **V3** | F major | **D4 → F5** (minor 10th) | **F4** | **F4 → D5**, rising **major 6th** | **bar 4** of every chorus | **F4 → F5** |
| **V4** | C major | **A3 → C5** (minor 10th) | **C4** | **C4 → A4**, rising **major 6th** | **bar 2** of every chorus | **C4 → C5** |

**The leap always lands on a word, and the word is different in all four:** *wait* · *up* · *now* ·
*hold*. ⛔ **Four different hooks** is satisfied by four different intervals in four different bars on
four different words — not by four rewordings of one idea.

## 2. FACET — THE RETURN, AND WHAT IS OWED FOR IT (E11, owned)

**Three things return in every variation, on three different timescales:**

1. ⭐ **The chorus, byte-identical, three times.** *A byte-identical chorus needs no defence and no
   note.* It is not written here as a concession to pop form; it is the **address** that the removal
   later empties. You cannot take a thing out of a place the listener has not learned.
2. ⭐ **The rhyme returns as a WORD, not as a sound.** The last line of **every verse ends on the same
   word** — V1 *cold* · V2 *wood* · V3 *word* · V4 *light* — and the chorus lives in that word's rhyme
   family. This is the return a listener notices without being able to say what they noticed.
3. ⭐ **The launch note returns.** Every chorus starts the leap from the same pitch. The ear learns an
   altitude, and the peak breaks it.

**⛔ REMOVAL IS A DEBT — and here is exactly what is removed and exactly what is paid.**

> **REMOVED:** the leap. In the coda, the chorus's leap line is sung again, **same words, same bar,
> same launch note — and stepwise.** The jump is gone.
>
> **PAID BACK, per variation, and never the same way:**
> - **V1** — a **struck bell** lands on the note the voice no longer reaches, and the record ends on its
>   decay. *The interval survives, in bronze.*
> - **V2** — **nothing** arrives. The harpsichord, which has answered every single chorus, does not
>   answer. *The debt is deliberately left unpaid, and that is the subject of the song.*
> - **V3** — the **string section plays the tune** with no words over it. *The words stop; the tune does
>   not.*
> - **V4** — the leap is **inverted**: the line falls by exactly the interval it used to rise, and a
>   tambourine is the last thing on the record. *The shape is kept and turned over.*

## 5. FACET — THE SUNG FACT: *thirty years*, four different grammars

⛔ One numeral per song, in words, **responded to, never recited.** The response is the point; the
number is the setup.

- **V1** — *"Thirty years, and I can hand it to you standing up."* → **"I can. That is the thing I am."**
- **V2** — *"She gave it thirty years, and I can give you the end of it in a breath."* → **"And I will."**

- **V4** — *"Thirty years, she said, and you will still be guessing at the blue."* → **"He said, I have
  not got that long. She said, nobody has. Begin."** *(the only one where the answer is another person's)*

⚠️ **A structural hazard I am writing down before it bites:** the verse architecture wants stressed
monosyllables at line ends, and *one · two · four · once · twice · third · half · single* are all
stressed monosyllables. **The form recruits numerals.** Mitigation is a rule, not vigilance: *no
number-word other than "thirty" may appear in a sung line*, enforced by a counter over the whole
lyric field, not by reading.

## 7. FACET — THE DENSITY BREAK, WHICH IS THE WHOLE REASON THE TUNE CAN MOVE

⭐⭐ **~48 sung lines in ~3:30 ≈ 14 lines per minute, against our habitual 20+.**

At **68 BPM** a 4/4 bar is **3.53 seconds**, and 3:30 is **≈ 59 bars**. The budget:

| section | bars | lines | lines/bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 2–4 | 0 | — |
| Verse ×3 | 8 each | **8 each** | 1.0 |
| Chorus ×3 | 8 each | **6 each** | 0.75 |
| Lift | 2–3 | 0 | — |
| Coda | 5–6 | **6** | ~1.1 |
| **total** | **59** | **48** | — |

⭐ **One line per bar is the point.** A line that owns a whole bar at 68 BPM has **3.5 seconds** — room
for a leap, room for a held line-end, room for the beat between the sharp violin desk and the
harpsichord to be heard on the held note. **At 20 lines a minute the tune has no room to be a tune,
and that is a large part of what Ash heard as flatness.**

⛔ **The `sung_lines` 70–120 band is a BAND, not a target, and its evidentiary basis is suspended in
this run** (it correlates +0.850 with the calendar). **Landing at 48 is the assignment.** The
run's own counter will report it as a hard fail; that report is expected, is recorded in step 09 and
step 10, and **is not a defect.** A gate whose number came from a correlation with the date is not
an instrument, and padding four songs to 70 lines to satisfy it would delete this slot's entire reason
for existing.
