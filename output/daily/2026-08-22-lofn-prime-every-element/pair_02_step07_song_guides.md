## 0. WHAT ALL FOUR SHARE — stated once so no guide restates it

**Tempo 68 BPM, 4/4, first bar to last. No key change, no tempo change, no gear change, no genre
shift.** At 68 BPM a bar is **3.53 s**; 3:30 is **≈ 59 bars**; **one sung line owns one bar.**

**The bed — strings and harpsichord as POP instruments, not as a museum.**
- **Strings** — a real section, close-miked, gut-strung, bowed warm and near the fingerboard. Wall-of-
  Sound density is permitted and used: everything printed into **one ambience**, not separate reverbs.
- ⭐ **One violin desk is tuned several cents sharp against the harpsichord.** On the held line-ends the
  two beat audibly. *(SOURCE2 → "detuned bliss", as physics rather than mood.)*
- **Harpsichord** — bright single manual, plectra and key noise left in. ⭐ **It is the hook
  instrument.** *(SOURCE2 → "a chiming synth riff": the hook is an INSTRUMENT, not the voice.)*
- **Tack piano** doubling the harpsichord's line an octave down from the first chorus onward.
- **Celesta** on the top note of each chorus only.
- **Percussion is furniture, not a groove:** felted timpani on the chorus downbeats, brushed kit far
  back, tambourine only where a guide names it. ⛔ No kit at all before the first chorus.
- **Bass** — bowed and plucked upright, low and slow, never a riff.
- **Vocal** — a warm mezzo, close, breaths left in, **no correction**. Single voice throughout; unison
  doubling on the third chorus only.
- **Master** — −14 LUFS, ≥10 dB crest, so the hushed verse can actually be quiet.

⛔ **NOTHING IN THIS PAIR IS GLITCHED, LOGGED, SUB-DRIVEN, STOMPED OR DUELLED.** Those are P03, P04, P05
and P06's owned elements. This pair is strings, a keyboard with a quill, and a tune.

### The melodic architecture, mechanically identical in all four *(it is the slot's identity)*

| move | where | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| **STATE (instrument)** | **bar 1**, before any voice | the harpsichord alone plays the leap as an interval. The hook exists before the singer does. |
| **STATE (voice)** | **chorus 1**, its assigned bar | the voice sings the leap on the leap-word for the first time |
| **CONFIRM** | **chorus 2**, same bar, same word, same pitches | the ear learns an altitude |
| ⭐ **ENLARGE — THE PEAK** | **chorus 3**, same bar, same word | the 6th **opens to an octave**, first and only time, strings doubling her at the top, celesta on the note |
| ⛔ **REMOVE** | **the coda**, same line, same words, same launch note | **the leap is gone.** The line walks stepwise. |
| **PAY THE DEBT** | the last bars | four different answers — see each guide |

⭐ **The arrival is the event.** The leap is played **five times** (harpsichord ×1, voice ×3, and once
enlarged) before it is taken away. You cannot feel a subtraction you never witnessed the addition of.

### The return, mechanically identical in all four

1. **Chorus byte-identical ×3.** *A byte-identical chorus needs no defence and no note.*
2. **The last line of every verse ends on the same word** — V1 *cold* · V2 *wood* · V3 *word* ·
   V4 *light* — and the chorus lives in that word's rhyme family.
3. **The launch note is the same pitch in all three choruses.**

### ⛔ The forbidden section

**The thesis may not land in the bridge.** ⭐ **There is no bridge in any of the four.** That slot is a
two-to-three bar instrumental **Lift** with no words in it, so the prohibition holds structurally.

# SONG GUIDE — V1 · THE APPRENTICESHIP
### "You Cannot Hear It Hot" · **D major** · 68 BPM · ~3:28 · the bell-founder

**Angle:** Ellis Marrow at the bell yard at Thrum, given a broom and told to wait. **The cost, plainly:**
a bell has no note until it is cold, so the apprenticeship is years of being unable to hear your own
work. **Lofn's admission lands in verse 3, the hushed one, and then she leaves the song.**

- ⭐ **PEAK — what, when, why.** **What:** the word *wait*, which has jumped a **major sixth (D4→B4)** in
  every chorus, opens to a **full octave (D4→D5)**; the string desks arrive on D5 with her, celesta
  strikes it, felted timpani under it. **When: 2:49**, the downbeat of bar 3 of the third chorus.
  **Why it is the highest point:** it is literally the highest note in the song, it happens once, and
  the listener has heard the smaller version of that exact gesture, in that exact bar, on that exact
  word, four times already.
- ⭐ **RANGE — interval and the bar it opens.** The tune spans **a minor 10th, B3 → D5.** The leap is a
  **rising major 6th, D4 → B4**, and it opens on **the downbeat of bar 3 of every chorus**, on *wait*.
  Verses stay between B3 and F#4 — under the launch note, deliberately, so the chorus is an ascent.
- ⭐ **CONTOUR.** **Quietest: verse 3, 2:07–2:35** — voice and harpsichord, nothing else; her breath and
  the plectra audible; the sharp violin desk enters only on the held *cold* at the end. **Loudest:
  2:49**, the octave. Everything between is a warm mid that never swells.
- **ARRIVAL & REMOVAL (S3).** *Arrives:* the major 6th, bar 1, harpsichord alone. *Occupies:* bar 3 of
  every chorus, on *wait*. *Removed:* the coda sings *"Wait. Let it go down in the sand and go cold"*
  from the same D4, **stepwise, no jump.** ⭐ **The debt is paid by a struck bell** landing on B4 where
  the voice no longer goes — and the record ends on its decay, unaccompanied. *The interval survives,
  in bronze.*
- **CHORUS (byte-identical ×3):** six lines, *hot / note / cold / old / is / hold*. The **-old** family
  is the verse-return word.
- **SUNG FACT:** *thirty years*, verse 3, answered by **"I can. That is the thing I am."**
- **CLOSING MOVE:** the song's last words are about **Ellis, not Lofn** — she is out in the dark with her
  palm on the ground, and she has been happy since the rain. ⭐ **The sharp violin desk lands on the word
  *happy*** — the happiness is a few cents out, on purpose.
- **OPENING (≤15 s):** harpsichord alone plays the rising 6th in **bar 1, 0:00.**

# SONG GUIDE — V2 · THE ONE THAT FAILED
### "It Never Spoke And I Kept It" · **E-flat major** · 68 BPM · ~3:28 · the luthier

**Angle:** Ruta Vane, a room above the flooded lane at Fennow, and the best-made instrument of her life,
which had no voice and nobody could say why. **The cost, plainly:** she keeps it on the wall and turns
it toward the light, and it is where she learned everything after. **Lofn's admission is the last thing
on the record.**

- ⭐ **PEAK — what, when, why.** **What:** the chorus *begins* on the leap in this song, so the peak is
  the **first sound of the third chorus**: the word *up* opens from a **rising minor 6th (G4→E♭5)** to a
  **full octave (G4→G5)** with no run-up at all. **When: 2:39.** **Why it is the highest point:** it is
  the top of the range, it is unprepared, and it lands on the downbeat after two bars of an unaccompanied

- ⭐ **RANGE — interval and the bar it opens.** The tune spans **a major 10th, E♭4 → G5.** The leap is a

- **ARRIVAL & REMOVAL (S3).** *Arrives:* the minor 6th, harpsichord alone, bar 2 of the intro.
  *Occupies:* bar 1 of every chorus, on *up*. *Removed:* the coda sings *"Hang it up where the window

  verse-return family.
- **SUNG FACT:** *thirty years*, the coda, answered by **"And I will."**
- **CLOSING MOVE:** the record's last line is Lofn naming what she cannot hand over — **the wall, the
  winters, the failure** — over strings that hold and a harpsichord that stays out.
- **OPENING (≤15 s):** whole string section, one struck chord, **bar 1, 0:00**, cut dead; harpsichord
  alone in bar 2.

# ⛔ THE BAN-ON-BANS REGISTER — every exclude term, discharged

> **The universal clause:** *no exclude field may forbid a device unless the brief names what does that
> job instead.* ⭐ **A register is not a summary.** The substantive replacement — the rising 6th opening
> to an octave, note-named and timecoded — discharges the *range* ban and nothing else. **Thirty-odd
> other terms were sitting undischarged**, and a prohibition with no named replacement is how a
> doctrine produces the mean. Every term below is quoted verbatim from the step-10 exclude field.
>
> **Counts:** V1 **38** terms · V2 **36** · V3 **36** · V4 **36**. **34 are common to all four.**
>
> **Classes:** **(a)** forbids a device that does a real job → **a named replacement is mandatory** ·
> **(b)** forbids an element **owned by another pair** → the replacement is *the owner does it, and I
> carry it at baseline* · **(c)** forbids **no job at all** — a production-hygiene or human-subject
> guard. ⭐ **(c) has to be declared, never assumed**, or it is an unpaired ban hiding in a hygiene list.
