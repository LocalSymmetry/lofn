## ⭐ 0. THE ONE QUESTION THIS SLOT HAS TO ANSWER, ANSWERED ONCE FOR ALL FOUR

> **How does a listener experience a rise with no level change?**

**Because the ear scores intensity as how much of the room is accounted for, not as amplitude.**

1. **A band that was empty.** Each entrant occupies a frequency region nothing else holds — handclaps in
   the 1–4 kHz transient window, tambourine / wood block / finger cymbals at 5–10 kHz, piano and Hammond
   in the 200–800 Hz sustain, stacked voices across 400 Hz–2 kHz, and the peak at the very top (or, in
   V3, the very bottom). ⭐ **By the fifth timbre there is no silence left to fill**, and a body reads a
   fully-occupied spectrum as loud even when the meter has not moved.
2. **A slot that was empty.** The grid fills: quarters → the backbeat → offbeat eighths → held whole
   notes → the peak's own slot. **Events per bar roughly quadruple.**
3. **The roof climbs.** The stacked voices put a note a third — then a sixth — above the lead's ceiling,
   and the fifth timbre puts one an octave above *that*, **while the tune never leaves its major sixth.**
   The ear assigns a rising ceiling to effort.
4. **The count of hands.** Each arrival is one more person in that parlour. The listener is tracking
   **how many people are in there.**

⭐ **And the payoff the steadiness buys:** because nothing has changed level for three minutes, **lifting
the handclaps out of beats 2 and 4 at the end is a cliff.** A record that had been swelling all along
could not make that gesture at all. ⛔ **The flatness is the instrument, not the absence of one.**

## ⭐ 1. THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS — declared here, verified in code at Step 10

Each song's chorus appears **three times, identical to the character**: no added ad-lib, no extra harmony
line written into the text, no "final chorus" variation, no tag word. ⛔ **A byte-identical chorus needs
no defence and no note.**

⭐ **What changes across the three appearances is not the chorus. It is what has arrived underneath it.**
Chorus 1 has handclaps and nothing else new; chorus 2 sits over two more entrants; chorus 3 sits over all
five. **The repetition is the measuring stick that makes the parade legible** — the same eight lines,
three times, in three different rooms.

**And it is what makes the forbidden section structural rather than a promise.** The thesis — Lofn's own
indictment — cannot land in the second chorus, because the second chorus is byte-identical to the first
and third, so a thesis there would be a thesis everywhere. It lives in the middle eight, and in V1 and V4
again in the last verse. ⛔ **Arithmetically impossible beats carefully avoided.**

## V1 — **PUT YOUR WHOLE FOOT DOWN** · E-flat

**GROUND, locked bar 9 (0:15), never altered again:** boot-stomps on all four on a wooden riser ·
electric bass in eighths · snare on 2 and 4 · shaker sixteenths.

| # | entrant | bar | time | slot it takes | band it takes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐ **handclaps** — three pairs of hands, dry, dead centre | **17** | 0:30 | beats 2 and 4 | 1–4 kHz transient |
| 2 | **tambourine**, struck hard, close, jingles ringing into the next beat | **33** | 1:01 | offbeat eighths | 5–10 kHz |
| 3 | **the answering voices split from bare unison into a stacked third and a sixth** | **49** | 1:31 | the answer word only | 400 Hz–2 kHz harmonic |
| 4 | **baritone saxophone** — one flat honk, no melody | **65** | 2:02 | beat 4 of alternate bars | 80–250 Hz |
| 5 | ⭐ **PEAK — three horns in unison playing the hook with no voice on it** | **81** | 2:32 | the vocal's own phrase, unoccupied | 500 Hz–3 kHz, doubled an octave up |

⭐ **THE PEAK, and why it is the summit:** the song's own tune, moved out of a mouth and into three metal
bodies, over a groove that has not flinched in two and a half minutes. **It is the figure outliving the
people who made it**, stated as arrangement rather than as a line.
**REMOVAL:** bar **105** (3:18) — handclaps lifted off 2 and 4; horns out; **the record ends on bare
boot-stomps and bass.** The first sound in the song is the last sound in it, minus the hands.
⭐ **FLOOR — bar 1, 0:00.0: one boot on a wooden riser, alone.** ⛔ By amplitude a stomp is not quiet; by
**the metric this pair actually argues — bands occupied — bar one is the emptiest bar in the record.**
One source, one rhythmic slot, silence in every other register. ⭐ **The floor and the peak are the same
subject:** the record opens on one foot and summits on three horns playing the tune that foot paid for,
and the distance between those two bars is the song.

⭐ **THE PEAK, and why it is the summit:** it is **the only thing in the record that lasts longer than the

the last sung line and stops mid-figure.** Somebody is still counting.

no sustained content anywhere** — two short transient bands with silence between them. Emptier than the
opening, where three voices shout broadband across 400 Hz–4 kHz. ⭐ **The song about the person nobody
credits ends with the only part of the arrangement that is pure bookkeeping, still keeping it.**

**GROUND, locked bar 5 (0:08), never altered again:** stomps on all four doubled by a floor tom on 1 and

| # | entrant | bar | time | slot it takes | band it takes |

| 4 | **finger cymbals** — a small bright ping in a record with no cymbals at all | **57** | 1:47 | beat 3 of alternate bars | 8–14 kHz |

⭐ **THE PEAK, and why it is the summit:** it is the largest decision in the pair. A SWAG summit is
normally the richest musical entrant; here it is **a thing that is not music at all**, arriving on the
exact bar the lyric names it, while the groove does not so much as breathe under it. ⭐ **A clean take
would be a tune. The bus is the reason you believe it happened.** It is SOURCE2's *"sonic events"* taken
at its word.

own short slap-back is the last thing on the tape** — the room, alone, for half a second.
⭐ **FLOOR — bar 107, 3:21.9: nothing is playing.** ⭐⭐ **The deepest floor in the pair, and it is zero
instruments** — the only sound is the parlour's own decay, about four-tenths of a second of it, after the
stomps stop dead on the downbeat. ⭐ **The room fought to stay out of this record and lost at 2:17; it
gets the last word alone.** Peak and floor are the same argument from opposite ends: the building is
audible at both.

**GROUND, from bar 1 (0:00), never altered:** stomps on all four · electric bass in eighths · snare on 2

| # | entrant | bar | time | slot it takes | band it takes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 5 | ⭐ **PEAK — one person whistling the hook, close to the microphone, no reverb** | **77** | 2:25 | the vocal's phrase, unoccupied | a near-pure tone at B5 |

⭐ **THE PEAK, and why it is the summit:** it is **the cheapest instrument there is**, carrying the tune
with no words on it, at the top of a full room. The record has escaped into a person's mouth, where
neither a label nor an archive can reach it. **THE HUM TEST, made literal.**
**REMOVAL:** bar **101** (3:10) — handclaps lifted; ⭐ **two bars of bare snare on 2 and 4 — the
subtraction heard naked** — and then at bar **103** (3:14) the whistle moves onto 2 and 4 and takes the

shallowest floor of the four, honestly:** it is a hole in *one* register while the ground continues
unchanged — **local, not global.** ⭐ That is precisely why this variation is the one that reoccupies: a
two-bar hole in the single address the ear has tracked all song is exactly deep enough for a whistle
moving into it to read as somebody filling it.

# ⭐ 2B. THE FLOOR — the quietest moment in each song

⛔ **"Steady by design" authorises a flat SHAPE. It does not authorise an ABSENT SPECIFICATION.** This
pair's entire defence is that its flatness is *deliberate* — and **a deliberate choice that is never
written down is indistinguishable from the default.** The universal clause requires every song to state
where it is quietest as well as where it is loudest, and the first draft of this guide named four peaks
and no floors.

⭐ **The floor is defined by the same metric as the rise:** §0 argues that a listener feels a rise because
"every entrant takes a frequency band and a rhythmic slot that were audibly empty a bar earlier." **Then
the floor is the bar where the most bands are empty.** ⛔ Not the bar with the lowest meter reading — no
dynamic drop was added to manufacture any of these, and no sung line was changed to find them. All four
were already in the arrangement; they were simply never called floors.

| | ⭐ **quietest moment** | bar · timecode | what is sounding | depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | the opening stomp | **bar 1 · 0:00.0** | one boot on a wooden riser, alone | **one source** |
| **V2** | the wood block outro | **bars 109–113 · 3:25.7–3:33.3** *(deepest bar 111, 3:29.5)* | stomps and wood block — no sustained content anywhere | **two transient bands** |
| **V3** | the room's own tail | **bar 107 · 3:21.9** | ⭐⭐ nothing — the parlour's decay, ~0.4 s | **zero instruments** |
| **V4** | the emptied backbeat | **bars 101–102 · 3:10.5–3:14.3** | snare alone at the clap address; ground continues | **one band, local** |

⭐ **The depths are deliberately unequal and the table says so.** Four identical claims would be the
hedging this run exists to end. V3 goes to actual silence; V4's floor is a hole in one register. ⭐ **And
in three of the four the floor is created by the S3 removal itself** — the handclaps leaving beats 2 and
4 is what opens the trough, which is the write/erase pair paying out twice: it is the record's largest
event *and* its quietest.

⛔ **THE PEAK TEST, self-administered:** could a listener tell the loudest moment from the quietest from a
description alone? **V1** — one boot on a board, against three horns in unison. **V2** — two dry ticks in
silence, against a vibraphone ringing an octave above a full room. **V3** — an empty room's decay,
against a diesel bus coming through a shut window. **V4** — a bare snare, against a whistle over five
timbres. ⭐ **Yes, in all four, and they are not close.**

# 4. MELODIC RANGE — a sixth in the lead, and the thirds carry the width

⛔ **The lead spans a major sixth and never leaves it, in all four.** A narrow tune is the most singable
thing a room can be handed; the width is a **harmonic** event.

| | key | lead (major 6th) | the stack | the peak's ceiling | total texture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | E-flat | B♭3 → G4 | B♭4 (a 3rd above the top), E♭5 on the answer (a 6th) | **B♭5** (2nd trumpet, octave double) | **two octaves** |
| **V2** | C | G3 → E4 | G4 (a 3rd above the top) | **G5** (vibraphone) | **two octaves** |
| **V3** | F | C4 → A4 | unison → A4 / C5 at bar 41 | ⭐ **downward, ~80 Hz** (the bus) | the widest *bottom* in the pair |
| **V4** | G | D4 → B4 | **G5** — a 6th above the top, the highest stack here | **B5** (the whistle) | ceiling a 6th higher than V1's stack |

⭐ **How the thirds widen it, precisely:** the lead's ceiling is the sixth degree. A voice a third above
puts the top of the texture **an octave above the lead's floor**; a second voice a sixth above puts it at
**an eleventh**; the peak timbre doubles that an octave up, at **two octaves.** ⭐ **The tune stays where
a room can sing it and the record still gets taller** — which is the whole reason stacked harmony is a
required instrument in this slot and not a garnish.

# 7. THE BAN LEDGER — carried into Step 10 in full

⛔ **THE BAN ON BANS:** no exclude may forbid a device unless this guide names what does that job
instead. The complete table ships in **Step 10**, above the four packages, and covers every term in every
exclude field: the **parade** replaces the crescendo, the **stacked voices** replace the key change,
**subdivision density** replaces the tempo change, the **entrant landing on the chorus downbeat** replaces
the fill, **four named endings** replace the fade-out, and **the fifth timbre carrying the hook** replaces
the big held note.

⭐ Two excludes are deliberately *not* covered by a replacement, because they are not bans at all —
`no handclaps in the tag` (all four) and `no ducking the bus` (V3) are **scheduling instructions wearing
the exclude field's grammar**, and they are flagged as such in Step 10's MAJOR DEVIATIONS.
