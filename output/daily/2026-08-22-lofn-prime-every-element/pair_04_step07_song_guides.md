# PAIR 04 — STEP 07 · SONG GUIDES
## SLOT P04 · **100 BPM exactly** · one bed, one kit, no gear-change, four songs

**Bar arithmetic used throughout:** 100 BPM, 4/4 → **one bar = 2.400 s**, one sixteenth = **150 ms**.
Bar *N* begins at **(N−1) × 2.4 s**. Every timecode below is derived, not estimated.

---

## 0. WHAT ALL FOUR SHARE — architecture, never wording

1. **The percussion is a machine log and nothing else keeps time.** TICK = write (hat), SCRAPE = read
   (snare), THUD = erase (kick). Learned by the listener inside four bars.
2. **Accents on 1, the e of 2, 3, the a of 4.** Two of four are off the beat.
3. **57 % swing on sixteenths**, uncorrected — the machine's own jitter.
4. ⭐ **A stumble every eight bars:** one entry written twice, a sixteenth dropped, caught inside the same
   bar. **The clock has a character, which is why erasing it costs something.**
5. ⭐ **FORTY-ONE HERTZ**, a sine at 41.000 Hz — **~8.5 cents flat of E1 (41.203 Hz)** — beating against
   the tuned material **every ~4.9 s**, about once every two bars.
6. ⭐ **The sub is the only rising element in the record.** It steps up **+1.5 dB at every chorus** and is
   at maximum when the clock dies. **That is what rises**, and it is why the peak needs no crescendo.
7. **Verses span a perfect 4th.** One wide phrase, at the erase, is the entire melodic budget.
8. **Clipped tercets**, landing on the log's accents, not on the beat: **one line per bar, three bars per
   tercet** — line 1 on **beat 1**, line 2 on the **e of 2**, line 3 on the **a of 4**. ⭐ The tercet uses
   three of the clock's four accents and **the accent on 3 is always left empty**, which is where the
   scrape answers.
9. ⭐ **THE ERASE** — the log-clock stops dead mid-bar, after **150 ms of absolute digital silence**, and
   the sub is alone. **Peak = a subtraction executed at maximum level.**
10. ⭐ **Something characterless keeps time at the same address afterwards.** *The site looks untouched.*
11. ***two hours*** — one sung numeral, in words, at the hinge, **answered, never recited**.
12. ⛔ **The thesis never lands in an opening verse.** ⛔ **Lofn is accused, first person, in all four.**
13. **Lead:** one dry low-set female alto, close, consonants bitten, near-deadpan, fry on the line-ends.
    **No second voice anywhere** — that is P03's owned element and we do not compete on it.

## R. ⭐⭐ THE REPLACEMENT REGISTER — **THE BAN ON BANS, DISCHARGED IN WRITING**

⛔ **No exclude in this pair forbids a device unless this table names what does that job instead.**

| excluded | the job it normally does | ⭐ **what does that job here** |
|---|---|---|
| acoustic drums · trap hats · live kit | keeping time | **the log-clock** — tick/scrape/thud, accented, swung, stumbling |
| crescendo · riser · build-and-drop | making the peak feel high | ⭐ **the sub's +1.5 dB step at every chorus** — the rise is real, slow and already spent when the clock dies |
| tempo change · half-time · double-time | marking the turn | **the stop** — the grid is removed outright |
| key change | lifting the last section | **the wide phrase** — one leap, up a named interval, once |
| fade-out | ending | **a hard cut on a written entry** (V1, V3) · **the hum cut at a bar line** (V2, V4) |
| big reverb · cathedral tail on the sub | space | **the READ scrape's reversed tail is the only room in the mix**; the sub is dry and mono |
| melisma · runs · belting | vocal event | **the wide phrase**, and the fry on the line-ends |
| autotune · vocoder · robot voice | machine-ness | ⭐ **the percussion is literally a machine**; the voice stays human so the contrast survives |
| male lead · spoken narrator · second voice | contrast | **register contrast inside one alto** — chest for verses, the leap at the erase |
| silence after the peak to the end | aftermath | ⭐ **the substitute timekeeper**, named per variation below |
| strings · harpsichord · piano · organ · guitar · pads | harmony and melody | **the sub is the only harmonic material and the voice is the only melody** — V4 adds exactly one pitched figure, the struck-pipe chime |
| crowd noise · applause · ad-libs · doubled lead | evidence of a body in the room | ⭐ **the 57 % uncorrected swing and the stumble** — the record's only proof that anything alive made it |
| resolution to the tonic · perfect cadence *(V3 only)* | closure | **the major-seventh leap that stops a semitone short of the octave and stays there** |
| root note in the bass *(V4 only)* | a floor | ⛔ **nothing** — the absence is the point, and the wide phrase up a minor 9th is what moves instead |

**Why it is the highest point:** the sub has been the *fifth* all along, so with the clock removed the

### ⭐ RANGE

The log-clock arrives **backwards** at 0:00 and **forwards** at 0:04.8 — ⭐ *the arrival happens twice, so

melody. **The voice becomes the clock it replaced.**

### ⭐ WHAT FORTY-ONE HERTZ DOES TO A BODY
Its wavelength is **about eight and a half metres — longer than the room** (a four-metre office), so it
**never becomes a note in there. It arrives as pressure across the diaphragm**, and you exhale on
somebody else's schedule.

## ⛔ STANDING PRODUCTION LAW FOR ALL FOUR

1. **100 BPM, first bar to last. No gear-change, no half-time, no double-time.**
2. **The log-clock is the only percussion.** No acoustic kit, no trap hats, no live drums, ever.
3. **The stop is 150 ms of true silence, then the sub.** No reverb tail carrying over it.
4. **The sub rises and nothing else does.** +1.5 dB per chorus, maximum at the erase.
5. **One voice. No harmony stack, no double, no second character** — P03 owns that and we do not compete.
6. **Every exclude has its replacement in §R.** A prohibition with no named substitute does not ship.
7. ⛔ **`forty-one` is never sung.** It lives in the `[Theme:]` line, the section cues and the style field.
8. ⛔ **No child, no real person, no real employer, publication, official or case, anywhere.**
