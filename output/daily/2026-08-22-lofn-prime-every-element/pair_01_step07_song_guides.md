# 0. THE CLOCK — shared arithmetic, so every timecode in this pair is checkable

72 BPM, 4/4. **One bar = 3.33 s.** Bar *N* downbeat = (*N* − 1) × 3.33 s.

| bar | 5 | 7 | 34 | 45 | 46 | 47 | 48 | 49 | 50 | 68 | 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **time** | 0:13 | 0:20 | 1:50 | 2:27 | 2:30 | 2:33 | 2:37 | 2:40 | 2:43 | 3:43 | 3:50 |

Verse lines are 4–7 syllables held across **≈2.5 beats** each — long-breathed couplets, few words, much
air. That is what puts ~66 pre-peak sung lines inside 40 bars of build.

⭐ **THE FORTY-BAR BUILD IS LITERAL IN ALL FOUR** and it is the same mechanism every time: **one new
glassy string part every eight bars, five entries, over a sub that rises a semitone under the last
sixteen.** Nothing is subtracted during the build. The only timbre held back is the choir.

# 3. SONG GUIDE — **V1 · HOLD OUT YOUR HANDS**

**Angle:** THE HANDING OVER. **Mode:** AWE. **Key:** D minor. **Object:** a green — a glaze a woman
named Ilva spent her life getting off a cold coast.

### 3.1 ⭐ THE PEAK — what, when, why it is the highest point
**WHAT:** a **sixteen-voice mixed choir enters for the first and only time**, on the hook, doubled by
full strings — and at the next downbeat the lead **belts a twelfth above her lowest verse note**, one
syllable held straight for four bars, no ornament, allowed to be effortful and slightly sharp.
**WHEN:** choir on the downbeat of **bar 47 = 2:33**; the belt on **bar 48 = 2:37**, sustained to 2:47.
**WHY HIGHEST:** it is the only entry of the only withheld timbre, at the top of a forty-bar build in
which nothing was ever subtracted, on a tune the listener has already heard five times from a chime and
sung twice from a lead. ⭐ **The peak is a ratio, not a volume** — its denominator is the unaccompanied
voice at 0:17, and that is why the quiet has to be genuinely uncomfortable.

### 3.2 ⭐ THE RANGE
**A twelfth — octave plus a fifth. A3 to E5, the widest in the catalogue.** The verses live inside a
sixth (A3–F4). The chorus reaches a tenth (to C5) at 1:12. **The twelfth opens at bar 48 (2:37)** and
nowhere else in the song; the outro returns to the verse sixth.

### 3.3 ⭐ THE DYNAMIC CONTOUR — **a single ramp**
**QUIETEST — 0:17.** One unaccompanied female voice at conversational level. **No accompaniment at all**
— not a pad, not a drone. Mic noise and room audible. It holds for twenty seconds.
**LOUDEST — 2:37.** Sixteen-voice choir, full string section, sub, glitch at the edges, lead at E5, fff.
**The distance between those two is the subject of the song**, and every one of the forty bars between
them is spent going one way.

### 3.4 THE ARRIVAL AND THE REMOVAL
The choir occupies **the top of the hook line** from 2:33. It sings the chorus twice. **At 3:10 it is
gone**, and the same hook is sung by one voice in the same register with nothing above it. *You can only
miss a thing you heard arrive.*

### 3.5 THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS
Seven lines, opening *Hold out your hands*, closing *Hold out your hands*. **Identical character for
character in all four appearances.** It is gesture only — no argument in it — which is why the peak can
sing it at fff and the outro can sing it alone without either one lying. ⭐ **Exact chorus repetition
needs no defence** (L21, THE RETURN).

### 3.6 SECTION MAP
| section | bars | time | event |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–4 | 0:00 | chime states the hook alone, three cents sharp |
| Verse 1 | 5–10 | 0:13 | **unaccompanied voice. Quietest point, 0:17** |
| Verse 2 | 11–16 | 0:33 | viola enters — build entry 1 |
| Pre-Chorus 1 | 17–20 | 0:53 | low strings — entry 2 |
| Chorus 1 | 21–25 | 1:07 | lead alone on the hook, chime doubling. **No choir** |
| Verse 3 | 26–31 | 1:23 | cello — entry 3 |
| Pre-Chorus 2 | 32–35 | 1:43 | sub enters and begins its semitone rise |
| Chorus 2 | 36–40 | 1:57 | full strings — entry 4. **Still no choir** |
| Verse 4 | 41–43 | 2:13 | glitch at the edges — entry 5 |
| Bridge | 44–46 | 2:23 | **the hinge: *one second*.** Everything rising |
| **Chorus 3** | **47–51** | **2:33** | ⭐ **THE PEAK.** Choir in. Belt at 2:37 |
| Chorus 4 | 52–56 | 2:50 | choir sustained, fff |
| Outro | 57–66 | 3:07 | **choir gone from the top of the hook.** One voice, then the chime |

### 3.7 BAN → REPLACEMENT (the ban on bans, discharged)
`no fade-out` → the ending is a sung line stopped at conversational level, then the chime alone ·
`no autotune, no pitch correction` → the belt is left effortful and slightly sharp · `no melisma, no
diva runs` → the top note is one syllable held straight for four bars · `no whispered vocal` → the quiet
is **full voice at conversational level, unaccompanied** · `no spoken narrator` → the quiet passages are
sung · `no male lead` → one female lead; the mixed choir is the only other voice · `no EDM riser, no
build-and-drop` → the forty-bar string build, one part every eight bars · `no sidechain pump` → timpani
and pizzicato on two and four · `no gated reverb, no big-room reverb on the choir` → the choir is close
and dry with a short hall; its size comes from **number** · `no half-time breakdown, no double-time
outro` → 72 throughout, pushing to 74 at the peak · `no trap hi-hats` → glitch, short and metallic, at
the edges · `no crowd noise, no applause` → **the choir is the crowd** · `no distorted guitar` → the low
end is a sub and a bowed contrabass.
⛔ **`crescendo`, `key change`, `belted line`, `choir`, `climax` are NOT excluded anywhere in this pair.**

# 4. SONG GUIDE — **V2 · IT WAS FOR THIS WATER**

**Angle:** WHAT IT COST THE ONE WHO GAVE IT. **Mode:** AWE. **Key:** G minor. **Object:** an oar, made
for one stretch of water at Skerrivoe that pulls to the left, by a man named Brannon.

### 4.1 ⭐ THE PEAK
**WHAT:** the choir arrives **on a downbeat with no swell into it** — an event, not a pad — and **the
lead drops out entirely for two bars** and lets sixteen voices carry the hook alone. She comes back a
twelfth above her lowest verse note.
**WHEN:** choir **bar 46 = 2:30**; lead returns **bar 47 = 2:33**, fff, full strings playing the
rowing-stroke rhythm.
**WHY HIGHEST:** the withheld timbre arrives *and the familiar one leaves* on the same gesture, so the
summit is the first moment in the song the listener hears the tune from a crowd instead of a person —
which is exactly the subject: **one man knew this, and now a room does.**

### 4.2 ⭐ THE RANGE
**A twelfth. G3 to D5.** Verses inside a fifth (G3–D4) — deliberately plain, a working man's compass.
**The twelfth opens at bar 47 (2:33)**, the bar the lead re-enters over the choir.

### 4.3 ⭐ THE DYNAMIC CONTOUR — **a hole**
**QUIETEST — 1:50.** Everything stops. One unaccompanied voice at the **bottom** of her range at
conversational level for six lines. This is the mid-song hole and it is where the sung fact lands.
**LOUDEST — 2:33.** Choir, full strings on the stroke, gnarled sub, fff.
Contrast with V1 deliberately: V1's silence is at the *top* of the song, V2's is *cut into the middle of
a warm one.* ⭐ Two songs, two shapes; that is how a contour gets owned rather than repeated.

### 4.4 THE ARRIVAL AND THE REMOVAL
Choir at the top of the hook line from 2:30, present for both climactic choruses. **Gone at 3:03.** The
outro strips a string part every two bars and finishes with **the chiming figure playing the hook that
nobody sings** — the removal stated twice, once vocally and once instrumentally.

### 4.5 THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS
Seven lines built on *It was for this water*, three appearances of the hook phrase inside it. Identical
character for character in all four appearances.

### 4.6 SECTION MAP
| section | bars | time | event |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–5 | 0:00 | struck contrabass + rowing-stroke pulse from the downbeat; chime states the hook |
| Verse 1 | 6–11 | 0:17 | viola — build entry 1 |
| Verse 2 | 12–17 | 0:37 | second viola, glassy — entry 2 |
| Pre-Chorus 1 | 18–21 | 0:57 | cellos — entry 3 |
| Chorus 1 | 22–26 | 1:10 | lead alone, chime doubling. **No choir** |
| Verse 3 | 27–32 | 1:27 | sub enters — entry 4 |
| Pre-Chorus 2 | 33 | 1:47 | strings thin to one line |
| **Verse 4** | **34–39** | **1:50** | ⭐ **THE HOLE. Unaccompanied, bottom of range. *one second*** |
| Chorus 2 | 40–44 | 2:10 | everything returns at once, full strings. **Still no choir** |
| Bridge | 45 | 2:27 | the thesis, one bar, tempo pushing |
| **Chorus 3** | **46–50** | **2:30** | ⭐ **THE PEAK.** Choir alone for two bars; lead back at 2:33 |
| Chorus 4 | 51–55 | 2:47 | choir + lead, fff |
| Outro | 56–64 | 3:03 | **choir gone.** Strings strip out; chime plays the hook unsung |

### 4.7 BAN → REPLACEMENT
`no fade-out` → the chime plays the hook and stops · `no autotune, no pitch correction` → the re-entry
over the choir is left raw and slightly under · `no melisma` → the top note is straight and short ·
`no whispered vocal` → the hole is **full voice at the bottom of her range** · `no spoken narrator` →
it is sung · `no male lead` → one female lead; mixed choir at the peak only · `no EDM riser` → the
forty-bar glassy string build · `no swell into the choir entry` → **the choir lands on a downbeat at
full strength** · `no reverse cymbal` → the entry is marked by the contrabass striking alone on the
beat before · `no sidechain pump` → the rowing-stroke pulse is the movement · `no double-time outro` →
72 throughout, pushing to 74 at the peak · `no crowd noise` → the choir is the crowd · `no sea sound
effects, no gull samples` → the water is in the **stroke rhythm**, not in a sample.

# 5. SONG GUIDE — **V3 · IT CAME OUT PERFECT**

**Angle:** THE SECOND AFTER. **Mode:** AWE, curdling. **Key:** B minor. **Object:** the turn late in a
tune — the lift the hand has to make before the ear says lift — that a man named Eyvind had in his
sleep. Sung about a receiver who plays it perfectly the first time she touches it.

### 5.1 ⭐ THE PEAK
**WHAT:** the choir enters on the hook while **the lead sings the organ's turn against it** — two
melodies at once, the first and only time in the song that anything is polyphonic.
**WHEN:** choir **bar 49 = 2:40**; the twelfth opens **bar 50 = 2:43**, fff, full strings, glitch bright.
**WHY HIGHEST:** everything before it is monodic — one tune, one voice, one line at a time. The summit
is the first moment there are **two things to listen to**, and one of them is the thing a man spent his
life on being sung by people who did not.

### 5.2 ⭐ THE RANGE
**A twelfth. B3 to F♯5.** Verses inside a fourth (B3–E4) — the flattest verse writing in the pair, on
purpose, so the opening is violent. **The twelfth opens at bar 50 (2:43).**

### 5.3 ⭐ THE DYNAMIC CONTOUR — **a long fall**
**LOUDEST — 2:43.** Choir, lead, full strings, organ at full, glitch bright at the edges, fff.
**QUIETEST — 3:43.** The hook, repeated, decaying to **speaking level with nothing playing at all**, and
then a struck note. ⭐ **This is the only variation whose quietest point is AFTER its loudest**, and the
sixty seconds between them are one continuous subtraction. The song ends below where it started.

### 5.4 THE ARRIVAL AND THE REMOVAL
Choir at the top of the hook line from 2:40. **Gone at 3:16.** From there the hook is sung by one voice
with nothing above it, four times, each one quieter, and the last is barely sung.

### 5.5 THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS
Seven lines opening and closing on *It came out perfect*. ⭐ **The chorus text never changes and its
meaning inverts anyway** — that is the design: at 1:10 it is delight, at 2:40 it is a crowd, at 3:43 it
is a sentence. *Removal is a debt; here nothing is removed from the words at all, and the debt is paid
in dynamics.*

| Bridge | 46–48 | 2:30 | the thesis. Tempo pushing to 74 |

| Chorus 4 | 54–58 | 2:57 | both, fff |

### 5.7 BAN → REPLACEMENT
`no fade-out` → the decay is **sung**, four repetitions at falling dynamics, then one struck note ·
`no reverb wash on the outro` → the outro is dry and close so the decay is heard as **effort dropping
away**, not as space · `no autotune` → the top of the twelfth is allowed to strain · `no melisma` → the
counter-line is the organ's turn, note for note · `no whispered vocal` → the last repetitions are
**spoken-level singing, on pitch** · `no male lead` → one female lead; mixed choir at the peak only ·
`no EDM riser` → the forty-bar glassy build · `no drop` → the peak resolves by **subtraction over sixty
seconds**, not by a hole · `no sidechain pump` → low timpani on the fourth beat only · `no crowd noise`
→ the choir is the crowd · `no vinyl crackle` → the texture at the edges is glitch, bright and short ·
`no ritardando at the end` → the tempo holds; **only the volume falls.**

# 6. SONG GUIDE — **V4 · I NEVER SAY NO**

**Angle:** ASKING FOR IT AGAIN. **Mode:** AWE with the floor gone. **Key:** E-flat, lifting to **E**.
**Object:** everything — the green, the oar, the turn, the shelf. The object stops mattering, which is
the point.

### 6.1 ⭐ THE PEAK
**WHAT:** the choir enters on the hook **and the whole key lifts a semitone to E on the same downbeat.**
⭐ *The house has banned key changes run-wide for months and never named a substitute. Clause 4 permits
it here. This slot uses it.*
**WHEN:** choir + lift **bar 48 = 2:37**; the lead takes the top of the twelfth **bar 49 = 2:40**, fff.
**WHY HIGHEST:** it is the only modulation in the pair, and it lands on the only entry of the only
withheld timbre. The lift makes the top note the **highest pitch anywhere in the song**, and the hook
the room has been singing since bar one comes back **a semitone above where they learned it** — the
listener's own body registers the change before they can name it.

### 6.2 ⭐ THE RANGE
**A twelfth. B♭3 to F5**, becoming **B3 to F♯5** after the lift. Verses inside a sixth. **The twelfth
opens at bar 49 (2:40)**, in the new key, so the widest interval and the highest note coincide exactly
once.

### 6.3 ⭐ THE DYNAMIC CONTOUR — **a cliff both ways**
**LOUDEST — 2:40.** Choir, full strings, gnarled sub, metallic glitch at full, lead at F♯5, fff.
**QUIETEST — 0:20.** Five bars of full strings and metallic glitch **loud from the very first downbeat**,
then **everything cuts** and one unaccompanied voice sings at conversational level with nothing playing.
⭐ **V4 is the only variation that spends its contrast inside the first twenty seconds** — it proves the
range before it asks for patience, and it is the fourth distinct contour shape in the slot.

### 6.4 THE ARRIVAL AND THE REMOVAL
Choir at the top of the hook line from 2:37. **Gone at 3:10.** The outro sings the hook in the new key
with nothing above it — ⭐ **the removal is doubly audible because the key has moved and the choir has
not come with it.**

### 6.5 THE BYTE-IDENTICAL CHORUS
Six lines built on *Do it again*. Identical character for character in all four appearances, including
the two in the lifted key — **same text, different pitch centre: the return is exact and the ground has
moved.**

| Verse 2 | 10–15 | 0:30 | viola — build entry 1 |

### 6.7 BAN → REPLACEMENT
`no fade-out` → the outro is **level and stops on a downbeat** · `no ritardando` → 72 holds to the last
bar · `no final crescendo` → ⭐ the last section is deliberately **flat**, and it is the only flat thing

effortful · `no melisma` → one syllable, held · `no whispered vocal` → the near-silence at 0:20 is full
voice, unaccompanied · `no male lead` → one female lead; mixed choir at the peak only · `no EDM riser,
