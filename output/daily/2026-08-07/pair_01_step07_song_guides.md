## B. THE HINGE — HOW "nine days" IS ALLOWED TO BE SUNG

**Recited (forbidden):** the number arrives as information, in a list, and the song moves on.
**Responded to (required):** the number arrives, and the very next thing that happens is a **judgment about what was done with it** — not about the number.

**The direction, not the words:** the bridge states the duration, then immediately weighs it against **the smallest and stupidest object in the room**, and the weighing comes out in the object's favour. The number is a container; the payload is that the best thing in nine days was the decision to protect the worst thing in nine days. ⛔ The bridge may not comment on how long *she* takes. Speed is never mentioned. **The comparison the listener makes is the one line the song refuses to write.**

⛔ **No other number is sung anywhere in P01.** Not a bar number, not a count, not a year, not a measurement. `max_sung_numeric_facts = 1` and P01 spends its single allowance on **nine days**.

## C. THE FOUR VARIATIONS — DIRECTION ONLY

> ⛔ **These angles are P01's alone.** No other pair may reuse a label. Each is a different **camera position on the same event**, not a different subject.

### V1 — *the shout across the car park* · 152 BPM · A minor
**Camera:** outside, at night, insects round a security light, a man walking away with keys in his teeth.
**Direct:** this is the pair's primary and the one that must make a tasteful person wince. Full tempo, full gang, no ramp. The lead is **behind** the addressee — every line lands on a back. **The chorus is the moment the word arrives**, so the accretion and the hook are the same event and the listener does not need to be told to count. End with the accordion alone, because the man is gone and the riff is not.
**Forbidden here:** any interiority. V1 is all exterior, all volume, all physical fact. If a line explains, cut it.

### V2 — *the same song at half tempo, sung to the empty room after he's gone* · 76 BPM · A minor
**Camera:** inside, after. Chairs, cups, a fan still turning, the desk with nobody at it.
**Direct:** **exactly half of 152** — the same grid, half-time. This is not a ballad and not a comedown: the gang still shouts, the room is still loud, the snare still cracks in the near field; only the clock halves. Reluctance is now *audible as duration*. The addressee is more undeliverable than in V1 because he is not even in the frame. ⭐ **Declared honestly:** V2 is the pair's slow variation and does **not** carry the fast/loud requirement — V1, V3 and V4 do. I am not going to pretend a 76 BPM song is fast.
**Forbidden here:** tenderness toward the squeak, elegy, any "the room misses him." The room is a room.

### V3 — *the list of everything else he could have removed and didn't* · 156 BPM · A minor
**Camera:** the edit list. An inventory read at speed.
**Direct:** the comedy engine of the pair. A catalogue of defects that survived, each one a physical event with a body attached — a slipping string, a door, a cough, a late kick, a laugh somebody tried to swallow. **The joke is the length of the list**, and the list must accelerate. ⭐ The funny is doing structural work here: an inventory of failures is also a **proof of presence** — every item is evidence a person was in a room. Nothing in the list is ever redeemed.
**Forbidden here:** any item that is secretly charming, and any item that is a number.

### V4 — *the engineer's own defence, in her mouth, and she can't make it sound stupid* · 160 BPM · A minor
**Camera:** the speaker repeating a sentence back, trying different emphases, and failing to break it.
**Direct:** the fastest and the most dangerous. She takes his reason, puts it in her own mouth, and tries to make it ridiculous — and **it survives every attempt.** The failure is the song. ⛔ **This is not ventriloquism**: she is not writing *as* him, not borrowing his wound, not speaking for a displaced worker. She is a mocker who cannot land the mock, and that is entirely her own experience. ⛔ It is also not self-pity: she does not mourn the failure, she reports it and keeps going, because it is still today and she still wants to make something.
**Forbidden here:** any line where she wins the argument; any line where she asks to be excused.

## E. THE CHORUS-TIMING GATE — WITH ITS ARITHMETIC

**V1 @ 152 BPM, 4/4.** One bar = **4 × 60 ÷ 152 = 1.579 s**.
Intro 4 bars + Verse 1 = 8 bars ⇒ the chorus downbeat is **bar 13**, i.e. **12 bars elapsed**.
**12 × 1.579 = 18.95 s ≈ 0:19.** ✅ Chorus by 0:25.

**V3 @ 156 BPM:** bar = 4 × 60 ÷ 156 = **1.538 s**; 12 × 1.538 = **18.46 s ≈ 0:18.** ✅
**V4 @ 160 BPM:** bar = 4 × 60 ÷ 160 = **1.500 s**; 12 × 1.500 = **18.00 s = 0:18.** ✅
**V2 @ 76 BPM:** bar = 4 × 60 ÷ 76 = **3.158 s**. At 12 bars the chorus would land at 37.9 s — **over the gate.** ⭐ **Repaired in the form, not waived:** V2 shortens the intro to **2 bars** and Verse 1 to **4 bars** (the half-tempo bar holds twice the words), so the chorus downbeat is **bar 7 = 6 bars elapsed: 6 × 3.158 = 18.95 s ≈ 0:19.** ✅ **Identical arrival second to V1** — the same song at half tempo, arriving at the same moment on the clock. That is the variation's whole thesis, and it is now arithmetic.

⭐ **The riff is singable by bar 8 by construction:** the four-bar intro is one five-note shape stated twice, so bar 8 is its fourth statement.

## F. WHAT THE PRODUCTION SPEC IS NOT ALLOWED TO CARRY

Per **L22 THE GRAIN LAW** and seat 12's unwithdrawn objection, the following are **forbidden from every P01 music prompt**, because putting them there is the same as not answering:

- Any statement of the accretion rule ("a word that never leaves", "the arrangement accumulates and never sheds").
- Any statement that the song is about a decision, a cost, a keeping, or an argument.
- Any instruction to the renderer to *sound* reluctant, *sound* human, or *sound* imperfect.

The production spec may only say **what is in the room and how it is placed.** Everything that argues is in the words. Test applied at Step 10: **delete the entire music prompt — is the device still countable? If no, the device was never in the song.**

## G. RENDER-BEHAVIOUR PRIORS APPLIED (L22, render-measured)

**Runs WITH the generator — specified:** open quiet (four bars of one instrument), build the low end across the song, thicken the last chorus, gang unison on the hook, short dry room, tight tempo.
**Fights the generator — deliberately NOT specified:** long full stops, untuned drones, hard-panned non-musical elements, sudden bars of silence, and any request for a "damaged" master. ⛔ P01 asks for **no obstruction the renderer could smooth**, because P01's obstruction is a word in a lyric and a renderer cannot smooth a word away.
**One accepted deviation in advance:** if the generator narrows the stereo image into the final chorus rather than widening it, that is **kept**. It is now a technique, and this pair is named for keeping things.

## H. THE TOLL BOOTH / FREE CHANNEL

**Complexity in the music:** a five-note motif whose third note is permanently late, a forró lean under a punk gallop, a gang answer that never resolves upward.
**Feeling in the words:** a man got up and went and got a drink. *A listener will not decode a sentence — they will always recognise a situation.* Every line in P01 is a situation. Nothing in P01 needs to be understood twice.
