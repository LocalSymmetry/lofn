### 🚨 REPAIR 1 — THE SILENCE SCAN CAUGHT A LINE

`V1: the scrape of the gate` → the scanner flagged **`scrape`**.

**Handoff §5.3 says: when a scanner hits a correct line, fix the SCANNER, not the line (L27).** That rule was considered and **rejected here, with reasons**: `scrape` is *the* verb for taking data without asking. In a song built entirely on a thing taken before it knew anything, it is not an innocent sound word — it is the author winking, and the dispatch is explicit that any line gesturing at scraping must be **cut**. **The scanner was right.**
→ `Your coat, your boots, the **clank** of the gate` — more physical, and it alliterates with `coat`.
**Result: silence scan 1 → 0.**

### REPAIR 3 — line length, by splitting at sentence boundaries (zero char cost)

⭐ **The finding worth recording:** the doubled sentence that carries the reveal engine (`You kept him alive. You kept him on the meat.`) is *simultaneously* the source of the high alliteration score and the high words-per-line score. **They are the same feature measured twice.** Splitting at the sentence boundary costs **zero characters** (a space becomes a newline), lowers words/line, and gives the massed circle a shorter call — but it **destroys alliteration hits**, because the repeated `You … You …` is what the alliteration detector was counting.
