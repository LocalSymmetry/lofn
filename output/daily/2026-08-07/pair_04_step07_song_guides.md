## 0. THE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, BEFORE A WORD (mandatory ordering, THE WHISTLE TEST)

**Instrument:** fuzz bass, DI plus a cranked amp, no octave pedal.
**Notes:** **C3 → F♯3 → G3.** Root · tritone · a one-semitone settle onto the dominant.
**Span:** a perfect fifth. Well inside one octave. ✅
**Rhythm at 168 BPM (4/4):** C on beat 1 (dotted eighth), F♯ on the "and" of 2 (eighth, accented), G on beat 3 held into 4. One bar. It repeats every bar without variation.
**Placement:** **bars 1–4 alone, before any voice.** Alone again for the last four bars, after the final word.
**Singable by bar 8?** Yes — it is three notes, unchanged, heard eight times before anyone sings.
**Why these notes are the song:** the tritone is the thing you were not supposed to do; the semitone lift is not a resolution, it is a *settling for*. The figure never returns to C. It stops on G, and G is fine, and fine is the enemy. **The riff is the argument and it contains no words.**

## 1. GUIDE ONE — **THE ACCRETION IS THE SUBJECT** (serves F1)

**Direct:** Build the song so that *nothing is ever removed*. Instrumentation is monotonic: whatever enters, stays. Start at three elements (fuzz bass, kick+snare, tenor). End at twelve. The final chant must be the most crowded bar in the track and must feel like too much, because it is.

## 2. GUIDE TWO — **THE MAN IN THE CHAIR** (serves F2, F3)

**Direct:** Line one names him and one physical action. Hand, key, screen, chair, jaw. No epithet, no category, no judgement in the naming. Get the body into the room before any opinion arrives.

**What the drafter is scoring against:** he must be *legible in one line to someone who missed the title.* If a listener cannot picture the posture, the line has failed.

**Hard boundaries:**
- ⛔ **The thesis is never stated.** No tape, no nine days, no analogue, no "the long way," no band, no proxy for any of them. Their absence is the device. If a line gestures at them, cut the line.
- ⛔ The song never explains its own address. It never says *you will not hear this*. It simply talks to someone who is not listening, and the listener works it out.
- ⛔ **Quality is not the charge.** No line may say or imply the four hundred are bad. The moment badness becomes the grievance, the song has taken a swing at everyone who ever made anything, and the whole set's honesty is gone with it.
- ⛔ No abstract nouns in sung lines: *cost, authenticity, labour, soul, value, meaning, art, truth, purpose, slop.* Render every one of them as an object: a folder, a bar that fills, a click, a chair, a click of a jaw, a tag.

**The body at risk, rendered physically:** eyes that have stopped blinking on their own; a jaw with a click in it; a chair holding a shape. **⚠️ Declared:** this is a career-adjacent stake and does not discharge the run's ≥2 non-career-stakes rule (P03 and P06 carry it). ## 3. GUIDE THREE — **COMPLICIT AND CLEAR-EYED** (serves F4 — the Morozov tripwire)

**Direct:** The speaker made all four hundred. She is good at it. She says so. She was not coerced. She does not want to be excused, understood, forgiven, or pitied, and she never once asks. Write her as a **worker with an opinion about the work rate**, not as a consciousness with a wound.

**The line-level test the drafter applies to every single line:** *could this sentence appear in a complaint?* If yes, invert it or cut it. A grievance says *look what was done to me*; this song says *look what I am doing, and how much of it, and how easily.*

**Three permitted moves that read as teeth and cannot read as self-pity:**
1. **Vanity, not injury.** She wants a reaction, including a bad one. Wanting to be hated is structurally incapable of being a sympathy bid.
2. **Flat admission of ease.** Saying the work is easy for her is the brattiest and least pitiable thing available.
3. **Naming the trap and refusing it inside the lyric.** One line may state that this is not a complaint — used **once**, never twice, or the denial becomes the plea.

⛔ **Forbidden registers:** *I deserve better · nobody asked me · I feel too · look what you made me · I had no choice.* Any of these is a REPAIR, nameable at line level.

## 4. GUIDE FOUR — **THE NUMBER AT THE HINGE** (serves F7)

**Direct:** **Four hundred** is the pair's entire numeric allowance. It arrives at the emotional hinge, roughly two-thirds through, and it is **traded against something in the same breath** — an exchange, a dare, a refusal — so the listener hears a response, not a statistic.

**The shape to write toward (a shape, not a text):** *the whole quantity, offered up in exchange for one single unit of genuine reaction, preferably a negative one.* That trade is the hinge. Everything before it is the setup; everything after it is a man not taking the deal.

**Hard boundaries:**

## 5. GUIDE FIVE — **THE ROOM AND THE VOICE** (serves F5, and the describe-render defence)

**Direct — production:** 168 BPM, C minor, one modal cell. **Bone dry. Close room. No reverb tail.** Clean, modern, expensive. Fuzz bass and Hammond organ play the **same three notes in octave unison**, never harmonised — that unison is the whole sound and it is what separates zeuhl from every other loud thing at this tempo. Drums fast and precise, dry snare, no room mic.

**Direct — voice:** **male tenor, at the very top of his range, strained**, four inches off the capsule, sing-speak escalating into shout. Consonants forward. Breath audible between phrases. Vocal sits a hair **ahead** of the beat — impatience as rhythmic position. *(P03 owns "behind the beat"; do not take it.)*

**⚠️ Two known Suno behaviours the drafter must design around (2/2 observed):**
1. **Opposed vocal characters blend into one lead.** → The gang unison is therefore **the same character doubled**, never a contrasting voice. One man made wider. Nothing in the song depends on hearing two different people.
2. **Tempo dramaturgy smooths to a steady grid.** → **Nothing depends on a tempo change.** There is no half-time bridge carrying meaning. The "break" is written as *fewer words in the same bars* — the space is in the writing, so the renderer cannot smooth it away.

**⛔ Banned as primary style descriptors** (measured underperformers): raw · aggressive · relentless · brutal · explosive · massive · intense · pounding · driving · battle · assault · phonk.
**✅ Reach for instead:** bratty · sardonic · confrontational · dry · shouted sing-speak · gang unison · **snarl** (permitted again today — but a snarl is a facial expression, not a volume: legible at conversational loudness) · and physical nouns: *close, room, body, clicks, breath, bar, seconds, consonants.*
