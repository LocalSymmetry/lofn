## 5. THE DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK — ONE PASS, ONE REPAIR (handoff §5.7)

**Prediction.** At 168 BPM in C minor with a strained male tenor, fast dry drums and a fuzz bass tritone figure, Suno will produce a fast, dry, loud track with shouted sing-speak, a legible three-note bass hook, and a crowded final third. The chants will render as gang-doubled unison. The additive arrangement will mostly survive because it is written as *instrument entries*, not as a dynamic curve.

**Adversarially: name the one way this renders generic.**
> **It renders as generic loud-guitar-adjacent hardcore with an organ somewhere in the mix.** "Fast, dry, shouted, minor key" is the most crowded corner of the entire model. Nothing in that description is *zeuhl* — it is just metal with a keyboard, and the chant becomes a breakdown.

**Self-repair (applied once, to all four style prompts).** The thing that makes this zeuhl and not metal is **unison**, and unison is a specifiable instruction. The prompts now state, in the front third where the parser weights hardest, that **fuzz bass and Hammond play the same three notes in octave unison and are never harmonised**, and that the chant sits on **one note, hammered, with no lift**. Those two sentences are the genre. A generic hardcore render cannot satisfy them, because generic hardcore harmonises and its breakdowns move.

**Two known Suno behaviours designed around (2/2 observed each) — put in the words, not the spec:**
1. **Opposed vocal characters blend into one lead.** → The gang is **the same tenor doubled**, never a second character. Nothing in any of the four songs depends on hearing two people. If Suno collapses them, the song is unharmed.
2. **Tempo dramaturgy smooths to a steady grid.** → **No tempo change anywhere.** The Break is written as *fewer words in the same number of bars* — the space is in the line lengths, so the renderer cannot flatten it out.
