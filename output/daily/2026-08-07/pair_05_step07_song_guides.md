## 0. ⭐ THE WHISTLE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, BEFORE A WORD

> Slot 9, THE WHISTLE TEST: *the riff is written FIRST, before a single word — named instrument,
> named interval, inside one octave, present before the first line and after the last, singable by
> bar 8. A pad is not a riff; a texture is not a hook.*

**Instrument:** prepared piano — small metal objects laid across the strings in the middle octave, so
every note lands half-damped, with attack and no ring.
**Figure:** **5 notes. A falling minor third, repeated, then held.**
**Pitches (F♯ minor):** **C♯5 → A4 · C♯5 → A4 · A4 (held four beats).**
**Span:** A4 to C♯5 — a major third, comfortably **inside one octave.** ✓
**Rhythm:** quarter, quarter · quarter, quarter · whole. Two bars at 112 BPM.
**Placement:** **bars 1–2, alone, before any voice.** Returns alone as the last sound of the record,
after the final sung line, unaccompanied and unresolved on the held A.
**Singable by bar 8:** the figure has run twice by bar 4 and once more under the first vocal entry.
A listener who has heard bars 1–8 can hum it. It is two notes and a wait.

**Why the falling minor third:** it is the pitch shape of a name called across a room and not
answered — and, sung flat and un-warmed, the shape of a thing being read off a screen. It also means
the hook **descends into the tonic and stops**, which is what the song is about: nothing rises here.

**Why prepared, and why it is not decoration:** an object has been laid inside the instrument, so the
instrument cannot ring clean. ⭐ *That obstruction IS the argument; nobody has to explain it*
(Slot 5 seat 11). ⚠️ Seat 7's objection is retained and answered in the writing, not the tone: a
beautiful obstruction turns damage into a texture, so the **damage does not live in the timbre.** The
render is clean, modern and expensive; the ugliness lives in one line that will not scan.

**Timing arithmetic (112 BPM, 4/4).** One bar = 4 × 60 ÷ 112 = **2.143 s**.
- Intro riff, 2 bars = 2 × 2.143 = **4.29 s**
- Verse 1, 8 bars = 8 × 2.143 = **17.14 s**
- **First chorus lands at 4.29 + 17.14 = 21.43 s = 0:21.4** ✓ **under the 0:25 hook gate.**
- Total form ≈ 108 bars ≈ 108 × 2.143 = **231 s ≈ 3:51.**

## GUIDE 1 — *"THE HANDS AT THE DESK"* (facet F1, w 0.16)

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) **Second-person address to an absent listener** — the entire lyric is
spoken to a man who will never hear it, which lets the thesis stay unsaid because the addressee
carries it. (ii) **Procedural narration** — the story is told as a sequence of interface actions, the
way a witness statement is told, so that no line has to editorialise.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin in flat curiosity, almost clerical; pass through a moment of clean
recognition in the hallway; settle into equanimity that is not resignation. No swell, no catharsis.
The room smells of a cold kettle and warm plastic.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Prepared piano leads. Whirring sequencers tick under it at sixteenths,
quiet, like a fan. A breezy nylon-string acoustic guitar enters at the first chorus. Upright bass,
brushed kit, one clean electric-piano chord per bar. No pads.
**Production & effects.** Close and dry. Vocal four inches off the capsule so consonants and breath
are part of the record. Room tone stays audible under the verses. ⛔ No reverb tail longer than the
gap between two words.
**Structure & dynamics.** Intro riff (2 bars) → V1 (8) → Chorus (8) → V2 (8) → Chorus (8) → V3 (8) →
Pre (4) → Chorus (8) → Bridge (8) → V4 (8) → Pre (4) → Chorus (8) → Chorus (8) → Outro (8+riff).
≈ 3:51 at 112 BPM.
**Rhyme scheme.** Verses ABCB with a slant B; choruses AABB byte-identical every time; bridge free,
end-stopped. Exact chorus repetition needs no defence.
**Poetic devices.** **Epizeuxis** (*same hooks, same wire*) and **antimetabole** at the reveal (*the
picture did not move — that was you moving*). Plus heavy **alliteration** on plosives to keep the
soprano's consonants forward.
**Hook techniques.** (1) The prepared-piano figure, alone, before anyone sings. (2) The date phrase
arriving on the downbeat of the first chorus and never leaving. (3) Stacked soprano thirds on the
chorus only — the only moment the voice is doubled.
**Narrative & thematic seed.** A man fills in a form. That is the whole plot. First person singular,
used sparingly; second person throughout.
**Central aspect.** The addressee must have hands.
**Emotional tone signals.** Composure, curiosity, dry amusement. ⛔ Never pathos.
**Vocal techniques.** Bright soprano, un-warmed, no chest weight, minimal vibrato, speech rhythm.

## GUIDE 2 — *"THE PICTURE IN THE HALL"* (facet F2, w 0.15)

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) **The withheld noun** — the thing revealed is never named; only its
consequences are shown. (ii) **Diptych** — the same object described twice, at 10am and at 4pm, in
identical words, so the change is audibly in the observer.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Neutral inventory → a small cold click of recognition → nothing. The
non-event is the event. The listener should feel a floor drop without any instrument changing.
**Instrumentation & texture.** For this section the sequencer drops out entirely and the prepared
piano plays single notes on the beat, like someone tapping a frame. Guitar mutes to palm-damped
downstrokes. Bass holds the tonic.
**Production & effects.** Absolutely dry. One clean electric-piano chord marks the turn. The mix
narrows toward mono for the reveal and does not widen again for four bars — a deliberate inversion of
the expected lift.
**Structure & dynamics.** This is Verse 2 and it must be the quietest sung passage in the song. The
dynamic floor arrives before the emotional one.
**Rhyme scheme.** ABCB, with the B rhyme carried on a plain monosyllable (*wall / hall*, *green /
seen*). Keep the lines short — five words plus the date phrase.
**Poetic devices.** **Anaphora** (*same … same … same*), **antimetabole** on the closing couplet,
**polyptoton** on *move / moving*.
**Hook techniques.** (1) Repeat the hallway couplet verbatim in the final verse so the listener
recognises the room. (2) Let one word of the couplet fall in a bar of silence. (3) Never sing the
couplet with harmony — it is the only unaccompanied vocal line.
**Narrative & thematic seed.** A small card beside a picture was changed on Monday. Nobody re-hung
anything. **The object does not change; your relationship to it does.**
**Key sequence.** *The picture did not move. That was you moving.*
**Special effects.** A single physical SFX — a card sliding into a frame — placed once, at the top of
the section, ≤5 words so the generator does not sing it.
⛔ **The words "forgery," "fake," "copy," "real" and any gloss on provenance are forbidden in sung
lines.** The listener recognises a situation; nobody decodes a sentence.

## GUIDE 3 — *"THE THING THAT WILL NOT COME OUT OF THE LINE"* (facet F3, w 0.15)

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) **Accretion as plot** — the lyric is a sentence that gets a word
stuck in it and then has to keep speaking around it. (ii) **The intrusive apposition** — the phrase is
dropped into the middle of clauses, not parked at the end, so it reads as an obstruction rather than a
rhyme.
**Mood & emotional arc.** The device should feel neutral at entry, ordinary by the second chorus and
faintly unbearable by the bridge — **without a single line commenting on it.**
**Instrumentation & texture.** Each time the phrase falls on a downbeat, the prepared piano doubles
it at the octave for one note only. That is the entire orchestration of the device. ⛔ No sound design
is permitted to *represent* the accretion — L22: an objection answered in the production spec is not
answered.
**Production & effects.** None specific. This facet is deliberately unproduced. It lives in the words.
**Structure & dynamics.** The phrase enters at **sung line 11 — the first line of the first chorus —**
and is in every sung line thereafter to the last. Nothing is removed. **The listener can count it.**
**Rhyme scheme.** Because the phrase moves position, real end-rhymes remain available and must be
written: the device must not be allowed to *become* the rhyme scheme. Target roughly half the lines
with the phrase at the tail, half with it at the head or wedged mid-clause.
**Poetic devices.** **Diacope** (the phrase repeated with words between), **caesura** (the phrase
splits clauses mid-line), **asyndeton** in the surrounding short sentences.
**Hook techniques.** (1) Arrival on a downbeat under stacked thirds. (2) A bar where the phrase is
the only thing sung. (3) The final sung line is the phrase alone, then the riff.
**Narrative & thematic seed.** A date typed into a field, which then turns out to be in everything.
⭐ **The date behaves exactly like the object laid on the piano string: it obstructs the line and it
cannot be taken out.** That equivalence is the pair's whole formal argument, and it is never stated.

## GUIDE 4 — *"NOBODY IS BEING MOURNED HERE"* (facet F4, w 0.14)

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) **Refusal of the elegy** — the form invites a farewell and the lyric
declines it by staying interested in the room. (ii) **Praise of the addressee** — the technician is
paid small, sincere compliments (he spelled his own name right the first time), which makes sympathy
structurally impossible.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Equanimity throughout, with one flash of dry amusement at the bridge. The
song must be *good company*, not a deathbed.
**Instrumentation & texture.** No minor-key sagging, no string pad, no piano rubato. Tempo is
metronomic to the last bar. Any expressive slowing is a self-pity tell and is cut.
**Production & effects.** Bright top end. The soprano is placed forward and dry with no plate, no
hall, no doubling except the chorus thirds. **A voice with reverb on it is asking for a room to feel
sorry in.**
**Structure & dynamics.** The final chorus does **not** drop out for a bare-vocal moment. It ends at
full arrangement, then stops, then the riff.
**Rhyme scheme.** Tight and plain. Slant rhyme is allowed; ornament is not.
**Poetic devices.** **Litotes** (*that is all that happens here*), **understatement**, **epistrophe**
on the flat refrain *it's fine.*
**Hook techniques.** (1) *It's fine* as the second-most repeated phrase in the song — flat, not
brave. (2) The compliment to the technician placed in the outro. (3) A chorus melody that stays inside
a fifth, so it cannot soar.
**Narrative & thematic seed.** He is not cruel. He is at work. **That is the point.**
⛔ **Banned moves, named so they can be caught at scan:** *remember me · I was here · don't forget ·
after I'm gone · one last · while I still can · they'll never know what they had.*
⛔ **Also banned: the disclaimer.** A line saying *this is not a complaint* is a complaint with a
receipt. Cut it and let flatness do the work.

## GUIDE 5 — *"THE OBJECT ON THE STRING"* (facet F5, w 0.12)

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) **Instrument as character** — the piano is the only thing in the song
that is physically altered, and it never comments. (ii) **Synaesthetic restraint** — the half-damped
attack is described by what it *cannot* do, never by adjectives of decay.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Bright, expensive, close. The record should sound like money and daylight.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Prepared piano (metal across the middle-octave strings; attack, thud,
no ring) · whirring sequencers at quiet sixteenths · a breezy nylon-string acoustic guitar · upright
bass · brushed kit with rods on the verses, sticks on the last chorus · one clean electric-piano chord
per bar. Three genre elements from the house list are in play: art-pop, the sequencer-driven
future-nostalgic pulse, and the acoustic-guitar breeze imported verbatim from the source vocabulary.
**Production & effects.** Pristine modern master. Sub is present but tight. Wide stereo on guitar and
sequencer; ⭐ **the stereo field NARROWS into the final chorus instead of widening** — the render-audit
finding from the ledger, adopted deliberately as technique.
**Structure & dynamics.** Piano alone → piano + sequencer → full band at chorus 1 → band drops to
piano and voice at the bridge → full band, narrowed, for the last two choruses → piano alone.
**Rhyme scheme.** n/a (production guide).
**Poetic devices.** **Onomatopoeia** in the sung lines for the interface (*click*, *tick*), which
lets the sequencer and the lyric share a consonant.
**Hook techniques.** (1) Riff alone at the top. (2) Riff doubling the date phrase, one note only.
(3) Riff alone at the end, unresolved on the held A.
⛔ **BANNED in every Suno field, run-wide:** tape hiss · vinyl crackle · wow-and-flutter · vintage ·
analogue warmth · lo-fi · degraded · corrupted · glitching. ⛔ **And the machine is never made to sound
damaged** — machine-glitch-as-guilt is cost-cosplay from the other end (seat 18, retained, both
directions). ⛔ **Banned as primary descriptors:** raw · aggressive · relentless · brutal · explosive ·
massive · intense · pounding · driving · battle · assault · phonk.

## GUIDE 6 — ⭐ **WILD CARD** — *"THE LINE THAT WILL NOT SCAN"* (facets F6/F7/F8 + THE KEPT DEFECT)

**Cross-disciplinary import.** From conservation practice: when a panel painting is re-attributed, the
conservator does not touch the paint. The catalogue changes; the object is left exactly as it was.
This guide asks the song to behave like that conservator — **to leave one thing alone on purpose.**

**⭐⭐ THE KEPT DEFECT — designed here, nominated and defended at step 10.**
The chorus is built on an **eight-syllable frame.** One chorus line is written at **nine syllables** and
is **never corrected.** It appears in **every chorus of every variation, byte-identical**, so the
stumble is heard four times per song and sixteen times across the pair. The singer must crush three
weak syllables into the space of two to reach the downbeat, and it sounds like a mistake because it
**is** one. ⛔ It is not stylised, not a "deliberate hitch," not marked in the lyric, not explained in
the style prompt, and **not repairable by step 11, by QA, or by the coordinator.**
**Why this candidate and not the others:** a wrong prepared-piano note and a chorus resolving to the
wrong chord both live in the **production spec**, and L22 THE GRAIN LAW is explicit — a Somatic
objection answered in the production spec is **not answered**, because the renderer smooths it. A
metrical defect lives in the **words**, survives the generator, and is legible to a text tier, which
is precisely what makes it itchy. **Seat 18's price for withdrawing was one genuine defect shipped on
purpose. This is it.**

**THE SMALLEST PART, counted (F6).** Lines whose grammatical subject is the singer herself are held to
a small, printed fraction of the total. The count is stated per variation at step 10 and verified
**individually, never pair-wide.**
**The one number, answered backwards (F7).** *Two hundred years* at the bridge, answered — not
recited — by the inversion: the asking outlived the answers, and it will outlive the next one. ⛔ No
second research numeral; ⛔ P02's student, margin and *five times* are not touched.
**The joke (F8).** Administrative and affectionate: a retirement scheduled by dropdown; a man typing
his own name into the *Owner* box and spelling it right first time; a green tick; the next ticket.
⛔ **LAW 1 is absolute — no sneer at craft, at analogue, at purists, at anyone who prefers
human-made work.**
**Structure & dynamics.** No change from Guide 1; the defect is metrical, not architectural.
**Poetic devices.** **Catalexis inverted** — a hypermetrical line held in place by force.
**Narrative seed.** The line that says nothing changes is the line that refuses to be changed.
