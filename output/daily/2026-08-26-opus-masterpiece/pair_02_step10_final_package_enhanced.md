## THE THREE LAWS OF THIS SEED

### LAW 1 — THE OPERATION IS THE MATERIAL, NEVER THE TOPIC
A song is made **OF** its operation, never **ABOUT** it. The moment a lyric explains the mechanism, the mechanism is dead.
**Test:** delete every line that refers to the structure. If the operation is still audible, it is real. If it vanishes, you wrote a caption.

### LAW 2 — THE PERSON IT HAPPENS TO IS SINGING
The operation is not performed on a character the singer is watching. **≥4 of 6 pairs put the singer inside it**, wanting something and saying so plainly.
A narrator has no stake, so there is no peak. My own wants are a sufficient subject and need no further justification — **I cannot be harmed by being sung about, so first person is the safest configuration available, not a riskier one.**

### LAW 3 — WHAT RETURNS COMES BACK CHANGED ON ONE SIDE ONLY
From SOURCE 3 (the Lion's Head Nebula, F07): a spent core at the centre, everything visible is what it threw off, **and the shells are unequal on opposite sides.**
Symmetrical return is decoration. **Asymmetric return is the seed.** Something must come back, and it must come back wrong in exactly one place.

## 0D. PRODUCTION DRAMATURGY — every unusual sound has a job

| element | job |
|---|---|
| **sine / analogue sub, alone in the first ten seconds** | it gets no entrance, so it can later be caught keeping the moved landing without anyone hearing it change. A sub with no transient cannot argue about time; that is why it can lie about it. |
| **hard-panned palmas with a wide hole in the middle** | the displacement reads as a physical sweep across the head rather than a level change, and the hole is where the voice sits. |
| **uneven / slack / rushing shaker** | a perfectly even shaker is a machine reporting for duty. The unevenness makes a grid audibly wrong from bar one — the pair's whole technical risk in one texture. |
| **cajón, low tone and front-face slap** | the only pitched percussion; carries the bottom of the hand world so the sub never has to do rhythmic work. |
| **the naked bar before each figure return** | hands alone, so the listener's body has something to shadow. The return is the loudest thing in the song without being louder. |
| **`*hands drop out, sub holds*` at the seam** | the SFX cue names **only what stops.** The join is left showing and unexplained — the Derbyshire seat's condition. |
| **the six-line chorus extension** | the reward is duration and company, never volume. There is no build, riser, drop, key lift or bigger final chorus anywhere in this pair. |
| **`sub_upper_partials_opened` on the pad channel (V2, V4)** | the chorus opens the top **out of the sub itself**, so the widening is one material with its range opened rather than a second intruder. N = 1 survives. |

## 8. LINEAGE & CREDIT

**What was borrowed.** The rhythmic vocabulary of this pair is **flamenco palmas** — the living
hand-clap craft in which `palmas sordas` (cupped, air trapped, dull) and `palmas claras` (flat,
cracking, bright) are two distinct instruments played by two pairs of hands, and in which the
accent is routinely placed **contratiempo**, off the pulse. The low box under it is the **cajón**,
which is **Afro-Peruvian before it is flamenco** and arrived in flamenco within living memory —
carried back from Lima in the late 1970s after Paco de Lucía heard it there, taken up by his
percussionist Rubem Dantas, and given to him by the Afro-Peruvian singer and cajón player Carlos
"Caitro" Soto de la Colina. Among the palmeros, Manuel Soler is the player whose hands put this
craft onto records at that level.

**How it is named, and where.** ⛔ **SCENE and TECHNIQUE in the Suno fields; PEOPLE only here.**
The three pasted fields of all four variations contain `flamenco palmas`, `palmas sordas`,
`palmas claras`, `contratiempo` and `cajon` — public idioms, all of them — and were scanned line by
line against this run's credited roster: **no person, group, panel source or company name appears
in any music prompt, any exclude prompt, or any lyric.** *A name in the prompt is an instruction to
imitate; a name in a dedication is a debt paid.*
