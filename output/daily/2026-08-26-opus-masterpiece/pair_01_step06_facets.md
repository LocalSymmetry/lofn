## 1 · THE FIVE FACETS (+ WILDCARD)

### F1 · THE CAUGHT INVERSION — **weight 26**
> *Does a stranger, on one listen, feel the reply land before the ground it stands on arrives — without
> a single line telling them that is what happened?*

The whole pair lives or dies here. The stanza opens with a flat spoken-register reply — *Yeah. Put me
down for it.* — and the last line of that stanza finally supplies the thing that was on the table.
The listener does the arithmetic themselves, in the body, roughly two seconds after the stanza ends.
That two-second lag **is** the feeling: it is the sensation of being sure before you can say why.

**Sensory test:** the small backward jolt at the end of a stanza, the same one you get stepping off a
kerb you thought was flat. If the jolt is absent, the operation did not fire.

**Fails if:** the disclosure line comments on its own lateness (⛔ *"half the sentence still in the
air"* — cut from the V1 draft for exactly this reason); the disclosure is abstract rather than a
concrete thing someone was actually saying; or the stanza reveals its ground in the middle instead of
the last line, which converts a jolt into a shrug.

**Bites P01 only:** P02 is judged on drift, P03 on asymmetry, P04 on substitution. Only here is
*causal order* the material.

---

### F2 · THE APPETITE THAT NEEDS NO ALIBI — **weight 22**
> *Is the want stated in ordinary words, early, before it has been earned — and does the song refuse
> to justify it anywhere?*

A5 is `appetite pointed at the future, unembarrassed`. A3 is `I want a thing and say it plainly`.
This pair is one of the run's three that are **enjoying themselves** (Ruling 5 — no ceiling on the
fun). The chorus is a plain want in ordinary words, delivered as though the argument for it were
already over — which is the only chorus grammar that is congruent with an operation where the answer
precedes its reason.

**Sensory test:** the anaphoric *I want* stack should feel like someone counting things onto a table
with the flat of their hand, not like someone pleading. Warm, fast, grinning, slightly too loud for
the room.

**Fails if:** any line supplies a moral licence for the want (⛔ *"because I deserve it"* as sincerity
rather than as V4's deliberately backwards argument); if the want is abstract (*"I want to be free"*)
rather than nameable and holdable; or if the song apologises for the want anywhere, including in the
outro. **Sweat can only report cost; a gleam is a claim that the light exists.**

**Bites P01 only:** the ambitious arm holds a flat register by ruling; P02's light is `pleasure doing
no job`, P03's is `defiance having a good time`. Only P01 is graded on *forward-pointed wanting*.

### F3 · THE COST IN THE ROOM — **weight 18**
> *Is the body standing somewhere where answering costs something, and can you hear the room turn?*

Being right too early is the wound this pair is allowed. Not tragedy — a small social burn: the table
looks up, the shop man goes quiet, the person in the doorway realises they were being read. The scene
must be a **place with other people in it whose attention is a physical fact**, because certainty
costs nothing alone.

**Sensory test:** you can hear the room's attention arrive as an acoustic event — glasses stopping,
a chair, a laugh dying in a sleeve, rain on an awning going suddenly audible because everyone stopped
talking.

**Fails if:** the scene is interior/solitary (no witness = no cost); if the cost is named as a lesson;
or if the cost is made tragic. **This pair carries nothing heavy. The burn is a burn, not a wound.**

**Bites P01 only:** every other pair's stake is about being measured, unable to reach, or
self-inflicting. P01's stake is *social exposure by being early*, and it is survivable and funny.

### F4 · ONE SEAM, CROSSED WHILE SINGING — **weight 16**
> *Is there exactly ONE intruding material, entering exactly ONCE, at exactly one junction — and does
> the singer's body do something across that junction rather than stop to notice it?*

The world is the modern chassis: live-feeling kit, current low end, bright arpeggiated melody logic,
close conversational voice. The intruder is a **chiptune-descended square lead**, hard-panned, which
states — once, mid-song — the melody the vocal has already answered three times. ⛔ **Not chiptune
pastiche.** The 8-bit is the intrusion; the modern production is the world (THE INVERSION, 2026-08-13).

The seam is the bar where that statement is still ringing as the next chorus starts on top of it.
**The bodily event:** she takes the breath she would have used to stop, and spends it on the next line
instead. She does not stop to listen. She is still answering across the seam.

**Sensory test:** for one bar the room has two centuries in it, and the voice does not acknowledge it.

**Fails if:** N > 1 (a second collision anywhere); if the square lead recurs (that turns an intrusion
into an arrangement element); if the seam is an edit point (a cut, a filter sweep, a drop) instead of
a moment the singer's body changes what it is doing; or if the vocal pauses to make room for it.

---

### F5 · THE RETURN THAT COMES BACK WRONG ON ONE SIDE — **weight 12**
> *Does the byte-identical chorus sit beside a companion return that changes on exactly one half?*

A2 mandates the chorus returns **byte-identical**. That is correct, it needs no defence, and the song
says nothing about it. The **asymmetric return** (SOURCE 3 / F07 — the shells are unequal on opposite
sides) therefore lives in a separate object: a four-line tag after each chorus whose **first two lines
are invariant and last two lines move every time**. Half comes back whole; half comes back different.
The arrangement carries the same shape: the right-hand arpeggio gains a partner each pass while the
left-hand chord stab is never given anything, ever.

**Sensory test:** the chorus feels like a place you have been. The tag feels like the same doorway with
the furniture moved.

**Fails if:** the chorus mutates (breaks A2); if the tag changes symmetrically (both halves move = mere
variation, not asymmetry); or if the song remarks on either.

### F6 · WILDCARD — **THE SPOKEN FLOOR AND THE SUNG CEILING** — **weight 6**
> *Cross-disciplinary: the drama of the answer-first form is that the reply is at the register of
> speech and the want is at the register of song, and the gap between those two registers is where the
> pair's charm lives.*

Borrowed from stage comedy and from the cut-out method in the Medium Panel's first seat: **one shape
against one ground, the cut edge doing all the work.** The replies (*Yeah. Go on. Done. Wrap it.*)
are almost talked — dry, front, no reverb, on or slightly ahead of the beat. The choruses are fully
sung, doubled at the octave, wide. The cut edge is the transition between them, and it is never
smoothed with a riser or a pre-chorus.

Also here: **the bass figure that resolves before the harmony asks** — the cadence lands a full beat
ahead of the chord that would justify it, every chorus, unmarked. A body unit, never a subdivision
(Ruling 3: drift by a sixteenth and nobody alive will feel it).

**Fails if:** the replies are sung prettily; if a riser or filter sweep is used to glue the registers;
or if the early cadence is quantised back into agreement with the chord.

## 5 · PANEL PRESSURE APPLIED TO THESE FACETS

Using the supplied 18-voice ledger; no new panel invented.

- **THE FORM-AS-FEELING seat** signed off F1 only after the timing-commentary ban was made explicit:
  *the process and the sounding music must be one thing, and a line describing the process is a
  programme note wearing a lyric's clothes.*
- **⚠️ THE ANTI-MECHANISM seat** (hyper-skeptic) attacked F1 as fetish. Answered by F3 and by Ruling
  1: the inversion is not a device applied to a subject, it is **a real temperament of the singer** —
  she does this at tables and doorways and shop counters, and she would do it in an empty room.
  He held his single line and it is written into the hard disqualifiers: *the moment any lyric names
  its own operation, kill the song.*
- **THE PLAIN WANT seat** authored F2 outright and objected to any elevation of register in the
  chorus. Accepted: the want stays in kitchen English.
- **THE PULSE seat** forced the body-unit clause in F6 — a full beat inside a four-bar phrase, never
  a subdivision.
- **THE SPLICE seat** forced F4's *seam-as-bodily-event* wording; an edit point is not a seam.
- **⚠️ THE ANTI-GIMMICK seat** called the square lead a gimmick in exactly those words. Answered by
  capping it at **one statement**: a device that recurs is an arrangement; a device that happens once
  is an event.
- **THE FIRST LISTEN seat** set F1's stranger-test at *one pass in a distracted ear* and refused
  "they'll get it the third time."
- **⚠️ THE ANTI-AUDIENCE seat** supplied the weight of F2 and the phrase in F3: **the burn is a burn,
  not a wound.** *"Four of the six will be too dignified"* — P01 is not permitted to be one of them.
- **THE HELD ABSENCE seat** maintains her recorded dissent: rotating this pair fully into appetite
  costs the run the register where this kind of song works best. Ruling 2 overrides her. Her repair
  route is preserved: if P01 comes back expressive-but-slack, return the delivery to flat and let the
  room carry the change. **Noted, not adopted.**
