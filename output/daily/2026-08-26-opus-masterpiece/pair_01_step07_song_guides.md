# 0 · THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — ANSWERED CONCRETELY, BEFORE ANYTHING

## 0.1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**It must be a place where answering costs something.** Answering costs nothing alone; it costs when
other people's attention is a physical fact in the room. So every variation puts her **in a witnessed
room with a hard floor, a door, and weather outside it** — never in a private interior, never in a
metaphorical space.

| var | the exact place | what the body is touching | what makes answering cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | a weeknight pub quiz, front-left table under a broken fruit machine | palm flat on a warm plastic buzzer, the plastic still holding the heat of her own hand | the answer is public and the whole table's night rides on it; being right too early makes the room look at her like she cheated |
| **V2** | the doorway between a kitchen and a hall, 1 a.m., a party thinned to five | one shoulder on the door frame, a mug she did not want going cold in both hands | she is answering something the other person never said out loud, so answering reveals that she has been reading them all night |
| **V3** | the wet loading ramp at the back door of a small venue, six in the evening | flight-case corners eating the flats of her palms, rain coming off an awning onto her wrists | the third time she says yes it moves her set to the empty hour, and the yes is the same yes as the first two |
| **V4** | a second-hand shop counter on a wet Tuesday, a beat-up synth on the glass | a bank card already out of her sleeve, coat dripping onto lino, a biro on a string | she has committed the money before hearing what is wrong with it, and the man behind the glass is kind about it, which is worse |

**Common physical grammar across all four:** a threshold (table edge / door frame / ramp / counter),
weather audible from outside, at least three other bodies whose attention can turn, and one small
warm object in her hands. ⭐ **The room's attention is written as an ACOUSTIC event, never a visual
one** — glasses stopping, a chair, a laugh dying in a sleeve, rain on an awning going suddenly loud
because everybody stopped talking. That is Flair 8 (The Body That Notices) and Flair 3 (Amplified
Quiet: silence heightened by what is placed against it, never by absence alone).

## 0.2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**Being right too early.** Not being wrong — being *right*, ahead of the evidence, in front of
witnesses. That injury has an exact shape and every variation carries it:

- You are correct and you cannot yet prove it, so certainty reads as arrogance, or as cheating, or as
  having been told.
- The reason arrives afterwards and vindicates you privately, which is no use, because the room has
  already decided.
- And the only defence available — *wait, let me explain* — is the one thing the song is forbidden to
  do, because explaining is the caption Adorno's seat said to kill the song for.

**So the injury is written as a small social burn, never as a wound.** She gets warm around the ears,
the table goes quiet for one bar, somebody laughs into their sleeve. Then she wants the next thing
anyway, harder, out loud, and the song's light is that recovery — `appetite pointed at the future,
unembarrassed`. **This pair carries nothing heavy. It is one of the run's three that are enjoying
themselves, and it is not required to smuggle depth.**

## 0.3 — THE BODY TEST (Ruling 3), ANSWERED

*Would the singer still be inside this operation if nobody were recording?*
**Yes.** This is a temperament, not a technique: she answers before the question finishes, at tables,
in doorways, on ramps, at counters, when the tape is off and when there is no tape. The song does not
apply an operation to her. It reports one.

*Stated in body units, never notation units:* **the reply lands a full beat before the last word of
the question clears the air** — one beat inside a four-bar phrase, which everybody feels, including
people who cannot name what happened. The bass cadence likewise lands **a whole beat ahead** of the
chord that would justify it. ⛔ Nothing in this pair is specified in sixteenths.

# 1 · GUIDE ONE — *THE CAUGHT INVERSION* (serves F1)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **the flash-forward disclosure**, borrowed from the seat after the
novelist who tells you the ending in chapter one so that the telling itself becomes the dread —
inverted here into delight: the reply is the flash-forward, and the last line of the stanza is the
belated chapter one; (b) **in-medias-res dialogue**, where the listener joins a conversation already
one turn old and must reconstruct the missing turn. The emotional arc runs *snap decision → the room
turning → a two-second lag → the small warm shock of understanding what she just agreed to*, and it
must run that way four to six times per song without ever becoming a joke with a punchline, because a
punchline resolves and this must stay a **reflex**. Instrumentation should keep the stanza floor bare
enough that the last line lands into space: a close kit with a short room snare, one bass, one dry
chord stab, and **nothing filling the gap before the disclosure** — Flair 3, silence heightened by
what is placed against it. Production is dry and forward, the voice four inches off the capsule with
audible breath, almost no reverb on the replies so they sit at the register of speech; the tail of the
stanza's last line is the only place a short plate is permitted, roughly the length of a room, so the
disclosure hangs for one beat and then the chorus takes it. Structurally the stanza must **never**
reveal its ground in the middle: the disclosure is always the final line, and the stanza lengths are
deliberately unequal at 8 / 8 / 6 so the listener cannot count their way to it. Rhyme is **kept and
dense**, AABBCCDD across four couplets, because a closed sound arriving in an open situation is
precisely what lets the ear file a reply as an answer before it knows the question — this pair pays no
rhyme debt because it removes nothing. Poetic devices: **anaphora** on the reply word at the head of
every stanza, and **hypozeuxis** (each short clause carrying its own verb) in the replies so they read
as separate decisions rather than one sentence. ⛔ **Not one line may comment on the ordering.**
Hook technique for this guide: let the disclosure line of verse one be the lowest, quietest, most
conversational line in the song, so its content does all the work and nothing in the production
underlines it.

# 2 · GUIDE TWO — *THE APPETITE THAT NEEDS NO ALIBI* (serves F2)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **the itemised list as characterisation** — a person is what they
name when asked what they want, and the naming is the plot; (b) **the direct address that skips
persuasion**, borrowed from the panel seat who holds that a poem is spoken to one person and needs no
elevation to earn its place. The arc is *want stated flat → want elaborated with increasingly small
and specific objects → want restated identically, at higher heat*, with sub-emotions running
`Eagerness → Delight → Zeal → Liberation`; never `Pride`, because pride is retrospective and this
light is future-pointed. Instrumentation: the chorus is where the arrangement finally opens — the
voice doubles at the octave, the bright arpeggio is joined by a second arpeggio a third above on each
successive pass, hand percussion or claps arrive at chest height, and the low end goes wide and warm.
Production keeps the chorus **dry-forward, not cathedral**: this is a want said across a table, not
announced from a balcony, so no long tails, no washes, no choir. Structurally the chorus returns
**byte-identical every time**; nothing about it is defended, mutated, or remarked upon, and the
arrangement's growth is the only thing that changes underneath it. Rhyme in the chorus is a paired
ABCB / DEFE lock with the anaphoric *I want* carrying the density, so it stays singable by a stranger
after fifteen seconds. Poetic devices: **anaphora** (the engine), **polysyndeton** in the run-up to
the last line, and **epanalepsis** where the chorus's first phrase returns as its last. ⛔ **No line
anywhere supplies a moral licence for the want** — the only permitted argument is V4's, and V4's runs
*backwards*, from the want to the entitlement, which is a different figure entirely. Hook techniques:
(1) the title phrase placed as line one of the chorus and again as its final line, so the fifteen-second
clip contains it twice; (2) the octave double dropping out for the penultimate line so one bare voice
says the plainest thing; (3) a single hand-clap layer that only ever appears under the words *I want*
and nowhere else, so the phrase acquires its own percussion.

# 3 · GUIDE THREE — *THE COST IN THE ROOM* (serves F3)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **the witnessed gesture** — a small physical act (a hand coming down
on hers, a thumb leaving a mug, a biro on a string going still) reported without interpretation, in the
manner of the grid painter's seat: the work is about a state, not a depiction, and the state arrives
through what is withheld; (b) **the overheard third party**, one bystander whose reaction is the only
evaluation the song ever offers. Emotional arc: *heat in the ears → the room's attention arriving as
sound → a beat of exposure → and then appetite again, unhumbled*. The turn required by the Golden Move
lives exactly here, past the midpoint: the third stanza must complicate the opening stance by making
the same reflex cost something it did not cost the first two times. Instrumentation carries the cost
through **subtraction, not through minor-key sadness** — for one bar the kit drops to rim and hat, the
bass holds instead of moving, and the room tone (glasses, talk, rain on an awning) becomes the loudest
thing in the mix. Production: capture the room as an actual bed, not a reverb — a stereo bed of low
chatter and glassware, or rain under an awning, wide and behind the voice, ducking under sung lines
and rising in the gaps, so the audience is physically present in the master. Dynamics: the cost bar
is the quietest bar in the song and it is **followed immediately by the loudest chorus**, with no
riser, no filter sweep and no drum fill bridging them — the cut edge does the work, one shape against
one ground. Rhyme: the shortest stanza (three couplets) is where the cost lands, and its couplets
should tighten to slant rhyme rather than full rhyme, so the ear registers a small imperfection
without being told about it. Poetic devices: **asyndeton** through the cost bar (clauses stripped of
connectives, breath-short), and **synaesthesia** in exactly one place — a temperature word applied to
a sound. ⛔ **The cost is never named as a lesson and never made tragic. The burn is a burn.**

# 4 · GUIDE FOUR — *ONE SEAM, CROSSED WHILE SINGING* (serves F4)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **the unremarked intrusion** — a second material enters and no
character acknowledges it, which is how a reader learns that the narrator is busy; (b) **the object
that answers back**, a device from folk narrative where a thing supplies the line a person will not.
The emotional register across the seam is *Fascination held at bay by Zeal* — she notices and refuses
to be diverted. The world of this song is a **current chassis**: live-feeling kit with a short room
snare, a modern low end, a bright arpeggiated melody logic, close conversational voice, present-day
edits. The intruder is a **chiptune-descended square lead**, hard-panned to one side, dry, slightly
aliased, arriving with a one-bar collapse of the whole mix's bit depth (Flair 15 — hi-fi to grit at
the exact moment certainty is at stake) and it states, **once, mid-song**, the melody the vocal has
already answered three times. ⛔ **Not chiptune pastiche.** The 8-bit is the intrusion, the modern
production is the world; if the square lead recurs it stops being an event and becomes an arrangement,
and the pair fails F4. Structure: the statement begins alone in a short break at roughly 55–60% of the
run time and is **still ringing when the next chorus starts on top of it** — that overlap bar is the
seam, and it is the only junction in the song where two materials are simultaneously load-bearing.
The bodily event across it, which is what makes it a seam and not an edit point: **she takes the
breath she would have used to stop, and spends it on the next line instead** — audible as a caught
inhale a beat early, and then she is simply singing, on time, over the top of the thing that explains
her. Production must resist the two obvious cheats: no filter sweep into the break, no drum fill out
of it. Poetic device relevant here: **antanaclasis** is available if a single word from the chorus can
be repeated once with a changed sense at the seam, but only if it can be done without commentary.
Hook technique: the square lead's melody must be **derivable by ear** from the chorus's own top line —
the same contour, plainer, unharmonised — so a listener recognises it without being able to say why.

# 5 · GUIDE FIVE — *THE UNEQUAL RETURN* (serves F5, and SOURCE 3 / F07)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **the refrain that is a fixed point**, against which everything else
is measured — the song's spent core, small and unchanging, while all the audible material is what was
thrown off it; (b) **the moving tag**, a stanza-end couplet that returns half-identical and half-new,
which is how oral narrative keeps a formula alive while updating the world. Emotional arc across the
returns: *Delight → Delight → Delight, at rising temperature but never at rising complexity.* The
chorus returns **byte-identical, every time, unremarked, undefended** — exact repetition needs no
argument and none is offered. The asymmetry therefore lives in a separate object: a four-line tag
after each chorus whose **first two lines are invariant and whose last two lines move every pass** —
half the shell comes back whole, half comes back different, and the two sides do not match (Flair 2,
The Unequal Shell). The arrangement executes the same rule with instruments: the **right-hand arpeggio
gains one new partner voice each pass** — a third above, then an octave, then a detuned unison — while
the **left-hand chord stab is never given anything, ever**, and stays exactly as bare as it was in the
first chorus. Production: pan the growing side and the static side genuinely apart, keep the sub mono
and locked, and let the stereo image become measurably lopsided by the last chorus without any
automation move announcing it. Structure and dynamics: four choruses, three tags, and the final
chorus is not bigger — it is *wider on one side only*. Rhyme: the tag's invariant half should be
end-stopped and rhyme with itself; the moving half should be enjambed, so the part that changes is also
the part that runs on. Poetic devices: **epistrophe** in the invariant half, **anadiplosis** between
the moving half and the following stanza's opening reply. ⛔ **Nothing in the lyric may remark on
either the sameness or the change.**

# 6 · GUIDE SIX — WILDCARD · *THE SPOKEN FLOOR AND THE SUNG CEILING*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **register collision as characterisation**, borrowed from stage
comedy, where a character who talks at one pitch and sings at another is understood to be two things
at once; (b) **the cut-out method** from the Medium Panel's first seat — one shape against one ground,
the cut edge doing all the work, and a horror of a second idea in the same frame. This guide governs
the physical craft of the transitions. The **replies are almost talked**: dry, front, centre, no
reverb, at or a hair ahead of the beat, with consonants intact and breath audible — the register of a
person deciding out loud. The **choruses are fully sung**: doubled at the octave, wide, warm, a whole
different acoustic. ⛔ **Nothing is permitted to glue those two registers together** — no riser, no
pre-chorus, no filter sweep, no reversed cymbal, no vocal-chop lift. The cut is the event. Underneath
all of it sits the pair's signature device: **a bass figure that resolves before the harmony asks** —
the cadence lands a full beat ahead of the chord that would justify it, every chorus, unmarked and
uncorrected, so the low end is permanently one step further along than the room it is in. It must be
specified in body units, a whole beat inside a four-bar phrase; ⛔ **a sixteenth-note version of this
idea is inaudible to a body and is an automatic repair.** Every variation additionally carries a
**kinetic defect** — an asymmetric groove that never resolves: a phrase nine beats long where the ear
expects eight, or a bar that drops its last beat every fourth pass, or a floor where the sub takes
beat one only every fourth bar. Rotate the defect per variation; never let two variations limp the
same way. Poetic devices for this guide: **caesura as wound** — one mid-line breath per song, placed
where the meaning changes — and **epizeuxis** in the tag's invariant half. Hook techniques:
(1) let the very first sound of the song be her voice saying a reply, before any instrument, so the
first five seconds are a decision; (2) put a single standalone `*SFX*` at the seam and nowhere else;
(3) end on the question, alone, with nothing under it.

# 9 · PANEL SIGN-OFF ON THESE GUIDES

- **THE ROOM seat** asked the canonical question — *can a stranger hear the process happening, or only
  read about it?* — and was satisfied only by §0.1's insistence that the disclosure be a concrete thing
  somebody was actually saying. An abstract disclosure is unhearable.
- **THE ATTENTION seat** required that the listener be a participant, not a target: the two-second lag
  after each stanza is deliberately left empty so the listener's own mind supplies the operation.
- **THE SUBSTANCE seat** rejected the premise that formal rigour and physical pleasure trade against
  each other, and is the reason the chorus is allowed to be the loudest, warmest, most straightforwardly
  enjoyable thing in the run's accessible arm.
- **THE THIRTY SECONDS seat** demanded the title phrase inside any fifteen-second window of the chorus
  — met by placing it as the chorus's first and last line.
- **THE LOST FUTURE seat** flagged the arcade lead as a nostalgia risk and was answered by THE
  INVERSION: nostalgia sold as depth is the failure; nostalgia used as a **one-bar intrusion into a
  present-tense world** is a formal event. The century of every variation is **now**, from second one.
- **THE SCENE DEBT seat** named the debt before discussing aesthetics: V3 draws an offbeat, negative-
  space and call/answer intelligence from living Caribbean scenes and therefore **requires a Lineage &
  Credit block at step 11**, naming the scene and the technique — with people credited in the
  dedication only, never in the prompt.
- **THE REPEAT LISTEN seat**, holding the *"inconsistency principle"* card, is satisfied by the
  unequal tag and the never-fed left-hand stab: on the fortieth play the lopsidedness is the reward.
- **⚠️ THE ANTI-MECHANISM seat** restates his one surviving objection as a standing order over step
  08: *the moment any lyric names its own operation, kill the song.* The deletion test at step 10 is
  his instrument and its result must be stated.
