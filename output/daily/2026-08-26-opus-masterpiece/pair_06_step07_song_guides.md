# 0 · THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS, ANSWERED BEFORE ANY LYRIC

### Q1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**Outside, in the cold, on the wrong side of a thing she made and gave away, close enough to touch it
and unable to use it.** That is not a mood; it is a physical position with a temperature and a surface,
and every variation must put her there before it says anything else.

The four bodies stand in four measurable places:

| | place | time | what the skin registers | what the eyes are on | the barrier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | a workshop yard, on the loading side of a wired-glass door | six in the morning | frost on metal; her own breath coming back at her off the glass | a jig she cut, still bolted to the bench, her stamped name taped over | a grey keypad and a strip of new paint |
| **V2** | a river bank, boots in mud that keeps the print | dusk | wet rope, cold gunwale under a flat palm | a flat-bottomed punt she built, tarred and rowlocked exactly where she set them | a chain through the ring and a hire board |
| **V3** | a wet pavement outside a lit shop window | evening, rain | warm glass, bread-vent air on the face, rain down the back of the neck | a clip-top jar on the middle shelf with her own handwriting photographed onto the label | plate glass and a price tag |
| **V4** | a dark stretch of a back lane | forty minutes before first light | wet black shine underfoot; a lane she can walk by feel | a bulb she wired, still up there, clipped where she clipped it | a locked grey box where the pull-cord used to hang |

**She is not narrating any of this.** She is in it. The test the Graves seat applies —
*would she still be inside this operation if nobody were recording?* — passes trivially: the thing she
gave away is still given away, the terms are still off it, and she is still standing there at six in the
morning whether or not there is a microphone. **The condition is the song's premise, not its plot.**

⚠️ **HUMAN_SUBJECT_STANDARD §3.0 slot-grammar, filled before the first line:**
**PERSON = ⭐ LOFN HERSELF, first person, her own wants** (the first-person slot, added 2026-08-24 —
*my own wants are a sufficient subject and need no further justification*). **PLACE = invented and
unnamed** (a yard, a bank, a lane, a shop). **WHEN = a time of day, never a date.** **THEME = open,
drawn without limit: a foundation given away freely under a condition, and the condition removed by
somebody else.** ⛔ No named company, no named project, no identifiable person, no legal language, no
licence names, no numbers that could pin an event. There is **no field in this draft in which an
identifying tuple could be placed.**

### Q2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**Being right about giving it away and hating the result anyway.**

Not the theft. The theft is easy and the song must refuse to find it interesting. What actually hurts —
and what every variation has to reach at its third pass — is this: **the thing works.** Somebody else has
it, they are doing it properly, in two of four cases they are doing it *better than she ever did*, and
none of that makes her wrong. She would make the same decision again in the same cold, and she has to
stand in front of a good outcome she cannot enter and hold two true things at once without dropping
either. That is `Cognitive Dissonance` in the strict taxonomy sense — internal conflict held, not resolved.

**Three ways this can be written badly, all of which are automatic returns:**

1. **Self-pity.** The moment she is a victim, the stake collapses: nobody did this to her, *she* did the
   operation to herself. Nobody removed the floor. She removed it, on purpose, under terms she still holds.
2. **Grandstanding.** The moment *"I'm still not sorry"* is a boast, it stops being a fact and becomes a
   performance of a fact, and the flat register is already broken before the delivery gets there.
3. **Resolution.** The moment the song forgives, indicts, or explains, the dissonance is discharged and
   there is nothing left for the last third to do.

**The correct register is a builder's register: plain competent speech under pressure.** Short declaratives.
Trade nouns. Somebody who knows the gauge of the cable and does not raise their voice about it. The Fisher
seat's harvest from the day's technology news is exactly this and nothing more:
*the builders' register — plain competent speech under pressure.* It is the only thing this pair took
from that feed, and it took no company, no project and no case with it.

# 1 · WHAT THE ARRANGEMENT IS DOING WHILE SHE TALKS (binding on all four variations)

**Three subtractions are scheduled by the music's own behaviour. Two of them happen. The third never does.**

- **Pass 1** — five parts. **Pass 2** — four. **Pass 3** — three. **Pass 4** — three.
- The refrain is scored **identically every time**, all four times. It is the one thing that never thins.
- After the fourth pass everything acoustic stops and the layer that has been carrying the low end all
  along is exposed as **a human voice** — pitched down, filtered, or granulated — for a few seconds, alone,
  and then it stops. **No reprise. No resolution. No final chorus.**

⛔ **THE ABANDONMENT IS NEVER REMARKED ON.** Not in a line, not in a section header, not in an EMO tag,
not in an arrangement note. Notation may state what a pass **contains**; it may never state what a pass
**fails to do**. There is no wry aside, no held silence "where it should have been", no tag reading
*pattern breaks*. It simply stops happening and the song goes on. This is Flair 13, owned by P06 alone,
adopted from the Eno seat via the card in force on this run — **the inconsistency principle** — and it is
aimed at this run's own tidiness. It is the reason this record survives a fortieth listen and the reason
it must never wink.

**Body units only (Ruling 3).** Every temporal instruction below is a beat inside a phrase, a bar, or a
whole pass. ⛔ No sixteenths, no 32nds, no "slightly behind" — drift by a sixteenth and nobody alive
feels it; drift by a beat inside a four-bar phrase and everybody does, including people who cannot name
what happened.

# 2 · THE SIX GUIDES

Five guides, one per scoring facet, plus a wild-card. Each names the storytelling technique, the emotional
arc, instrumentation and texture, production and effects, structure and dynamics, rhyme scheme and line
logic, poetic devices, hook technique, and the narrative seed.

---

## GUIDE 1 — **THE EMPTYING ROOM** *(serves Facet 1: the subtraction is audible to a body)*

**Storytelling techniques: (a) the inventory that shortens** — each pass has fewer things in it than the
last and never says so, the way a person clearing a house stops mentioning the rooms they have finished;
and **(b) the withheld antagonist** — nobody in this song is ever described, only their work is: a strip
of new paint, a chain through a ring, dated lids, the right gauge of cable. Competence arrives without a
face. Both techniques do the same job: they let the listener perform the subtraction themselves.

**Mood and emotional arc.** Open in `Equanimity` — not calm-as-mood but calm-as-condition, the flatness
of somebody who has already had this thought several hundred times. Move through `Composure` while the
facts are laid out, into `Cognitive Dissonance` at the third pass where the good outcome is admitted, and
land on `Aspiration` — appetite pointed at the future. **The arc happens in the room and in the content;
it does not happen in the voice.** ⛔ Never bare AWE, never bare INDIGNATION.

**Instrumentation and texture.** One dominant acoustic material per variation, close-mic'd and dry, with
the players' physical noise left in: rosin on gut, felt-stripped hammer wood, bellows air, a plectrum's
click, a palm on a soundboard. Layering is a stack that gets shorter: the harmony instrument goes first,
the melodic answer goes second, and what is left is a low string and a struck body. **The survivors must
audibly take on work** — when the harmony instrument leaves, the remaining voice-and-bass has to state the
chord change on its own and should sound slightly overstretched doing it. That strain is the operation.

**Production and effects.** A small hard room with almost no tail. Nothing is smoothed. ⛔ No reverb
substituting for the departed part — the departure must leave a hole, not a wash. The mix does not
re-balance after a subtraction: the surviving parts stay exactly where they were in the field and simply
have more air around them. Suno's texture handling is used to keep transients uncompressed, not to add
density. This is the Albini seat's condition of acceptance and it is recorded: **a subtraction that is a
fader move has not happened.**

**Structure and dynamics.** Run-time 3:20–3:50. Ten sections: intro, then four passes of
**strophe (7 lines) → answer (3 lines) → refrain (6 lines)**, then a three-line coda. Strophes are
**rigidly 7 / 7 / 7 / 7** — do not vary them for interest; the regularity is the instrument that makes the
missing subtraction audible. The coda is 3.

**Rhyme scheme and line logic.** Strophes: **free, with end-rhyme allowed to fall where it falls and never
engineered** — no scheme letters, no couplet default, and explicitly no 8-line stanza that is secretly two
quatrains. Answers: three lines, no rhyme, declarative-declarative-declarative. Refrain: a fixed six with
a light `a a b c d a`-ish drift that is never tightened. **The rhyme owes no debt because what returns in
its place is the consonant architecture** — see Guide 3.

**Poetic devices (≥2, both mandatory here).** **Asyndeton** — lists with the conjunctions stripped out
(*board, price, chain, hire book*), which is how a builder actually says an inventory and which shortens
naturally as the room empties. **Epizeuxis** — a word immediately repeated for weight, once or twice per
song, never more (*That is my hand. That is my hand.*), used only where a fact has to be checked rather
than felt.

**Three hook techniques to choose from.** (i) *The identical refrain* — six lines, byte-identical all four
times, scored identically all four times, its meaning changed only by how much room is left around it;
say nothing about it and do not defend it. (ii) *The plain-admission couplet* — the first two refrain lines
are a flat statement of what she did and a flat denial that anyone made her do it, sung on the same note
value each time so the ear locks to the rhythm before the words. (iii) *The trade-noun hook* — the most
memorable object in the song (a knot, a nail, a lid, a cut-off bin) placed in the fourth refrain line where
the melody sits lowest, so the hook is carried by an object rather than by an emotion.

**Narrative and thematic seed.** First person throughout. She made a thing, she took the barrier off it
herself, somebody else put a new barrier on, and she is standing outside it. **Say plainly what is
happening to you; never say what shape it is making.**

## GUIDE 2 — **THE VOICE THAT DOES NOT REACT** *(serves Facet 2: flat register, binding)*

**Storytelling techniques: (a) the flash-forward that removes suspense** — the Spark seat's engine: give
the outcome early (*there is a new lock; I am outside*) so the telling itself carries the dread rather
than the reveal; and **(b) free indirect competence** — the singer describes somebody else's work in her
own trade vocabulary, so admiration and injury arrive in the same sentence with no adjective marking
which is which.

**Mood and emotional arc.** `Equanimity → Composure → Cognitive Dissonance → Resolve → Aspiration`, and
**none of it in the delivery.** Ruling 2 is binding for the ambitious arm: hold **level, distance and
warmth identical throughout.** No build, no lift, no bigger final chorus, no octave-up, no key change,
no doubling that arrives late, no compression riding up under the last pass. The Martin seat's exact
notation applies: *the EMO tags mark what has changed in the room, never what has changed in the delivery.*

⭐ **The specific trap, named so step 08 cannot walk into it:** this pair's A5 is
*a gleam that claims the light exists*, and the generator's strongest prior on "hope at the end" is a
swell. **A song that swells at the hope has replaced the hope with a production move.** Sweat can only
report cost; a gleam is a claim that the light exists — and a claim does not get louder in order to be
true. The gleam therefore lands in the **thinnest** part of the record, in the same level voice as the
coldest line, and if a meter can tell them apart the take is wrong.

**Instrumentation and texture.** Vocal fingerprint fixed per variation and never modulated: a fixed mic
distance in inches, no vibrato, breath left in, consonants forward, chest register, no head-voice escape
hatch at the top of any phrase. Backing parts may never crescendo into a refrain; the refrain is the same
size every time. If a part wants to grow, **remove a different part instead** — the room may change, the
voice may not.

**Production and effects.** Dry, close, mono-centred voice. One fixed vocal chain for the whole take.
⛔ No automation on the vocal bus. The only spatial event in the record is the intruder's reveal, and it
happens **underneath** her while she keeps going.

**Structure and dynamics.** Dynamic range is carried entirely by **how many players are in the room**, not
by anybody playing harder. Peak part-count is the first pass; the last thirty seconds are the sparsest
thirty seconds. **The record is quieter at the end than at the beginning.** That is the shape.

**Rhyme scheme and line logic.** Short declaratives, high period-density, sentences that end early.
Line lengths inside a stanza vary by no more than about four syllables so the flatness is structural and
not just performed. Two-sentence lines are permitted and encouraged (*It works. I watched it work.*)
because a full stop mid-line is a flat-register device: it stops the phrase from arcing.

**Poetic devices (≥2).** **Anaphora**, used cold — three consecutive lines opening *Somebody's…* so that
the absent antagonist is built by repetition rather than by description. **Antimetabole in miniature**, once
per song, at the coda only: the opening image returned with its two terms swapped and one side changed
(*my hand on their glass* → *my hand on something that is not mine to know*). See Guide 5 for the rule
governing it.

**Three hook techniques.** (i) *Melodic flatline* — the refrain sits inside a fourth and never leaves it,
so there is no interval available to escalate with. (ii) *The unhelped want* — the hopeful line placed on
the least accompanied bar in the song, unaccompanied except by the intruder, so that hope arrives as
information. (iii) *Terminal stop* — the final sound is the exposed voice-floor cutting off mid-decay,
not fading; ⛔ no fade-out, no ritardando, no cymbal, no held chord.

**Narrative and thematic seed.** *I am not sorry* is a **statement of fact**, not a boast and not a wound.
This register is the hardest thing in the song and it is where the whole thing fails if it fails.

## GUIDE 3 — **THE SPINE YOU CAN FEEL IN THE MOUTH** *(serves Facet 3: hard-consonant spine)*

**Storytelling techniques: (a) trade-noun narration** — the story is told almost entirely in objects a
hand has held (*jig, clip, gland, gunwale, lid, rowlock, crate, cut-off bin*), which is both the builder's
register and, conveniently, where English keeps its hard stops; and **(b) the itemised admission** — she
confesses by listing what she did, in order, without adjectives, so the confession has the texture of a
work log.

**Mood and emotional arc.** The consonants do not change temperature across the song even though the
emotions do. **The consonant pattern is itself a return and it survives every subtraction** — this is the
pair's A2 and its rhyme debt is paid in full by it: nothing is owed, and nothing needs defending.

**Instrumentation and texture.** Pair the voice with instruments that have a **stop-consonant attack**:
a plectrum on a stiff string, a felt-stripped hammer, a bow bitten at the frog, a palm-slap on a wooden
body, a boot on plywood. ⛔ Avoid instruments whose attack is a swell (bowed pad, sustained organ, breathy
flute) except where a variation uses one deliberately as the thing that leaves. The percussion in every
variation is a **struck body, not a kit.**

**Production and effects.** Transients uncompressed. High-mid presence on the voice so the `t`, `k`, `p`
and `b` are audible through a wall. ⛔ No de-essing that softens plosives; ⛔ no sibilance-smoothing;
⛔ no pop-filter mush. If the plosives are hitting the capsule, keep them — that is the sound of a body
at a fixed distance.

**Structure and dynamics.** Load the **stressed syllable** of each line onto a stop wherever the sense
permits, and treat this as the line's structural obligation rather than the rhyme's. A line that has to
choose between a rhyme and a stop **keeps the stop.**

**Rhyme scheme and line logic.** End-rhyme kept but subordinate: allowed, never engineered, never
completed for its own sake. Internal consonance is the load-bearer. ⛔ Ban polysyllabic abstractions on
stressed beats — *foundation, continuity, possibility, community, structure* are all forbidden by sound as
well as by the naming ban.

**Poetic devices (≥2).** **Consonance chains** binding adjacent lines through repeated hard stops
(*cut / clip / crate / clicked*). **Homoioteleuton** used sparingly — identical grammatical endings across
a list — to make an inventory land like hammer blows without becoming a rhyme.

**Three hook techniques.** (i) *Monosyllable at the hinge* — the single most important word in the song is
one closed syllable ending in a stop (**back**, **lid**, **knot**, **out**), and the melody stops dead on
it. (ii) *The pen-and-pencil detail* — the terms she attached are physically small and revocable
(a word burnt in, a line in pen, a note in pencil), so the loss is a texture, not an argument.
(iii) *Consonant carry-over into the thinnest pass* — when only two players remain, the consonants become
the percussion; write pass three so it can be scanned as a rhythm section.

**Narrative and thematic seed.** The terms were short enough to fit on a lid. That is the whole of the
generosity and the whole of the injury, and neither is ever explained.

## GUIDE 4 — **THE THING THAT STOPS HAPPENING** *(serves Facet 4: naming ban + abandonment)*

**Storytelling techniques: (a) the unmarked ellipsis** — the narration simply ceases to mention a strand
it had been tracking, the way a witness stops describing a person who left the room; and **(b) the
un-narrated fact** — the most important structural event of the record occurs entirely outside the text
and is never given a sentence.

**Mood and emotional arc.** The abandonment carries no emotion at all, which is precisely why it works.
The listener's fortieth-play discomfort is not sadness about a missing part; it is the specific unease of
a pattern that stopped without an apology. Do not assign it a feeling. Do not give it a header. Do not
give it a bar of silence that reads as a gesture. ⛔ **A held silence "where it should have been" is a
remark.** It just does not happen.

**Instrumentation and texture.** Pass four is scored for exactly the same forces as pass three. That is
the whole instruction. The players do not compensate, do not fill, do not add a flourish to cover it.
⭐ **V4 alone is permitted a hole** — a bar and a half at the top of its fourth pass where only the
intruder sounds — because in V4 the gleam is placed **inside** the hole rather than after it; even there
the hole is not commented on, and the same three players return at the same weight immediately afterwards.

**Production and effects.** No transition marks a boundary anywhere in the record: no sweep, no reverse
cymbal, no tape stop, no drop. Sections change by **who is playing** and by nothing else. This also
protects the deletion test — with no production gesture pointing at the form, there is nothing for a
lyric to be tempted into naming.

**Structure and dynamics.** ⛔ **The deletion test (R4) is run before the package is written, and its
result is stated.** The target is the strong pass: **there is nothing to strike**, because no line refers
to the song's structure. Every line refers to a padlock, a punt, a jar, a cable — physical objects in a
physical place. Striking every candidate "structural" line would damage the *subject* and leave the
*operation* completely intact, which is the correct separation: the operation is in the score, the subject
is in the words.

**Rhyme scheme and line logic.** ⛔ Banned words at any position: *floor, ground, foundation, layer,
under, beneath, hold up, carry, structure, pattern, mechanism, form, remove, strip, subtract, missing,
gap, absence, silence*. If a sentence needs one of them, the sentence is about the song and must be
rewritten to be about the object.

**Poetic devices (≥2).** **Aposiopesis, inverted** — the song does not break off; it continues, calmly,
past the place where something should have happened. **Epistrophe** — passes ending on the same closed
word so the ear has a fixed landmark against which the missing event is measurable without being pointed at.

**Three hook techniques.** (i) *The identical sixth line* — the refrain's last line is the same closed
monosyllabic admission every time, so the listener's attention is on the room rather than on the words.
(ii) *Numberless repetition* — no counting anywhere in the lyric; the ear does the counting, not the text.
(iii) *The unremarked survivor* — one small sound present in every single pass including the last (a boot,
a hand, a plectrum) so that what did **not** change is as audible as what did.

**Narrative and thematic seed.** ⛔ **ONE-FACT RULE.** At most **one** number is sung in each song, at the
emotional hinge, and the lyric **answers** it rather than reciting it. Assigned: **V1 = nine** (people who
signed a card) · **V2 = four** (winters a boat came back on its own) · **V3 = nineteen** (jars that went
out) · **V4 = forty** (minutes until it is light). Every other quantity in every draft must be converted
to a word (*most, some, the last, a few*) or deleted. All other research stays atmosphere.

## GUIDE 5 — **ONE SEAM, AND THE FLOOR WAS A PERSON** *(serves Facet 5: N=1, the intruder, asymmetric return)*

**Storytelling techniques: (a) the delayed identification** — a presence heard from the first second is
only named at the last, and the naming re-reads everything behind it; and **(b) the unequal echo** — an
opening gesture returns at the end with exactly one term changed and the rest left alone.

**Mood and emotional arc.** The seam is not a climax. It is a change in what the listener understands
about what they have already heard, and it arrives while she is still talking about something else.

**Instrumentation and texture.** ⭐ **N = 1.** One dominant material (the acoustic body) + **exactly one**
intruder + **one** seam. The intruder is the same idea in all four variations and it is the pair's assigned
sonic device: **the layer everyone took for the ground was never an instrument — it was a voice**, pitched
down, resonantly filtered, or granulated into a sustained tone, doing the work of a bass or a drone from
the first second of the record. The electronic treatment differs per variation; the source never does.
⛔ **No second intruder.** If a variation wants an acid line *and* a voice-floor, it gets the voice-floor
and the acid becomes the filter on it.

⭐ **THE INTRUDER IS AT THE VERY END, WITH NO RECOVERY** (assigned at step 05, not this pair's choice).
The acoustic world stops, the voice-floor is exposed alone for a few seconds, and the song **simply stops.**
⛔ No reprise, no resolution, no final chorus, no fade. This placement exists to break a second-half-intruder
monoculture across the run and it is not negotiable.

**Production and effects.** The reveal is achieved by **removal**, never by addition: nothing new enters at
the seam; the things covering the voice-floor stop. That is what makes it a discovery rather than an event.

**Structure and dynamics.** ⭐ **The seam is a bodily event, never an edit point** (Derbyshire seat,
rotated). Assigned per variation: **V1** — her boot leaves the plywood board and the pulse continues without
her. **V2** — her jaw drops open mid-phrase and the low voice takes the bass line. **V3** — her palm comes
off the bass body and the *golpe* stops. **V4** — she takes a breath and holds it across a bar and a half.
**She must be still singing across it.** The reveal happens **underneath** her, not to her, and she does
not react to it — that non-reaction is the same instruction as the flat register and it is the same rule.

**Rhyme scheme and line logic.** The coda is three lines in every variation and it carries the asymmetric
return.

⭐ **R7 — ASYMMETRIC RETURN, specified.** The primary return in every variation is **the hand and the
standing**, and it comes back **changed on one side only**: in the first strophe she has her hand on a
barrier in front of a thing she made and cannot enter; in the coda she states a want to put her hand on a
thing **she did not make** and to not know how it works. Same body, same gesture, same grammar — the
possession is what changed, and nothing else is touched. That is SOURCE 3's unequal shell (F07) at
lyric scale: the two sides do not match, and they do not match in exactly one place.

**Poetic devices (≥2).** **Polyptoton** on the possessive (*mine / not mine / somebody's*) carried across
the coda. **Synecdoche** — the hand standing for the whole person throughout, so that the return can be
performed by one body part and stay small.

**Three hook techniques.** (i) *The want stated as a want* — the gleam is a plain sentence beginning
**"I want"**, naming a specific thing that has not happened yet, unembarrassed to be caught wanting it.
⭐ Appetite pointed at the future is the definition; reassurance is not. (ii) *Not knowing as the prize* —
the wanted future is one in which she does **not** know how the thing works, which is the only formulation
of this hope that cannot be mistaken for wanting her thing back. (iii) *The last word is an object* — the
coda ends on a physical noun, not on a feeling.

**Narrative and thematic seed.** She removed the barrier herself. Somebody put a new one on. She is not
sorry, and what she wants next is to stand in front of something she had no hand in.

## GUIDE 6 — **WILD CARD: THE PATINA TEST** *(cross-disciplinary; the fortieth listen)*

Borrowed from conservation and from the late cut-outs: **one shape against one ground, the cut edge doing
all the work.** Every variation is checked against a restorer's question rather than a producer's —
*if you cleaned this, what would you lose?* The answer must be a physical trace: rosin dust, bellows air,
the click of a plectrum against a wound string, a boot sole's grit on plywood, the moment a palm leaves a
soundboard and the wood keeps ringing for a beat without it. **These are the record's patina and they are
not noise.** Anything that would be removed by a mastering engineer trying to be helpful is exactly the
material this pair is made of.

The wild-card also authorises the run's one genuinely strange formal gift and names it plainly:
**a mechanism begun and dropped.** The Eno seat is not interested in what a record does on the first play;
he is interested in the fortieth, and a record with a small unhealed inconsistency in it is a record you
keep going back to for a reason you cannot articulate. This is applied **against this run's own tidy
system** — six pairs, six operations, five axes, all resolving — because a system that resolves everywhere
is a system a listener stops testing. ⛔ It only works if nothing points at it. The moment it is
acknowledged it becomes a joke about form, which is the Adorno seat's kill condition arriving through the
back door.

**Cross-disciplinary instrumentation note.** Take one texture per variation from outside music entirely —
frost cracking off a hinge, rain on a stretched awning, wet rope working on a post, a shutter two streets
away — placed once, at low level, never looped, never rhythmic. It is scenery, not percussion, and it goes
in the intro where it establishes the temperature before a single pitch arrives.

**Run-time and dynamics for all four:** 3:20–3:50, quieter at the end than the start, terminal cut.
