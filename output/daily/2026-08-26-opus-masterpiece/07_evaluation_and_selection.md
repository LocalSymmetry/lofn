## THE INSTRUMENT

Six dimensions, 0–10. **Dimension 1 is weighted ×2.** Maximum 70.

1. **THE OPERATION IS AUDIBLE (R1, ×2)** — would a listener *catch* the formal move on one pass, and is it a **literal condition of the singer** rather than a metaphor for one?
2. **THE NAMING BAN HELD (R4)** — deletion test run by hand on every lyric.
3. **THE STAKE IS REAL (Law 2)** — inside it, wanting something, with something to lose; still inside it with the tape off.
4. **FIRST-LISTEN LEGIBILITY** — scene AND subject retold in one sentence; subject plain at least once.
5. **WOULD SURVIVE THE GENERATOR** — read as a producer. Does the spec make the intended behaviour **physically necessary**, or only forbid the alternative?
6. **IT IS ALIVE** — would I play it twice.

⚠️ **A note on how I used dimension 5.** I penalise unrenderability hard when **the operation itself** is the thing that will not survive (P02 V4), and only lightly when the risk is to the **room** while the operation survives regardless (P06 V2). Those are different failures and I have not treated them as one.

## 2 · PER PAIR — WHAT SEPARATES THE WINNER

### P01 → **V4 "The One in the Window"**

**The separator is verse two.** Every P01 variation runs the same grammar — reply first, disclosure last — and three of the four spend that grammar on inventory. V4 spends it on an argument: eight consecutive couplets opening on *Because*, arguing her entitlement to want the thing **backwards from the wanting**, delivered by a singer who takes her breath mid-line because she will not stop at a line break. That is the only place in the pair where the operation stops being a syntax and becomes a person who cannot be interrupted. It is also the only P01 stanza whose disclosure costs her something real: *"he was asking, kindly, whether I could afford it."* The other three disclose a quiz round, a request to stay, and a request to carry a monitor.

**Runner-up: V1 "I Want the Loud Table."** It does two things better. Its room is communal where V4's is transactional — a table, a barman, Marnie laughing into her sleeve — and warmth in a crowd is harder to write than warmth alone. And its outro question is the best single line in the pair: *"who in this room wants it badly enough to look stupid?"* — a question that indicts the listener rather than the singer, arriving after the answer has been sung thirty-two times.

### P02 → **V3 "Let Me Land"**

**The separator is that V3 puts its operation in the words.** All four relocate a clapped figure by a whole beat; only V3 also encodes the direction in text the generator is obliged to sing — *"Now — now — now"*, *"that's it, that's it, that's it"*, *"Let me land, let me land, let me land"*, *"Here comes the next of me. Tell her to hurry."* The pair's own declared point of failure is a generator quantising the displacement onto a grid. In V1, V2 and V4, that failure empties the song. In V3 the acceleration survives in the lyric even if the clock is straightened. It is also, plainly, the most propulsive thing in the run and the strongest single answer to *"there is no ceiling on the fun."*

**Runner-up: V4 "I Go With My Hands."** It has the better idea by some distance — voice and hands keeping two different counts for two passes, then the voice defecting to the hands — and it is the only P02 variation where the seam is a **decision** made by a body rather than an event that happens to one. Its handling of the one-fact rule is the sharpest in the pair (*"I'll spit out fourteen seconds"* — the number arriving through a mouth full of sea). It loses on one thing only, and it is fatal: a Suno prompt asking for two independent timing layers, sustained across two passes, is asking for the one thing the generator will certainly not do. It also carries a live rule violation (see §5).

### P03 → **V1 "You Can Have It When"**

**The separator is where the asymmetric return lands.** V1 and V4 tie at 60 and are, structurally, the same chorus with different scenery — four bare refusal lines, then a loaded list of four conditions, then *"Go on. Ask."* The tiebreak is R7. The chorus is this pair's most-returned object, sung three or four times per song; in V1 it comes back **changed on the furnished side only** — a response vocable accreting nothing → one → two while the bare half never once gains anything. In V4 the chorus returns byte-identical, so R7 is discharged only by the verses. Nothing in P03's A2 mandates an invariant chorus (that is P01's axis, not this one), so V4's invariance is an unforced surrender of the pair's own rule on the pair's own flagship object. V1 also carries the pair's best line — *"They call it whole. It's two percent short. / I can hear the two. I'm not going to un-hear it."*

**Runner-up: V4 "Here's What It Costs."** Two things better. Its refusal is the most compressed in the run — *"No. That's it. / That's the whole of it. No."* — three seconds of song against a five-line price, which is the widest bare/loaded ratio anywhere in the pair and the most catchable version of ONE SIDE ONLY. And it is the only P03 variation whose conditions include a want pointed at the future rather than at the deal: *"I want to still want it in a year."* That is the run's stated missing ingredient, unforced, inside the fun arm.

*Rejected, and worth saying why:* **V3 "Now Ask Me Again"** is the most alive song in the pair — 150 BPM, a chopped break, *"I'm not going to say it twice — actually I am. I'll say it all day."* It loses because its distinguishing move is **purely spatial**: the whole ornament load crossing to the opposite channel in one bar, on the word *now*. A hard channel crossing on a named word in a named bar is the least survivable instruction in the accessible arm, and unlike V1's accreting vocable there is no textual copy of it. The best idea in the pair is stored in the one place a generator does not read carefully.

### P04 → **V1 "What They Called It"**

**The separator is that nothing else moves.** Twenty-four lines of verse, chorus and tag, returned three times, byte-identical except one word: *hers → yours → mine*. There is no intro, no bridge, no outro, no build, no new instrument after bar one, no key change, and a vocal double locked at a fixed short delay so the substituted word is heard twice on the pass it changes. A listener cannot miss the operation because the song has removed everything a listener could attend to instead. Its siblings all add something the ear can chase — an inverted ladder, a withheld substitution, a talking machine — and every addition is a place the pronoun can hide.

**Runner-up: V2 "The Record Says Me."** It does one thing better and it is not small: it runs the ladder **outward** — *me → you → her* — so the clean fear lands on erasure rather than on arrival. Being written out of your own account is the less obvious and more frightening destination, and the pair ranked this variation fourth of four. I disagree with that ranking. V1 still wins because the descent to first person is the version a listener resolves in real time; V2's requires them to notice they have been demoted to a third party, which is a beat slower and one beat is what this song has.

*Named for the record:* **V3 "The Name Is Correct"** scored 46, the lowest in the run, and could not have scored otherwise. See dissent §4.2.

### P05 → **V3 "The Jar Comes Back Too Close"**

**The separator is that V3 gives the listener something to catch instead of something to miss.** NOTHING RECOMBINES is an operation of absence, and absence is the single thing a first listen is worst at detecting — V1, V2 and V4 all ask the ear to notice that things stopped coming back. V3 converts it into a **positive event**: one element returns from a depth it never had, at eight inches from the ear, closer than anything in the record including the singer, and stays wrong. And it duplicates that event in the lyric — Inventory Three should hold the two survivors of Inventory Two; it holds one survivor and **the jar, which had already gone**, with one line changed on one side (*"I go easy"* → *"and none sweet"*). Neither fact is ever mentioned. If the generator flattens depth entirely, the operation still lands, because it is also a word. That is the strongest survivability construction anywhere in the run.

It also earns the pair's A5 exactly: *"It is right there and it is not. / I liked that lid."* and then, at the close, *"I am not moving. It can stay."* — the only P05 variation where she makes a decision rather than sustains a condition.

**Runner-up: V4 "Enough To Read By"** (tied at 62). Two things better: the ending, which is the most graceful in the pair — *"Lamplit: how much I still want things. / Lamplit: the wanting, and I am keeping it."* — and the safest spec in the ambitious arm, one sustained mechanical pulse that never leaves and never speeds up. It yields for two reasons: its operation is legible only as thinning, and its temperature (*appetite kept*) sits closer to **P06's** assigned gleam than to P05's own *tenderness with no apology*, which would have duplicated a colour the portfolio already spends.

### P06 → **V2 "A Word on the Back Board"**

**The separator is that the floor turns out to be a person, not a texture.** The pair's own §4 names the sonic device — *"the removed floor is revealed to have been carried by a VOICE the whole time"* — and its §6 requires her to be still singing across the reveal. All four deliver a voice; three deliver it **processed**: a pitched-down hum (V1), a voice-bass through a resonant sweep (V3), a granulated held vowel (V4). A listener hears each of those as a synthesiser that happens to have a human origin. V2 alone delivers an unprocessed contralto taking the bass line while her jaw drops open mid-phrase and she does not stop singing. The difference between *the floor was a machine I never noticed* and *the floor was a person* is the whole assignment, and only one of the four is on the right side of it. Its subtractions are also the most salient possible — you cannot fail to notice a voice leaving — where the others subtract a fiddle, a violin, a viol.

And it holds the best writing in the run: *"I don't need it to be mine. / I need it to be there. / That is not the same thing and I know it."* That is the AGPL anchor's entire moral content, said plainly by a person about a boat, with no legal language, no company, and no self-pity.

**Runner-up: V4 "I Tied a Knot in It"** (tied at 64). Two things better. It carries the run's hardest single assignment — *the gleam placed inside the hole rather than after it* — and discharges it: a bar and a half at the top of pass four with only the sustained tone in it, an inhale held across it, and *"I want to walk under a light I did not put up"* sung on the tail of that breath, with the same three players returning at the same weight immediately after and no transition on either edge. And it carries the best notation sentence in the package: *"The hope line and the coldest line are sung at the same level, from the same distance, with the same warmth. If a meter can tell them apart, the take is wrong."* It also has the run's best ending: *"I want to stand under it before the light / with my hands in my pockets, doing nothing."* It yields on dimension 1 only: its reveal — a tone that has been sounding since the first second and nobody heard — is a second-listen discovery, and the seed requires a first-listen catch.

## 3 · THE STRONGEST SONG IN THE RUN

### **P04 V1 — "What They Called It"** (66/70)

It is the only song here in which the formal operation is the entire content, and it is the only one where I would bet money that a stranger catches the move without being told.

Three passes of one block — twelve-line verse, eight-line chorus, four-line tag — returned **byte-identical**, with a single token different each pass: *The heading is **hers** / **yours** / **mine** from the moment it's signed.* No intro. No bridge. No outro. Nothing enters after bar one; the instrument list at the last bar is the list at bar one. The delivery is held at four inches, dead centre, bone dry, with a level-matched short-delay double that never widens, so on the pass the word changes it is heard twice and the second time it is already a record of itself.

**Why it clears the seed's own one-sentence brief where the other twenty-three only approach it.** *Something is being done to me that I would still be inside if nobody were recording; you can feel it in your body before you can name it; I never tell you what it is.* A classification is being applied to her in a room she cannot leave; it is accurate, everyone is polite, and that is what makes it unbearable. The deletion test has nothing to strike — not one line refers to the substitution, the repetition, the passes, or the form. And the one-fact rule gets its best discharge in the run: *"The box marked cause has a zero in it, / and zero means there is no one to blame."* A depth-zero seismic reclassification becomes a tick-box, answered rather than recited, and the answer **is** the horror.

It is also, by some way, the most renderable ambitious spec in the package. Its risk class is a generator supplying an unrequested build at the third chorus — and the pair guards it with four independent controls: an affirmative statement of invariance in the prompt (not merely a prohibition), an exclude field whose first forty terms are build grammar, `no_swell_no_new_layer` in the Disc_Pad channel, and a Role string asserted byte-identical across all nine headers. That is the difference between *forbidding the alternative* and *making the intended behaviour physically necessary*, and this is the only pair in the run that built both.

**Near miss:** P06 V2 at 64. It has the better lines. It does not have the better instrument.

## 4 · THE WEAKEST OF THE SIX SELECTED

### **P01 V4 — "The One in the Window"** (59/70)

It is a fine, funny, warm song and it wins its pair on merit. It is also the only one of the six that does not satisfy the seed's own opening clause, and the failure is structural rather than executional.

**The problem: nothing is being done to her.** The seed says *"Something is being done to me that I would still be inside if nobody were recording."* P01's assigned operation — THE ANSWER ARRIVES FIRST — is a temperament the singer **performs**, not a condition she is **inside**. She is the agent of her own operation in all four variations. Every other selected song has an operation applied from outside: a count that relands without her permission, an arrangement whose halves are unequal, a classification, a room that empties, a floor removed. P01 has a woman who answers early. That is a character trait, and a character trait is closer to a metaphor for a condition than to one.

**The second problem is a consequence of the first.** Because the operation is syntactic, its audibility depends entirely on a listener noticing that the *last* line of each stanza is the only line that is not a reply — inside dense AA couplets at 118 BPM. Nothing in the arrangement or the notation marks that line as different from the seven above it.

**What would have to change.** One production instruction fixes most of it: **strip the accompaniment under the final couplet of every stanza**, so the disclosure arrives in a hole. The reveal then stops being something the listener must infer from syntax and becomes something the room does to them — which is the difference between an operation and a rhyme scheme, and it costs the song nothing it currently has. The deeper fix is not the pair's to make: the axis needs re-pointing so the answering is something happening **to** her — being *already answered for*, having her yes assumed — which is the direction V2 alone gestures at and which the arm's fixed A1 forbids.

## 5 · RULE VIOLATIONS FOUND

### ⛔ VIOLATION 1 — **R3, THE BODY TEST. P03, all four variations.**

R3: *"Temporal operations specified in **body units** (a beat in a four-bar phrase), never notation units."* Enforcement: *"a sixteenth-note drift is `REPAIR`."* P03 specifies every kinetic defect and two of four vocal placements at a magnitude **below** a sixteenth:

- `pair_03_step10_final_package_enhanced.md`, **V1 music prompt** — *"phrasing **a hair** ahead of the kick"* … *"Kinetic defect: the clap arrives **a hair** late every fourth bar and is never fixed."*
- **V2 music prompt** — *"sitting **a hair** behind the beat"* … *"Kinetic defect: the stomp drags **a hair** on beat three and stays dragged."*
- **V4 music prompt** — *"Kinetic defect: the snap sits **a hair** ahead every second bar and is never pulled back."*
- **V3 music prompt** — *"the break's snare pushes early every second bar"* — no magnitude at all.
- Also §5 vocal-fingerprint table: *"a hair **ahead** of the kick"* / *"a hair **behind** the beat."*

*Drift by a sixteenth and nobody alive will feel it.* "A hair" is vaguer than a sixteenth. **This is not a hindsight finding:** P01 identified the identical phrase inside this run and repaired it, recording the reason in its own Major Deviation 5 — *"it previously read 'a hair ahead of the beat', which is vague and is not a body unit."* Two pairs met the same instruction; one obeyed it. The defect does not damage P03's operation (which is spatial and syntactic, not temporal), and it does not change my selection, but it is a live `REPAIR` on the selected V1 and a two-word fix: *a hair late* → **a whole beat late**.

### ⛔ VIOLATION 2 — **NO REAL COMPANY NAMES IN THE THREE SUNO FIELDS. P02 V2 and V4.**

The handoff: *"⛔ **NO REAL ARTIST, GROUP, PANEL-SOURCE OR COMPANY NAMES IN ANY OF THE THREE FIELDS.**"* The lyrics field is one of the three, and the `Disc_Channel` block is inside it.

- `pair_02_step10_final_package_enhanced.md`, **V2 lyrics field** — `[Disc_Sub: Minimoog_Bass | felt_in_chest_not_ears | no_level_movement | Mono_Sub_Lock]`
- **V4 lyrics field** — `[Disc_Sub: Moog_Taurus_Bass_Pedals | felt_in_chest_not_ears | no_level_movement | Mono_Sub_Lock]`

*Minimoog* and *Taurus Bass Pedals* are Moog Music Inc. product names. P02's own verification table asserts **"real names in the three fields | none | none | none | none."** This is precisely the class the coordinator logged as FINDING 8 (*"a supplied palette is not a cleared palette… a proper-noun heuristic clears both; only a roster scan catches it"*) — except that here the roster scan misses it too, because Moog is not on this run's credited roster. **The roster scan is the wrong instrument for brand tokens; it needs a manufacturer list, not a contributor list.**

Note that P05 hit the same class, recognised it, and declared a carve-out on the record — *"Instrument backdoor tokens (`Rhodes`, `parlour organ`) are sanctioned by `vault/DISC_CHANNEL_GUIDE.md` and name an instrument, never a maker of records."* P02 neither declared nor cleared. Neither affected variation is selected; both are one-token repairs (`fat_analog_mono_sub`, `low_synth_bass_pedals`).

## 6 · ⚠️ DISSENT — WHERE THE BRIEF CONSTRAINED THE WORK BADLY

*The pairs executed well. Most of what went wrong went wrong before they opened their briefs.*

### 4.1 — Four of six pairs had to break one of your rules in order to obey another, and you made them file it as a deviation

`COORDINATOR_FINDINGS.md` §1 logs this for P01 and calls the blame correctly. It is not a P01 problem. **It happened five times, in four pairs:**

| pair | collision | resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **P01** | A2 mandates a **byte-identical** chorus; R7 requires the returning object to come back **changed on one side**. The chorus structurally cannot do both. | Invented a four-line tag to carry R7, declared as Major Deviation 1. |
| **P05** | The brief's vowel ladder *"climbs across each stanza, **one step per line**, resetting at each inventory."* Inventory One is 24 lines. There is no 24-rung usable vowel ladder. **The instruction was arithmetically impossible as written.** | Rebuilt as an eight-rung ladder run three times, declared as Major Deviation 1. |
| **P05** | V2's assigned angle — *"the voice leaves too"* — is a head-on collision with R2, the binding flat register (*"level, distance and warmth identical end to end"*). | Sent the voice as **material** and held the delivery, declared as Major Deviation 2. |
| **P06** | V3's assigned angle — *"a layer comes back partway through"* — competes with the pair's own owned flair, which requires the abandoned mechanism to be the **only** count irregularity. | Demoted the return to a texture, declared as Major Deviation 3 — and it cost V3 its distinguishing move. |
| **P04** | The body-noise contract locates noise in **Intro / Bridge / Outro**; step 05 assigned nine sections and *"nothing else exists in the song."* | Placed noise inside the pass, declared as a deviation. |

Every one of these was resolved correctly and disclosed honestly. That is the problem: **the pairs did the coordinator's adjudication work five times, under isolation, without being told which constraint yields.** A brief with six prohibitions and zero permissions makes every collision indistinguishable from a violation, and it makes the pair that resolves it correctly look like the pair that broke a rule. One paragraph — *when A2 and R7 collide, R7 yields; when a variation angle collides with a Ruling, the Ruling wins* — would have cost nothing and prevented all five.

### 4.2 — Three of the twenty-four variation angles were specified to lose

**P04 V3, "THE PRONOUN THAT NEVER ARRIVES," is the sharpest case, and the brief refutes itself in writing.** The angle: two passes descend, the third *"withholds the substitution entirely and repeats pass two verbatim."* On the same page, the CUT LEDGER kills THE UNTURNED HEAD with this reason:

> *"the operation is a **non-event**; nothing audible happens, so it fails the body test (Ruling 3)"*

The brief cut a whole concept for being a non-event, and then assigned a non-event as a variation angle inside a pair. It scored **46 — the lowest in the run — and no execution could have raised it**, because the song's third pass is, by mandate, indistinguishable from its second. A quarter of P04's budget was spent producing a song the brief had already disqualified in principle four sections earlier.

**P03 V2, "THE WITHERING HALF,"** is a temperament collision. Withering is incompatible with the pair's fixed A5, *defiance that is having a good time*. The variation obeyed the angle and landed on tired warmth — *"I'm not angry. I'm just tired."* It is the only P03 variation off its own axis, and the axis and the angle were fixed by the same document, one page apart.

**P03 V4, "THE TERMS IN THE CHANGED HALF," is not an angle at all.** It is verbatim the pair's own §3 hook grammar: *"The refusal lives in the bare half; the conditions live in the blooming half."* One of four slots was spent restating the default. It happened to produce a 60-point song. That is luck, not design.

> **The transferable rule: a variation angle is a hypothesis about how the operation can be varied. Three of these were not hypotheses — one was a null, one was an axis contradiction, one was a restatement.** Angles should be checked against the pair's own axis values and against the cut ledger before dispatch, by the same distinctness pass that already checks the six pair columns.

### 4.3 — You cut the moon, then handed it to everybody

### 4.4 — Pair isolation guarantees portfolio collisions and nothing between step 05 and selection reconciles them

### 4.5 — "Four songs, not four mixes" is not true, and the run's cardinality is six, not twenty-four

P06's §3 is headed **"THE FOUR VARIATIONS ARE FOUR SONGS, NOT FOUR MIXES"** and evidences it with scene, chassis, key, tempo, voice, number, seam and intruder. **This is not a criticism of the pairs.** Nothing in the contract asked them to diverge internally; `PAIR ISOLATION` binds *across* pairs and is silent *within* them, and the four angles they were handed are parameter flips on a single axis. If eighteen alternates is the goal, that is a defensible design and should be stated, because it changes what selection is for.
