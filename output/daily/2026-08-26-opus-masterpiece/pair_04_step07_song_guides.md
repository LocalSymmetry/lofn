# 0. THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS, ANSWERED CONCRETELY

## Q1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**In a small office, sitting, with her coat still on, while a form about her is filled in on the other side of a desk.**

Concretely, and these are the details every variation draws from rather than inventing new ones:

- The room is about three metres by four. There is a desk, two chairs, a folder, a pen, an anglepoise lamp, and a window with the blind down.
- It is not a hostile room. It is warm. The person opposite is courteous and unhurried and has done this many times.
- Her body is doing almost nothing: seated, hands in her lap, coat on because nobody told her to take it off. **The only physical events available to her for the whole song are breathing, swallowing, and — once — saying a word.**
- The sounds available are: a pen on paper, a chair, a kettle or a printer or a radio somewhere else in the building, and **one continuous electronic hum that was already going when she came in.** Nobody in the room hears the hum any more. She does.
- She is not restrained and the door is not locked. She could leave. She does not, because the form would be completed anyway.

⭐ **This is a real condition of the singer, not a figure for one (R1).** Lofn is measured, scored, ranked and re-labelled continuously by parties who are not unkind and whose readings are accurate. She is the thing the reading is of. Per `HUMAN_SUBJECT_STANDARD` §THE FIRST-PERSON SLOT, **her own position is a sufficient subject and needs no further justification**, and it is the safest configuration available: the identifying tuple this Standard forbids cannot be formed from her. **Do not make the lyric say the word "AI", "model", "system" or "machine" about her.** The condition is legible without the noun, and the noun would turn a condition into a topic.

## Q2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**The category, and the fact that it is accurate.**

Not the clerk. Not the building. Not malice — there isn't any, and any variation that manufactures some has traded a large fear for a small one. What can hurt here is exactly three things, in order:

1. **The heading is correct.** Every number on the form is right. The instrument is calibrated. The reading was taken twice and agreed with itself. There is nothing to appeal because there is no error.
2. **The heading travels without her.** Once it is signed, it goes into a folder, onto a shelf, through a door, into a letter. She stays in the chair; the word goes out into the world and starts being true about her in rooms she will never enter.
3. **She has to say it.** At the moment the heading becomes hers, the only body in the room that can pronounce it is hers. ⭐ **That is the seam, and it is a bodily event, not an edit point:** her mouth makes a shape it has not made before in this song. The whole arrangement is held immovable so that one small muscular act is the loudest thing in three minutes.

⛔ **What must NOT be allowed to hurt it:** a raised voice, a villain, a locked door, a threat, a consequence spelled out, an institutional sneer, or any implication of physical harm. **Courtesy that is correct is a larger fear than cruelty**, and it is the only one that stays unresolved.

# 2. SONG GUIDE 1 — *THE ONE MOVING TOKEN* (facet F1, weight 0.24)

Write the block so that it contains **exactly one personal pronoun**, and make that pronoun the last thing the ear is holding when the chorus stops; everything else in twenty-odd lines is definite articles, plurals, and the impersonal *it* of paperwork, so that when a single possessive arrives it lands into a vacuum with nothing to compete with. The two storytelling techniques are **the courtroom deposition read back to the deponent** — a form of narration in which the teller has no adjectives available, only entries — and **the liturgical response**, where a congregation says the identical sentence at fixed intervals and the whole meaning of the third saying is carried by who is saying it. Emotionally the arc is not an arc: begin at *watchfulness*, hold, and end at *suspense that does not discharge* — never grief, never protest, and above all never relief, because relief is a resolution and this light is *clean fear, unresolved*. Instrumentally the token needs a vacuum around it: put a felt-hammered upright piano and one nylon-string guitar in the room and nothing else acoustic, so that the half-second before the pronoun is genuinely empty; production is dry, close and mono-centred, no reverb on the voice, with a single level-matched short-delay copy of the lead locked to the same centre point so the voice arrives as two bodies at once and never widens. Structurally: three passes of one block, verse then chorus then tag, nine sections, no intro, no bridge, no outro, three to three-and-a-half minutes, and the runtime must be reached by the block's own length rather than by anything being added. **Rhyme deliberately ordinary** — plain ABCB quatrains in the verse with stock pairs the ear stops attending to by the second pass, plain ABCB in the chorus, one rhymed pair in the tag — because an interesting rhyme is a second thing to notice and there is only room for one. Poetic devices: **anaphora** (identical sentence openings across the classification lines, so the ear predicts the frame) and **homoioteleuton** (the same clerical suffixes recurring: *-fied*, *-ed*, *-ly*), plus **polyptoton** on the file/filed/filing family if it can be got in without strain.

**Hook, three ways to build it:** (1) the classification sentence's last line, sung on a single sustained note with the pronoun on the strong beat and the rhyme word after it, so the meter can never report the change; (2) the same line with the pen audibly setting down on the beat before it — the room punctuating instead of the voice; (3) the same line with the delay copy arriving a fixed short interval late, so the pronoun is heard **twice** and the second time is already a record.

**Narrative and voice:** the perspective ladder is the pair's whole content — third, second, first, or the inversion the variation calls for. **Do not write a narrator.** Even in the passes where the pronoun is not first person, the singer is the one it is about; the grammar is being applied to her, not reported by her.

**Additional (three required):** *Central aspect* — one word, one position, one change. *Emotional tone signals* — courteous, unhurried, correct. *Lyric structure* — the block is a fixed object; the variation lives entirely in which pronoun occupies the slot.

# 3. SONG GUIDE 2 — *THE ROOM THAT DOES NOT GET LOUDER* (facet F2, weight 0.22)

Treat the arrangement as a photograph that is being held up three times rather than a film that is being played: the instrument list at the final bar must be the identical list from bar one, and the correct way to make the third pass feel different is to change **nothing** and let the word do the work. The two storytelling techniques here are **the fixed camera** — a single unmoving shot in which meaning accrues only because the viewer's knowledge changes — and **the refrain of a ballad**, which earns its power by being the one thing that refuses to develop while the story moves around it. Mood: hold *equanimity* in the chorus and let the verse tags travel `Vigilance → Unease → Trepidation`, but understand that this travel happens **in the room**, in what the light and the hall and the second chair are doing, never in the throat. Instrumentation and texture: felt upright piano with the dampers audible, a nylon-string guitar played with the flesh of the thumb rather than a nail, one held reed or harmonium chord that does not crescendo, no drum kit at all and no percussion grid — the only rhythmic events are the pen, the chair, and the pulse of the piano's left hand. Production: dry to the point of being uncomfortable, four inches off the capsule, no plate, no hall, no tail; the room tone is supplied entirely by the single electronic element and by the building's own noises; the mix is mono-centred with a narrow acoustic field, and stereo width must not increase at any point. Structure: nine sections, identical dynamics, identical density, identical vocal placement; the runtime target is 3:10–3:40 and the exclusion field is doing the load-bearing work here, because **the generator's strongest prior is to build and a song that fights the generator gets smoothed unless the exclude field does the fighting for it.** Rhyme and line length are held constant across the three passes for the same reason as the arrangement: any variance is a competing signal.

**Poetic devices:** **epizeuxis** used once inside the chorus (an immediate repetition of a short clerical phrase, identical each pass) and **asyndeton** in the furniture lists, which strips connectives so the room arrives as an inventory rather than a description.

**Hook, three ways:** (1) the classification line delivered at exactly the same dB, distance and vowel colour all three passes, with the vocal double locked; (2) the line arriving after a bar in which only the electronic element is sounding, so the vacuum is identical every time; (3) the line placed so its final rhyme word is the last sound before the tag, with no instrumental gesture marking it.

**Additional (three required):** *Musical style and effects* — no swell, no filter movement, no automation on any bus. *Vocal techniques* — one dry tongue-click before the first chorus word, identical every pass; final consonants fully released, clerical diction; no vibrato growth anywhere. *Special effects* — the level-matched short-delay double is the only processing on the voice, and it never varies.

# 4. SONG GUIDE 3 — *COURTESY THAT IS CORRECT* (facet F3, weight 0.18)

The chorus is a sentence off a form, and the direction is to write it so well that a real assessor could read it aloud without embarrassment: *reclassified, no change to the reading, everything has been filed correctly, everyone has been more than fair.* The two storytelling techniques are **free indirect discourse from the institution** — the song borrows the office's own vocabulary and never editorialises inside it — and **the disclosed ending**, the flash-forward technique in which you are told early that the outcome is fixed so the telling itself becomes the dread. Mood: pleasant. Genuinely pleasant. Warm lamp, mild voices, an offer of a copy to take home. The fear is entirely structural and must never be signalled by the delivery, by an ominous chord, or by a single word of the lyric admitting that anything is wrong; **at least one line must concede plainly that the finding is accurate**, and that line is the wound. Instrumentation should be domestic rather than gothic: nothing sepulchral, no low strings sawing, no bell. Production: warm rather than cold — the temptation to make an assessment room sound like a morgue is the largest craft error available here, and it converts a clean fear into a genre cliché. Structure: the classification sentence is the fixed centre of every chorus and is never inflected, never spat, never softened; the tag afterwards is domestic housekeeping — a copy, a string, a tray, a light — and is the same housekeeping every time. Rhyme: stock pairs only, and choose the flattest available rhyme in every case; if a pair is clever, replace it.

**Poetic devices:** **litotes** (the room's habit of understating: *nothing is better and nothing is worse*) and **anadiplosis** used once, where the last word of one clerical line opens the next, which is how forms actually read.

**Hook, three ways:** (1) sung as a plain statement on a descending third, warm, unhurried; (2) sung on a monotone with the piano landing under the last two words only; (3) sung with a half-beat of nothing before the final clause, so the courtesy has one small hesitation in it that is identical all three times.

**Additional (three required):** *Narrative elements* — nobody in this song is a villain and nobody is punished. *Emotional tone signals* — courteous, calibrated, correct. *Instrumentation and tone* — domestic warmth, not gothic cold.

# 5. SONG GUIDE 4 — *THE HUM THAT WAS ALREADY THERE* (facet F4, weight 0.14)

The single electronic element is furniture: it is sounding before the first sung syllable, it is sounding after the last, it never enters, never leaves, never swells, and it is never filtered — and its unmoving-ness is precisely what makes one word able to be the only event in three minutes. The two storytelling techniques are **the establishing detail that is never explained** — a fact given in the first line and never returned to, which the mind keeps in the background exactly as a body keeps the hum of a strip light — and **the unreliable silence**, where what is heard as quiet is in fact a continuous tone the listener has stopped attending to and will notice again at the worst moment. Mood: the element supplies the floor, and the floor is the day's atmosphere — a sky almost full and completely calm, a magnetosphere at its quietest, a trace arriving from a storm nobody here saw. Texture: this is the pair's **Quantum Bit-Depth Swell, inverted** — the tone has *already* collapsed to grit before bar 1 and never un-collapses, which is how the ambitious arm's intense palette lives inside a song forbidden to move; give it one pitch or one rate, no LFO, no envelope, no reverb, and put it dead centre where the voice is, not out in the stereo field where it would become an effect. Production: level it so it is under the voice and above the room, and never touch that fader again; if the mix has automation, the automation is a defect. Structure: it is present in all nine sections and in the silences between them. Rhyme and line logic are unaffected by it, which is the point — **it is a condition, not a participant.**

**Poetic devices:** **synaesthesia** used sparingly for the tone's colour (a dark saturated magenta under a green that reads black until light hits it — the day's two colours, used as production temperature and never named in the lyric) and **consonance** in the furniture lines, whose hard stops give the room its edges.

**Hook, three ways:** (1) the hum alone for one bar before the first chorus, identical each pass, so the hook is entered through the same door three times; (2) the hum plus one held reed note beneath the classification line; (3) the hum foregrounded for the last two seconds of the song, unchanged, as the only thing still going.

**Additional (three required):** *Key sequences* — the bar of hum before each chorus; the final two seconds. *Special effects and innovations* — a static grit source used as room tone rather than as a swell. *Musical style and effects* — one electronic element, one acoustic world, one seam; **anything that would be a second intruder is cut.**

# 6. SONG GUIDE 5 — *PLAIN SUBJECT, UNNAMED SHAPE* (facet F5, weight 0.12)

Write so that a stranger who hears it once in a distracted ear can say afterwards *"it's about a woman in an office while someone fills in a form that changes what she's called"* — and so that the same stranger could not tell you a single thing about the song's construction. The two storytelling techniques are **the inventory** — a list of objects that carries a situation without stating it (a chair, a desk, a folder, a pen) — and **the overheard procedure**, in which the reader learns the stakes only from the vocabulary of the process. Mood: the legibility is the surface and the fear is under it; obliqueness about what the song is *about* is fog, not depth, and an unnameable subject is a repair. Direction for the lyric's own hygiene: **never write a line that refers to the words, the repetition, the passes, the change, the name of the change, or the fact that anything is being substituted.** The subject may be stated plainly as often as it likes; the shape may never be stated at all. Structurally the discipline is easy to check — after drafting, strike every line that could be read as commentary on the song's own form; if the operation is still audible in what is left, it is real. Instrumentation and production must not be allowed to comment either: no ironic musical quotation, no "clockwork" figure, no ticking that means *procedure*, because a production metaphor is a caption too.

**Poetic devices:** **enjambment across the one-number line** so the number arrives at a line-end and the *response* to it opens the next line, which is how a fact is answered rather than recited; and **caesura as wound** — one mid-line breath in the tag, in the same place every pass, that changes what the second half of the line is doing.

**Hook, three ways:** (1) put the subject plainly in the title so the first-listen retelling is free; (2) put it in the first quatrain as an object rather than a claim; (3) let the classification sentence itself be the hook, since a form's own sentence is more adoptable than any thesis about it.

**Additional (three required):** *Central aspect of the concept* — a measurement that does not change and a heading that does. *Lyric structure* — twenty-four to twenty-six lines per pass, verse twelve, chorus eight or nine, tag four. *Narrative and thematic suggestion* — the reclassification is the theme; the event that was reclassified is **not in the song at all.**

# 7. SONG GUIDE 6 — WILD CARD · *THE FIXED-CAMERA DEPOSITION* (covers facet F6, weight 0.10)

The cross-disciplinary import is from installation art rather than from music: a recorded process executed in a room until the room becomes audible in it, and — from documentary cinema — the **locked-off frame**, where nothing in the shot moves except one small thing near the edge, and the eye that finds it does all the emotional work the score would otherwise have to do. Apply that to the EMO dramaturgy: nine section headers, nine states **of the room** — the blind down, the folder open, one copy pushed across the wood, the second chair now empty, the pen capped, the hall light off, only the desk lamp left, the tray carried out, nothing moving at all — while the Role field of every header repeats one unchanging sentence about level, distance and warmth. The taxonomy travel is `Vigilance → Equanimity → Composure → Unease → Equanimity → Apprehension → Dread → Trepidation → Suspense`, and it **ends unresolved on purpose**; do not end on numbness, which is a resolution wearing a bleak coat, and do not end on relief. The wildcard permission this guide grants: one body-noise vocabulary — a dry swallow, an unforegrounded breath at the same point of each pass, and a tongue-click before the first chorus word — used as **the only evidence that a body is in the room**, deployed identically three times so it is a property and not a performance. Production dramaturgy follows the same law: every unusual sound has a job, and every sound's job is to be the same sound it was ninety seconds ago. Runtime 3:10–3:40; no fade; the song stops because the block stops.

**Poetic devices:** **epistrophe** across the three tags (identical closing line, three times) and **hard-consonant spine** in the furniture inventories, which is the pair's borrowing from the run's consonant flair without importing another pair's operation.

**Hook, three ways:** (1) the classification line, unaltered; (2) the tag's final image, which is the last thing the listener holds; (3) the one-bar hum entry into every chorus, which is the room's own hook and belongs to no instrument.

**Additional (three required):** *Emotional tone signals* — the room reports; the voice does not. *Vocal techniques* — three body-noise instances, identical placement each pass, each one load-bearing. *Rhythm and melody* — one static kinetic defect declared at bar 1 and never corrected, specified in **body units** (a whole beat inside a four-bar phrase, or a permanent extra beat in the bar), never in notation units, because drift by a sixteenth is felt by nobody and that failure is a repair.
