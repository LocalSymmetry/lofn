## CRITICS' PASS — the two chosen judges, and what they did

**Judge A — THE FIRST LISTEN** *(after Susan Rogers)*. Criterion: what a distracted ear retains
from one pass. Verdict: *"The question-and-answer hook is the only reason the number survives.
Take the question away and 'fourteen seconds' is trivia. All four keep the question."*
Her correction, applied: **the subject must be plain before the first chorus, not at it.** V4 was
burying itself in body detail; `I have been thin and cold and huge / I have been dark all the way
down` now lands in verse 1, so the listener knows what she is by 0:50.

**Judge B — THE THIRTY SECONDS** *(after Kate Bush)*. Criterion: extreme formal risk delivered
inside pop legibility. Verdict: *"The risk is real and the surface is a beach. That is the
architecture. But three of these want a bigger last chorus and they cannot have one — the whole
mechanism is that nothing gets louder."* Her correction, applied: **the final chorus is longer,
never bigger.** All four extensions are six lines added on one side, at the same level.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-AUDIENCE** *(after Willis)*, unresolved and on the record: *"V1 is the best-made and
V3 is the most alive. If the run picks V1 because it scores highest, the run has picked the
dignified one again."* → Recorded, not resolved. The pair ships four; the selection is not mine.

## THE DELETION TEST — RUN, NOT CLAIMED (R4)

**Method.** Strike every line in each final lyric that refers to the song's structure — its
landing, its count, its lateness, its earliness, its meter, its return. Then ask whether the
operation is still there.

**Candidates found and struck at step 09, before this file existed** (six lines, all deleted
rather than rewritten, at the Adorno seat's insistence):
`Not the way I was made to move` · `that is not what I meant to do` (×2) · `My mouth said stay and
my hands said no` · `Nobody's counting` · `My whole weight went out ahead of me`.
Two more were struck here at step 10: `my chest went first` (V1) and `My knees went first, my
mouth came after` (V3).

**Result of the test as run on the four FINAL lyrics: ZERO lines strike.**
There is no line left in any of the four that refers to the structure — not the displacement, not
the return, not the homecoming, not the withholding. **Nothing vanished, because there was nothing
left to remove.** The operation is carried entirely by (a) where the clapped figure lands, which
is a production instruction in the music prompt and never a lyric, and (b) the sub keeping the
moved landing at the seam. A listener who reads only the words learns that a wave arrived at a
beach and is pleased about it, and learns nothing whatsoever about a figure relanding.

**The nearest surviving line, named so the test is falsifiable rather than self-congratulatory:**
V4's `Oh — my hands went off on their own. / I felt them go before I knew.` This is the closest
any lyric comes to the mechanism. It survives because it reports a **sensation** and never a
**structure** — it does not say what the hands did, only that the body registered it first. If a
reviewer rules it a caption, the repair is deletion, not rewriting, and V4 loses two lines and
keeps its operation intact.

## THE BODY TEST (R3) — STATED IN BODY UNITS

> **The clapped four-line figure relands ONE WHOLE BEAT further inside its four-bar phrase on each
> return** — a displacement of one beat in sixteen, roughly **0.59 s at 102 BPM (V1), 0.63 s at
> 96 (V2), 0.51 s at 118 (V3), 0.68 s at 88 (V4)** — never a sixteenth, never a tick, never a
> notation unit. At every tempo in this pair the move is longer than half a second, which is
> comfortably above the threshold at which a listener's shoulder arrives before the clap does.

**And the second half of R3 — *would she still be inside this if nobody were recording?*** Yes,
and it is the only honest answer: a swell arriving at a beach does not stop arriving when the tape
stops, and a body clapping a figure it likes keeps clapping it. The song has no audience inside
it. Nobody sent for her. That is the fifth line of every chorus.

## DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK — ONE CAPPED PASS

**What these prompts would literally PRODUCE.** First five seconds: a low sine (V1/V3), round
analogue (V2) or enormous slow (V4) sub alone, centred, with one close breath — no percussion, no
pad, no swell. Hands enter on top around 0:06–0:10 as hard-panned palmas with a wide hole in the
middle; the voice arrives close, dry, dead centre, mid-register, audibly grinning. Groove is
bouncing hand percussion and cajón at 88–118 BPM with no kit anywhere. The bold device — the
clapped figure relanding a whole beat — lands three times, at each figure return, and the seam
(sub keeping the moved landing, hands out) sits in the bridge, roughly two-thirds in. Final chorus
is longer, not louder.

**Adversarially: name the one way this would render generic.**
> **Suno hears "handclaps" and reaches for a clap-track sample, which is a grid — and a grid
> silently quantises the displacement away. The record then comes back as a perfectly pleasant
> warm hand-percussion pop song with no operation in it at all, and it passes every text gate we
> own, because the text is fine. That is the failure mode, and it is invisible from here.**

**Self-repair, applied once:** every exclude field now spends its **first ~180 characters** on that
single risk, in six vocabularies (`quantized handclaps, programmed clap track, drum machine claps,
click-tight percussion, corrected timing, gridlocked groove`), before it spends a character on
taste. The music prompts were amended to name the palmas as **played** (`cupped`, `cracking`,
`uneven`, `slightly rushing`, `trailing half off the beat`) rather than as a texture, and the
shaker is specified as **uneven / slack / rushing** in three of four so a grid is audibly wrong
from the first bar. **This is a mitigation, not a fix.** It cannot be verified without a render,
and the honest status of the operation until then is **UNRENDERED**. `lofn-render-audit` under THE
BLIND RULE is the only instrument that can close it.

**Second diff, against the Golden Seed and the Golden Move:** register rotated — alto not soprano;
D Mixolydian / B-flat major / E Dorian / G major (none is A major); 88–118 BPM (none is ~110);
palette is warm salt water, not frost-and-cosmos. One wounding fact, answered. The turn is past
the midpoint in all four and it is a **choice**, not a darkening: she stops describing arriving and
goes over to the low side. Fear is braided once per song, in a verse, unresolved (`The bad sky is a
long way back` · `The storm's a long way back and spent` · `The bad sky is spent and gone` · `The
dark I came out of is done`). The subject appears plainly in verse 1 in all four.
