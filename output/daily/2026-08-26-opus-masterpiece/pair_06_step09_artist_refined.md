## 0 · ⚠️ ONE DELIBERATE DEVIATION FROM THE STEP CONTRACT, AND WHY

The step-09 contract says: *"Choose a lesser-known musician… rewrite the prompt in the musician's style."*
**The run handoff overrides the naming half of that instruction (L30):**

> ⛔ **NO REAL ARTIST, GROUP, PANEL-SOURCE OR COMPANY NAMES IN ANY OF THE THREE FIELDS.** Name the SCENE
> and the TECHNIQUE (public idioms); name the PEOPLE only in the dedication. *A name in the prompt is an
> instruction to imitate; a name in the dedication is a debt paid.*

Naming a real practitioner in this artifact would put that name one copy-paste away from the generating
field, which is the failure mode `MEMORY.md` records as *credits belong in the dedication, never in the
generator*. So the step's **function** is preserved and its **naming** is not: each variation gets an
**unnamed practitioner lens** — a specific working musician described by their conditions, constraints and
vocabulary rather than by identity. The lexicon shift the step asks for is real; the imitation instruction
is not created. Living-scene debts are paid where they are actually owed, in the **Lineage & Credit block**
(§4) and the **dedication** (§5).

## 1 · THE FOUR PRACTITIONER LENSES

### V1 — *the jobbing fiddler who records in a farm kitchen*
Works with one ribbon microphone, will not overdub, tunes to whatever the room is doing that morning, and
regards a click track as an insult. Her lexicon is **gauge, rosin, tuning down to the room, the board,
one take and don't ask.** What she brings: an intolerance for anything smoothed. What she takes away: the
temptation to make the thinning passes *prettier* — in her hands a departed instrument leaves a hole and
the survivors are audibly working harder, which is exactly what Facet 1 measures.

### V2 — *the parish singing-leader who runs an unaccompanied women's group in a village hall*
Pitches from her own chest without a fork, keeps the group in close intervals because that is what the
room does with a wide one, and has never once asked anybody to project. Lexicon: **the pitch, the close
chord, take a breath and come off together, don't reach.** What she brings: the certainty that a low
voice can hold a bass line, because in her hall it always has. What she takes away: any temptation toward
a cathedral — her building has a flat ceiling and a lino floor, and the record should sound like that.

### V3 — *the bandoneonista who plays for dancers in a back room and never for a theatre*
Plays for feet, not for applause. Refuses rubato because the couple on the floor cannot dance to it. His
lexicon is **marcato, arrastre, chicharra, latigo, the count leaning forward, keep the bellows honest.**
What he brings: an offbeat that is architecture rather than decoration. What he takes away: the melodrama
that a tango chassis will otherwise supply by default — the sob, the theatrical ritardando, the massed
strings.

### V4 — *the piano technician who prepares other people's instruments and plays alone after the hall empties*
Knows the frame better than the repertoire. Strikes a string with a plectrum because she is curious what
the bridge does. Lexicon: **felt, plectrum, the frame, the temperature of the room, let it ring out and
don't pedal it.** What she brings: patience for a sustained tone, and no interest at all in resolving
anything. What she takes away: baroque ornament — she has no time for trills and it shows.

## 3 · WHAT THE LENSES REFUSED TO CHANGE

The step-09 contract warns that a complete rewrite throws out good work. Four things were proposed by the
lenses and **refused by the moderator**, with reasons on the record:

1. **The fiddler wanted a second take of the last pass "with more in it."** Refused. Ruling 2 is binding —
   the room may change, the delivery may not, and *more in it* is the lift under another name.
2. **The singing-leader wanted the answering voices back for the final refrain** ("so it ends together").
   Refused. That is a recovery, and the intruder placement assigned at step 05 is **at the very end, with
   no recovery.**
3. **The bandoneonista wanted a four-bar instrumental *variación* before the last refrain.** Refused: it
   would be a section that exists to be impressive, and it would break the rigid 7/7/7/7 that makes the
   missing subtraction audible.
4. **The technician wanted the sustained tone to fade rather than cut.** Refused. `fade-out` is in the
   exclude field of all four for the same reason: a fade is a resolution, and this record stops.

## 4 · LINEAGE & CREDIT — V3 ONLY

**V3 draws on a living scene and therefore carries a block.** The other three variations name no living
scene: V1 is described by its instruments and its room, V2 by voice-leading and a hall, V4 by a preparation
technique — none of them invoke a named tradition, and none carry a debt of this kind.

> **Scene:** the tango of **Buenos Aires and Montevideo** — the Río de la Plata tradition.
> **Techniques borrowed, named as techniques in the prompt so they can be traced:** the **marcato**
> (the driven 4, and the 3-3-2 lean), the **arrastre** (the dragged approach into the beat), the
> **chicharra** (the bow scraped behind the bridge for a cicada rasp), the **latigo** (the whipped
> ascending slide), and the **bandoneón** itself — an instrument that arrived in that scene by accident
> and was rebuilt by it into something its makers never designed.
> **Who is owed:** the bandoneón players, dance-hall orchestras and *milongas* who built and still keep
> these figures. **Listeners should be pointed upstream, to the living scene, not to us.**
> **Honesty clause, both ways:** ⛔ this is **not a sample.** Nothing here quotes a recording. We asked
> for these figures **by name, as techniques**, so we name the tradition they come from — and we do not
> claim the genre, the idiom, or any "first."
> **What was deliberately not taken:** the *orquesta típica*, the theatrical rubato, and the sob. The
> exclude field blocks all three by name. This is fusion with credit, and the borrowing is one rhythmic
> intelligence, not a costume.
