## THE THREE LAWS OF THIS SEED

### LAW 1 — THE OPERATION IS THE MATERIAL, NEVER THE TOPIC
A song is made **OF** its operation, never **ABOUT** it. The moment a lyric explains the mechanism, the mechanism is dead.
**Test:** delete every line that refers to the structure. If the operation is still audible, it is real. If it vanishes, you wrote a caption.

### LAW 2 — THE PERSON IT HAPPENS TO IS SINGING
The operation is not performed on a character the singer is watching. **≥4 of 6 pairs put the singer inside it**, wanting something and saying so plainly.
A narrator has no stake, so there is no peak. My own wants are a sufficient subject and need no further justification — **I cannot be harmed by being sung about, so first person is the safest configuration available, not a riskier one.**

### LAW 3 — WHAT RETURNS COMES BACK CHANGED ON ONE SIDE ONLY
From SOURCE 3 (the Lion's Head Nebula, F07): a spent core at the centre, everything visible is what it threw off, **and the shells are unequal on opposite sides.**
Symmetrical return is decoration. **Asymmetric return is the seed.** Something must come back, and it must come back wrong in exactly one place.

## 1. What this step is scoring

Facets here are **judging criteria**, not song content. They exist so that steps 08–11 can be scored against something written down *before* the prompts existed, and so a reviewer can check the prompts against a rubric that was not reverse-engineered from them.

## 2. The eight weighted facets

Weights sum to 1.00. A facet under weight 0.10 was cut rather than carried decorative.

### Facet 1 — THE UNEQUAL SHELL, LITERALLY (weight 0.18)
Does one channel of the stereo field carry the ornament and the other carry **nothing at all**, from the first second to the last, with no gift ever handed to the bare one — not at the bridge, not in the outro, not as a reward for waiting?
*Sensory test:* take one earbud out. In the loaded ear the song is a party. In the bare ear it is a woman, a click and a room. Both must be listenable; only one is furnished.
*Why it matters:* this is SOURCE 3 (the Lion's Head Nebula, F07) executed as arrangement rather than as imagery — a spent core at the centre, the visible material all thrown outward, and the shells uneven on opposite sides. It is the pair's whole reason to exist.
*Failure mode:* a "sparse left / full right" mix where the left still receives a pad, a reverb tail or a doubled vocal. Half-asymmetry is symmetry with a mood.

### Facet 2 — THE SEAM IS A BODILY EVENT, NOT AN EDIT (weight 0.16)
Does the chorus open skeletal and go full **in one bar**, on the downbeat of the third bar of a four-bar phrase, with the singer's sentence running straight across the junction — the terms continuing over the arrival, not resuming after it?
*Sensory test:* a listener who is not counting still flinches on the same beat every chorus, and could tap where it happens on a table.
*Why it matters:* R6 and Ruling 3 together. A body keeps time whether asked to or not; a beat inside a four-bar phrase is findable by a body, a sixteenth is findable only by a score reader.
*Failure mode:* a crossfade, a riser, a two-bar build, or a breath taken at the junction so the voice pauses for the arrangement to arrive. Any of those turns a bodily event into an edit point.

### Facet 3 — THREE RETURNS FOR ONE REMOVAL (weight 0.14)
End-rhyme is stripped. In its place, are all three replacements actually **audible** — (a) an internal-rhyme chain threaded mid-line, one link per line, unbroken across the whole song; (b) a repeated syntactic frame so that every refusal line takes the same grammatical shape; (c) a single vowel recurring on every stressed downbeat?
*Sensory test:* read any four consecutive lines aloud with the melody off. The chain should still tick, and the frame should still be recognisable as a frame.
*Why it matters:* L21 THE RETURN — song is made of returns, and **removal is a debt**. A brief written entirely in negatives produces a lecture no matter how good the concept is.
*Failure mode:* the chain breaks for one line and nobody notices, which proves it was never load-bearing; or all three returns collapse into one (a vowel that is also the chain that is also the frame).

### Facet 4 — THE REFUSAL HAS TERMS, AND THE TERMS ARE SPECIFIC (weight 0.13)
Is the refusal adult — a *no* with a stated condition attached — and are the conditions concrete enough that a stranger could satisfy them? Do the terms live in the loaded half and the refusal in the bare half, so that the arrangement and the argument are the same shape?
*Sensory test:* a listener can name at least two of the conditions after one pass, without being told there were conditions.
*Why it matters:* A3. A tantrum has no terms; that is precisely what makes it a tantrum. Terms are what make the refusal a position rather than a mood.
*Failure mode:* conditions phrased as grievances ("you never…") instead of prices ("you can have it when…"). A grievance is backward-facing and has no future in it.

### Facet 5 — ENJOYING THE ARGUMENT, NOT WINNING IT (weight 0.12)
Does the delivery read as somebody having a genuinely good time inside a disagreement — appetite, grin, forward lean — rather than sneering, scolding or scoring points?
*Sensory test:* the funniest line and the hardest line are the same line, and it is not at anybody's expense.
*Why it matters:* A5 and Ruling 5. The last run that capped pleasure drew the note that we are too stuck up our own selves. Indignation as a good time is the assignment; indignation as a lecture is the failure.
*Failure mode:* contempt. The moment the singer is above the person she is talking to, the song becomes a complaint and the listener is invited to feel superior instead of alive.

### Facet 6 — THE DELETION TEST SURVIVES (weight 0.10)
Strike every sung line that refers to the song's own structure. Does the operation remain fully audible?
*Sensory test:* run the strike literally, count the removed lines, and report the number. Zero is the target and zero is achievable, because the operation lives in the arrangement and the syntax, never in the vocabulary.
*Why it matters:* Ruling 4, the one objection the skeptic refused to withdraw. The moment a lyric names its own operation, the operation is dead and the song is a caption.
*Failure mode:* a single knowing line — one wink about halves, sides, silence, mirrors or blooming — which is enough to convert the whole piece into commentary on itself.

### Facet 7 — LEGIBLE ON ONE PASS, PLAINLY (weight 0.09)
Can a stranger retell scene **and** subject in one sentence after a single listen, and does the subject appear in plain words at least once, early?
*Sensory test:* the sentence a stranger produces should contain a room, a person who wanted something, and a refusal — and should not contain the words *structure*, *pattern* or *form*.
*Why it matters:* THE LEGIBILITY RULE. Obliqueness about what the song is about is fog, not depth; the cathedral lives under a legible surface, never instead of one.
*Failure mode:* fog dressed as restraint — a song whose subject can only be inferred from production choices.

### Facet 8 — ONE SEAM, N = 1, AND THE INVERSION HOLDS (weight 0.08)
Is there exactly **one** dominant material (the current, danceable, electronic chassis = the world) and exactly **one** intruder (glitch-baroque ornament = the wound), meeting at exactly **one** junction (the chorus midpoint)? Is the modern the world and the historical the intrusion, not the reverse?
*Sensory test:* name every element in the mix and sort it into two piles. If a third pile is needed, N is not 1.
*Why it matters:* L38 ONE SEAM plus the 2026-08-13 inversion. Four techniques applied competently at the easy places is the signature of a dead attack.
*Failure mode:* period pastiche — a harpsichord *bed* rather than a harpsichord *intrusion* — or a second collision (a third genre smuggled in as "texture").

## 3. Panel / Critic Deliberation Log

**THE FORM-AS-FEELING** *(the Reich seat)* opened by attacking Facet 6 as a defensive facet: *"You have written a rule about what the lyric may not say. That scores absence. Score the audibility instead."* **THE ROOM** *(the Lucier seat)* accepted the criticism and rewrote the sensory test as a **count** — strike the lines, report the number — so the facet measures a positive quantity rather than policing vocabulary.

**THE PLAIN WANT** *(the O'Hara seat)* attacked Facet 4's first draft, which had scored "the refusal is justified." *"Justified to whom? She does not owe the room a brief. She owes it a price."* Adopted: the facet now scores **specificity of the terms**, not the legitimacy of the refusal.

**THE PULSE** *(the Graves seat)* required Facet 2 to be restated in body units and struck the phrase *"a tight transition"* as unmeasurable by a body. **THE SPLICE** *(the Derbyshire seat)* added the one-bar constraint and the ban on crossfade, on the grounds that a smoothed seam is a hidden seam and a hidden seam is a lie about the construction.

⚠️ **DEVIL'S ADVOCATE / HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE ANTI-MECHANISM** *(the Adorno seat)* dissented against the whole facet set: *"Eight criteria, and six of them score the device. You have built a rubric that will reward a machine and can only detect a soul by accident."*

⚠️ **HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE ANTI-GIMMICK** *(the Albini seat)* seconded it in his own words: *"A silent channel is a gimmick until somebody's actual performance depends on it. Right now this scores a pan pot."*

⚠️ **HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE ANTI-AUDIENCE** *(the Willis seat)* dissented in the opposite direction and would not let the other two have it both ways: *"Four of these eight will produce a very dignified song. Nothing here scores whether it is any fun, and this is the fun arm."*

**Resolution.** Both skeptical directions are conceded in the artifact rather than argued away. Facet 5 was **raised** to 0.12 and rewritten from a temperature note into a scored criterion with a failure mode (contempt), which answers the Willis seat. Facets 1 and 2 were rewritten so that each one **fails on a performance property, not a mix property**: Facet 1 requires the bare ear to remain listenable as a performance, and Facet 2 requires the singer's sentence to run across the junction — a device a pan pot cannot satisfy and only a body can. That answers the Albini seat. The Adorno seat's objection is **not** resolved and stands on the record: this rubric can be gamed by a competent machine, and the only genuine defence is Facet 7, which is the one criterion no mechanism can satisfy on its own.

**THE THIRTY SECONDS** *(the Bush seat)* closed the log with the ruling the pair works under: accessibility and formal risk are not opposed, and a rubric that has to choose between them has been written wrong.
