## 0. THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — ANSWERED CONCRETELY, BEFORE ANY LINE EXISTS

### **Where is the body standing?**
**In the water. Arriving.** Not on the sand, not on the deck, not on the bluff with a coffee.
The body is the moving water itself — chest-deep and rising, the last hundred yards of a thing
that started four days and an ocean away in a low that no one on this beach ever heard about.
Concretely, per variation: **V1** the last shallows at gold hour, sand coming up to meet her.
**V2** still out past the bar at night under a nearly-full moon, in no hurry at all. **V3** the
outer edge of the shorebreak with the dry sand *in sight*, leaning. **V4** inside the break,
tumbled, half under, mouth full of sea and laughing into it.
She is **3.9 feet high on a 14-second period** — small here, enormous where she started, and the
gap between her and the next of her is the only surviving evidence of the distance.

### **What could hurt it here?**
**Nothing, and that is the entire point.** The danger was elsewhere and long ago — a storm she
was made by and never saw the inside of, a whole ocean of attenuation between then and now. At
this beach she is four feet of warm water in sixty-eight-degree air with kelp in it. Nothing on
this coast can do a thing to her. **The clean fear is not in front of her, it is behind her, and
it is enormous, and it is already spent.** Braid it in *once*, as size, without comment, and never
resolve it — do not turn it into a lesson and do not let her flinch at it. She is glad. That is
the whole weather report.

## 1. THE INVARIANTS STEP 08 MAY NOT NEGOTIATE

- **The figure:** four lines, clapped, monosyllabic, physical. It returns **four times**. Its text
  is the *invariant*; only its **landing** moves — one whole beat, inside a four-bar phrase.
- **Section order:** Intro (sub alone) · Figure A · Verse 1 · Figure A±1 · Chorus · Verse 2 ·
  Figure A±2 · Chorus · Bridge (counts collide) · Figure A-home · Final Chorus · Outro.
- **Stanza lengths 6 / 6 / 4** — verse stanza 6, chorus stanza 6, figure stanza 4. A section may
  carry **two** stanzas of its own length; no stanza may be any other length.
- **The intruder is in the first ten seconds.** The sub does not enter. It was already running.
- **The seam:** hands stop, sub is already keeping the moved landing, does not correct — and **her
  weight goes over to it.** One seam. One intruder. Nothing else crosses.
- **One number sung: fourteen (seconds), at the hinge, as an answer.** Nothing else numeric —
  no digits, no other number-words anywhere in the sung lines.
- **No sung line refers to the structure.** Not the landing, not the count, not the lateness.
- **Every prompt names the SCENE and the TECHNIQUE. Every PERSON lives in the dedication.**

## GUIDE 1 — for FACET 1 · **THE RELANDING A BODY CATCHES** (w 0.28)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *anaphora as a handhold* — the figure's third line opens the same
way every pass, so the ear has a fixed post against which the moved landing is measured; (b)
*epizeuxis on a verb of looking* — an immediate self-repetition at the figure's close, which is
what a body does when it is pleased with itself and wants you to watch again.
**Mood & emotional arc:** begin at plain physical satisfaction, move to open glee, end at a
settled, grinning steadiness. No dread anywhere. The single shadow — the size of the storm — is
placed once in a verse and never looked at twice.
**Instrumentation & texture:** hands are the lead instrument. *Palmas sordas* (cupped, dull, air
trapped) carry the low clap; *palmas claras* (flat, cracking, bright) carry the accent; cajón
front face for the slap and corner for the low tone; a shaker in the near field; thigh and chest
for the body layer. Extended technique: the accent clap plays **contratiempo**, off the pulse, so
the ear is already in a state of pleasant disagreement before anything moves. **No drum kit.**
**Production & effects:** a small dry outdoor space, close mics, almost no tail — the drift is a
*timing* event and reverb is timing mud. Hands wide and hard-panned so the displacement is a
physical sweep across the head, not a level change. Leave real air between the figure and
everything else; the hole is the instrument.
**Structure & dynamics:** 3:20–3:50. The figure lands true, then +1 beat, then +1 again (or the
variation's own direction), then home. Nothing crescendos. The final chorus is not bigger — it is
**longer**, and that is the only reward.
**Rhyming scheme:** figure = slant AABA on last-three-character rimes; verse = loose ABCB with a
mid-stanza consonant echo; chorus = loose AABBCC held on open vowels.
**Poetic devices:** anaphora; epizeuxis; hard-consonant clustering in the figure to make it
clappable.
**Hook techniques:** (1) hands alone for a full bar before the figure returns, so the return is
the loudest thing in the song without being louder; (2) the last figure lands home with the
shaker *also* home, so two bodies agree at once; (3) a single dropped-out beat before the last
chorus — a hole a listener leans into.
**Narrative & thematic seed:** *I got here and I am pleased with myself, and my hands got here on
their own schedule.* First person, present tense, no addressee until the very end.
**Central aspect:** the displacement is the feeling; catching it is the delivery.
**Emotional tone signals:** Eagerness → Delight → Elation → Contentment.
**Special innovation:** the figure's text is byte-identical every pass; **only where it lands
changes.** Say nothing about it anywhere.

## GUIDE 2 — for FACET 2 · **THE MOUTH THAT OPENS** (w 0.22)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *inventory-of-the-immediate* — the verse names only what is
within arm's reach, in the shortest available words, which is what a working mouth does; (b)
*the widened answer* — the chorus answers a question the verse never asked, and answers it in
long words, so the release is lexical before it is musical.
**Mood & emotional arc:** verses are busy and low-slung and amused; the chorus is the jaw
dropping. Return to the short mouth immediately afterward, unembarrassed.
**Instrumentation & texture:** verses ride hands and shaker with the sub low and steady — almost
no sustain, so the consonants have somewhere to be. Choruses open the top: a sustained low-brass-
adjacent synth pad or bowed-adjacent long tone under the voice, wide, and stacked thirds on the
last polysyllable only. **The pad is not a new intruder** — it is the sub's own upper partials
opened up; if it reads as a second material, cut it.
**Production & effects:** verse vocal dry and two inches too close, breath left in; chorus vocal
same distance, same warmth, just **more air in the room behind it**. No shift in level, no
compression change — the openness is written into the words, not automated.
**Structure & dynamics:** verse stanzas 6 lines; chorus 6. The chorus's first polysyllable is the
first word in the song longer than two syllables. Guard that.
**Rhyming scheme:** verse — monosyllabic, slant, ABCB; chorus — the polysyllables carry the rime,
so the rhymes are trisyllabic-ish and loose (`delivered` / `shivered` class), never forced.
**Poetic devices:** consonance chain running through each verse on a single stop consonant;
assonance tunnel in the chorus carrying one long vowel across all six lines.
**Hook techniques:** (1) the number is the *reply* to a spoken question — write the question into
the line, not the arrangement; (2) the chorus's last line drops back to monosyllables for its
final three words, so the mouth closes on the hook; (3) a non-lexical open-vowel hum tail after
the chorus, one bar, hands only.
**Narrative seed:** *ask me how far.* The chorus exists to answer that and nothing else.
**Lyric structure:** short lines (4–7 words) throughout; the chorus may reach 9 and no further.
**Emotional tone signals:** Playfulness → Delight → Liberation.

## GUIDE 3 — for FACET 3 · **THE INTRUDER THAT WAS ALREADY THERE** (w 0.20)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *in medias res without announcement* — the song is already
running when the recording starts, so nothing gets an entrance; (b) *the unremarked hand-off* —
a change of who is carrying the weight, narrated only by what the body does next.
**Mood & emotional arc:** from unbothered to bodily delighted. The seam is not a crisis; it is a
lean.
**Instrumentation & texture:** ONE synthetic element: a sine-family sub, felt in the chest, no
overtones, no filter movement, no sidechain. It is present at second zero, alone, for several
seconds before a single hand exists. Everything else — palmas, cajón, shaker, thigh, chest,
breath, voice — is acoustic and stays acoustic. **Declare both facts explicitly in the prompt.**
**Production & effects:** sub mono and centred, everything else wide. The sub never changes level
across the whole song — not once. Its only event is that, at the seam, it is **already keeping the
moved landing** and does not correct.
**Structure & dynamics:** the seam sits in the bridge. Hands stop dead. The sub continues, wrong
and content. Her body goes over to it — the weight shift is the event, and it is the *only* place
in the song where the arrangement does something a listener would call dramatic.
**Rhyming scheme:** the bridge abandons end-rhyme for its first stanza and gets it back in its
second — the return is on one side only.
**Poetic devices:** caesura as a bodily hinge (a mid-line break at the weight shift); polysyndeton
after it (`and … and … and`) so the recovery sounds like a body finding its feet.
**Hook techniques:** (1) the SFX cue at the seam is five words or fewer and names only what stops;
(2) the sub's steadiness is the hook you don't notice until it is the only thing left; (3) the
figure that follows the seam is the one that comes home.
**Narrative seed:** *something under me had a hold and I let it.*
**Special innovation:** the intruder gets no entrance and no exit. It bookends the song by simply
never having left.
⚠️ **Albini seat, unwithdrawn:** if the seam can be executed with a fader, it is not a seam. The
event must be **her**, not the mix.

## GUIDE 4 — for FACET 4 · **PLEASURE DOING NO JOB** (w 0.18)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *the boast with no audience* — a plain claim of pleasure made to
nobody; (b) *comic bathos of scale* — an enormous origin reported in a household register
(luggage, groceries, deliveries), which is funny and true at once.
**Mood & emotional arc:** Delight → Glee → Elation → settled Contentment. **There is no turn
toward significance.** The Golden Move's "turn" is honoured *inside* the pleasure: past the
midpoint she stops describing arriving and starts *choosing*, which complicates the opening
stance without darkening it.
**Instrumentation & texture:** bounce is the mandate — hands, thigh, chest, shaker, a cajón that
is played like an instrument and not a metronome. Let the shaker be slightly uneven; a perfectly
even shaker is a machine reporting for duty.
**Production & effects:** leave laughter in. Leave the breath before the line in. Leave a clap
that lands slightly harder than the others in. The record should sound like people are having a
better time than the mix engineer.
**Structure & dynamics:** no build, no riser, no drop, no key lift. The reward is duration and
company, never volume.
**Rhyming scheme:** the chorus's rhymes are allowed to be a bit obvious. Obvious is a pleasure.
**Poetic devices:** diacope (`exactly, exactly`); a non-lexical hum used as punctuation.
**Hook techniques:** (1) an audible grin — write words that cannot be sung with a closed mouth;
(2) a line that is purely useless and beautiful, kept because it is nice; (3) a final chorus that
extends rather than climaxes.
**Narrative seed:** *nobody sent for me and that is exactly why I'm glad.*
⚠️ **Willis seat:** if the finished song is admirable rather than fun, this guide failed. **There
is no ceiling on the fun.** The clean fear behind her keeps its own place and polices nothing.

## GUIDE 5 — for FACET 5 · **THE EVIDENCE SINGS, PLAINLY** (w 0.12)

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *first-person report from inside the event* — she is the thing
that arrived, stated in ordinary words, at least once, early; (b) *the interval as the only
souvenir* — the gap is what she carried, and she treats it as luggage, not as a symbol.
**Mood & emotional arc:** open plainly, stay plain. The one shadow (the size of the storm) is a
single verse image, unresolved and unremarked.
**Instrumentation & texture:** the voice is the legibility instrument — close, mid-register alto,
grin audible, edges unpolished, breath kept. **Not a soprano, not crystalline, not breathy-
ethereal.** If it sounds like the house winner's fingerprint, the register was not rotated.
**Production & effects:** dry, present, no vocal reverb, no doubling except on the chorus's last
polysyllable. Diction over beauty.
**Structure & dynamics:** the number lands at the emotional hinge — the chorus's opening pair of
lines — as a **reply**, and the following line *responds* to it rather than restating it.
**Rhyming scheme:** whatever the section owns; legibility outranks the scheme at the hinge.
**Poetic devices:** metonymy (the interval standing in for the whole crossing); understatement.
**Hook techniques:** (1) the question-and-answer shape makes the hook self-explaining in one
listen; (2) the title phrase sits inside the chorus intact; (3) the last chorus turns outward to
the next swell behind her without ever leaving first person.
**Narrative seed:** *I am the swell that came in tonight, and the gap is all I brought.*
**LEGIBILITY TEST that step 08 must be able to pass:** a stranger, after one listen, says —
*"It's a wave singing about arriving at a beach after coming a very long way, and she's delighted,
and there's a clap that keeps landing in a funny place."* If they can only manage the third
clause, the song is fog and routes back here.

## GUIDE 6 — **WILD CARD** · *the cross-disciplinary slot*

**The import: a stop-motion animator's exposure sheet, and a swimmer's turn.**
Stop-motion is shot on twos — every drawing held for two frames — and an animator who slips a
*single* frame produces a movement the eye registers as **alive** rather than as an error. That is
the whole aesthetic argument for this pair in one sentence: **the deviation is not damage, it is
the evidence of a hand.** Import the exposure-sheet discipline literally — decide the landing of
each figure *in advance*, on paper, in beats, before a word is written; never improvise the
displacement.
The second import is **the tumble turn**: a swimmer arriving at a wall does not stop and reverse,
she *rolls through* and the wall gives her the speed back. Build the seam that way. Her weight
does not fall into the sub — it rolls through it and comes out faster.
**Colour import (today's palette, as texture only, never as subject):** `#082600` *Deep Fir* —
a green so dark it reads black until light hits it. Use it once, as a verse image, for the water
she came out of. `#882266` *Big Dip O Ruby* — a bruise-adjacent magenta, not a pretty pink — is
the low sky behind her, and it belongs in the **production** register (the pad's colour), never in
a lyric that would make it pretty.
**Register import:** the run's own note — *frivolity and fun; we are too stuck up our asses.*
One line in every variation must be **useless**. Not ironic, not a wink: useless and enjoyable.
Kept because it is nice.
**What the wild card forbids:** a second collision. This is N = 1. The animator, the swimmer and
the paint chips are *method*, not material — none of them may become an audible element.
