## WHAT THESE FACETS ARE FOR

These are the rubric the four variations get scored against at steps 09 and 10. They are **not** generic music-quality criteria — a generic facet set would let a variation pass without ever touching this pair's operation. Each facet below is derived from **P05's own fixed axis values** and each names its own failure mode, so a facet can actually reject something.

## THE SEVEN FACETS (weighted — total 100)

### F1 · THE DEPARTURE IS TO A DEPTH, AND THE DEPTHS ARE ALL DIFFERENT — **weight 22**
Every sounding element enters at ONE shared distance from the listener and each leaves toward its **own** named depth; by the last refrain nothing occupies the same distance as anything else, and none of it comes back into alignment. Score high when each element's exit depth is stated as a physical distance a mixer could execute (feet, tail length, band-limit, sub frequency, side); score zero when departure is described as "fading", "dissolving", "drifting" or any other undifferentiated word. **Automatic REPAIR:** any movement scheme indexed by colour, hue, spectrum-as-palette or "five colours / five angles" grammar — that is the sibling song's dimension and this pair exits on **DISTANCE**.
*Flairs: #2 The Unequal Shell · #4 The Audible Join · #9 Sparse Spectra.*

### F2 · THE EDGE IS HERS AND IT IS NOT A PUNISHMENT — **weight 18**
The singer is inside the condition, in the first person, wanting things out loud. The tragedy is not that the world ran out — it is that **she cannot reach further**, and she has never seen why. Score high when the lyric states a want plainly and without an alibi and when nothing in the song points at the cause, hints at a cause, or offers her the possibility of learning one. **Automatic REPAIR:** any line naming or implying a stem, root, rope, cord, leash, anchor, chain, or "something holding me here"; any narrator watching this happen to someone else.
*Flairs: #14 Tender Power · #7-inverted (appetite belongs to the accessible arm; here the want is stated at the same level as everything else).*

### F3 · THE LADDER IS AUDIBLE TO A BODY THAT IS NOT COUNTING — **weight 15**
End-rhyme is stripped and the debt is paid three ways: an eight-rung **vowel ladder** on the final stressed vowel of each line (`ah · aw · oh · oo · uh · eh · ay · ee`), an **anaphoric opening** shared by every inventory line, and a **byte-identical refrain**. Score high when the ladder climbs the whole eight rungs across a stanza and resets, so a listener hears a staircase rather than a scheme; score low when the ladder is technically present but the rung-words are unstressed, buried mid-line, or dialect-ambiguous. **Body test:** a listener who cannot name a vowel must still hear the climb arrive somewhere.
*Flairs: #8 The Body That Notices · #2 The Unequal Shell.*

### F4 · THE REACH IS AN EVENT WITH A DURATION, NOT AN ATMOSPHERE — **weight 20**
ONE seam, N=1. It happens at a stated place and lasts a stated number of seconds, the singer is **doing something across it**, and the sound goes to a synthetic depth no acoustic source in the song can occupy. Score high when the moment is short, physical, and **slightly embarrassing** — a body failing at a small ordinary task. Score zero for a beautiful reach, a soaring reach, a swell, a widening, a held note that "reaches for" anything, or any version in which the reach is a mood rather than a thing that happens. **This is the pair's declared liability and it is scored hardest.**
*Flairs: #5 One Stroke Loaded · #4 The Audible Join · #15 Quantum Bit-Depth Swell.*

### F5 · TENDERNESS WITH NO APOLOGY, AT ONE LEVEL — **weight 12**
Warmth delivered without pleading, without irony as cover, and without a single dynamic lift to prove it. Score high when the tenderness is in the **content** and the delivery never argues for it; score low the moment the voice gets louder, closer, breathier or more vibrato-laden to signal feeling. **Automatic REPAIR:** a bigger final refrain.
*Flairs: #6 Flat Delivery, Changed Room · #14 Tender Power · #3 Amplified Quiet.*

### F6 · SHE SINGS FROM AFTER AND NEVER MENTIONS IT — **weight 8**
Latency is a **tense**, not a topic. Score high when named things carry an unremarked mark of having already been used — a handle warm on one side, a ring on a sill, wool that still smells of something — and the lyric never once discusses evidence, traces, memory, aftermath, or time passing. **Automatic REPAIR:** any line containing "I remember", "what's left", "used to", "back then", "all that's gone".

### F7 · A STRANGER RETELLS IT AFTER ONE PASS — **weight 5**
Scene and subject both recoverable in one sentence after one listen, with the subject appearing plainly at least once, and at most ONE numeric fact sung — at the hinge, **answered rather than recited**, spelled in words. Say plainly what is happening to you; never say what shape it is making. **Automatic REPAIR:** an unnameable subject (`REPAIR — FOG`); a recited data point.

## SELF-EVALUATION (step 06 §2)

- **Do these facets discriminate?** Yes — F1, F2 and F4 each carry an explicit automatic-REPAIR clause that a plausible-sounding draft can actually fail. F4's clause ("a beautiful reach") is aimed squarely at the artist's own declared failure mode, on the record at step 03.
- **Are they weighted honestly?** F4 carries 20 and F1 carries 22 because the operation and the seam are the two things this pair can lose while still sounding finished. F7 carries only 5 not because legibility is optional but because it is cheap to satisfy and expensive to fake.
- **What they deliberately do NOT score:** loudness, build, climax, catchiness of a lift, "emotional payoff". Scoring any of those would import the accessible arm's rubric into a flat-register pair and reward the exact defect Ruling 2 exists to prevent.
- **One risk this rubric carries:** F1 rewards precise depth notation, and notation is cheap. A variation could score well on F1 with an immaculate Disc_Channel block and still render as an undifferentiated wash. F4 is the counterweight — the reach is the one moment the depth scheme becomes an *event* — which is why F4 is scored hardest and why steps 09 and 10 must check F1 and F4 together, never separately.
