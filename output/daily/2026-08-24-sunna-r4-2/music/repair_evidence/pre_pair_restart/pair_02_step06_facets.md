# Pair 02 Facets — Make It Nine

### Facet 1 — Competitive flirt clarity

Why it matters: The song must read immediately as Sunna challenging one chosen adult at miniature golf, with desire expressed through the pleasure of meeting an equal rather than securing compliance.

Failure mode: The wager becomes generic romance, dominance fantasy, a consent explanation, or a narrator describing two other people.

### Facet 2 — Scorecard-round architecture

Why it matters: Written scores, the blue ball, and compact Round One and Round Two exchanges make the scorecard the song's playable form; Round One establishes “make it nine” as an unconfirmed dare and the final round confirms it once.

Failure mode: The game becomes scenic decoration, the rounds cannot be distinguished, or the exact title repeats as a redundant chorus block.

### Facet 3 — Counter-time answer hook

Why it matters: The chosen adult must alter Sunna's wager with the short sung answer “Nine-oh-five / Your turn,” keeping the score nine distinct from the counter-time nine-oh-five and proving reciprocal nerve through action.

Failure mode: Nine becomes an appointment pun, the adult merely accepts, the hook grows into an explanation, or score and clock meanings blur.

### Facet 4 — Blade-cut rhythmic debt

Why it matters: The moving windmill blade must interrupt the long-A hook in the sung lyric and remove exactly one beat once, with dry rocksteady organ occupying the exposed seam.

Failure mode: The cut exists only in production commentary, becomes a long stop or repeated gimmick, or removes no perceptible beat from the form.

### Facet 5 — Two-roll restoration

Why it matters: Two adjacent, mutual blue-ball rolls must restore the full bar after the cut, so the second adult repairs the missing pulse with Sunna rather than watching her finish alone.

Failure mode: One ball restores the rhythm, percussion returns arbitrarily, or the repair lacks two adult hands and therefore loses reciprocity.

### Facet 6 — Mythic pressure returns to hands

Why it matters: An ordinary blue ball may enlarge in sung action into a cut blue world or planet, but after the paired rolls it must contract to ordinary balls in two laughing hands at the scorecard.

Failure mode: The lyric explains its symbolism, stays cosmic through the ending, substitutes abstract fate for touch, or never brings the image back to hands.

### Facet 7 — Sunna vocal and emotional fingerprint

Why it matters: A late-twenties-to-early-thirties adult woman with a light Scandinavian English accent, bright conversational chest-led mezzo, crisp consonant attacks, and a smiling command-catch must sound glossy, shameless, attentive, and delighted by the other adult's nerve.

Failure mode: A childlike, breathy, coy, generic, or male lead drains the dare of adult agency; solemnity or moral instruction turns the flirt song into a thesis.

### Facet 8 — One-world production and receipt

Why it matters: Glossy 105 BPM dembow-pop stays dominant through elastic rounded synth bass doubled by electric bass, dry kick and snare, rim, guiro, muted guitar, and candy claps; dry rocksteady organ is the sole seam, while the same three-note bass pickup and two-note organ answer bracket the vocal as a receipt.

Failure mode: Genre collision, acoustic-kit drift, dub fog, festival build language, or an unrelated outro obscures the blade mechanism and breaks the pre-vocal/post-vocal return.
