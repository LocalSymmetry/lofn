### Song Guide 1 — V01 Season the Open Side

Build a concrete object-chain cumulative call-response: Sunna cuts the lime, opens the chair, and leaves its second side empty; each return adds the chosen adult’s next seasoning action. Start with an ascending five-note walking electric-bass pickup that turns down to a held second, answered by two dry right-side guitar strokes, and return that exact contour after the voice. Keep 102 BPM rocksteady dominant—centered melodic bass, late rim-click, shallow organ bubble, sparse brass—and let the rim-click disappear when the adult takes a full lower-alto `Salt too?` phrase. Sunna’s `Yes—right there` sends the shaker across hands and triggers the sole dry hand-dembow entry. Enlarge green lime and white salt once into a sung tide line, then return to citrus sting on her thumb and palm-to-palm passing. The final title is earned only after the open side has become shared work, never by confirming a rendezvous.

### Song Guide 2 — V02 Green First, White After

Make color order the accumulating grammar: Sunna calls green, the other adult eventually answers white, and later rounds combine their actions rather than replaying the first call. Open with a descending five-note bass walk that rebounds upward and holds the second, plus two dry left-side guitar strokes; repeat the same figure after the last lyric. The rocksteady pocket stays spare and glossy while bass remains exact and rim-click drifts late. Give Sunna a round, bright chest-mezzo and question-ending drop-offs; the adult’s lower melody must own `Salt too?` over the missing rim. The shaker transfer admits one hand-played dembow pattern and no other intrusion. Sing a green-and-white tide line only after both colors have owners, then collapse it into lime bite, salt grains, thumbs, and exchanged hands. Close on the new title as a seasoning order, not a day/time hook.

### Song Guide 3 — V03 Shake It Into Mine

Construct a hand-to-hand action round whose verbs accumulate: cut, hold, shake, squeeze, taste, pass. Begin with a zigzag five-note walking-bass pickup confined to a fourth and ending on a held second, answered by two center-left guitar upstrokes; the identical zigzag is the post-vocal receipt. Sunna’s full-chest lead should use long-I glides and breath-held caesuras while remaining hot, direct, and amused. The second adult changes owner and melody on `Salt too?`; Sunna answers after one beat, lowers her palm, and only shaker contact unlocks the hand-dembow figure. Let every post-seam call contain one action from each adult so reciprocity rewrites the round. Magnify their green and white trail into one tide line, then make citrus sting Sunna’s thumb as the shaker returns to her. The title must refer to seasoning motion, never a meeting.

### Song Guide 4 — V04 The Lime Comes Back

Use tasting revisions as the cumulative form: Sunna offers sharp, the chosen adult proposes salt, Sunna asks for one more shake, and each response changes the next refrain. The HUM figure is a syncopated five-note bass climb with a dropped fourth note and a held second, answered by two narrow guitar strokes; name it before the first line and after the last. Maintain lacquer-clean rocksteady with late cross-stick, compact organ, and two-note brass punctuation. Sunna’s lacquer-bright mezzo uses offbeat pickup bites and clean final-word cutoffs; a dry adult alto owns `Salt too?`, producing an unmistakable vocal handover. Her shaker-to-palm transfer triggers the only hand-dembow seam. The green-and-white tide line should appear as the largest sung image, then return through citrus sting, thumb, shaker, and hands. The exact final title means the lime has returned through shared seasoning, not that a date is scheduled.
