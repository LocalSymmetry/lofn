# Blind Alternative-Gold Tier-2 QA — Stage 1

Judged only from the anonymized packages, as songs intended to remain compelling beyond a first clever impression. Rankings balance hook and return, lyrical precision and singability, emotional movement and stakes, central image or event, musical identity and renderability, and whether the form changes or deepens meaning.

## Set 01 — C > A > B

1. **C — “Triple Arch Over Me.”** The triple arch is an immediate, uncommon image, and the repeated title can carry a genuinely panoramic chorus. The song moves from stunned smallness through mortality to the earned resolution, “I am not the center, I am included”; that transformed return gives the form real purpose. Cosmic-pop, pulse, cello and widening choir belong to one legible sonic world. A few lines are over-written or hard to sing—especially “auroral math and magnet shame” and the full 510-kilometres-per-second datum—but the emotional architecture survives them.
2. **A — “Friday Looks Good on Me.”** The four-note guitar/fork receipt, melodic bass and exact title make this highly renderable, and the empty seat/two-forks action turns the last chorus from wish into confirmation. It has appetite, forward motion and a clean pop return. “Daring dare,” “pie machine,” and several mechanically narrated actions lower the lyrical finish, so the song occasionally feels designed rather than lived.
3. **B — “Look Again, The World Is Lit.”** Sink water, wet spoon and copper pipe offer a strong intimate lens, and the title is easy to retain. The lyric then repeatedly explains its awe, expands into generalized choirs/cosmos/hymns, and accumulates self-conscious phrases faster than melody can naturalize them. The bio-synth identity is promising, but pseudo-technical production language and a long, thesis-like form blur the original kitchen-light discovery.

## Set 02 — A > C > B

1. **A — “Triple Arch Over Me.”** A durable central spectacle, a chorus built for scale, and the strongest emotional turn in the set: insignificance becomes inclusion rather than reassurance. The final chorus materially answers the first. Its densest scientific and poetic lines need ruthless melodic handling, but the song has enough image, hook and arc to justify the risk.
2. **C — “Beat My Score.”** The bass/organ receipt and mini-golf wager make a specific, playable pop world; “Beat my score / pick our round” is a natural recurring challenge whose meaning grows into mutual pursuit. The adult answer gives the flirtation stakes and agency. Score, hole and time coordinates become confusing, while “two blue balls” and the paired-roll passage are over-literal; those moments keep a very good concept from feeling fully inevitable.
3. **B — “Look Again, The World Is Lit.”** The title and sink-water premise are warm and accessible, with real lift in “the room did not grow larger / I only grew less armored.” Too many later lines restate the thesis in inspirational abstractions, and several prose-length observations resist singing. The final expansion dilutes rather than intensifies the original object.

## Set 03 — C > A > B

1. **C — “Triple Arch Over Me.”** The title return is simple enough to hold the song’s otherwise ambitious diction, and the bridge-to-final-chorus change delivers actual emotional movement. Its alpine/cosmic sonic identity is coherent and distinctive. Some ornate phrases call attention to writing technique, but the event and existential stakes remain clear.
2. **A — “Turn When I Lower.”** The zipper-to-guitar motif, stop/go groove and answered commands create an unusually exact soul-pop performance grammar. Obedience to the raised palm establishes trust, and the lowered hand makes the rhythmic restart mean something rather than serving as a decorative break. The lyric becomes mechanistic in “tooth” and “ladder” repetitions, and the title arrives too late and reads less naturally than the central “Up means stop / Down means go” hook.
3. **B — “Look Again, The World Is Lit.”** It contains memorable domestic images and an instantly understandable refrain, but the language repeatedly shifts from observed wonder to declared moral. The long sequence of intro, verse, pre-chorus, post-chorus, bridge and breakdown does not create a comparably strong change in the singer’s situation.

## Set 04 — C > A > B

1. **C — “Triple Arch Over Me.”** The visual event, musical scale and existential question reinforce one another; the final “included” return pays off both the hook and the bridge. Even with several crowded lines, it offers the clearest combination of identity, stakes and formal resolution here.
2. **A — “Look Again, The World Is Lit.”** The song’s best move is perceptual and personal: dread and armor loosen because an ordinary room is seen differently. Its refrain is more durable than its surrounding slogans. Generalized “choir/cosmos/hymn” language, production jargon and the extended structure make the piece less precise as it grows.
3. **B — “Shake It Into Mine.”** Dry rocksteady bass, shaker contact and reciprocal call-and-response could produce an infectious record, and the seasoning verbs give performers concrete rhythmic material. On the page, however, actions such as “I cut, you clasp” and “pass it / pocket it / spin it” are hard to visualize, the tide line is under-motivated, and the emotional stakes live more in section labels than lyric. The title is distinctive but not an effortless sung phrase.

## Set 05 — A > C > B

1. **A — “Triple Arch Over Me.”** It has the set’s most memorable event and strongest meaning-changing return. The sequence from awe, through the fear of dispensability, to chosen brilliance and inclusion gives grandeur a human cost. Prosodic congestion is the main risk, not lack of song identity.
2. **C — “Pretty Busy Tomorrow.”** The photo-strip rondeau is formally intelligent: tearing the strip, allowing the third frame to be chosen, and returning to the opening refrain all alter who controls the image. Harpsichord sunshine-pop and the five-note receipt are vivid and renderable, while “Pick the picture you keep / I pick what happens next” lands cleanly. The title remains oddly oblique, and repeated descriptions of frames and hands sometimes explain the mechanism instead of releasing the feeling.
3. **B — “Look Again, The World Is Lit.”** The hook and kitchen-scale imagery give it genuine appeal, but the chorus states its conclusion before the long form has earned further escalation. Later cosmic and devotional generalities flatten the specificity that made the opening persuasive.

## Set 06 — B > C > A

1. **B — “Hot Girl Future Tense.”** The question-to-declaration design is the set’s sharpest use of form: the missing downbeats, pen-touch hinge and signed names physically change the title’s punctuation and emotional status. The glam-funk palette is clear, the title is audacious and chantable, and the singer risks a direct desire that receives an equally concrete answer. Repeated “pink” diction and a few obscure details (“white stack,” “Thursday, pick the dress”) could date or clutter it, but the central pop idea is unusually complete.
2. **C — “Triple Arch Over Me.”** A powerful near-equal: its chorus, alpine image and smallness-to-inclusion arc have more timeless emotional reach, and the final return deepens rather than merely enlarges. It loses the top slot narrowly because several conspicuously literary/scientific lines are less singable and less economical than B’s central mechanism.
3. **A — “Look Again, The World Is Lit.”** Its domestic premise and title remain appealing, but too much of the lyric names awe, miracle, mercy and cosmos instead of continuing to discover them through the sink. The large number of sections and abstract production terms add complexity without an equally decisive formal hinge.

## Six-set summary

| Set | Best → worst | Decisive advantage of winner |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **C > A > B** | C turns a singular sky event into an earned inclusion refrain. |
| 02 | **A > C > B** | A has the fullest emotional arc and most durable image-hook union. |
| 03 | **C > A > B** | C’s final return resolves the song’s existential stakes. |
| 04 | **C > A > B** | C most completely aligns event, sound world and formal payoff. |
| 05 | **A > C > B** | A’s chorus transformation carries more emotional weight than the competing mechanisms. |
| 06 | **B > C > A** | B makes punctuation, arrangement and mutual choice enact the hook. |

## Near-ties

- **Set 01, C/A:** C wins on emotional reach and lyrical afterlife; A is at least as immediately recordable and hook-forward.
- **Set 02, A/C:** C could overtake with an exceptional groove-led render, but A is the stronger song on the page.
- **Set 04, A/B:** A has deeper interior movement; B has tighter performance notation and rhythmic identity. Neither is fully polished lyrically.
- **Set 05, C/B for second:** C’s form and agency narrowly outweigh B’s more immediately legible refrain.
- **Set 06, B/C:** the closest winner decision. B has the cleaner song-form invention; C has the broader emotional durability.
