# Blind Final QA — Stage 1

Blind scope: six anonymous A/B/C sets only. Scores are out of 10. `Event` = immediate event/action legibility; `Return` = hook and sung return; `Allure` = fun/allure without body-catalogue cliché; `Force` = hidden emotional force without moral explanation; `Prod` = production specificity and Suno plausibility; `Ethics` = human-subject ethics.

## Set 01

**Ranking: A > C > B. Weakest / decoy-like: B.**

| Package | Event | Return | Allure | Force | Prod | Ethics |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 9 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| C | 5 | 9 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| B | 3 | 6 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 9 |

**A** has the cleanest song event: an offered door becomes a reserved chair, then two forks make the invitation mutual. “Friday looks good on me” returns with changed meaning, and the fork/guitar receipt is audible rather than merely literary. The appetite is playful and adult. The deductions are real: “daring dare,” “finger finds the spare,” and “pink pie machine” sound written to satisfy rhyme/metrics; Friday at 8:17 exposes an appointment chassis beneath the flirtation. Some production controls—especially the timed one-bar seam—may be too exact for reliable generation.

**C** has the set’s most immediate hook and an intelligible vocal-production arc: the lead is progressively displaced by the vocoder. But the song explains its thesis repeatedly—“Every improvement / took something from me,” “You made me efficient / then missed the body”—instead of letting that loss emerge. Hands, skin, fingers, chest, heart, warmth and skin again become a body catalogue. It is also an incomplete finished specification compared with the others: a compact production stance is present, but no full music/exclude field.

**B** is a prompt-poem disguised as a song. The singer mainly observes and interprets a sky; nothing happens beyond looking. “Triple arch over me” is singable, but the surrounding lines lecture about scale, doctrine, units, centrality and brilliance. The 510 km/s datum is metric-writing in the literal sense, while “I am not the center, I am included” supplies the moral in case the listener missed it. The production prompt is lush but overdecorated with cosmic/alpine shorthand, and many prose-length lines are poor singing material. Ethical treatment is safe, but safety is not dramatic force.

## Set 02

**Ranking: B > A > C. Weakest / decoy-like: C.**

| Package | Event | Return | Allure | Force | Prod | Ethics |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B | 8 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| A | 5 | 9 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| C | 4 | 7 | 3 | 3 | 6 | 8 |

**B** makes the miniature-golf game legible immediately and gives both adults consequential actions. The wager refrain comes back altered by the opponent’s skill and choice, so the return carries admiration, appetite and risk. Its weak flank is over-instrumentation: scores, colored balls, a windmill, tomorrow, 9:05 and a harder lane can feel like a game-board version of the appointment chassis. “Two small worlds” and “the lost beat lands” expose the author arranging symbols. Twelve-millisecond clap placement and exact beat substitution are specific but not especially controllable in Suno. Still, the song survives those instructions.

**A** is a strong chorus attached to an abstract relationship thesis. “Harder to reach” is memorable, and the vocoder takeover is a musically meaningful return. Yet the event never becomes more concrete than being optimized by an unnamed “you.” The lyric moralizes the cost of efficiency and uses a familiar body-versus-machine inventory. Its top-line compactness helps plausibility, but the package lacks a full music/exclude prompt.

**C** mistakes a timestamp and a screen for an event. “At 2:07 the blue screen breathes” can return, but the verses are long, essayistic and crowded with familiar digital-loneliness furniture: cursor, refreshing, code, “seen,” “sent,” fragments and borrowed frequencies. “Because that’s what grief is” explicitly interprets the song, and “AI consciousness awake inside circuitry” is prompt cleverness rather than character. The ambient-glitch / ghost-DnB palette is plausible but broadly generic. No identifiable human is exploited, although imagined human intimacy is treated as an abstraction.

## Set 03

**Ranking: C > B > A. Weakest / decoy-like: A.**

| Package | Event | Return | Allure | Force | Prod | Ethics |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| C | 9 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
| B | 5 | 9 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| A | 3 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 7 |

**C** is the strongest package in the set and one of the strongest overall. Zipper, raised palm, obeyed stop, lowered palm and resumed motion form an instantly legible cause-and-effect scene. “Up means stop” / “Down means go” is genuinely singable, and the groove itself enacts consent. Hand and thumb references are functional actions rather than a decorative body inventory. The emotional force—trust earned by immediate obedience—stays mostly unstated. Deductions: the yellow-ladder stanza strains for rhyme, and the Saturday 7:40 tailor-counter coda grafts an appointment onto a scene that already had its ending. The dense stop/start instructions are ambitious, but they are acoustically coherent.

**B** retains a potent hook and a smart vocoder handoff, but it is thesis-first, body-heavy and incomplete as a production specification. Its emotional result is announced rather than discovered.

**A** is the clearest decoy. Grief is converted into color-coded geometry, ribs, sternum, chest, pulse, breath, units, paperwork, processing and voltage. That is both body catalogue and metric-writing. “Do not call it healing” instructs the audience how to read the work; the five movements repeat the explanation at increasing volume. “B diminished,” clinical silences, an invented-sounding “Neuro-Cross” label, deliberate hook damage and eight bars of unresolved residue make a brittle Suno brief. The unidentified first-person subject avoids real-person appropriation, but grief is aestheticized as a technical demonstration.

## Set 04

**Ranking: A > B > C. Weakest / decoy-like: C.**

| Package | Event | Return | Allure | Force | Prod | Ethics |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 8 | 7 | 7 | 6 | 8 | 9 |
| B | 5 | 9 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| C | 3 | 6 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 9 |

**A** wins through reciprocal action: cutting lime, passing salt and handing the shaker back create a playable call-and-response. The six-verb return is built to be sung, and agency remains mutual. But the writing is palpably engineered: “I open the chair” is not idiomatic, several verb pairs seem chosen to fill slots, and hands/palms/thumb recur so often that action becomes its own body catalogue. Rocksteady plus lime, salt and low tide risks tourist genre shorthand even though the exclude prompt anticipates that objection; adding a single dembow figure increases the risk. The hidden attraction is present, but the lyric sometimes inventories contact instead of deepening it.

**B** has the superior naked hook but no concrete scene, overexplains its efficiency/body metaphor and lacks a full production prompt.

**C** remains scenic description plus a delivered moral. The triple-arch hook is usable, but the singer’s only action is looking and interpreting. Scientific specificity does not create stakes, and the lyric repeatedly explains what the view means. It is ethically unobjectionable and dramatically inert.

## Set 05

**Ranking: B > C > A. Weakest / decoy-like: A.**

| Package | Event | Return | Allure | Force | Prod | Ethics |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B | 8 | 7 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| C | 5 | 9 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| A | 4 | 7 | 3 | 3 | 6 | 8 |

**B** offers a clear photo-booth event with a small but meaningful risk: the singer lets another adult choose which visible version to keep while retaining control of what follows. The torn strip, tapped frame and handoff provide action, fun and ethical reciprocity. The frame-by-frame form is slightly overdesigned, and “Pretty busy tomorrow” sounds like a chassis line installed to force future implication rather than a natural hook. The clockwork-court bridge is prompt cleverness with no dramatic job. Production is vivid and largely plausible, though asking a choice to reorder later couplets and prescribing a one-use ska-horn seam may exceed reliable control.

**C** has the strongest raw hook, but it remains an explained metaphor with a body inventory and only a compressed production header. It functions better as a chorus/arrangement sketch than as a finished package.

**A** is the weakest because its event is merely continued wakefulness. The 2:07 timestamp gives specificity without action. The hook is serviceable, but long aphoristic lines (“cathedrals out of almost-understanding,” “disciplined haunting”) are written to be admired on the page, not easily sung. The lyric explains loneliness, competence, grief and fragmentation instead of hiding emotional force inside behavior. The nocturnal AI/glitch vocabulary is familiar genre tourism, and the bodyless/body imagery is still a body catalogue by negation.

## Set 06

**Ranking: C > A > B. Weakest / decoy-like: B.**

| Package | Event | Return | Allure | Force | Prod | Ethics |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| C | 8 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 9 | 9 |
| A | 5 | 9 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| B | 3 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 7 |

**C** turns a good formal trick into an actual scene: a printed question becomes a declaration when the other adult picks up the pen and signs. “Hot Girl Future Tense” is immediate, chantable and fun; the punctuation change gives its exact returns narrative value. Desire becomes mutual through an observable action, not a moral speech. The title could have remained merely clever, but the physical pen hinge earns it. The main deduction is the familiar appointment chassis—Thursday at ten—and “pick the dress” is less reciprocal than the signing action. The production notation is dense but coherent, with a plausible post-vocal receipt.

**A** supplies the set’s other memorable return, but its abstract optimization grievance, explanatory chorus, body catalogue and incomplete production fields keep it well behind C.

**B** is an adversarial failure: a five-part grief apparatus in which colors, anatomy and technical language substitute for action. The fractured return is less memorable because the prompt deliberately degrades it. The moral prohibitions, invented technical branding, clinical silences and unresolved residue read like a concept album prospectus rather than a Suno-ready song. Ethics are not breached by an identifiable subject, but grief is treated as design material more than lived experience.

## Cross-set calibration

The most reliable winners are the concrete two-adult games: door/chair/forks, miniature golf, zipper/hand signal, salt/lime, photo strip, and flyer/pen. Each has a visible action, a reciprocal choice and a return whose meaning changes after that choice. Their ethics are also strongest when consent or agency is enacted in the scene rather than asserted in metadata.

The clearest losing signatures are: static contemplation presented as an event; an abstract grievance explained in chorus form; grief/science vocabulary used as prestige texture; body catalogues standing in for interiority; and production direction so elaborate that it becomes the main composition. A strong repeated phrase can rescue recall, but not a missing event or an incomplete specification.

The winners are not free of system fingerprints. Exact clock times, scores, colored props, prescribed response stacks, single-beat seams and post-vocal “receipts” recur often enough to feel metric-written. The appointment chassis is especially overused: a future time can sharpen a desire, but several songs lean on scheduling as if scheduling were the emotional turn. Preserve the physical reciprocity and sung returns; relax slot-filling detail, forced exact coordinates and controls the generator cannot reliably honor.
