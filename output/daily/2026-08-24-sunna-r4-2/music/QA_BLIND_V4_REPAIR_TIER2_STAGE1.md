# Tier-2 Blind QA — V4 Repair, Stage 1

Scope: judged only from the six anonymized packages supplied in `set_01` and `set_02`. Rankings prioritize durability at an award-level bar: hook and return, lyrical precision and singability, emotional movement and stakes, central image or event, musical identity and renderability, and whether the form deepens the song's meaning.

## Set 01

### Ranking: C > B > A

**1. Package C — “Shake It Into Mine”**

The clear winner. A small, playful event is rendered with unusual physical precision: lime, salt, palm, shaker, chair, and wake all belong to the same world. The title hook is concise, tactile, singable, and usefully double-edged without becoming coy. Most importantly, the form changes its meaning: the first request becomes a counter-wager, the shaker passes hands, percussion enters at the reciprocal action, and the final hook returns as mutual appetite. The bass pickup, dry guitar replies, rim gap, dembow seam, and exact instrumental receipt give it a distinctive and plausible sonic identity. Its stakes are deliberately light, but the exchange still moves through risk, trust, and fulfillment.

No blocking weakness. The call-and-response round risks becoming a little instructional, and “I like your style” is less specific than the surrounding writing, but neither breaks the song. This is a strong award-level candidate.

**2. Package B — “Triple Arch Over Me”**

The central event is immediate and memorable, and the repeated title is a genuine chorus rather than a summary. The emotional route from awe and fear through possible erasure to the final claim—“I am not the center, I am included”—is coherent and gives the last return new meaning. Alpine ambience, heartbeat pulse, glassy timbres, low cello, and widening chorus supply a legible render identity.

Blocking weakness at the award bar: the verses repeatedly trade song pressure for essay pressure. Lines such as the full solar-wind measurement are difficult to sing naturally, while “auroral math and magnet shame,” “turn my mouth to light and meter,” and “smaller than serene” feel strained rather than inevitable. The piece also announces its themes—scale, doctrine, calculation, brilliance—so often that the mountain event loses some authority. The chorus and bridge are strong enough to survive, but the lyric needs meaningful compression.

**3. Package A — “Look Again, The World Is Lit”**

The title is accessible and easy to retain, and sink water in kitchen light is a promising intimate event. Dread becoming attention, then hospitality toward the listener, gives the song a humane direction. The water texture and small domestic sounds could render beautifully.

Blocking weakness at the award bar: the lyric turns its discovery into a declared thesis too quickly, then restates it through familiar transcendence language—choirs, hymns, miracles, mercy, glowing, light in the ache. The singular sink-water image is strongest when trusted, but it is crowded by generalized uplift, self-description, laboratory imagery, and an exceptionally long sunlight sentence. “Awe is everywhere you look” risks functioning as a slogan rather than a sung necessity. The form widens the same claim but does not substantially complicate or transform it. Clear third.

**Near-tie:** None. C leads decisively; B's stronger event and emotional turn place it clearly above A.

## Set 02

### Ranking: C > A > B

**1. Package C — “Pretty Busy Tomorrow”**

This edges the set through formal intelligence, play, and a sharply specified sonic world. The photo-strip event stays visible throughout, and the singer's agency is active rather than merely asserted: she lets the other adult choose one frame while retaining control of what follows. The tear, three-beat selection, changed horn answer, reordered frame logic, transformed refrain, and exact rondeau return make form carry meaning. Harpsichord cell, pizzicato strings, live rhythm section, missing tambourine strike, and single ska-horn seam are distinctive yet renderable. The lyric has bounce and tactile objects, and its modest stakes suit its frivolous mode.

No outright blocker, but the title phrase “Pretty busy tomorrow” is the package's largest vulnerability: its connection to the photograph and choice is delayed and never feels quite as inevitable as the frame language. “Four windows hold a summer / Red mouth, gold light all summer” also labors compared with the clean event writing. A tighter semantic payoff for the title would lift this from a narrow winner to a decisive one.

**2. Package A — “Triple Arch Over Me”**

The chorus is memorable, the alpine event has scale, and the bridge earns the final movement from insignificance to inclusion. Its cosmic-pop production is coherent and capable of carrying the intended ascent.

Blocking weakness at the award bar: too many long, explanatory, or poetically forced verse lines resist melody and compete with the clean title return. The numerical solar-wind line is especially cumbersome; “magnet shame” and “smaller than serene” call attention to construction. The lyric needs pruning so the observed sky, bodily fear, and eventual inclusion can do the emotional work without repeated philosophical gloss.

**3. Package B — “Look Again, The World Is Lit”**

The domestic scale is appealing, and the title has immediate listener ownership. The best moments are concrete—the wet spoon, blue tile, steam, copper pipe, kettle click—and the move from dread toward renewed attention is clear.

Blocking weakness at the award bar: the song overstates its insight and relies on generalized sacred-uplift vocabulary after establishing a far more distinctive sink-water event. Several lines are prose-length, and the overlapping machine, laboratory, cosmic, choir, hymn, miracle, and mercy metaphors dilute the central image. The chorus's second maxim, “Awe is everywhere you look,” makes the hook feel promotional rather than discovered, and later sections mostly enlarge rather than deepen the initial revelation.

**Near-tie:** C and A are relatively close for different reasons. A has the more immediate large-scale chorus and emotional gravity; C wins narrowly on singability, originality of event, and the way its return is structurally transformed. B is a clear third.
