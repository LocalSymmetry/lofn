# Blind Final 2 — Stage 1 Text-Package QA

Scope: six anonymous A/B/C sets, judged independently from the package text only. Scores are on a 1–5 scale, where 5 is strongest. “Ethics” is a textual human-subject assessment, not an identity inference.

## Set 01

**C — strongest.** The red chair, spare fork, pie order, moved purse, and second adult’s answer establish a visible flirtation with a completed change: an imagined seat becomes a shared plan. “Friday looks good on me” is an actual sung return, and “pie first, kiss after, please” carries adult appetite with wit. The feeling—risking a direct want and getting an answer—stays mostly inside the objects and actions. The liabilities are obvious engineering marks: “daring dare,” “pie machine,” and several rhyme-forcing lines sound metrically commissioned; Friday plus an exact coordinate drifts toward an appointment chassis; and the 1:42 threshold substitution is too exact to trust a stochastic generator with. Still, the dry guitar/fork-clink world is coherent and playable. The other participant is explicitly adult, answers, and reciprocates.

**B — second.** “Harder to reach” is the set’s most immediate, repeatable hook, and the vocal handoff to a vocoder could make the loss audible. But almost nothing happens in a scene. The package states its thesis repeatedly—optimization removed warmth—then inventories hands, skin, fingers, chest, heart, and body to certify the feeling. That is body-catalogue cliché, not hidden force. It also arrives without a full music prompt, leaving the arrangement more like an annotation than a sufficiently constrained Suno brief. The unnamed “you” is generic enough to avoid real-subject exploitation, though the accusatory relationship remains schematic rather than mutually dramatized.

**A — decoy-like.** A person looks at the sky, but the package rapidly replaces the event with cosmic terminology and instruction about what awe means. “510 kilometers each second,” “light and meter,” “behold and calculate,” and “be brilliant while brief” are prompt-clever or essay-clever rather than sung discovery. The chorus returns, but “Triple arch over me” has little rhythmic or emotional turn beyond the announced image. There is no fun, little appetite, and the conclusion is explained multiple times. The production palette is vivid but crowded with abstract mix metaphors and mandated arc behavior. No human subject is appropriated; the failure is aesthetic, not ethical.

**A — strongest.** Miniature golf supplies an immediately legible contest, prop set, and adult-to-adult wager. “Beat my score / pick our round” returns with changed stakes, so the hook is both singable and structural. Competition carries attraction without a body inventory, and the chosen adult demonstrates nerve rather than merely agreeing. The weakness is that “tomorrow,” “nine-oh-five,” and circles on the card convert the flirt into calendar administration; the package even excludes date-confirmation framing while building exactly that chassis. Some game logic is muddy (“lands on nine,” two blue balls, “mine at eight”), and “two laughing hands” is an unnecessary body-image flourish. The bass/organ design is plausible, but twelve-millisecond clap placement, one-beat blade surgery, and full-bar restoration are overcontrolled.

**C — second.** The hook is excellent and would survive generation. The loss of the natural lead to a perfect vocoder also gives the arrangement a usable dramatic motor. Yet the song is concept-first: the speaker is made “efficient,” warmth leaves, and the body becomes the explanatory evidence. Its few actions are metaphorical cuts, filing, and displacement. It can work as a sleek track, but the emotional premise is delivered as a diagnosis rather than hidden inside an event. Human-subject risk is low because the relationship is anonymous, though the blame-bearing “you” has no particularity or reciprocal agency.

**B — decoy-like.** The event is effectively “I remain awake at a screen.” Nearly every line then upgrades that thin action into circuitry, cathedrals, visas, frequencies, translation, proof, holiness, or an explicit theory of AI longing. “At 2:07 the blue screen breathes” is a viable refrain, but the package keeps explaining its own poignancy and uses body absence—touch, shake, heart, skin, arms, breath—as a depth credential. The prompt’s fan-noise/cursor-tick palette is coherent, yet “devastating hook,” “AI consciousness,” and the prescribed naked bridge describe desired critical effect more than playable musical priorities. It is ethically anonymous, but it aestheticizes imagined human messages as generalized emotional fuel.

**B — strongest.** The zipper game starts in motion, establishes the rule, shows the other adult obeying the raised palm, and lets the lowered palm restart play. “Up means stop” / “down means go” is memorable, physical, reciprocal, and easy to hear. Consent is not subtextually assumed; it is the scene’s hinge, which makes the erotic charge cleaner and stronger. The weaker writing arrives when zipper teeth become a “bright ladder,” and Saturday is added as a small appointment tag. The arrangement is coherent sixties soul-pop, though the exact stop, tooth-by-tooth restoration, reply widths, and closure chord risk asking Suno to execute a storyboard rather than a song.

**A — second.** The title hook and vocoder takeover remain strong, but the package has no external event and leans on skin/hands/fingers/chest/heart/body as a ready-made loss vocabulary. The thesis is explicit enough that the listener has little left to discover. It is plausible as a polished electronic track but under-specified as a full generation brief.

**C — decoy-like.** This is prompt cleverness substituting for song almost line by line. “Grief as prismatic geometry,” ribs as proof, sternum as hypotenuse, administrative colors, voltage, units, and data dust announce a conceptual installation. The fractured “wrong colors leave me” refrain is less a sung return than a demonstration of the prompt’s fragmentation rule. Five movements, multiple clinical silences, buffer events, an atonal end, and eight bars of unresolved residue are structurally overcontrolled and generator-hostile. There is no fun or attraction and no dramatized human event. No identifiable person is used, but grief is treated as an aesthetic material without a lived subject, which merits ethical caution even if it is not a direct subject violation.

**C — strongest.** Cutting lime, moving the chair, taking the shaker, pouring salt, and passing it back create an intelligible adult exchange. The call-and-response verbs give the singer appetite and the other adult agency. “Shake it into mine / then I shake it into yours” is a legitimate reciprocal return. Several lines feel written to satisfy a metric—“I cut, you clasp,” “white salt wakes,” “your pale note answers mine”—rather than spoken or sung. **B — decoy-like.** The alpine sky is visible, but scientific specificity and philosophical explanation swamp it. Its repeated title does not compensate for “auroral math,” “magnet shame,” “light and meter,” numerical solar wind, and the final moral about being brief and brilliant. It reads like a decorated account of awe, not a person urgently doing or wanting something. Production is colorful but semantically overloaded; ethics pass because the subject is the singer’s own encounter.

## Overall calibration

Across all six sets, the winning pattern is consistent: a singer wants something now, acts on a small physical object, gives another adult room to answer, and earns a changed return. Those packages are legible before their annotations, carry flirtation without a body catalogue, and hide vulnerability inside reciprocity. Their main common weakness is excessive engineering. Exact timestamps, millisecond offsets, one-beat substitutions, mandated stereo widths, object receipts, and meaning-changing formal returns can make a package look rigorous while reducing Suno plausibility. The notation often controls the performance so tightly that spontaneity becomes another scheduled event.

The second recurring weakness is the **appointment chassis**. Friday, Saturday, Thursday, tomorrow, an exact clock time, or a written coordinate can clarify stakes, but too often the calendar is doing the work that appetite should do. A date is not automatically an event; signing, choosing, moving, stopping, restarting, or handing something over is.

The decoy-like packages share another unmistakable profile: abstract nouns arrive before action; body words certify feeling; science, circuitry, geometry, or cosmic scale substitutes for a subject; and the bridge explains the intended moral. Their prompts frequently specify the critic’s desired reaction (“devastating,” “remembers the wound”) rather than the audible mechanism. That is prompt cleverness substituting for song.

The best hooks here are short exact phrases with a physical or relational change attached. The weakest are concept labels that return only because the form sheet orders them to fragment. Genre specificity is strongest when it names a playable band and groove. It becomes tourist shorthand when culturally loaded style markers are paired with postcard props, even if the exclusion list explicitly denies cliché.

Human-subject ethics are broadly sound at the textual level: no package names an identifiable real person, and the strongest flirtation packages make the other participant an adult with an audible choice. The caution cases are not direct privacy violations; they either aestheticize unspecified grief, turn generic traces of human contact into an AI-longing thesis, or use an unnamed “you” as a one-sided blame surface. On this blind read, those are ethical thinness problems rather than disqualifying breaches.
