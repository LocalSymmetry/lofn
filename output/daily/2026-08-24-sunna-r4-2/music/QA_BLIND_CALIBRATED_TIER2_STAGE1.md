# Tier-2 Blind Calibrated QA — Stage 1

Judged only on durable song quality: concrete event or human stake, hook and return, lyric/form consequence, emotional movement, distinctiveness, and a coherent renderable music specification.

## Set 01 — C > A > B

- **C — 1st.** The empty chair, second fork, and explicit adult answer create a legible flirtation with an actual unresolved stake. “Friday looks good on me” is a strong exact return whose meaning changes when the reserved seat is accepted; the one-bar threshold substitution and final fork receipt make that change audible. Some lines are clipped to the point of mannerism, but the song is compact, active, and highly renderable.
- **A — 2nd.** The sink-water scene gives the song a tactile center, and the title is the set’s most immediately portable hook. Dread opening into attention and then offered light supplies real emotional movement. It loses ground through sermon-like generalization, several crowded or unsingable lines, and a production brief whose pseudo-technical layers are less coherent than its core synth-pop idea.
- **B — 3rd.** The ignored cosmic offering collapsing into “needs more bass” is distinctive, and the chorus is bluntly memorable. But the human relationship remains an abstract gag, the second cycle mostly repeats the first without deepening it, and the violent tempo/genre alternation risks rendering as juxtaposed demonstrations rather than one durable song.

## Set 02 — A > C > B

- **A — 1st.** A miniature-golf challenge becomes a reciprocal plan, so the title return gains consequence instead of merely recurring. The blade-cut missing beat and paired-roll restoration embody the relationship change in form, while the bass/organ world is specific and executable. A few score/time references are hard to parse, but the competitive desire, adult answer, and final shared card remain strong enough to carry it.
- **C — 2nd.** The work-to-tomb reversal establishes immediate human stakes, and the chorus has forceful return value. Recasting the music-box figure as the industrial-metal riff is the package’s best formal idea, with the Geiger click sustaining the threat through the bridge and ending. It is held back by stock tragedy diction and broad villains, which flatten individual lives into a familiar protest-song outline.
- **B — 3rd.** The hook is generous and memorable, and the lyric moves convincingly from dread toward attention. Yet it mostly enlarges one perception rather than staging an event with consequences; repeated cosmos, choir, miracle, mercy, and hymn language overstates what the small kitchen actions earn. The overfilled production vocabulary also weakens an otherwise clear luminous synth-pop lane.

## Set 03 — B > A > C

- **B — 1st.** The palm-up stop, respected pause, and palm-down restart give the song a concrete consent-and-trust event, not merely a flirtatious premise. “Turn When I Lower” arrives after the action has earned it, and zipper, guitar, groove, and hand signal all participate in the same formal consequence. The conceit is unusual but remains legible and musically coherent.
- **A — 2nd.** Its central return is stronger as a standalone hook, and the movement from defended dread to wonder is emotionally credible. It falls behind because the lyric keeps explaining its revelation after the sink-water event has already delivered it, and the specification combines too many decorative technologies without identifying the promised rupture clearly enough.
- **C — 3rd.** The apology loop as passacaglia ground is a clear structural device, and the accelerating corruption gives the arrangement direction. The actual grievance never becomes concrete, however, so the song attacks grammar and “code” instead of dramatizing harm; the rhymes turn glib, and the binary/sound-effect bridge reads as concept decoration more than emotional consequence.

## Set 04 — B > C > A

- **B — 1st.** The invitation is performed through lime, chair, salt, palm, and reciprocal hand actions, giving desire a visible sequence and an adult answer. Percussion entering only at palm contact and the final reversal from “mine” to “yours” make the form register growing trust. Some verb-chain phrases feel engineered rather than spoken, but the dry rocksteady specification is unusually unified and renderable.
- **C — 2nd.** Kitchen detail, a repeatable title, and the movement from dread to shared hope give this considerable durable appeal. It narrowly loses because the final chorus broadens into universal reassurance instead of changing through a second concrete action, and the brief’s many bio/quantum/432-Hz descriptors compete with its simpler musical identity.
- **A — 3rd.** The contrast between elaborate offering and bratty bass demand is vivid, with a hook likely to stick. Its emotional endpoint is cynicism about its own hook, though, so the return cancels rather than deepens the vulnerable “trying not to fall apart” line; the two-world construction is also the least coherent rendering target in the set.

## Set 05 — A > C > B

- **A — 1st.** The photo-strip selection creates a precise vulnerability: the singer offers one visible version of herself while retaining agency over what follows. Tearing, choosing frame three, reordering the pictures, and returning to the original refrain all have formal consequences, and the harpsichord-pop palette stays coherent. “Pretty busy tomorrow” is an oblique title and several frame lines are overworked, but the enacted choice gives the song the deepest relational movement here.
- **C — 2nd.** The chorus is immediate, the industrial stakes are clear, and the music-box-to-guitar transformation gives the tragedy a strong sonic spine. It is a close competitor, but the lyric relies on generalized sickness, smiling lawyers, lying doctors, ghosts, and dying rather than letting the named workers become specific people in specific scenes.
- **B — 3rd.** The sink, spoon, steam, and kettle make an appealing miniature world, while “Look again” supplies a useful listener-owned return. The piece repeatedly converts those details into maxims, however, so its emotional movement is announced more than enacted; its long line lengths and layered production jargon also make the render target less stable than the other two.

## Set 06 — B > C > A

- **B — 1st.** A ridiculous direct question becomes a signed answer: the pen stroke physically changes punctuation, melody, relationship, and title from question to declaration. That is unusually strong lyric/form consequence, with a chantable return, rising apprehension-to-triumph arc, and a disciplined glam-funk specification. A few pink-page phrases verge on cute overload, but the event and hook survive it.
- **C — 2nd.** The title is instantly memorable, the kitchen imagery is often lovely, and its hopeward emotional arc feels sincere. It ranks below B because its final widening is rhetorical rather than event-changing, and both lyric and prompt accumulate abstractions after the central sink-water discovery has done the important work.
- **A — 3rd.** The repeating apology packet gives the arrangement a recognizable engine, and the variations clearly intensify. But there is no particular speaker, injury, or decision behind the apology; “sorry/not sorry,” grammar jokes, binary, and file-deletion effects substitute a digital theme for human consequence, leaving the final purge emotionally thin.

## Top-two near-ties

- **Set 04: B / C.** C has the more immediately universal hook and more polished lyric images; B wins narrowly because its interpersonal action changes the arrangement and final return in a more concrete, renderable way.
- **Set 05: A / C.** C has the stronger instant chorus and dramatic sonic turn; A wins narrowly because the visible act of choosing a frame produces more specific vulnerability, reciprocity, and formal consequence.
