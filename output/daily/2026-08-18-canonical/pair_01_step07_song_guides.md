# ⭐ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS

*A guide that cannot answer both of these with a place and a stake is not ready to direct anything.
Answered here first, before a single line of any of the four was written.*

## Q1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**On wet sand, below the high-water mark, on a public beach, at hour five of the work, kneeling.**

Precisely: inside a roped square about the size of a small room, on the flat between the last groyne and
the water. Her knees are in it. Her forearms are grey to the elbow. Her right hand holds a **flat blade** —
a plasterer's trowel — and her left is flat on the packed face of a turret roughly chest-high when she
stands. There is a bucket beside her knee with maybe a third of a load of grey sand in it, carried up from
the low water. Behind her, a **rope** and a **queue of children**, littlest at the front. Beyond them the
beach is emptying: deckchairs stacked, windbreaks folding, the ice-cream van gone, somebody's radio walking
away up the track. The sun is hard and low enough that the groyne's shadow has reached her working face.
There is a man with a plate camera on a stick, waiting.

**It is a real place with a floor, a temperature and other people's noise in it. Everything in these four
songs is reachable from where she is kneeling without a metaphor.**

## Q2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**Two things, and only two, and they are different sizes.**

**The stake, small and immediate — THE SEEP.** Water arrives from *underneath* before it arrives across.
A shine appears in the base of a wall. Push a thumb in and the thumb goes through. This is what she is
actually fighting all afternoon and it is what makes her hands move. It is boring, technical, and it is
the reason the song has a body in it at all.

**The stake, large and printed — THE HOUR.** High water is at nine and it says so in the paper. That is
not a threat, a fate, a character or a mood; **it is a timetable.** It cannot be argued with, negotiated
with, addressed, or personified, and this pair will do none of those things. It gets a time and no
adjectives.

**⭐ And the thing that actually hurts, which is neither of those:** in every one of these four songs **she
is the one who breaks something.** The tide never gets a chance to be the villain, because she gets there
first, on purpose, with her own arms, and pays for the part she wants:

| | she gives up | to get |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | the moat she dug at low water | weight under the turret |
| **V2** | the last photographic plate | the children's side in the record |
| **V3** | forty minutes of the whole thing's life | the top of the turret finished |
| **V4** | the turret everyone came back for | the sand to build the stair |

**That is the answer to "what could hurt it here."** Not the sea. **Her own hands, spending a thing she
owns, in public, while a band that has no idea plays the best afternoon of the summer straight.**

# THE STANDING DIRECTION — BINDING ON ALL FOUR

**Bed:** modern power-pop, one bed, held, **124 BPM**, varied by arrangement **density only**. ⛔ No
mid-song gear-change. ⛔ Swagger is never distortion.

**The strut:** melodic electric bass front of mix as the second lead vocal · clean electric rhythm guitar
chopping sixteenths, three notes, used as percussion · drummer ahead of the grid, dry room kit · handclaps
and tambourine dry and dead centre · horns, where used, punctuate and leave inside a bar.

**⭐ THE HOLE:** the bass walks four notes and **drops the third every bar.** Stated alone for two bars
before the first word; plays the record out alone. **This is the hum-test object.**

**The voice:** full chest, breath left in, audible effort, no transparent pitch correction. **Stacked
girl-group thirds on the hook are a required instrument**, plus one counter-melody that argues with the
lead and **one moment where two voices split and the room hears the gap open** (it is written into the
seam in every song).

**The seam, once per song, made of people and instruments otherwise absent:** three answering voices,
castanets, a hand-played floor-tom triplet. ⛔ **It never lands on the wolf line.**

**The register:** the hard line arrives in exactly the voice that sang the funny one. ⛔ No EMO tag in
this pair may instruct a hush, a drop-out or reverence, and none does. The wolf verse in all four is cued
**"band walks straight on"** and carries **no SFX cue**.

**The one-fact rule:** exactly one numeric fact per song, **spelled in words**, at the hinge, **answered**
rather than recited. Four different facts across four songs.

**The radiant:** late, once, plain, **not the title**, **never explained**, no relative as its origin.
The line immediately after a radiant is always an instruction to her own hands — it moves, it does not
gloss.

**Source 2, imported verbatim, case-sensitive, mid-sentence, at least two per song.**

# GUIDE — V1 · **WET TO THE ELBOW** *(angle: HOUR FIVE — deep in it)*

**The job:** the body busy, the tool in the hand, one technical problem solved on stage. This is the song
where the listener learns how sand actually works, and never notices they were taught.

**Where the body is:** kneeling at the base of the turret with a shine coming up through it.

**Density arc:** chant on claps and bass only → verse adds guitar chop and kit → chorus adds thirds and
tambourine → seam swaps in the trio, castanets and the tom → wolf verse **drops back to exactly the verse
density, no change of any kind** → last two choruses stack the counter-melody → outro is the bass figure
alone with the hole open.

**Hook (imperative, plural, physical state, six syllables):** *Get wet to the elbow!*

**Byte-identical chorus, internal rhyme at the third stress (`tight` / `right`):**

> Get wet to the elbow! / Get wet to the elbow! / **Pack it tight, cut it right,** let the whole thing
> show! / Get wet to the elbow!

**The numeric fact:** **nine.** A woman in the queue asks what time she stops. She answers *"Nine. The
water's in at nine,"* the woman says ta and gets back in the line, and the band does not react. **Answered,
not recited.**

**⭐ THE WOLF EVENT AND ITS COST:** the base wants weight; the only packed grey she can reach is **her own
moat bank**. She puts the blade in it, takes the bank down, and buckets her own moat up the tower. **Cost:
an afternoon of finished work, destroyed by her, before the water gets near it.** Where the moat was, there
is a flat bit of beach. ⛔ She never says it was worth it, and nothing in the arrangement moves.

**The seam sits on:** a child asking *"What's the flat one for?"* The trio answers her. **Across it she puts
the blade in the bucket and finishes the corner with her hands** — she gives up her precision instrument
mid-repair and uses her palms.

**The radiant (bridge, line seven):** *I came down here to be busy in front of people.*
Preceded by the honest counterfactual (she could have done it up the far end past the rocks with nobody
watching), followed immediately by *"Right — where's my bucket."* **No gloss.**

**The return (assigned shape — along the beach, never away):** blade in the bucket, bucket on the hip,
**past the flat patch where the moat was**, arms rinsed in the shallow until the grey lets go, and she
walks the low line. The steps up off the beach are behind her and stay behind her.

**Source 2 in the style field:** `brightly-colored production` · `the distant rollerdisco claps` ·
`a two-beer chill`.

**Watch it for:** *"the good part of the beach is the end of the day"* — that sentence is one inch from
**beautiful BECAUSE it ends** and it must be a craft fact about sand, not a moral about time.

# GUIDE — V2 · **COME BACK SANDY** *(angle: THE QUEUE OF CHILDREN)*

**The job:** she lets strangers help, badly, on purpose, and it costs her the record. This is the pair's
warmest song and its sharpest trade.

**Where the body is:** standing at the rope with her hands deliberately behind her back, watching a child
put a shell where a shell should not be.

**Density arc:** chant on claps only → verses add bass and guitar chop → chorus adds thirds, tambourine and
the children's voices in the room mic → seam brings the trio, castanets, tom → wolf verse **identical
density to verse two** → final choruses widen the chant → outro on the bass figure alone.

**Hook (imperative, plural, physical state, four syllables):** *Come back sandy!*

**Byte-identical chorus, internal rhyme at the third stress (`down` / `round` / `ground`):**

> Come back sandy! / Come back sandy! / **Both hands down, dig it round,** get your knees on the ground! /
> Come back sandy!

⛔ **No numeral in this chorus.** A count sung five times is a sung fact, and this song's fact is elsewhere.

**The numeric fact:** **one plate.** The photographer says he has one plate left in the bag — *one plate,
one go, and then that is that.*

**⭐ THE WOLF EVENT AND ITS COST:** her waterside front is the best thing she has made all day. The
children's west side has a doorway that leans and a lolly stick in it for a tree. **She turns the stick
west and puts the last plate on theirs.** She steps out of the frame. **Cost: the record.** *They
commissioned the survival* — and the surviving image is of the worse side, while her good side goes under
with nobody there. ⛔ Said flat, in the joke voice, band straight on.

**The seam sits on:** *"Who wants the flat one?"* — she hands her only precision tool to a child, thumb
instruction and all. **Across it she gives the blade away.**

**The radiant (bridge, line seven):** *I came for the bit where somebody asks if they can help.*
Preceded by the counterfactual (first light, whole beach empty, rope on its own), followed immediately by
*"Right. Both hands on that corner. Shove it, don't pat it"* — which is a **return** of the instruction
from verse one, not a comment on the radiant.

**The return (assigned shape):** she walks the wet **picking up the plastic spades the queue left behind** —
red one, green one, one with a crack — going the way the queue went, bucket on her back.

**Source 2 in the style field:** `the distant rollerdisco claps` · `a two-beer chill`.

**Watch it for:** any trace of irony toward the children's wall. **She genuinely prefers it.** *"Worst-
looking wall on the beach, and I love it"* must be flat sincerity or the whole song collapses into a bit,
and Sunna does not do bits at anyone's expense.

# GUIDE — V3 · **FORTY MINUTES EARLY** *(angle: THE NORTH WALL)*

**The job:** the hour arrives in the middle of a decision, and she chooses the useless beautiful thing over
the useful one, out loud, and does not defend it. This is the pair's hardest gate: the trade must be named
and **not** argued.

**Where the body is:** standing still for the only time in the pair — a bucket in one hand, a north corner
going soft to her left, an unfinished top to her right.

**Density arc:** chant → verses (bass, chop, kit) → chorus (thirds, tambourine, one short horn stab that
answers and leaves) → **wolf verse at plain verse density, nothing added, nothing removed** → chorus →
**seam** → bridge → two choruses stacked → outro, bass alone.

**⛔ STRUCTURAL NOTE, DELIBERATE:** the seam is placed **after a full byte-identical chorus** following the
wolf verse. Left adjacent, three new voices and a tom fill would land on the heels of the trade and the
arrangement would be commenting. **The band does not know. It is kept from knowing by a chorus.**

**Hook (imperative, plural, physical state, four syllables):** *Keep your arms up!*

**Byte-identical chorus, internal rhyme at the third stress (`weight` / `straight`):**

> Keep your arms up! / Keep your arms up! / **Hold the weight, take it straight,** get it over the top! /
> Keep your arms up!

**The numeric fact:** **forty minutes.** The north wall would buy the whole sculpture forty minutes longer.
She repeats it once as an answer — *"Forty minutes. Fine."* — and puts the bucket down at the turret.
**Answered, then paid.**

**⭐ THE WOLF EVENT AND ITS COST:** she abandons the north wall. **Cost: forty minutes of the whole thing's
life, spent on a finial nobody needs.** The wall stops where it stopped, the flat stays flat, the whole
thing goes early, and she says *"I'm alright with that"* in the same voice she used for the radio walking
up the track. ⛔ There is **no line anywhere in this song saying the trade was worth it** — that sentence
would convert two adjacent facts into a moral and repair the seed.

**The seam sits on:** the last of the grey going onto the top. **Across it she lifts it with both arms over
her head** — the trio answers *"Press and stop!"* and her hands are above her.

**The radiant (bridge, line seven):** *I came down for the top of it, not the rest of it.*
Preceded by the sensible version she did not do (wall first, top last, whole thing standing while the front
water passed), followed by *"Right. Sand off the blade. Where's the flat bit?"*

**The return (assigned shape):** blade, bucket and plank on her back, walking the wet with her shadow out
flat, **watching the north side go from further along the beach — and still walking.** She does not stand
and watch, and she does not leave.

**Source 2 in the style field:** `lush, aerodynamic beats that instantly hits cruising altitude` ·
`brightly-colored production` · `a two-beer chill`.

**Watch it for:** the REMARK TEST. This song's structure is the most severe in the pair (a decision stated,
priced and paid inside eight lines) and **none of that may be visible.** If a listener notices the
architecture, bury it.

# GUIDE — V4 · **BIGGER ON TUESDAY** *(angle: THE RETURN — the second one is better)*

**The job:** the seed's actual argument, sung by the person doing it. *They came back Tuesday and did it
again* — and the second one is better because she learned the sand, not because loss taught her anything.

**Where the body is:** knee-deep in a trench she is cutting for a stair, on the same stretch of wet, on a
Tuesday, with a half-built copy of Saturday's turret standing at her shoulder.

**Density arc:** chant → verses → chorus → seam (the six arrive) → **wolf verse, plain, band straight on**
→ chorus → bridge → final two choruses with a choppier drum-pattern flip underneath, same bed, density only
→ outro, bass alone with the hole open.

**Hook (imperative, plural, physical state, four syllables):** *Come back bigger!*

**Byte-identical chorus, internal rhyme at the third stress (`base` / `face`):**

> Come back bigger! / Come back bigger! / **Wider base, longer face,** taller than the one before — /
> Come back bigger!

⛔ **No numeral in this chorus either** — the drafted *"Twice the base, twice the face"* is a fact recited
five times and is cut in step 09.

**The numeric fact:** **six.** Six people came back down midweek. She answers the number with appetite,
not gratitude, and then acts on it.

**⭐ THE WOLF EVENT AND ITS COST:** one of the six asks for the turret again — *that was the good one,
that's why we came.* The sand for the stair **is** the sand in the turret. **She puts her elbow straight
through it.** Shoulder in, top comes down, carry it over. **Cost: the encore** — her best-known work and
the exact thing her audience travelled for, broken by her own arm, in front of them. *Her sets famously end
one song early.* ⛔ Nothing in the arrangement acknowledges it.

**The seam sits on:** the greeting — *"You came back!" / "(We came back!)"* — and she hands the spare
trowels out. **Across it she puts tools in six pairs of hands.**

**The radiant (bridge, line seven):** *I came back because Saturday wasn't enough of it.*
Preceded by the two exits she declined (stop Saturday with a good one; keep the picture and leave it
alone), followed by *"Right — pass the bucket. Load it up. That's it."*

**The return (assigned shape):** blade in her belt, a bucket in each hand, **walking the long wet with the
six**, heel-dragging a line in the flat where the next one goes while the water comes up.

**Source 2 in the style field:** `a choppier flip of the buttery soul sample to a new drum pattern` ·
`brightly-colored production` · `a two-beer chill`.

**Watch it for:** the second-cliché trap the BRIDGE panel named — *"loss is secretly fine"* is a consolation
and consolation is pleasure doing a job. **Tuesday is better because she learned where the grey is.** That
is a craft fact. It is not a lesson the tide taught her, and no line in this song may imply it was.
