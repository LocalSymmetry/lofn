## Major Deviations

**1. ⚖️ THE V4 SEAM RULING — the honest exception the pair recorded, and my verdict: UPHELD, unchanged.**
- **The question.** V4's seam names *a tambourine played from across the room*, but V4's bed already carries
  a tambourine. Step 09 stated this openly rather than hiding it and claimed the element qualifies on
  **performance** rather than on **instrument**, with the seam clearing anyway on trombone and tenor sax.
- **My ruling: the pair is right, on both legs, and the honesty is worth more than the tidy version.** The
  gate says the seam must name *at least one of* — an instrument not otherwise in the record, a player not
  otherwise in the record, **or a performance a modern session would not produce.** It is a disjunction. A
  tambourine struck from across a room, off the microphone, arriving late and wide because it is fifteen
  feet from the capsule, **is a performance no modern session produces** — a modern session puts the player
  on a close mic dead centre, which is exactly what V4's bed already does with the same instrument. So the
  same object appears twice under two incompatible performances, and the difference between them *is* the
  seam. That is stronger than a fresh instrument would have been, not weaker.
- **And the second leg is independently sufficient.** V4's seam also names **trombone and tenor saxophone
  in unison a beat apart** — two instruments and a performance that appear nowhere in V4's bed, checked by
  name against the bed instrument list in the seam table above. If the tambourine clause were struck
  entirely, V4 still clears the gate.
- **What I refused to do:** substitute a different percussion instrument to make the exception disappear.
  That would have bought a cleaner-looking table at the cost of the one detail in V4 that is genuinely
  about distance — and distance is what the whole song is about, since she has to walk down a platform to
  reach a stranger. ⛔ **I am not repairing an honest disclosure into a cosmetic pass.**

**2. 🔧 EXCLUDE-FIELD REPAIR — `half-time breakdown` removed from all four, replaced with

cannot collide with the seam or with the one-bar hole.

## WHAT THIS PAIR IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Four songs at 104 BPM in which a woman kneels on a railway platform at night with a document scanner she
should not have, points it at an empty track, holds it dead still, and lets a train do the moving. **The
procedure is the form** — verse instructs, chorus reports, four steps, the same eight lines each time
meaning something different by where they fall. **The seam is people who are not otherwise on the record** —
a drummer playing a one-drop with a cross-stick, an organ bubble, a horn a beat late — and she does
something across all four of them with her hands. **The radiant is an instruction she ignored**, arriving
late, said once, never explained. ⛔ **There is no wolf, no clock and no cost anywhere in it**, and every
one of the four ends with a stranger holding a piece of paper she made wrong on purpose.
