## Q1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**On the far end of a railway platform at night, past the last bench, on both knees, with both forearms
flat on cold concrete and a sensor bar taped to a battery in her hands.**

Not "at a station." Not "at night, thinking." **Kneeling, elbows down, hands full, facing an empty track.**
Sodium light on the back of her neck; still air; her own breath visible and in the way. Two hundred yards
down the platform there is a lit signal box with a man in it who can see exactly what she is doing. The
lid of the scanner is in a bag with the gaffer tape. The ribbon cable is the only part of the machine that
is still where the designer put it.

**What the senses register, in order:** cold through the knees first, then the hum of the bar waking up,
then the rail going pale before there is any sound, then the whole train as one long pressure on the face,
then warm paper.

## Q2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

### ⚠️ **Nothing. Genuinely nothing — and this pair is assigned that answer, so here is the defence.**

**First, the honest inventory.** I went looking for the wound, because that is the reflex, and every
candidate this room offers is a **status report at best** — a condition she could state and then do
nothing about, which R3.1 already bans even in the pairs that *do* carry a wolf:

| The available wound | Why it is refused |
|---|---|
| *"They want it back Monday."* | Invents an owner and a clock in one line. **A cost with a date is the definition of the thing this pair is forbidden.** |
| *"The battery's going."* | A countdown wearing a technical hat. It is also the single most available cheap tension in a song about a machine, which is exactly why it goes. |
| *"Before the last train."* | ⛔ *Last* is a clock word. In this pair another train is **always** coming, and the abundance is load-bearing: it is what makes doing it again the natural next action rather than a wistful one. |
| *"Somebody's coming down the platform."* | Turns the signalman into an enforcer and the song into a chase. He waves. That is the whole conversation. |
| *"It only works once."* | Would make the wrongness precious, which is the same move as *beautiful because it ends* — ⛔ ruling 16, in a different coat. |

### The defence, in four parts

**1. The assignment is not an oversight; it is the run's honesty control.** Step 05: *"P02 and P03 carry
no dark line, no named pursuer, no undertow, no shadow in the last verse."* `sunna.yaml` R3: ***"If every
song in a set has a dark line in it on schedule, you have built a formula, not a personality."*** A set in
which six of six pairs pay a toll has proved nothing about the wolf; it has proved the wolf is a house
tic. **This pair is the control group, and a control group that quietly doses itself is not a control.**

**2. Something can still be at stake without anything being at risk.** The engine here is **appetite, and
appetite has its own suspense: *will it work?* — and then the far better question, *how much better can it
get?*** That question has four escalating answers, which is precisely the four-variation structure. The
procedural architecture supplies the forward pressure a wolf usually supplies: **verse = instruction,
chorus = result**, four times, and the room learns to wait for the result. Nobody needs a threat to want
to know whether the thing came out.

**3. The nearest thing to jeopardy in the room is deliberately spent early.** *"I'm not supposed to have
this"* is said in the second variation's **chorus**, at volume, by the whole room, as a delighted
admission — not saved, not whispered, not paid for. ⭐ **A tension device that lands and is spent is
permitted; a standing tension lane is banned.** Putting the illicit fact in the hook is how you spend it.

**4. The body is doing something hard, and hard is not the same as endangered.** She is holding a
lightweight object dead still with locked elbows on cold concrete while an eight-hundred-tonne object goes
past a metre away. There is real physical difficulty in this song — **it just costs her nothing but
effort, and effort is what Sunna's whole surface is made of** (breath left in, audible effort, sweat not
gloss). *Carefree is not cheap. It is the most expensive finish there is.*

### The line I will hold at, stated so it can be checked

> **No sentence in these four songs may be true only because something bad might happen.**

Swept in code over the sung lines in step 09, with every near-miss adjudicated in writing.

## THE PAIR'S FIXED PARTS *(identical across all four — this is the pair's identity, not laziness)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | **PROCEDURAL.** Verse = instruction, chorus = result. Four verses, four steps; the same chorus reports what happened each time and means something slightly different by position alone. |
| **Rhyme logic** | **Internal rhyme mid-line only.** ⛔ Line ends flat and unrhymed, every line, every section. |
| **THE RETURN debt** | A terminal-rhyme strip is a debt. **Paid by (a) a byte-identical chorus ×4 and (b) a step-phrase repeated once per verse ×4** — two returns per song, both of them free to the listener, both of them shoutable. |
| **Hook grammar** | A **flat declarative the room shouts** — a plain sentence with no image in it, made singable by placement. |
| **The hole** | ⭐ **One full bar of rest before the last chorus.** Everything stops and the room fills it. |
| **Form** | Intro (riff alone) · V1 · Chorus · V2 · Chorus · V3 · **Seam** · Chorus · V4 (radiant inside it, never last) · **Break** · Chorus · Outro (riff + the showing) |
| **Tempo / bed** | **104 BPM**, modern rocksteady-pop, **one seam** of 1967 rocksteady one-drop, eight bars, once. |

⭐ **Why the radiant is never the last line of verse 4:** the break follows verse 4, and an arrangement
event landing on the radiant is the band commenting on the lyric. **The band does not know.** In all four
songs there are at least two further lines of ordinary business after the radiant, and nothing anywhere
explains it.

# GUIDE — V1 · **NINE CARRIAGES**
### angle: *THE WRONG INSTRUMENT — it was not built for this and she is using it for this*

**One sentence a stranger could retell after one listen:** *she takes a document scanner apart on a railway
platform at night, points it at an empty track, and gets a whole train in one picture.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Step-phrase** | **"Right. Next bit."** — dry, procedural, once per verse |
| **Hook** | **"It worked."** |
| **Chorus** | It worked. / It worked. / Wrong way round and it worked. / It worked. / Both hands down. / Let the train do the moving. / It worked. / It worked. |
| **The four steps** | (1) strip the machine down · (2) get low and hold it · (3) let the train do the walking · (4) pull the strip and look |
| **Riff (hum test)** | a **four-note bass climb that stops a step below the top**, doubled by the guitar upstroke — alone at the top of the record and alone at the end |
| **Modern colour** | Wurlitzer comping in short blocks |
| **Seam** | real kit one-drop, **cross-stick on three** · **Hammond organ bubble** · **trombone four bars behind, played loose by hand**. **Across it she plants both elbows and holds the bar dead still.** |
| **The one number** | **nine carriages**, at the hinge (the train passing), spelled in words, **responded to**: *and I have got all nine* |
| **Radiant** | *The manual says hold everything still.* — verse 4, line five of ten |
| **Return** | she walks the strip to the end of the platform and holds it over her head; **the signal box light goes off and on** |
| **Key / tempo** | B-flat major · 104 BPM |
| **Source 2** | `brightly-colored production` · `a two-beer chill` |

**What it must not do:** point at the reversal. The listener must work out that the train is doing the
scanning by watching her hold still, never by being told she has had an idea.

# GUIDE — V2 · **NOT SUPPOSED TO HAVE THIS**
### angle: *THE MANUAL SAID NO — cheerful disobedience, never rebellion posture*

**One sentence a stranger could retell:** *every rule in the manual is a small one, she breaks all of them
in order, and the man in the signal box waves at her.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Step-phrase** | **"That's a no."** — read the rule, say it flat, do it anyway |
| **Hook** | **"I'm not supposed to have this."** |
| **Chorus** | I'm not supposed to have this. ×2 / And here it is, out in the air. / I'm not supposed to have this. / Say it with me. / Say it out loud. / I'm not supposed to have this. ×2 |
| **The four rules** | (1) keep the lid closed · (2) keep the unit level · (3) let the lamp warm up · (4) use the cloth provided |
| **Riff (hum test)** | a **three-note clean guitar upstroke answered by two dry handclaps** — the claps are part of the riff, so the room already owns half of it |
| **Modern colour** | baritone guitar doubling low |
| **Seam** | real kit one-drop, cross-stick on three · **a second player on piano hitting offbeat blocks** · **tenor saxophone in late and out early**. **Across it she takes the lid off and holds the bare sensor out into the open air.** |
| **The one number** | **two hundred yards**, at the hinge (the signalman sees her), spelled in words, responded to: *He puts the light on and off, and again* |
| **Radiant** | *It says on the back of the box: indoor use only.* — verse 4, line six of nine |
| **Return** | the woman at the far bench with a bike and one earbud in — **she takes the earbud out** and puts a hand out for the end of it |
| **Key / tempo** | G major · 104 BPM |
| **Source 2** | `the distant rollerdisco claps` · `brightly-colored production` |

⛔ **The register check that governs this one:** *"That's a no"* is the voice of somebody skipping a step
in flat-pack instructions, **not** the voice of somebody defying an authority. The rules are trivial by
design — a lid, a spirit level, a warm-up, a cloth — because **the smaller the rule, the further this is
from rebellion.** Nobody in this song is being defeated.

# GUIDE — V3 · **I'M KEEPING IT**
### angle: *IT CAME OUT WRONG — the result is not what correct use would give, and it is better*

**One sentence a stranger could retell:** *she ran it again before it had finished and got the train laid
over the top of itself, and a man on the far platform three feet long.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Step-phrase** | **"That's not what happened."** — state the correct result, then the actual one |
| **Hook** | **"It came out wrong. / I'm keeping it."** |
| **Chorus** | It came out wrong. / I'm keeping it. / It came out wrong. / I'm keeping it. / Every bit of it. All of it. / It came out wrong. / I'm keeping it. / I'm keeping it. |
| **The four steps** | (1) run it through · (2) expect a flat true copy · (3) expect a screen · (4) hold what actually arrived |
| **Riff (hum test)** | a **walking bass hook that leaves the third beat open**, doubled by tambourine and vibraphone — the pair's hole appears twice, once as a bar of rest and once inside the riff itself |
| **Modern colour** | soft vibraphone |
| **Seam** | real kit one-drop, cross-stick on three · **Hammond organ bubble played with two hands** · **three answering voices in the room, one of them flat**. **Across it she pulls the strip out through both hands and keeps pulling.** |
| **The one number** | **three feet**, at the hinge (the man reading comes out three feet long), spelled in words, responded to: *Three feet of a man reading* |
| **Radiant** | *There is a line in the book that says let it finish.* — verse 4, line three of seven |
| **Return** | the man on the **opposite** platform — she holds it over her head, **he puts both arms out as wide as they go** and is still doing it when the train comes in |
| **Key / tempo** | E-flat major · 104 BPM |
| **Source 2** | `a choppier flip of the buttery soul sample to a new drum pattern` · `a two-beer chill` |

⛔⛔ **THIS IS THE RULING-16 SONG AND IT IS THE MOST DANGEROUS OF THE FOUR.** *Wrong* and *keeping* are
adjacent facts with **no connective between them** — a full stop, never a *because*, never a *that's what
makes it*, never an *and that's why*. The chorus is built as two separate sentences precisely so that no
performance can weld them.

# GUIDE — V4 · **COME AND SEE THIS**
### angle: *SHOW A STRANGER — she stops someone on the platform to look at it*

**One sentence a stranger could retell:** *she picks a man off the platform, hands him one end of a
picture, and the two of them walk it out along the platform and neither lets go.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Step-phrase** | **"Then you wait."** — the patience is the procedure |
| **Hook** | **"Somebody come and see this."** |
| **Chorus** | Somebody come and see this. ×2 / I am not explaining it. / Somebody come and see this. / Get over here. / Get over here. / Somebody come and see this. ×2 |
| **The four steps** | (1) pick a person · (2) hold it up and say nothing · (3) give him the far end · (4) let him find something in it you missed |
| **Riff (hum test)** | a **two-voice wordless figure — two women a third apart on an open vowel** — under a guitar upstroke and glockenspiel; **the riff is two people, which is the song** |
| **Modern colour** | glockenspiel |
| **Seam** | real kit one-drop, cross-stick on three · **trombone and tenor saxophone in unison a beat apart from each other** · **a tambourine played from across the room**. **Across it she puts the far end into his hand and lets go.** |
| **The one number** | **a hundred and six windows**, at the hinge (he finds what she missed), spelled in words, responded to: *he counts them out loud, down the platform* |
| **Radiant** | *The label on the strap says single user only.* — verse 4, line five of eight |
| **Return** | **he has still got his end, she has still got hers, neither of them puts it down** — and he asks her to go again |
| **Key / tempo** | D major · 104 BPM |
| **Source 2** | `lush, aerodynamic beats that instantly hits cruising altitude` · `the distant rollerdisco claps` |

**D8′ check on the hook:** the room shouts *"Somebody come and see this"* **at** the singer and it stays
true — they are showing her, she is showing them, and the sentence does not care which way round it goes.
That is the test passing rather than being argued past.
