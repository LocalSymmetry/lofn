# 1 — WHAT I REFUSE

**I will not sell the line.** Four songs, four places where she gives something up, and in none of them does
the band find out. No hush, no drop-out, nobody goes breathy, nothing slows down, nothing gets a reverb
tail it did not have in the verse before. If a producer wants to help me land the forty minutes, the answer
is no. **I'll say the line. I won't sell it.**

**I will not be ironic about that wall.** In V2 a queue of children build the west side and it is genuinely
worse than mine and I genuinely prefer it. There is no wink in that song. If it reads as a bit, cut the
song, not the sincerity.

**I will not explain a radiant.** All four radiants are followed by an instruction to my own hands —
*"Right, where's my bucket," "Both hands on that corner," "Sand off the blade," "Pass the bucket."* Nothing
after a radiant looks back at it.

**I will not put *worth it* anywhere in this pair.** It was in the V3 draft and it is the single most
dangerous sentence available to this seed. It converts two adjacent facts into a moral and hands the tide a
character. It is gone and it does not come back in another costume.

**I will not let the water be anybody.** It is at the poles, at the far post, up under the base, in at nine.
It has no adjective, no motive, no temperature and no name, and no one in these four songs talks to it.

**And I will not leave a clever thing where you can find it.** Three lines in this draft were nice. That is
exactly what is wrong with them.

# 3 — WHAT I SHARPENED (kept, but harder)

* **V1's hinge is now a two-beat exchange and then nothing.** *"Nine, I tell her. The water's in at nine. /
  She says ta, and she gets back in the line."* She asked, I answered, she left. **That is the register
  never moving**, written as an event instead of as a delivery note.
* **V3's cost is stated in the currency it is actually paid in.** Not *"the tide will get it"* — **forty
  minutes**, a duration, traded for a finial. You can price it. You can disagree with the trade. That is
  what makes it a cost and not weather.
* **V4's wolf is the audience's line, not mine.** *"Do the turret again, that was the good one, that's why
  we came."* The thing being destroyed is asked for out loud by the people who travelled for it, one line
  before the elbow goes in. **Her sets famously end one song early.** This is that, with sand.
* **V2's cost is the one the seed cares most about.** *They commissioned the survival* — and she spends the
  last plate on somebody else's lump and steps out of the frame. The good side goes under with no record at
  all, and she never once says that was generous.
* **Every seam now has her doing something across it with her hands** — blade down and palms in (V1), blade
  into a child's hand (V2), both arms over her head (V3), trowels out to six people (V4). **A seam you can
  only hear is a plug-in.**
* **The hole stayed open.** Four-note bass walk, third note dropped every bar, alone for two bars before
  the first word and alone at the end, in all four. It is the only element that is identical across the
  pair, on purpose: **it is the hum-test object and it is what makes them one pair.**

# 4 — THE TWO SWAP TESTS, RUN ON THE ACTUAL TEXT

## ⭐ THE WOLF-SWAP TEST — **PASS**

*Fisher's falsification, and the predecessor's P01 failure: it sang the byte-identical string* `Tide's at
nine` *in all four songs, so four wolves were one cross-song refrain.*

**Mechanical first:** the four wolf verses share **no line in common**, and across the whole pair no sung
line appears in more than one song (measured in step 10, `identical lines shared across songs: 0`).

**Then the real test — move each wolf into the next song and see what survives:**

| move | what happens |
|---|---|
| **V1 → V2** | The moat bank and the seep arrive in a song with no moat and no seep. V2's photographer is left holding one plate with nothing to point it at, and its radiant — *the bit where somebody asks if they can help* — has no relationship to a moat. **Breaks.** |
| **V2 → V3** | *One plate, turn the stick west* arrives in a song with no photographer and no children's side. Worse: V3's bucket of grey is still in her hand at the end of the song, undecided. **Breaks, and leaves the song unfinished.** |
| **V3 → V4** | *Abandon the north wall to save the turret* arrives in the one song where the turret is the thing she destroys. **Breaks by inversion** — the transplant argues against its host. |
| **V4 → V1** | *Elbow through the turret for the stair's sand* arrives on Saturday, where there is no stair and no second build, and where the turret is the object being fed rather than quarried. **Breaks.** |

**Four different events, four different costs, four different currencies** — finished labour, the record,
duration, the encore. **Reported result: PASS.**

## ⭐ THE RETURN-SWAP TEST — **FAILED ON THE DRAFT, PASSES ON STEP 10**

*The predecessor's worst finding was a pair that closed all four variations with one sentence, noun-swapped
— and it was invisible to every similarity gate we own.*

**The draft failed.** V1 closed *"walking the low line, and the low line's long"* and V2 closed *"and I'm
walking the low tide. Steps can wait."* **Those swap cleanly. They are one sentence in two costumes**, and
I did not see it by reading — I saw it by putting them in each other's songs. Both were rewritten (**C4**,
**C8**).

**Re-run on step 10's finished text:**

| move | what happens |
|---|---|
| **V1 → V2** | *"past the flat patch where the moat was … back to the low where I got it"* — V2 dug no moat and carried no grey up from the low. **Breaks.** |
| **V2 → V3** | *"Plastic spades … red one, green one, one with a crack … the way the queue went"* — V3 has no queue and no spades. **Breaks.** |
| **V3 → V4** | *"North side goes and I watch it from here"* — V4 has no north wall, and its ending has to carry the six who came back. **Breaks.** |
| **V4 → V1** | *"walking with the six down the long wet sand … heel in the flat where the next one goes"* — V1 is Saturday, alone, with no six and no next one. **Breaks.** |

**Reported result: PASS, after one real failure that the test caught and reading did not.**

**⚠️ AND THE HONEST RESIDUE:** V1's closing block is the least self-anchored of the four. Its specificity
lives in lines two and three (*the flat patch where the moat was*, *the low where I got it*); **line one —
"Blade in the bucket and the bucket on my hip" — would survive a transplant into any of the other three.**
It stays because it is the tool-pickup the assigned return shape requires and because the block around it
holds, but it is the weakest seam in the pair and it is named here rather than passed.
