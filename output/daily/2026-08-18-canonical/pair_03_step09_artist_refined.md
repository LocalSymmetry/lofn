## 1. ⛔ THE ZERO-WOLF VERIFICATION

### The sweep, as run

The wolf list is fifty-odd tokens across five families — **clocks** (`clock, hour, minute, deadline, late,
hurry, last, final, before, until, run out`), **costs** (`cost, pay, owe, debt, due, fine, borrowed,
return it, give it back`), **failure** (`battery, dying, gone, lost, lose, miss, one chance`), **pursuers**
(`caught, trouble, sacked, police, guard, coming for, take it back, report me`), and **shadows**
(`shadow, never again, one last`), matched with **word boundaries** — because a naive sweep matches `rage`
inside `garage` and `amen` inside `ornament`, and a censusing instrument needs boundaries.

### Draft result: **16 hits across the four songs.** Final result: **9, all adjudicated, zero wolves.**

## 2. WHAT SHE REFUSES

1. ⛔ **The lesson.** *"Everything's an instrument if you're rude enough about it"* (V4 v3) — **CUT.** It is
   the concept as a slogan. She does not perform being deep and she does not leave a clever thing at the
   front of a song so you know a smart person made it. **The listener may notice the misuse; they may
   never be handed its moral.**
2. ⛔ **The word *trick*.** *"That is the whole trick, that bit"* (V4 v3) — **CUT.** Two reasons: it is
   craft-as-credentials in a single noun, and the sweep flagged it as a **terminal rhyme** (`it` / `bit`)
   in a pair whose rhyme logic is internal-only.
3. ⛔ **Rebellion posture, at the register level.** V2's four rules stay deliberately trivial — a lid, a
   spirit level, a warm-up, a cloth. **The smaller the rule, the further this is from defiance.** I read
   the whole of V2 aloud checking for a single line that needs somebody to be defeated. There is none, and
   the signalman is written as an ally in the middle of it precisely so the song cannot be heard that way.
4. ⛔ **A borrowed line from my own past.** V1's *"it has never let anybody down"* is the predecessor run's
   P03 almost verbatim. **CUT** and replaced with *"it has been perfectly happy."* Self-repetition is the
   quietest kind of formula and no similarity gate in this run is pointed at it.
5. ⛔ **An aphorism hiding in a seam.** V2's *"I am the only still thing here"* — **CUT.** It reads as
   wisdom, and it **leaks V1's radiant across songs**, which would have made two of my four trace back to
   one origin. Replaced with **"Both thumbs on the tape."**

## 4. ⭐ THE RETURN DEBT — how a terminal-rhyme strip is paid for

Stripping end-rhyme is a debt under THE RETURN, payable **only** by naming what comes back in its place.
This pair pays it **twice per song**, and both payments are free to the listener:

| Payment | What returns | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The byte-identical chorus** | the same eight lines, unaltered, meaning something different by position alone — the procedural architecture doing the work a lyric variation would otherwise have to do | **×4 per song**, machine-verified identical |
| **2. The step-phrase** | *"Right. Next bit."* · *"That's a no."* · *"That's not what happened."* · *"Then you wait."* — one per verse, a second chantable unit the room owns by the second verse | **×4 per song** |
| **3. Internal rhyme, mid-line** | *flat on the mat* · *the rail goes pale* · *bent where it went* · *the cold gold of the sodium* · *green, then white* — the rhyme is still there; **it moved to where the body feels it instead of where the ear expects it** | throughout |

⭐ **A byte-identical chorus needs no defence and no note.** This one gets a note anyway, because the
*reason* it is byte-identical here is architectural: **the chorus is the result field of a procedure**, and
a result that reworded itself each time would be a different experiment.

## 5. ⛔ THE RETURN-SWAP SELF-TEST — run, not asserted

**The four closing lines, read back out of the finished file:**

| | Closing line |
|---|---|
| **V1** | *Right. Next bit.* |
| **V2** | *She puts a hand out for the end of it.* |
| **V3** | *He is still doing it when the train comes in.* |
| **V4** | *He says: go on then, do another.* |

**Every swap breaks, and here is what breaks:**

* **V1's into any other** — *"Right. Next bit."* is V1's step-phrase and only V1's. In V2 it lands after a
  woman has taken her earbud out and reads as walking away from her; in V3 and V4 there is a second person
  physically holding the paper, and a procedural full stop abandons them mid-hold.
* **V2's into V1** — nobody is within two hundred yards to put a hand out. The line needs a body **near**
  her, and V1's entire ending is built on distance.
* **V3's into V4** — *"He is still doing it when the train comes in"* requires a man **across the tracks**
  measuring with his arms; V4's man is beside her with one end of the strip in his fist, so the line
  describes an action nobody in that song is performing.
* **V4's into V3** — *"go on then, do another"* is a request from someone who has been let in on the
  procedure. V3's man is a hundred feet away and has never spoken; putting a spoken request in his mouth
  invents a conversation the song did not have.
* **V4's into V1 or V2** — same failure, harder: in V1 the only other person is behind glass at distance.

⭐ **The deeper check, because the sweep found it before I did.** The draft had **V1 and V3 sharing
*"I hold it up and he sees it"*** in their outros — not the closing line, so a closing-line swap test would
have passed while the pair was already writing one ending twice. Repaired: V1 now *"I hold it up over my
head"*, V3 now *"I get it up over the tracks, both arms."* **Measured cross-song line overlap on the
finished four is 0.000 on every pairing.**

⛔ **No ending in this pair lands on food, a hot drink or a domestic interior.** Sweep result on the
domestic ban across all four songs, all sections: **zero hits.**
