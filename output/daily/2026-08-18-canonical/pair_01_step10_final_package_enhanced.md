## Major Deviations

*Where step 11 exercised agency. Everything below is a choice I made, with the reason it beats the
obvious alternative.*

**1. Field headings are the step-11 canonical block names, and every field is fenced.**
Step 10's `### SUNO STYLE PROMPT` / `### SUNO EXCLUDE` / `### SUNO LYRICS` are correct **for step 10** —
that is the format contract in `05_pair_assignments.md`. The reader that exists on disk for *enhanced*
packages, `verify_enhanced.py`, matches `1. MUSIC PROMPT` / `1B. SUNO EXCLUDE PROMPT` / `2. LYRICS` /
`3. TITLE`, and against the step-10 names it returns **EMPTY EXTRACTION — a hard error, not a score.**
I wrote for the reader that exists. The fences make extraction exact (that reader prefers the first
fenced block), so no annotation of mine can ever leak into a character count. The old field names are
kept in the heading text so both conventions are visible to a human.

**2. The exclude fields were rebuilt, not padded: 405 / 396 / 383 / 339 → 782 / 687 / 618 / 545.**
Three of four were under the floor. Every added clause is a refusal of something this specific record
can actually be turned into:

* ⭐ **The beach drift** — *sea shanty, nautical accordion, hornpipe, seagull cries, surf field
  recording, ocean wave sound effect.* The seed's absolute exclusion is enforced on us as a **vocabulary**
  rule and the lyric passes it clean. The generator can walk into it through the **arrangement** instead:
  a 124 BPM record with a crowd chant, sand in every verse and water at the poles is one gull cry away
  from a novelty maritime track, and **no text gate this house owns can see that failure** — it only
  exists in the render. This is the most valuable line in the four excludes.
* **Synth bass, 808 sub slide.** The walking electric bass is the hum-test object *and* the second lead
  vocal, and the whole sonic device is the note it refuses to play. A synth sub substitution deletes the
  hook and makes the hole meaningless. Worth spending characters to refuse in all four.
* **Period pastiche across the whole mix** (*sixties pastiche for the entire record, vintage/period tape
  emulation across the whole mix*). This protects ONE SEAM from the inside. The intruder is **eight bars**;
  the live risk is Suno reading *girl-group* and applying it to the world. THE GRAIN LAW says a spec that
  fights the generator gets smoothed — so the refusal goes where the generator reads it, not in a note.
* **Final-chorus band drop-out, a cappella last chorus, half-time breakdown, dramatic pause before the key
  line, ritardando.** THE REGISTER NEVER MOVES is written as a lyric rule but its failure mode is a
  production event: the generator's instinct is to *help you land the line*. Sunna's step-09 refusal was
  *“I'll say the line. I won't sell it.”* This is that sentence, moved to where a generator can obey it.
* **Long fade-out ending, long reverb tail ending.** THE HUM TEST requires the bass figure **alone after
  the last word**. A fade eats exactly that.
* **Per-song refusals.** V2 carries the novelty-children block (*nursery-rhyme delivery, novelty
  children's choir, cartoon voices, glockenspiel twinkle, music-box intro, cutesy toy percussion,
  pitched-up child vocal doubling*) because V2 is the song with a queue of children in it and **sincerity
  is the one thing that song cannot lose** — Sunna's own step-09 line was *“if it reads as a bit, cut the
  song, not the sincerity.”* V4 carries *medley reprise of an earlier section* and *power-ballad final
  chorus* because it is the return song and a reprise would make Saturday a flashback instead of a cost.

**3. The Disc strip leads with `Disc_Sub`, not `Disc_Rhythm`.**
The guide is explicit that earlier channels get more generative weight, and the house rule is THE RIFF IS
THE ARGUMENT. This pair's riff is a **bass figure with a hole in it**. It goes first; the kit goes second.

**4. ⛔ The seam is deliberately ABSENT from the Disc strip.**
Castanets, the hand-played floor-tom triplet and the three answering voices appear **only** in each song's
`[Seam ...]` header. A channel declaration is global by construction — putting the intruder in the strip
would make it **the world**, which is precisely how ONE SEAM dies. The strip declares the world only. If a
reader expected the seam instruments in `Disc_Texture`, this is the deviation and it is on purpose.

**5. `Disc_Pad` is a room, not a pad.**
This record has no pad — *ambient pad wash* / *choir pad* / *orchestral pad* are refused in every exclude
field. The guide assigns the Pad channel to **room tone and atmospheric sustain**, so it carries the real
room and the crowd chant instead. Naming a synth there would have contradicted the exclude field in the
same paste.

**6. Section cues were trimmed exactly where the strip now declares them globally — and nowhere else.**
Five phrases, applied identically across all four songs: the intro/outro bass-figure description (now
`Disc_Sub`), `tambourine` in the chorus and out-chant cues (now `Disc_Rhythm`), and `bass walking
underneath` in verse two (now `Disc_Sub`). **Every gate-work cue is verbatim:** `full chest, band walks
straight on` on all four verse threes (the wolf verse) and `full chest, same voice, band keeps walking` on
all four bridges (the radiant), plus all four Seam headers with their instruments intact. **Nothing inside
a section carrying a radiant or a wolf was touched.** The reason is arithmetic, not taste: the Suno lyrics
field is a **hard 5000-char render limit**, MAX configuration spends about 700 characters on Theme +
SONG FORM + the strip, and the skill's own order of operations is *cut sung lines → tighten headers → move
the strip out of the field*. I refused the first (84 sung lines is this pair's measured strength) and the
third (the reader requires `[Disc_` **in** the field), so the middle lever is the one that pays — and it
pays by deleting **duplication**, not information. All four landed under the 4800 target with margin.

**7. V2's style field was raised off the Source-2 floor: two exact imports → three.**
`brightly-colored production` added mid-sentence, lowercase, at the master clause, so the case-sensitive
import check reads it. The floor is two and V2 was sitting **exactly on it** — which is how a floor gets
breached later by an innocent edit nobody re-measures. The other three style fields are byte-identical to
step 10.

**8. ⛔ I refused the four-element EMO header.**
`EXECUTION.md` shows `[Section - EMO:<emotion> - <Role> - <cue>]`. This run's format contract fixes the
shape at `[Section Name - EMO:SpecificEmbodiedEmotion - Voice/instrument cue]` and gives the reason in its
own heading: **ONE RUN SHIPS ONE SHAPE** — written after three EMO conventions survived a whole run.
Adding a Role element to fifty-six headers would cost roughly 300 characters against a hard cap and would
make P01 the only pair in twenty-four songs with a fourth element. The run's shape wins over the general
skill text. The EMO tags themselves are untouched: fourteen per song, every one a specific embodied
emotion, not one of them instructing a hush, a drop-out or reverence.

**9. `[SONG FORM:]` tells the truth about V3, which means the four are not identical.**
V1, V2 and V4 run `Seam` between the second chorus and verse three. **V3 holds the seam back past the
third chorus**, so its form tag names that sequence. A single pair-wide form string would have been a lie
in one song out of four, and the tag is only worth anything if it describes the song actually written.

**10. What I refused to touch at all.** No new sung lines, no new sections, no re-titling, no tempo
change, no edit to any of the four radiants, the four wolf events, the four sign-offs, the byte-identical
choruses, or the two SFX cues per song. The pair's cross-song lyric similarity (0.036 against a 0.42
ceiling) and its zero shared sung lines are step-10 wins and step 11 had no business near them.

**11. One correction to the brief, measured.** The spawn brief states **85 sung lines each**. Step 10's own
table says 84, and my independent re-stat of the finished file says **84**. The count did not change in
step 11 — it was 84 before and it is 84 now.
