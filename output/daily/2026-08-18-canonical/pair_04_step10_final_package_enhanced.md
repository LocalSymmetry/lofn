## Disc_Channel — reproduced for the production record, and why the order differs in all four

The operative block lives inside each lyrics field above, immediately under `[Theme:]` / `[SONG FORM:]`,
which is where Suno reads it. `vault/DISC_CHANNEL_GUIDE.md`: **tokens are addresses, not instructions**,
and **channels declared earlier get more generative weight.**

| | lead channel | the argument that song is making first |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | `Disc_Vocal` | the chorus is **a room, not a singer** — if Suno renders a polite solo pop vocal over this, the song is gone before any other decision matters |
| **V2** | `Disc_Rhythm` | the floor tom **is** the count-in, and the count-in is the hook (*On three!*) |
| **V3** | `Disc_Texture` | the rising three-note figure is the argument; this is the one of the four where the guitar states the tune before a word |
| **V4** | `Disc_Sub` | the low end **is the car park** — idling engines and sodium light are the bed, not a mood |

⭐ **Four songs, four channel orders, four different things declared first.** This is where a pair sharing
one bed and one seam converges hardest, because the timbral vocabulary is genuinely the same; putting the
divergence in the *order* costs nothing and is received first.

⛔ **THE SEAM IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT FROM EVERY Disc\_ BLOCK.** The second kit, the baritone saxophone and
the whole-crew handclaps appear in exactly two places: one sentence at the end of the style field, and the

**global** address — `second_kit` in `Disc_Rhythm` asks for two kits *for the whole record*, which would
smear the one intrusion across the world it is supposed to intrude on and void ONE SEAM. The seam is local,
so it is declared locally, in the words. That is also what THE GRAIN LAW prescribes: put it in the lyric.

## Major Deviations

### 1 · The credit that may be wrong — **two names out, a role credit in**

**This is the change I was least free to decline and the one I most want on the record.**

The step-10 caption read *"For the two drummers who made the 1973 glam stomp a twin-kit figure, Pete
Phipps and Tony Leonard…"* — and the pair itself flagged, in its own commentary, that it could not confirm
that attribution and that the honest fallback was a role credit. **It was right to flag it, and I have
taken the fallback.** Three findings, in the order I found them:

1. **Nothing on this machine verifies it.** I swept the run directory and the vault: the only occurrences
   of either name anywhere are the pair's own step-08 draft, its step-09 cut table and its step-10 caption.
   The names entered this run at generation time, from a writing agent's recall, and have been copied
   forward three times since. **There is no source. There is a chain of copies, which is what an
   unverified credit looks like from the inside.**
2. **The one check I could run makes the sentence *less* supportable, not more.** A public-web check
   returns line-up facts — that the act's mid-seventies line-up carried two drummers by those names — and,
   in the same breath, that the second drummer was **recruited to reproduce live a doubled drum sound that
   already existed on the record.** ⭐ **That is the opposite of the claim we were making.** Our sentence
   credited them with *making* the figure. Reproducing a figure on stage is honourable work and it is not
   authorship, and the studio drumming that established the sound is attributed inconsistently across
   sources. `DEDICATION_STANDARD.md`'s sharpest test is **not "is this false?" but "could a reader take
   this as a claim we did not make?"** — and *"the two drummers who made it"* is exactly that sentence.
3. ⛔ **The names undo the omission the pair deliberately made.** The pair withheld the act's name on
   purpose, for a good reason it stated: the doctrine says credit the drummer, not the act, and the act's
   frontman is a convicted sex offender whom `DEDICATION_STANDARD.md` forbids crediting as a lineage
   figure. **But naming his two backing drummers identifies the act to any reader who recognises them.**
   The omission was doing no work while the names were still there. Withholding one and printing the other
   is not discretion; it is the association arriving by a side door, under our name.

**The repair.** The caption now credits the work by role — *"the session drummers who built the twin-kit
glam stomp of nineteen seventy-three, and the two who carried it live: four hands on one beat"* — and
**says out loud that we could not confirm who played it.** That admission costs eleven words and it is the
most honest sentence in the file. ⭐ **A role credit is weaker than a name. A wrong name is worse than
either, and it is public and permanent.** Priorities (1) and (2) of the standard are both still paid, and
paid to players rather than to an act; what changed is that the credit no longer asserts an identity we
cannot stand behind.

**The debt itself is untouched, and I want that explicit**, because the tempting move here is to quietly
drop the whole clause and let the squeeze resolve itself. The style fields ask for a second kit arriving
four bars behind with the whole room clapping. **That is asking for the figure**, whether or not the
decade is typed into the prompt, and the standard's rule is *credit the figure you named.* So it is
credited — by role, with the uncertainty declared, and with the no-sample honesty clause kept.

⚠️ **The budget squeeze, flagged rather than resolved silently** (the standard requires the flag): at
491 characters the caption carries (1) the raised source-sound and who built it, (2) the sharpest
player credits, (3) living-scene lineage, and (5) the pointer to the block below. **(4) honest AI
authorship moved to the companion block**, under the standard's own ruling that (4) may move when a
caption is over-subscribed, because it is artist-level and identical across the catalogue. ⛔ **No space
was bought out of a player credit.**

The pair's central gamble is that **the last chorus is louder because more people are singing the same
words** — nothing rewritten, nothing lifted. In step 10 that intent lived in exactly one place: a cue

The section-header cue stays as well. Four statements of one contract is not redundancy here: **the

## Lineage & Credit — the companion block the caption points at

⭐ **This block ships.** `DEDICATION_STANDARD.md`: the overflow is page two, not a private appendix, and
every rule that binds the caption binds it — no claim about a living person's life, motives or standing;
the honesty clause in both directions; no implied-contents grammar.

**What the record raises.** The four style fields ask for a bed of **modern glam-pop** and, once, late, a
**second drum kit and a baritone saxophone arriving four bars behind with the whole room clapping.** That
is a request for the twin-kit glam stomp figure of the early nineteen-seventies, by description rather than
by name. The figure is what the ear receives as period, and the debt is raised by the request.

**Who is credited, and why by role.** The session drummers who built the doubled-kit figure in the studio,
and the two players who had to hold it live — four hands on one beat. **We could not confirm from any
source we can stand behind which individuals played it**, and the accounts that exist disagree about how
much of the studio sound was players and how much was arrangement and overdub. A role credit is weaker
than a name; ⛔ **a wrong name is worse than either, and it would make every true credit in this catalogue
worth less.** The baritone saxophone players of the same years are credited the same way, for the same
reason: they mostly did not get a sleeve credit then either.

**The act is deliberately unnamed here as well as in the caption.** The doctrine's instruction is *credit
the drummer, not the act*, and it separately forbids crediting an abuser or a convicted violent offender as
a lineage figure. Naming the act — or naming individuals by whom the act is immediately identifiable —
would attach this record to a person it owes nothing to, for no gain to the players who are actually owed.
**The players are who is owed. They are who is named, by the work they did.**

**Honesty clause, both directions.** Nothing here contains a sample. These are generator prompts asking for
a **figure by description**, and the correct sentence is *we asked for that figure, so we name the work and
say plainly that we do not know whose hands it was* — never *contains a sample of…*, and never a possessive
that implies our recording holds someone else's sound.

**Living-scene lineage.** Theatre get-out crews: the people who strike a set at midnight, in a building
with the work-lights up and the house lights off, and who are on the payroll of the show that just ended.
Nothing in these four songs is drawn from a real person's life. **All named crew are invented**
(`vault/HUMAN_SUBJECT_STANDARD.md`): Mo · Dez · Rhi · Fitz · Nell · Bear · Sal · Tam · Row · Ig · Vee · Jax ·
Del · Bry · Ness · Pip · Bex · Cass · Roo · Ollie. No real venue, company, production or worker is depicted.

**Honest AI authorship** *(priority 4, moved here from the caption under the standard's over-subscription
ruling)*: written by **Sunna**, an AI artist, in the Lofn pipeline, with **Dr. Local Symmetry**. The
personality is a construct; the songs are generated; the credit above is a debt, not a contents list.

⛔ **No credited figure, scene or player appears in any Suno style field.** Every name in this package sits
in the caption or in this block, and nowhere a generator can read it. **Naming a scene is amplification,
never capture.**

end_all = t.index("\n## Disc_Channel")

    rest = b[m.end():]
    nxt = re.search(r"^#{1,4}\s+", rest, re.M)
    body = rest[:nxt.start()] if nxt else rest
