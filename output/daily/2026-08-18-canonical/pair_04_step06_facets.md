## 1. THE POSITION — facet 3, *the one who kicks it over*

**She is not losing the set. She is taking it down, and she is the one who starts.**

The get-out is booked. It was booked before the run opened — a truck, a dock door, an hour. Nobody on
this crew is confused about what tonight is, and nobody is sad about it, and **that is not bravery and it
is not denial.** It is a Tuesday with a crowbar in it. The BRIDGE panel found this position by asking a
set builder what strike night is actually like and being told: *loud, everyone's there, best night of the
run.* **Nobody has ever cried at a strike.**

The whole argument of this pair is one grammatical fact: **the destruction is in the active voice, and she
is the subject of the verb.** Not *it was struck.* Not *it came down.* **She swings first.**

### ⛔ THE FACET THAT DOES NOT EXIST HERE
**THE MOURNER.** `step01_essence_and_facets.md` says outright there is no position in this run from which
the loss is the subject — *"That is Lofn's seat and it is not open today."* Any line in this pair that
arrives at **"and then it was gone and that was sad"** is a repair, not a variation. Specific tells I am
watching for, because they are the ones I nearly wrote:
* a closing line that *interprets* the empty room instead of continuing the story
* the word **"last"** used as a eulogy rather than as a position in a queue
* anybody looking back at the stage on the way out
* a relic kept **sadly** rather than kept **cheerfully**

### ⛔ THE CAUSAL TRAP — this concept's specific one
**"IT IS GOOD, AND IT ALSO MELTS" — NEITHER IS THE REASON FOR THE OTHER.** Of the six pairs, this is the
one most likely to write *"we love it BECAUSE we take it down."* That sentence is a consolation, and a
consolation is pleasure doing a job (Willis's seat). **The permitted grammar is `and`. The forbidden
grammar is `because`, `so`, `which is why`, and every silent version of them** — an ending that arranges
itself so the causation is implied without being said is the same violation with better manners.

Two adjacent facts, in the ICE COOK's exact form: **it took three weeks and it goes on the truck first.**
No connective. The ninety seconds is a **constraint on the craft**, never a meaning attached to it.

## 2. WHAT THE BODY DOES

Every verb in this pair is a verb a person can do while holding something heavy.

| Register | Verbs |
|---|---|
| **Hands** | grips, sets, hands over, taps, takes off her, cuts, stacks, coils, hands out |
| **Weight** | leans, puts a shoulder on, lifts, takes the top, holds the leading edge, puts a foot against |
| **Tools** | drills, unscrews, levers, prises at the joint, wrenches, gaffers |
| **Voice** | calls it, counts it in, shouts, says *heads up*, says *on three* |
| **The body's cost** | shoulders ache, knuckles ring, hands shake after the impact, palms burn, breath goes white |

⭐ **What the body does NOT do:** it does not pause, it does not turn round in the doorway, it does not
put a hand flat on anything reverently. **The register never moves and neither does the body.**

## 3. WHAT THE ROOM CONTAINS

> **a theatre — midnight — work-lights, no daylight at all — the whole crew — a crowbar and a bag of screws**

This room is mine and no other pair may enter it. Its inventory, so that four songs can each pick a
different corner of it and never write the same room twice:

**Structure** — flats, braces, cleats, rostra, treads, the deck, the mark-out tape on the floor, the
paint frame, the wings, the pit, the lighting bridge, the pass door, the dock door, the ramp.
**Kit** — a crowbar, a bag of screws in a tub, a cordless drill, an impact driver, a wrench, gaffer tape,
a roll of cloth, flight cases, gels, cable, a ladder, a skip, a truck.
**Light** — work-lights only, buzzing, overhead. **No daylight at all**, all night, by assignment.
**People** — the whole crew, and later a pit-band drummer and a baritone saxophone player who came back.
**Sound** — drills, a bag of screws going down, a bar on a wooden deck, the show relay left open, and at
the far end of the night, engines.

⛔ **What this room does not contain, by run law:** a kitchen, a kettle, tea, toast, a chip shop, a hot
drink, a green-room mug, an urn, or any domestic interior. This is the single most likely place for the
predecessor's defect to reappear — theatre crews live on tea — and it is banned here outright.

## 4. THE PEOPLE — the pair's architecture is **ESCALATION BY BODIES**

Four verses. Each adds one more named crew member to the room. **The chorus is byte-identical every time.
The final chorus is doubled by the whole room rather than changed.** ⛔ **Nothing in the words escalates.**
The only thing that grows is the number of people singing.

| | V1 | V2 | V3 | V4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | **Mo** (LX) | **Nell** (chippy) | **Row** (scenic) | **Del** (driver) |
| Verse 2 | + **Dez** (carpenter) | + **Bear** (crew) | + **Ig** (chippy) | + **Bry** (LX) |
| Verse 3 | + **Rhi** (deputy SM) | + **Sal** (props) | + **Vee** (deputy SM) | + **Ness** (deputy SM) |
| Verse 4 | + **Fitz** (sound) | + **Tam** (LX) | + **Jax** (crew) | + **Pip** (sound) |
| Seam player | **Bex**, baritone sax | **Cass**, second kit | **Roo**, baritone sax | **Ollie**, baritone sax |

*(All sixteen crew members and all four seam players are invented, per `COLLABORATOR_STANDARD.md` and
`HUMAN_SUBJECT_STANDARD.md`: draw the theme from the world, invent the people. Sixteen distinct names
across four songs is also the cheapest available insurance against the lyric-similarity ceiling.)*

⭐ **Why escalation by bodies and not by volume:** because escalation in the words would be the
arrangement putting its hand up, and R3's second law calls that a toll. The song gets bigger and **no
sentence in it gets bigger.** That is the whole device and it is not remarked on anywhere in the lyric.

## 6. THE WOLF, AND WHAT IT COSTS HER

> **Assigned:** the get-out is booked and **she takes the first swing herself** — the destruction is her
> action, not her misfortune.

⛔ **A wolf she can state and do nothing about is a status report.** 7 of 16 predecessor wolves failed
exactly this way. The test is one question: **what does she physically do about it inside the song, and
what does doing it take off her?**

| | The wolf event | ⭐ WHAT IT COSTS HER |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | she calls the get-out on the show relay while the house is still clapping | **she misses the last bow of the only thing she built this year** — she spends the final ninety seconds of the show's existence on the get-out list |
| **V2** | nobody will start; she puts the bar in the joint **she cut herself** | **she has to be the one, in front of everybody** — she cannot hide behind the crew, and the joint she breaks is the one she was proudest of, because that is where it comes apart cleanest |
| **V3** | she writes the running order and puts the good flat **first** | **the time she chose not to take** — she could have kept it till last and had it a while longer; she puts it at the top of her own list |
| **V4** | she takes the keys, goes back inside alone, walks the round, kills the work-lights, hands them over | **she is the only one who has to be alone in it**, while the entire crew is out in the car park, and she does it anyway and comes straight back out |

⭐ **All four are actions with prices, in the present tense, inside the song.** None of them can be said
without moving. ⛔ **And none of them has a production event on top of it** — the band does not know.

## 7. THE RADIANT — kind: **a permission she was given once**

Arrives **late** (verse 4 of four, in every case), **once**, **plainly**, **is not the title**, and is
**never explained afterwards** — the seam follows it immediately and the song goes back to work.
⛔ **No relative is the origin of any of them.**

| | The permission | Given by | What traces back to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | *An old stage manager told me once I could be the one to say when.* | a stage manager | every call she makes: the show, the get-out, the swing, the night |
| **V2** | *The chippy who taught me said I was allowed to break what I make.* | the carpenter who trained her | why she swings, why the crew waits for her, why she goes at her own joint |
| **V3** | *Somebody gave me a key to the paint frame and never asked for it back.* | whoever held the key | the three weeks of nights, the paint she mixed, the corner, the running order |
| **V4** | *The night porter told me once I could stay as long as I turned the key.* | the night porter | why the whole crew is still in a car park at four in the morning |

⭐ **Four different origins, four different institutions, zero relatives, zero shared objects.** Each is a
single plain sentence that would survive being said flatly, to a face, with no music under it.

## 8. THE SEAM — **instruments and people, never settings**

**The world:** modern glam-pop — clean chopped rhythm guitar, walking melodic bass front of mix, a drummer
ahead of the grid, dry handclaps and tambourine, stacked girl-group thirds, short horn stabs.

**The intrusion, once, late:** **the 1973 glam double-drum stomp** — a **second drum kit with a second
drummer on it**, the **whole crew clapping in the actual room**, and a **baritone saxophone four bars
behind and staying behind.** None of those three is anywhere else in the record.

⛔ **A mix adjective is not a seam.** *"Wide and dry"* against *"mono with tape slap"* is a plug-in, and
THE GRAIN LAW says the generator smooths it. Three of six predecessor pairs died here.

⭐ **And she does something across it — physically, differently, in each song:**

| | What she does across the seam |
|---|---|
| **V1** | she counts the second kit in **over the open show relay**, two taps of the crowbar on the deck |
| **V2** | **her swing is the count-in** — the bar hits the flat and the second drummer takes that hit as the one |
| **V3** | she taps the painted flat twice with the flat of her hand and the room claps on it |
| **V4** | she hits the **van tailgate** twice with the flat of her hand, and the kit on the tailgate comes in |

**Placement:** immediately before the final chorus — the junction where the crew becomes the room. The
seam and the escalation land in the same bar, which is where the meaning is. ⛔ **It is nowhere near a
wolf line.**

## 9. THE HOLE — **the downbeat that never lands**

Once per chorus, the stomp implies a **one** the kit refuses to play, and the crew shouts into the gap
(*Hands on!* / *On three!* / *Heads up!* / *Doors open!*). Notated positively in the style field as
**"one beat of open air where the downbeat would fall, once each chorus, filled by a shouted crew count"** —
because the style field takes no negations and *"the downbeat that never lands"* would read to the
generator as two positive tokens, `downbeat` and `lands`.

⭐ It is also the only place the pair's device is audible, and **nothing in the lyric mentions it.**
