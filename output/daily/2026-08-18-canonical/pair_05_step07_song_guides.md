# ⭐ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS

*A guide that cannot answer both concretely — place **and** stake — is not ready to direct.*

## Q1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**Standing.** That is the answer before the room is.

She is in an **airport lounge at three in the morning**. Fluorescent tubes, one of them ticking. No
windows that tell her anything; **no weather at all**, which is not atmosphere, it is a fact about the
room — the only device in here claiming to know the time is a departure screen, and it is lying. There is
a **cleaner** working a mop or a floor buffer down the middle. There are **two strangers**: one asleep
across the fixed-arm seats with his shoes off, one who put his coat over an arm rest and left it. The
desk under the gate screen has **nobody behind it**. Her coat is on, then over her arm. Her phone is at
chest height and her thumb is moving. In her pocket is a **boarding pass for a flight that no longer
exists**, and the gate above it is **still lit** and **still says on time**.

She is upright. There are chairs in this room and she is not in one, and by verse three she works out that
**she has never once been sitting down for any part of this**: not at the counter with the pen on a
string, not at the kiosk between two shops, not in the room with no chairs in it. ⭐ That is the radiant,
and it arrives late and is never explained.

⛔ No kitchen, no kettle, no hot drink, no food anywhere in these four songs. The domestic register is not
excluded by intention, it is excluded by inventory.

## Q2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**Three things, and by the end of each song she has lost all three.**

1. **THE ACCOUNT.** The password still works. It opens on a logo she does not know. Eleven years of
   everywhere she went, transferred with the seats and the tugs and the gate leases.
2. **THE RECORD.** Somebody put a mark on her file meaning *read this*, and it carries a timestamp, and
   the timestamp is a night she can remember from inside her own head. In V2 she takes her own photographs
   of that night off her own phone, herself, so **the only copy left is theirs**.
3. **THE ARGUMENT.** This is the one that actually hurts, because she never gets to have it. She makes it
   out loud at three in the morning to a cleaner who says *love, I only do the floors*, and he is right.
   She files a deletion request and it **works perfectly**, removing exactly what she named and keeping
   the shelf it sat on, and she is asked whether she is satisfied and **she ticks yes**. She says *thank
   you* out loud, in an airport, at night. And in V4 she finds out why there was never an argument
   available: **she already had it, years ago, in a queue, with her coat over her arm, and she lost it in
   the time it takes to sign.**

⭐ **Each of these is an ACTION she takes that costs her.** ⛔ Not one of them is a condition she can state
and then stand still inside. *"They sold my data"* is weather. *"I ticked yes"* is an event.

**And the thing that hurts is not the loss — it is that the schedule was published.** Somebody could have
read it. Somebody did. That is the seed inverted: the other five pairs get a tide table in the paper, and
this one gets a notice on a page she does not open.

# THE PAIR'S STANDING SPEC — applies to all four

**Bed (the world):** modern nu-disco. **118 BPM**, stated in every style field so it can be measured.
Walking melodic bass front of mix; clean rhythm guitar chopping sixteenths as a percussion instrument;
handclaps and tambourine dry and centre; short unreverbed horn stabs that answer and leave; live-feel kit
eight to fourteen milliseconds behind the grid with the bass locked on it.
⛔ **Swagger is never distortion.** There is no aggression token anywhere in this pair — the anger is in
the words and only in the words. **The band does not know.**

**Intruder (the seam, once):** a **1972 Hohner Clavinet D6 through a wah pedal**, played by a **guest who
is on no other bar of the record** and leaves when the bar ends.

**Architecture:** two-column verses — *told* / *true*, alternating, eight lines, no connective, no comment.
**Rhyme:** end rhyme in the *told* lines only. **Hook:** contract language sung as a compliment.
**Hole:** one instrument leaves at the seam and never returns.
**Register:** the worst line in exactly the voice that sang the funny one. ⛔ No hush, no drop-out, no
breathy reverence — enforced positively in the cues and negatively in the EXCLUDE field, which carries
`drop-out, hushed vocal, whisper` on purpose.

**Four distinct shapes** so the pair does not ship one skeleton four times:
V1 fourteen sections with a bridge · V2 opens with a **chant** the room learns before the first chorus ·
V3 **delays the first chorus behind two verses** and has no bridge · V4 replaces the pre-choruses with two
**list** blocks and carries the longest stage address.

**One numeric fact per song**, spelled in words, at the hinge, responded to and never recited:
V1 *eleven years* · V2 *ten past three on a Tuesday* · V3 *seven years* · V4 *eight cents a head*.
*(The only other numerals in the pair are the count-in in V4's stage address — a performance cue, and the
only one, deliberately.)*

# V1 — HIGH VALUE SEGMENT
### angle: **THE GATE IS STILL LIT** — the airline is gone and its screen is still on

**The event, with a day and an object.** She puts her card against the lounge door and it opens. The app
opens on a logo she has never seen. The help line rings and rings and then stops. The gate screen above an
empty desk still says **on time** for a flight number that goes nowhere. **The cost:** she makes the
argument out loud, at three in the morning, to a cleaner with a mop and a kind face, and he says *love, I
only do the floors*, and he is right, and that is the whole of the argument she gets to have.

**Hinge fact:** *Eleven years of everywhere I went / Sold with the seats and the carts and the gates.*
Responded to, not recited: *I would have asked for more. / Nobody asked me at all.*

**Radiant (late, plain, not the title, never explained):**
> *I signed it standing up, with a pen on a string.*

**Riff (HUM TEST):** a five-note walking bass hook, alone in the first bar and the last.
**Hole:** the tambourine leaves at the seam. *"Somebody played a bar and went home."*
**Seam:** clavinet takes a bar; columns swap; she answers — *"Seamless for who."* and *"So am I."*
**Return:** from the stage she **calls the buyer by the dead airline's name** — the wrong container, the
one that is gone — and cues the chorus. *"I still say the old name for it / Nobody's corrected me /
Nobody's here to / Come on —"*

# V2 — SPECIAL CATEGORY
### angle: **READ THIS** — somebody marked her file for attention and she finds out which night it happened

**The event.** She asks for everything they hold. It arrives as a folder that opens like a diary she never
wrote, and there is a **mark by her name** meaning *read this*, and the mark has a date and an hour on it.
The hour is a night she remembers from inside: she was asleep across the fixed-arm seats with her phone
nearly out and a stranger put his coat over the arm. **The cost:** she goes and finds her own photographs
of that night — the blurry ones, the ones with her thumb in — and takes them off her phone herself, so
**the only copy left is theirs.**

**Hinge fact:** *At ten past three on a Tuesday / Somebody thought I was worth a look.* Response:
*I have never been so seen / And I would like it back.*

**Radiant:** > *I signed it with my finger, standing up, for a parcel.*

**Riff:** a two-note clean guitar upstroke answered by the walking bass, stated before the first word and
after the last. **Chant-first:** the room learns *"somebody read me first / read me twice"* in section two.
**Hole:** the **handclaps** leave at the seam — so the room has to do them. *"The clap goes out and the
room does it themselves."*
**Seam:** F11's exact shape, in office language — *"The mark is gone and the row is still there."* Columns
swapped, she answers twice: *"Removed from where."* and *"It does."*
**Return:** she **names the room wrong from the stage** — *"This is the departure lounge / Nothing departs
/ I'm calling it that anyway / Here it comes —"*
**Source 2:** `lush, aerodynamic beats that instantly hits cruising altitude` · `a two-beer chill`

# V3 — WHERE TECHNICALLY FEASIBLE
### angle: **THE DELETION REQUEST** — a machine in someone else's building that is brilliant at exactly one job

**The event.** She sends the request. It works. **Perfectly.** It takes the exact thing she named and
leaves the whole shelf it was sitting on. The confirmation arrives in the middle of the night: a green
tick, and a line saying the rest is kept for seven years, and one question asking whether she is
satisfied. **The cost:** she says *thank you* out loud, in an airport, at night — and at the seam **she
ticks yes.** She wins completely and there is nobody to appeal to, because they complied.

**Hinge fact:** *They keep the rest for seven years / They said it like good news.* Response: *I said thank
you / I said thank you out loud, in an airport, at night.*

**Radiant:** > *I signed it standing up at a kiosk between two shops.*

**Riff:** a bass ostinato **with a rest where the fourth note is expected** — the hole is inside the hook
itself, stated alone at the top and at the end.
**Hole (departure):** the **horn stabs** leave at the seam.
**Seam — the sharpest in the pair:** the clavinet plays **the phrase the horn section was about to play**,
and the horns get up and go. *A performance a modern session would not produce, named as people and
instruments.* Columns swap; she answers *"Concludes."* and then does the thing that costs her.
**Return:** she **names the deletion wrong from the stage** — *"They call it a deletion / So do I / I'm
going to keep calling it that / Say it with me —"*
**Source 2:** `a choppier flip of the buttery soul sample to a new drum pattern` · `brightly-colored production`
⛔ **Sweep note:** the failed airline in this song **went to auction at the end of the quarter.** It is a
bankruptcy and a lot. It is not an accident and it never will be.

# V4 — SUCCESSORS AND ASSIGNS
### angle: **THE CONTAINER, NAMED** — ⭐⭐ the clause spent, once, from the stage, audible in the WRITING

**The event.** She reads the filing. There is a list of what was sold, and she is on it, between **ground
equipment** and **gate leases**. Not a victim — a line item, at eight cents a head. **The cost:** all
three at once. The account is gone; the record is theirs; and the argument turns out to have been over
before she got here, because **she had it herself, years ago, in a queue, with her coat over her arm, and
she was the one doing it.**

**Hinge fact:** *Eight cents a head / That's the part I'd argue with / Not the sale, not the terms /
Eight cents.* ⭐ She would rather haggle than mourn. That single move is the difference between Sunna
and everybody else on this brief.

**Radiant:** > *I signed it standing up in a room with no chairs in it.*

**Riff:** a three-note horn-and-bass unison figure, alone before the first line and after the last.
**Hole:** the **electric piano** leaves at the seam — *"The piano stops and does not start again."*
**Seam:** she takes their sentence and says it true — *They said nothing about you has changed / Nothing
about me has changed.*

## ⭐⭐ HOW V4 MAKES THE NAMING AUDIBLE IN THE WRITING, NOT IN THE PRODUCTION

Three things, all of them on the page, none of them a mix decision:

1. **She says the sentence.** From the stage, plainly, in exactly the voice that sang the joke:
   > *I made you a dance record about this / **That is the wrong container for it** / I know exactly what
   > it is / I'm doing it anyway / Two, three, four —*
   A listener does not have to infer anything. **The container is named, out loud, once.**
2. **The arrangement refuses to acknowledge it, and the writing proves that.** The chorus that follows is
   **byte-identical** to every earlier chorus, and the section cue says *band does not change*. ⛔ There is
   no hush, no drop-out, no reverent pause, and the EXCLUDE field forbids all three at the generator.
   ⭐ **The naming lands because nothing catches it.** That is the clause's condition — *audible in the
   writing rather than merely intended* — satisfied by the writing declining to be helped.
3. ⭐ **The one rhymed TRUE line in the whole pair, and it is the last line of the record.** Across four
   songs, sixteen two-column verses and a hundred and twenty-eight alternating lines, the *told* column
   rhymes and the *true* column never does. In V4's outro the told line returns verbatim from verse one —
   *"They said the purpose stays as told"* — and the true line **rhymes with it**: *"I'm between the tugs
   and the seats, and I'm sold."* The fact finally speaks in the official language, once, at the end, and
   **nothing explains it afterwards.** It is felt, not noticed (THE REMARK TEST), and it is the container
   named a second way — in the *shape*, not in the mix.

⛔ **The clause is spent here and nowhere else.** V1, V2 and V3 name *a* wrong container from the stage
(the dead airline's name, the departure lounge, "a deletion") — none of them names the bright container as
the wrong one, which is the clause proper.

**Return:** the stage address, then the byte-identical chorus, then the outro that rhymes.
**Source 2:** `the distant rollerdisco claps` · `lush, aerodynamic beats that instantly hits cruising altitude` · `a two-beer chill`
