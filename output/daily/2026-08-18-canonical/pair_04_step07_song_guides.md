# ⭐ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS

*A guide that cannot answer both concretely — **place** and **stake** — is not ready to direct. Answered
first, for the pair, then again per song inside each guide.*

## ① WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**On the deck of a theatre at midnight, downstage right, three feet from the north end of a scenic flat
she built, with a crowbar held low in her right hand and a tub of screws on the floor by her boot.**

The work-lights are on and the house lights are off, so the auditorium behind her is a black hole with a
few hundred empty seats in it and she has her back to it. **There is no daylight in this song and there
will not be any** — the entire piece happens between midnight and four in the morning under buzzing
fluorescent bars that make everybody look worse. The mark-out tape is still on the floor in four colours.
The dock door is open at the far end and the cold is coming in along the deck at ankle height. Somebody's
drill is going. Somebody has already put the ladder in the wrong place.

She is not watching anything. **She is in the middle of a job with a truck booked at the end of it**, and
the specific sub-problem in front of her right now is which joint to open first so the flat comes apart
in two pieces instead of four.

*(By V4 the body has moved forty yards: a wet car park behind the dock door, sodium light, six vans, and
she is standing in a T-shirt in October with her breath going white.)*

## ② WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**Three things, and only one of them is in the room.**

1. ⭐ **THE THING IN THE ROOM: she has to start it, and everybody is watching her hands.** Nobody on a
   crew will put the first bar into a flat that somebody else built. They will wait, politely, forever.
   So the cost is not the set — **the cost is that she cannot be one of the crew for the length of one
   swing.** She has to step out, be the one, and step back. It costs her the last bow (V1), her own best
   joint (V2), the extra hour she could have had with the good flat (V3), and eleven minutes alone in a
   dark building while everyone she likes is outside laughing (V4).
2. ⛔ **THE THING THAT COULD HURT THE SONG: the mourner.** One interpreting line at the end and the whole
   position collapses into an elegy and I have written Lofn's seat, which is not open today. The failure
   mode is not a sad word — it is **a sentence that explains the empty room instead of continuing the
   story.**
3. ⛔ **THE THING THAT COULD HURT THE ARGUMENT: `because`.** The moment the song says the set is good
   *because* it comes down, it has become a consolation and the pleasure is doing a job. **`and` only.**

**Physically** she is fine. Knuckles ring, shoulders ache, hands shake for about a minute after the first
impact, one thumbnail goes black about a week later. **None of that is the wound and none of it is sung
as one.** The wound is being the one who says when, and she was given permission for that exactly once,
by somebody who is not in the room.

| | |
|---|---|

| **Seam (intrusion, once, late)** | 1973 glam **double-drum stomp** — second kit + second drummer, whole-crew handclaps in the room, **baritone saxophone** four bars behind |
| **Tempo** | **126 BPM**, stated in every style field |
| **Architecture** | **ESCALATION BY BODIES** — 4 verses, one more named crew member each; chorus byte-identical; final chorus **doubled by the room**, words unchanged |

| **Hook grammar** | **first-person-plural declaration** — *we* doing a physical thing, shoutable by people holding something heavy |
| **The hole** | **the downbeat that never lands** — one beat of open air each chorus, filled by a shouted crew count |
| **Riff** | a clean guitar figure alone **before the first word and after the last** (THE HUM TEST) |

⭐ **THE RETURN, spent not stripped.** This pair pays for P02's and P03's removed rhyme. Every verse is a
true ABAB quatrain pair; every chorus is byte-identical five times; every song opens and closes on the
same four-word chant. **A byte-identical chorus needs no defence and no note** — this line is the note
that says there will be no note.

# V1 — **TURN THE ROOM AROUND**
### angle: **THE LAST ONE ENDS** — the final performance finishes and the room changes function in ninety seconds

**Where is the body standing?** Downstage, deck, tabs just in, the house still emptying behind the iron.
Bag of screws by her boot, bar already down on the deck. **What could hurt it here?** She is on the show
relay when the last curtain call happens, and she calls the get-out over the top of it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Key / tempo** | E major, 126 BPM |
| **Crew (escalation)** | Mo · +Dez · +Rhi · +Fitz |
| **Hook** | *Hands on! / We turn the room around.* |
| **Chorus grammar** | crew call + plural declaration; the open beat sits before the last line |
| **Wolf event** | she calls the get-out on the relay with the house still clapping |
| ⭐ **What it costs her** | **she misses the last bow of the only thing she built this year** |
| **The one numeric fact** | **ninety seconds** — verse 3, at the hinge, spelled in words, and *responded to* by Rhi rather than recited |
| **Radiant (verse 4, last line)** | *An old stage manager told me once I could be the one to say when.* |
| **Seam** | second kit **in the pit**, Bex on baritone sax four bars behind, whole crew clapping on the floor — **she counts them in over the open relay mic, two taps of the bar on the deck** |
| **Return** | out the pass door into the cold; **the room behind them is a floor with tape on it, and nobody is looking at the floor** |
| **Source 2 (exact)** | `brightly-colored production` · `the distant rollerdisco claps` |

**Direction.** This is the fastest of the four and it should feel like a room changing use while you watch.
The verses are pure inventory — who is doing what, in what order — and the wolf arrives inside the
inventory, at exactly the same line length as everything around it. ⛔ **No production event on the wolf
line.** The band is playing the best Tuesday of the year and does not know that she missed the bow.

# V2 — **WE TAKE IT AT THE JOINT**
### angle: **SHE TAKES THE FIRST SWING** — the maker destroys it; strike night is a party

**Where is the body standing?** Three feet from the north end of the flat she built, bar low in the right
hand, four people standing still around her doing nothing. **What could hurt it here?** Nobody will start.
The crew is waiting, and they will wait all night, and the only way this becomes a party is if she goes
first at her own work in front of all of them.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Key / tempo** | A major, 126 BPM |
| **Crew (escalation)** | Nell · +Bear · +Sal · +Tam |
| **Hook** | *On three! / We take it at the joint.* |
| **Chorus grammar** | the count is the hook; *on three* is a real thing shouted by people holding weight |
| **Wolf event** | she puts the bar through the joint she cut herself, first, in front of everyone |
| ⭐ **What it costs her** | **she cannot hide behind the crew** — and the joint she opens is her best one, because that is where it comes apart cleanest |
| **The one numeric fact** | **two hundred screws** — verse 3, at the hinge, spelled in words; the response is the swing, not a comment |
| **Radiant (verse 4, last line)** | *The chippy who taught me said I was allowed to break what I make.* |
| **Seam** | second kit on the deck, Cass on it, whole crew clapping — ⭐ **her swing is the count-in: the bar hits the flat and the drummer takes that hit as the one** |
| **Return** | car park; **she is still holding the bar, and Nell takes it off her and puts it in the van** — *nobody says well done, nobody has to* |
| **Source 2 (exact)** | `a choppier flip of the buttery soul sample to a new drum pattern` · `a two-beer chill` |

**Direction.** The heaviest-footed of the four — floor tom leading, the stomp closest to the surface. The
first two verses are **stillness**: four people not moving, described plainly, which is funny and which
loads the third verse without a single word of build. ⭐ **The turn is at verse 3 and it is a complication,
not an escalation** — the song opens on *we* and discovers that *we* requires an *I* to start it.

# V3 — **THE GOOD ONE GOES FIRST**
### angle: **THE FLAT THAT TOOK THREE WEEKS** — the most-loved object goes first and fastest

**Where is the body standing?** At the bottom of the paint frame stairs with the running order in her
hand, looking up at the biggest flat in the building. **What could hurt it here?** She writes the order.
She could put the good one last and have it for another hour. She puts it first, because it is the biggest
and the truck loads biggest-first, and she does not make a speech about it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Key / tempo** | D major, 126 BPM |
| **Crew (escalation)** | Row · +Ig · +Vee · +Jax |
| **Hook** | *Heads up! / We take the good one first.* |
| **Chorus grammar** | *heads up* is a warning call, so the room shouts a warning at itself — plural, physical, no irony available |
| **Wolf event** | she puts the good flat at the top of her own get-out list |
| ⭐ **What it costs her** | **the time she chose not to take** — *"I could have kept it to the last"*, said once, flat, and then she gets on with it |
| **The one numeric fact** | **three weeks** — verse 3, at the hinge, spelled in words, answered by Vee's question rather than recited |
| **Radiant (verse 4, last line)** | *Somebody gave me a key to the paint frame and never asked for it back.* |
| **Seam** | second kit in the wings, Roo on baritone sax four bars behind, whole crew clapping in the room — **she taps the painted flat twice with the flat of her hand and the room claps on it** |
| **Return** | car park; **Jax cut the corner off the good one and it is face up on the dashboard** — nobody asked him to and nobody is taking it off him |
| **Source 2 (exact)** | `lush, aerodynamic beats that instantly hits cruising altitude` · `brightly-colored production` |

**Direction.** ⛔ **This is the song where the causal trap lives.** *Three weeks* and *first on the truck*
must sit next to each other as **two adjacent facts with `and` between them and nothing else.** Every draft
line that wanted to be *"and that's why it's beautiful"* is cut on sight. The correct emotional colour is
**pride, flatly delivered** — she names which corner is the good corner, in detail, because Ruthless
Specificity is the proof she was paying attention. ⭐ The souvenir at the end is **cheerful**, not
elegiac: somebody stole a bit and is pleased with himself.

# V4 — **ENGINES RUNNING**
### angle: **THE CAR PARK AT FOUR** — everyone still there, nobody going home yet

**Where is the body standing?** In a wet car park behind the dock door, in a T-shirt, in October, leaning
on a van that is ticking over with its doors open. **What could hurt it here?** Somebody has to go back
inside alone, walk the round, put the work-lights out and hand the keys over — and she has the keys.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Key / tempo** | G major, 126 BPM |
| **Crew (escalation)** | Del · +Bry · +Ness · +Pip |
| **Hook** | *Doors open! / We leave the engines running.* |
| **Chorus grammar** | the most literal plural declaration of the four; the physical thing is refusing to drive away |
| **Wolf event** | she takes the keys, goes back in alone, walks the round, kills the work-lights, locks it |
| ⭐ **What it costs her** | **she is the only one who has to be alone in it** — she can hear the whole crew shouting outside while she does it, and she does not linger and does not mention it again |
| **The one numeric fact** | **four in the morning** — verse 3, at the hinge, spelled in words, and the response is the walk-round |
| **Radiant (verse 4, last line)** | *The night porter told me once I could stay as long as I turned the key.* |
| **Seam** | a floor tom and snare on the **van tailgate**, Ollie on baritone sax, whole crew clapping round the vans — **she hits the tailgate twice with the flat of her hand and the kit comes in** |
| **Return** | ⭐ the return **is** the song; it closes on **nobody pulling out** — Pip turns the radio up, Ness sits back down on the ramp |
| **Source 2 (exact)** | `a two-beer chill` · `the distant rollerdisco claps` |

**Direction.** The warmest and the slowest-feeling of the four at the identical 126 — carried by air in the
arrangement rather than by tempo. ⭐ **The structural risk is that this song has nowhere to return TO**,
since it is already standing in the pair's assigned return. The fix is that the wolf sends her **back
inside**, so the song still goes all the way out (an empty dark building at four) and comes back somewhere
warmer (six idling vans and a radio). **F17's arc is intact and it happens inside one car park.**

| Gate | How each guide satisfies it |
