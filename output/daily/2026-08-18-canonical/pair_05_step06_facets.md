## 1. THE POSITION — what it *is*

Not "a company sold my data." That is a headline, and a headline is a status report.

**The position is:** *you already agreed to this, standing up, in ninety seconds, years ago, at a counter,
with a queue behind you — and the agreement outlived the company you made it with.* The other party to
your contract has died and its half of the contract is now owned by someone who has never heard of you and
does not need to.

Everyone has stood in this. The pre-ticked box. The "we've updated our terms" mail in the Promotions tab.
The password that still works, into a logo you don't recognise. The deletion request that succeeds
perfectly and changes nothing. **Nobody needs it explained. That is why it can stay plain.**

**Source facts** *(named here as research, never in a lyric)*:
* **F19 #2** — *"Google has acquired the data of failed US airline Spirit."* What outlives a dead company
  is its record of you.
* **F11** — complexes that **ligate a tag meaning READ THIS**, and proteases **whose only job is severing
  that one bond.** ⛔ **Translated out of the laboratory entirely and permanently** — a *flag on a file*
  and *a machine that removes exactly the one field you named and keeps the shelf it was sitting on.*
  ⛔ **No molecular vocabulary reaches any lyric.** That would be LOFN BLEED, which is a writing defect
  here, and F11's value is its SHAPE, not its nouns.

⛔ **NO REAL PERSON AS SUBJECT** (`vault/HUMAN_SUBJECT_STANDARD.md`). No company is named in any lyric.
The buyer is *"a logo I don't know."* The people are invented: a cleaner, a stranger with his shoes off,

## 2. THE SEVEN FACETS OF C06

**F-1 · THE GATE THAT IS STILL LIT.** The airline is a dead **company** — a bankruptcy, a lot, an auction
— and its screen is still on above an empty desk. ⛔ **NEVER AN ACCIDENT.** No crash, wreck, nosedive,
mayday, black box, last transmission, going down. The fleet **went to auction.** This is a hard line on
top of the run-wide exclusion, and it is swept in code at step 10.

**F-2 · THE TAG THAT MEANS READ THIS.** Somebody marked her file for attention, and the mark has a
timestamp on it, and the timestamp is a night she can remember from the inside.

**F-3 · THE BLADE THAT CUTS ONE BOND.** A machine in a building she will never stand in, brilliant at
exactly one job: it removes the precise thing she named and leaves everything that thing was attached to.
**She wins, completely, and it changes nothing, and there is nobody to be angry at because they complied.**

**F-4 · THE LINE ITEM.** In the filing she is between *ground equipment* and *gate leases*. Not a victim —
an **asset class**, and a cheap one.

**F-5 · THE OFFICIAL LANGUAGE IS BEAUTIFUL.** *High value segment. Special category. Where technically
feasible. Successors and assigns.* These are gorgeous phrases and a room can shout them. **The hook
grammar of this pair is contract language sung as a compliment** — and the room shouting it back at her
with total delight is the entire argument, delivered without one word of comment.

**F-6 · SHE SIGNED IT STANDING UP.** The radiant. Four different documents, four different counters, the
same posture: **upright, in a queue, pen already out, no chair anywhere.** *(Radiant Registry: mine alone.
⛔ No relative is the origin — no parent, no grandparent, nobody's family.)*

**F-7 · THE ROOM SHE IS IN WHILE SHE FINDS OUT.** Assigned, and shared with no other pair.

## 3. WHAT THE BODY DOES

Standing. Coat still on, over the arm by verse three. Phone at chest height, thumb moving. Weight on one
foot at a counter that has nobody behind it. **She is upright in every single one of these songs**, which
is the whole point of the radiant: *nobody has offered her a chair at any moment of this transaction, and
she has never once noticed that until now.*

Concrete physical events across the four:
* she puts her card against a door reader and it opens for a company that no longer exists
* she says her argument out loud, at three in the morning, to a cleaner who is kind and is not the person
* she takes her own photographs off her own phone with her own thumb
* she says *thank you* out loud, in an airport, at night, to nobody
* she **ticks yes** on a one-question satisfaction survey

⭐ **Every one of these is an ACTION, and every one of them costs her.** A status report is not an event.

## 5. THE FOUR MACHINES OF THIS PAIR *(mine, from step 05, nobody else's)*

**ARCHITECTURE — TWO-COLUMN.** Every verse alternates **what she was told** with **what was true**. No
connective. No comment. Eight lines, four of each. ⭐ **The seam is the ONE place the columns swap order —
true first, told second — and she notices**, by answering back for the only time in the song.

**RHYME — the official language rhymes and the fact does not.** End rhyme lives only in the *told* lines
(AABB across the verse). The *true* lines never rhyme. ⛔ **Never explained.** It is felt, not noticed
(THE REMARK TEST), and it is the argument in a form nobody has to parse.
⭐ **The exception is spent once, in V4, in the last two lines of the record.**

**HOOK — a phrase lifted from the terms, sung as if it were a compliment.** Four titles, four chants:
`HIGH VALUE SEGMENT` · `SPECIAL CATEGORY` · `WHERE TECHNICALLY FEASIBLE` · `SUCCESSORS AND ASSIGNS`.
**D8′ check:** the room can shout every one of these *at* her without it becoming false — **they are all
in the lot too.**

**THE HOLE — the instrument that never returns after the seam.** One element leaves when the clavinet
arrives and is simply absent afterwards, unremarked. Four different departures so the device does not
become a signature: **V1 the tambourine · V2 the handclaps · V3 the horn stabs · V4 the electric piano.**
⭐ Per THE GRAIN LAW the departure is written into the **form and the lyric**, not only the production
spec — the later section cues name only the surviving instruments, and in three of four the departure is
stated as a sung line. *"Somebody played a bar and went home." "The clap goes out and the room does it
themselves." "The horns get up and go." "The piano stops and does not start again."*

## 9. THE TWO ROT POINTS, checked against this concept

1. ⛔ **The tide gets a time and no adjectives.** My tide is a **schedule**. It is never personified,
   moralised or addressed. There is no second person aimed at a system, no *"they don't care."* The terms
   are quoted; they are never characterised.
2. ⛔ **"It is good, and it also melts" — neither is the reason for the other.** Nothing in this pair says
   the record is precious *because* it was sold, and nothing says the party is justified *because* of the
   anger. The good time and the loss are two adjacent facts with no connective, which is also, exactly,
   the two-column architecture. **The form is the ruling.**
