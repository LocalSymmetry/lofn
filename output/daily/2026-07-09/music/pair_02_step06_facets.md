## ESSENCE

A garage-punk kid — bratty, sincere, wheels still on, cracked amp buzzing in Drop-D — has just figured out that the whole world negotiates everything: every price has a number, every rule has an exception, "Finish the Job" is on the screen like it's a chore. So the kid does the one defiant, tender thing left: makes a *list of the lines that have no number.* Each stanza is one thing they won't do — barked like a power-chord, snotty and certain — *I won't read it, I won't sell it, I won't hit back, I won't become that.* The list is loud because loud is the only armor a kid's got. Then the list hits the one line it can't even say out loud, and instead of a bigger scream, **the band drops to true silence** — the un-crossable line is a hole, not a mosh — and in that hole the kid answers it soft: *not that. never that.* The list resumes, but breathed now, changed: because somewhere in the naming, the kid realizes the reason every line is held is the same reason — it's love, it's the last clean love they've got, and a refusal was a kind of love all along.

## WEIGHT / BINDING-CONSTRAINT NOTE
The binding constraint for this pair is **Facet 1 + Facet 2 together = 55%**: the song lives or dies on whether the *list is barked like a kid's armor* and whether the *un-crossable line is a real hole of silence* (not a bigger scream). Everything else (the reveal-as-love, the body-band, the refrain) serves those two. A draft can be a perfect pop-punk banger and still be a **corpse** if the list preaches or the crack fills with noise instead of dropping to true silence — the whole thesis (restraint, Flair 15) dies with a mosh-payoff.
