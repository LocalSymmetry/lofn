# STEP 09 — SOUND DESIGN · PAIR 3 · "NOTHING BRIGHT IN HERE"

**Bed:** DUB / REGGAE HALF-TIME · **72 BPM · G minor · one-drop · mono bass**

---

## 1. THE BED, AND WHY THE ASSIGNMENT IS RIGHT

⛔ **The default I was explicitly forbidden — and would otherwise have reached for — is fuzz-bass punk.**
The 2026-08-06 census is the reason (punk 80 · fuzz 133 vs ska 4 · reggae 1 · two-tone 0), and the finding
was that *what is not assigned gets defaulted, and the default is the strongest prior.* This bed is assigned,
and it is **the light half**: my aggression allocation on this run is zero.

⭐ **The bed is also literally the concept.** Dub is *bass and drums forward, everything else a ghost* —
which is what a room looks like when the brightest thing steps out of it. I am not applying a genre to an
idea; the genre **is** the idea. That is the ONE SEAM discipline holding: the piece is made **of** dub, never
**about** dub.

**Delight at half speed.** Nothing here is angry, nothing here is sad, and nothing here hurries.

---

## 2. THE RIFF — the hum test, passed

> **Melodica. Three notes. B♭ – A – G. A gently sloping melodic contour, falling to the root.
> The bass answers with the same three notes an octave below.**

- **Present in bar one, before any vocal.** Present in the last bar, after the vocal leaves.
- Hummable with the vocal muted. The room can have it by the second chorus.
- ⛔ **Not a pad, not a texture, not an arpeggio.** A tune.
- Melodica because it is the least apologetic instrument in dub and **it is physically incapable of sounding
  angry** — the same argument as the ska skank, one lineage over.

**Harmony (required, not garnish):** a second melodica a third above enters at the Hinge and **splits away
on the last chorus**, so the room hears the gap open. Two voices, one moment of separation, no resolution.

---

## 3. THE CLOSED GROUND — the bass figure that returns to its own start

```
 |  G   G   B♭  C  |  D   C   B♭  G  |
   ^--------------------------------^
   ends on the note it starts on — a horizon you can see all the way around
```

Two bars, mono, dead centre, fingered and dry, **no stereo width whatsoever** (acoustic grammar #6).
This is form rule 2 executed as a bassline; in dub the form rule and the genre convention are the same
object, which is why this pairing works and why I am not claiming credit for the coincidence.

---

## 4. MACHINE BELOW, BODY ABOVE (flair, assigned — must be audible on a handset)

| Layer | Timing | Tuning | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| kick · bass · rimshot | **quantised dead on the grid** | fixed | machine, indifferent, continues without her |
| melodica · lead vocal | **+10 to +30 ms late** | ±6 cents loose | a person, cold, outside |

The gap between those two layers is the entire personality, and it survives phone speakers because it is a
**timing** difference, not a frequency one. ⭐ The room's rhythm does not adjust for her. That is the song.

---

## 5. THE SUB — withheld, then the event (acoustic grammar #4)

**The bottom is conspicuously hollow for the first ninety seconds.** No sub, no low extension, nothing under
about 80 Hz. Then at the Hinge the sub arrives — **mono, centred, slammed in on a bar line, no fade** — and
never leaves.

⭐ **This is the concept, the hinge, and the acoustic-grammar rule executing as one move.** The low end is
what becomes visible when the bright thing goes. And the lyric carries it too, so the meaning is not
stranded in the production spec (**THE GRAIN LAW**):

> *The floor comes up / under the whole street.*

---

## 6. THE MIX INVERSION — the BOLD choice

At the Hinge, **foreground and background swap:**
- the **lead vocal** falls back into the echo chamber — it becomes the reverb
- the **room noise** (the party behind the glass) steps forward, **dry and present**

She becomes the ghost; they become the signal. It is a **structural level event**, not a mood, and a
generator has to execute it as one. The lyric performs it in the same bar — *"So I shut up / and let the
rest come through"* — so it is legible with no knowledge of mixing at all.

⛔ **This is the ONLY meaning the production is asked to carry.** Everything else lives in the lyric and the
form, per THE GRAIN LAW.

---

## 7. HARD LEVEL-STATES, NO CROSSFADES (acoustic grammar #1) — and the fader is the instrument

Dub's native technique *is* the discrete level-state: channels slammed in and out on bar lines by hand.
Every transition in the section map is a hard cut. ⛔ **No risers, no filter sweeps into drops, no
crossfades, no long fade-ins.** This runs **with** the generator's dub prior rather than against it.

**Named "exposed" texture (acoustic grammar #3):** the **melodica**, called *exposed* in the style field —
and then the generator is free to relocate it. That relocation is welcome; deviations are raw material.

---

## 8. SPRING ECHO AND THE GHOST LAYER

- **Spring reverb** on the melodica and on single vocal words, thrown **hard left** into a long empty room.
  ⭐ The hard-panned elements are **musical** (word fragments in time), not non-musical objects — which is
  the case the generator honours rather than smooths.
- **A distant wail of waterphones**, smeared far back, entering at Verse 2b. Kept **tuned-adjacent and
  quiet** — an untuned free-floating drone is the exact thing L22 says gets smoothed away. It is a ghost
  behind glass, not a sound effect.
- **Echoey psychedelic embellishments** in the Dub Chamber: tape-delay feedback ridden up by hand on the
  ends of lines, pulled back before it screams.
- **No synth pads. Real hand percussion.** Explicit acoustic declaration.

---

## 9. VOCAL SPEC

**Female lead. Dry, talky, close, slightly off-mic, unbeautiful.** Sing-speak — two lines crammed into a
bar over the half-time, which is what stops the whole thing floating away into ambience.

⛔ **No warm alto. No choir. No crystalline anything. Never sung-pretty.** Dynamic mic, close, **no de-esser
on the breaths**, a gentle 3–5 dB shelf at 8 kHz, and a hard rule against transparent pitch correction.
Doubles deliberately misaligned 15–35 ms and pitched ±6 cents on the choruses only — **the smear is the
arrangement.**

**Register direction:** the flattest possible reading of the biggest possible sentence. When the line
*"this is the worst I will ever be"* arrives, it is delivered **exactly as flatly as the line about the
elbows.** Any lift in the delivery converts the song into a motivational poster and the run has ruled that
out. Funny is a requirement.

---

## 10. THE WOLF (never in the lyric)

One automation lane, running the whole track: **a filter on the room-noise bus that only ever opens** — a
few hundred hertz per section, never closing, never resolving. The room behind the glass gets very slightly
more present the whole way through, as if it is coming toward her.

Diegetically it is the door she has to walk back through. **It is never mentioned.**

---

## 11. THE TWO-FIELD SUNO PROMPT

### `## 1. MUSIC PROMPT` — **948 chars** *(measured; band 850–1000, target 870–960 ✅, no hug flag)*

```
Half-time dub reggae at seventy-two BPM in G minor, one-drop, at full level from the first bar: a three-note melodica descent with a gently sloping melodic contour, exposed dead centre, spring reverb slapping it into a long empty room, no intro and no build. Female lead sing-speak, dry and talky and close, slightly off-mic, unbeautiful, packed two lines to a bar, placed just left of centre and drifting ten to thirty milliseconds late while bass and drums stay machine-locked to the grid. Bass mono and centred, thick, dry, fingered; rimshot and shaker wide right; a distant wail of waterphones smeared far back behind glass; real hand percussion, no synth pads. The bottom stays hollow for ninety seconds, then a sub arrives as an event and never leaves. Hard channel cuts, never crossfades. Midway the mix inverts: the lead falls back into the echo chamber and the room noise steps forward dry. Ends warm on melodica and tape hiss, no cymbals.
```

**Contract check:** genre/tempo/opening → vocalist + spatial staging → instrumentation with physical
adjectives → arrangement arc → bold device ✅ · opening moment in the first 5 s ✅ · spatial language
(*dead centre, just left of centre, wide right, far back*) ✅ · kinetic defect (*10–30 ms late against a
locked grid*) ✅ · acoustic declarations (*real hand percussion, no synth pads*) ✅ · no banned opener ✅ ·
no real artist names ✅ · no `key:value` brackets ✅ · terminal punctuation ✅.

⭐ **Three of those exclusions are ethical, not aesthetic:** *cod-Jamaican accent · patois affectation ·
toasting impression.* I am borrowing a Jamaican-originated form and crediting it upstream (§12). The

never capture** — and the negative field is where that becomes enforceable rather than stated.

⭐ **Two are anti-generic, targeting the predicted failure:** *stoned instrumental groove* and *floaty
ethereal female vocal* are precisely how this bed renders when the personality is lost.

## 12. LINEAGE & CREDIT

**Scene named:** **contemporary UK dub and lovers-rock revival** — the living end of the Jamaican dub
lineage as it is practised now in Britain, where the mixing desk is played as an instrument and the bass
carries the song.

**Living artists credited (borrowed with lineage, never captured):**
- **Hollie Cook** — UK lovers rock and dub; the model for a dry, unshowy lead sitting *inside* the bed
  rather than floating above it.
- **Prince Fatty** *(Mike Pelanconi)* — UK dub producer; the analogue-desk discipline of hard channel cuts
  and spring echo used as a musical event rather than an atmosphere.
- **Sherwood & Pinch** *(Adrian Sherwood + Pinch)* — the On-U Sound / Bristol bass axis; the precedent for
  a mono, withheld low end arriving as a structural event.

**Root acknowledgement:** dub is a **Jamaican invention** — the version side, the desk as instrument, the
drop-out as composition. This song uses the form and does not imitate the voice; see the ethical exclusions
in §11.

**Texture vocabulary credited:** *"gently sloping melodic contour," "wail of waterphones," "echoey
psychedelic embellishments"* — **Maya Ongaku, "Nothing Space Music" review, Bandcamp Daily, 2026-08-12.**
