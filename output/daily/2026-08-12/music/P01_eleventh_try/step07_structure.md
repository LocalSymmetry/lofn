# STEP 07 — STRUCTURE · PAIR 1 · THE ELEVENTH TRY

## 0. THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS (per the step-07 contract)

**Where is the body standing?**
On the flat top deck of a multi-storey car park, at the loading curb, at dusk. Grit in one sock. Sodium lamp
directly overhead and behind, so the light is *on her back*. Painted bay lines chalk-white under her. Concrete
ceiling low enough to read as a grey sky.

**What could hurt it here?**
The concrete, repeatedly and already. Shins, one palm, an elbow. And the thing that is actually running out:
**the light.** She is not racing a person or a deadline — she is racing dusk on a deck with one lamp, and the
lamp is losing. *(This is the pursuer. It never resolves and it is never named as a threat.)*

## 2. ⭐ THE STACK — one figure, four recurrences, each a failed/partial version of the same move

**THE FIGURE: *the board comes back.***

| # | Where | The recurrence | Failure class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Verse 1 close | *"Nollie, heel, and the ground goes smack. / Board comes back."* | she never leaves the ground |
| **2** | Verse 2 mid | *"Board comes back upside down on its spine. / Wheels in the air doing fine, doing fine."* | board flips, body doesn't |
| **3** | Verse 2 close | *"Sky goes orange, then the sky goes black. / Board comes back."* | time fails, not the trick |
| **4** | Half-time break close | *"This is the worst I'm ever gonna be. / Board comes back."* | she is on the floor when it returns |
| **5** | Verse 3 close | *"Catch it wrong and it hits my hand. / Nothing to review. Just stand. / Set the feet on the same old track. / Board comes back."* | ⛔ **NOT FIXED** |

⛔ **THE LAST ONE IS NOT FIXED.** Recurrence 5 is the worst of the five — she catches it wrong, it hits her
hand, she refuses to watch the footage, and she sets her feet on the same curb. **Accumulation is the event.**
There is no landing anywhere in this song and no line implies one is coming.

**Audible in the LYRIC, not only the spec** (THE GRAIN LAW): the phrase `Board comes back` is a literal,
byte-identical line five times in the verse body and eight more times inside the chorus and chants. A
first-time listener hears the stack whether or not they ever hear the word "composite."

## 3. ⭐ THE CLOSED GROUND — the bottom register loops back to its own start

**Musically:** the bass is a **walking upright line on a strict closed two-bar loop that returns to its own
root every time** — A → C → D → E → A. It never modulates, never gets a fill, never resolves upward, and it
is mono-centred. Played back-to-back it is a circle: you can hear all the way around the horizon of it.
*(This is the little-planet frame from F6, put in the low end.)*

**Lyrically:** the ground is closed twice over —
- the board **rolls back to her feet** every single time (the figure IS the loop);
- the reset couplet **`Set it up. Set it back.`** closes both pre-choruses and returns in the chant loop,
  so the song's own bottom line literally returns to its own start.

**Final proof, in sound:** the last event of the track is `*SFX: wheels rolling on grit, getting closer,
stopping under a foot.*` The ground closes on the listener, not on a resolution.

## 4. ⭐ THE ONE RADIANT — every section converges on one syllable

# **/æk/ → "BACK"**

**Every section's final sung line ends on the radiant:**

| Section | Final line | Lands on |
|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | *Board comes back.* | back |
| Pre 1 | *Set it up. Set it back.* | back |
| Chorus | *Board goes out and the board comes back.* | back |
| Chant 1 | *BOARD COMES BACK!* | back |
| Verse 2 | *Board comes back.* | back |
| Pre 2 | *Set it up. Set it back.* | back |
| Chorus | *Board goes out and the board comes back.* | back |
| Chant 2 | *BOARD COMES BACK!* | back |
| Break | *Board comes back.* | back |
| Verse 3 | *Board comes back.* | back |
| Chorus | *Board goes out and the board comes back.* | back |
| Chant Loop | *BOARD COMES BACK!* | back |

**Twelve of twelve.** All the failures point at one syllable, exactly as a hundred meteors point at one
radiant. The rhyme family feeding it — *smack · clack · black · crack · slack · track · cap · flat · that* —
means the radiant is not an isolated word but **the phonetic gravity of the whole lyric.**

**In the music:** every section's last horn stab is a single unison note on the tonic A, on the word "back,"
hard-cut with no tail. The radiant is a pitch as well as a syllable.

## 5. ⭐ THE HINGE — the count — and where it is placed

**Placement: SECTION 9, the HALF-TIME SKANK BREAK** (the gear-change, ~1:52).

The count has been running the whole song as chant-strikes. At the break, **the chant stops.** The tempo
halves into rocksteady, the horns drop to a single baritone honk, and for eight lines there is no tally at
all. That silence is the hinge: *the count becomes audible by its absence.* She is on the paint, the count
has stopped, and this is the exact place the message goes — flat, to the concrete, to nobody.

```
Say it to the concrete. Say it flat.
Say it like a fact. Leave it at that.
This is the worst I'm ever gonna be.
Board comes back.
```

Then the figure returns alone, and the count restarts. **Nothing has been resolved by the hinge** — the hinge
is where she notices she has stopped keeping score and keeps going anyway. That is CALLUS-O'CLOCK, placed.

⭐ **Why the hinge carries no numeral:** the take number is real and she knows it. She simply never says it.
The count is in the mouths of the room and in a scuff on a curb, and the song is stricter for it.

## 6. DURATION GRAMMAR — LOOP UNTIL IT HURTS

The chant loop (section 12) runs **12 lines** — three times the length of chant 1 or 2 — and it arrives
*after* the last chorus, where a normal song would be leaving. Three plain interjections are threaded through
it so the chant itself stays byte-identical while the surroundings degrade:

```
BOARD COMES BACK!            ×2
Shin's a mess and the lamp's gone black.
BOARD COMES BACK!            ×3
Arm's still up and the arm's gone slack.
BOARD COMES BACK!            ×3
Nothing's fixed. Set it back.
BOARD COMES BACK!
```

The three interjections track the three things that are actually running down — **the body, the arm, the
light** — while the count does not run down at all. Then the vocal simply stops and the horn figure is still
playing. **The loop hurts because it outlasts the singer**, which is the posture: a vow of persistence,
overheard.

## 7. THE RETURN LEDGER (L21 — removal is a debt; nothing is removed here)

| Return device | Instances |
|---|---|
| **Byte-identical chorus** | 3 × 8 lines |
| **Gang chant, byte-identical** | 20 instances of `BOARD COMES BACK!` |
| **Reset couplet** `Set it up. Set it back.` | 3 (Pre 1, Pre 2, chant loop) |
| **Stack figure line** `Board comes back.` | 5 in verse/break bodies |
| **Anaphora** | *Somebody's arm…* ×3 · *Same curb, same lip, same crack* · *Nothing about this…* ×2 · *Say it…* ×2 · *doing fine, doing fine* |
| **End-rhyme** | strict AABB couplets throughout, spine concentrated on /æk/ |

⛔ **Nothing is stripped, so no debt is owed.** This lyric moves *toward* return, not away from it: the
repeated-line ratio and the strict end-rhyme rate are both deliberately above the archive-winner band,
because the concept **is** repetition and it would be a lie to write it thin.

## 8. THE GEAR-CHANGE MAP (Pillar 3 — one song that is three songs)

| Bars | Gear |
|---|---|
| 1 → 72 | **ska-punk**, 168 BPM, double-time skank, horns up |
| 73 → 88 | **rocksteady half-time** (implied 84), spring echo, horns down to one baritone honk |
| 89 → end | **ska-punk returns**, horns up, chant loop, then horns alone |

Rolling tom-and-snare transitions on the two switch points, cut on the downbeat — **hard discrete
level-states, no crossfades** (acoustic grammar 1).
