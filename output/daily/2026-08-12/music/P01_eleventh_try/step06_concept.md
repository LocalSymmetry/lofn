# STEP 06 — CONCEPT · PAIR 1 · THE ELEVENTH TRY
**Voice:** SUNNA (Pantheon seat 2) · **Arm:** ACCESSIBLE · **Axis B:** EXISTENCE
**Genre bed (assigned, not optional):** SKA-PUNK / TWO-TONE SKANK WITH HORNS
**Sound/Return axis:** gang-chant return · **Hinge:** the count · **Duration grammar:** loop until it hurts
**Sung numeral:** NONE · **Verse architecture:** short declarative couplets, breath-length lines, even 4-line stanzas

---

## 1. THE SITUATION (not the sentence)

> **The flat top deck of a multi-storey car park, dusk. One body. The same trick. Somebody is filming.
> The falls are the footage. She never lands it.**

The situation is doing the thinking, and the situation is one everybody has stood in and nobody has a word
for: **you have been bad at a thing in front of a witness for long enough that the being-watched part has
worn off.** The friend holding the phone is still holding the phone. The arm is shaking now. The lamp came
on. The sky went orange and then it went out. Nothing has been solved and nobody has left.

**What the listener has to recognise, and does, instantly:** the point in an afternoon where you stop
performing the attempt and start *working* it. Same body, same curb, completely different weather inside.

⛔ **She never lands it in the song.** No landing exists in this piece. The eleventh try is not shown and is
not implied — the song ends with the board rolling back under her foot and the horns still going.

---

## 2. ⭐ THE NAMED OBSCURE EMOTION

> ## **CALLUS-O'CLOCK**
> *n. — the exact minute in a session when the shame scabs over and the thing you are failing at stops being
> a humiliation and becomes a shift you are working. Not relief. Not resolve. A clock-in. The audience in
> your head does not leave; it stops being an audience and becomes a timer.*

**Why this one and not a generic:** "embarrassment," "determination," "grit" are all *outcomes*.
CALLUS-O'CLOCK is a **transition with a timestamp** — it happens at a minute, it has a physical metaphor
(dead skin over a wound is not healing, it is *tooling up*), and it is neither positive nor negative, which
is why no existing word covers it. It is also structurally correct for the run's ruling: a person at
callus-o'clock **has no evidence about the outcome** and is not asking for any.

⛔ **The word never appears in the prompt, the lyric, or any Suno field.** It is enacted, never named. Its
lyric surface is one flat line: *"Everything hurts in a boring sort."*

---

## 3. ⭐ THE ONE DEVICE — IN FOUR WORDS

> # **"THE CHANT COUNTS UP."**

The gang chant **is** the tally. Every return of `BOARD COMES BACK` is one more take. The room does the
counting with its mouth, and **no number is ever said.** This single device resolves five constraints at
once and is why nothing else needed inventing:

| Constraint | How the one device answers it |
|---|---|
| SOUND/RETURN = gang-chant return | the chant is the primary return, byte-identical every time |
| HINGE = the count | the count *is* the chant; it has no other carrier |
| SUNG NUMERAL = none | a tally made of repetitions cannot contain a numeral |
| DURATION GRAMMAR = loop until it hurts | the final chant loop is literally the count getting long |
| CLOSED GROUND (form rule 2) | "comes back" is a loop returning to its own start, said out loud |

**Four-word nameability test:** *the chant counts up.* ✅ One device. Not six.

## 4. THE TWELVE SUBSTANTIVE FACETS

1. **PLACE (Golden Move 1 — stand somewhere real):** the flat top deck of a multi-storey car park at dusk;
   bay lines, a loading curb with a chipped lip, a sodium lamp that buzzes on before it is actually dark.
2. **BODY:** shins, palm, one elbow with a red patch, a bruise "like a bottle cap," breath gone short.
3. **THE SECOND BODY (D2 — the phone written like a hand):** somebody's arm, holding the frame flat, going
   from steady to shaking to slack. **The phone is never a villain and never a subject — it is an arm that
   is tired.** No line asks anyone to put anything down.
4. **THE TALLY:** a scuff on the curb where she keeps the count. *"Curb's got a scuff where I keep the
   clock."* The count is a physical mark, so it never has to be a number.
5. **THE TRICK:** one jargon word only — *nollie, heel* — for credibility. Everything else is plain
   ("the board," "the deck," "the curb," "the line"). Golden Move 6: the surface names its subject.
6. **THE STACK FIGURE:** *the board comes back.* It returns four times, each a different failure, and the
   last is **not fixed** (see step 07).
7. **THE RADIANT:** the syllable **/æk/ → "BACK."** Every section's final line lands on it. Its rhyme family
   (back · smack · clack · black · crack · slack · track · stack) is the phonetic spine of the whole song.
8. **THE MESSAGE, positioned:** said flat on the paint, in the half-time break, **to the concrete** —
   *"Say it to the concrete. Say it flat. / Say it like a fact. Leave it at that."* Still inside the failure.
   Nothing resolved. Offered to nobody. We overhear it. ✅ D8, ✅ the tense ruling.
9. **THE POSTURE (threat + comfort, never picks):** the threat is carried by *persistence*, never by an
   address — *"Nothing's fixed. Set it back."* There is no version of this that stops, and nobody is told so.
10. **THE PURSUER (SOLAR "A" — bright with something gaining):** the light. The lamp comes on, the sky goes
    orange, then black. She loses the light, not the will. Never named as a threat, never resolved.
11. **THE WOLF (production, never in the lyric):** the sub, absent until the first chorus, then creeping a
    semitone across the rest and never arriving anywhere.
12. **THE REGISTER:** dry, eye-rolling, funny by requirement. *"Everything hurts in a boring sort."*
    *"Nothing to review."* ⛔ Never earnest, never reverent, never explaining.

## 5. THE TEN BANS — HOW THIS CONCEPT CLEARS EACH

| Ban | Clearance |
|---|---|
| D1 no adult in the room | there is no second speaker; nobody comments on her; nothing is a lesson |
| D2 phone is not the villain | the phone appears **only** as an arm that tires. ⛔ zero banned tokens |
| D3 generation not a subject | one body, one afternoon, one curb |
| D4 no real person | invented body, invented friend; the ska credits are a **lineage block**, not characters |
| D5 present tense only | every sung line is present. The message's future clause is grammatical, not epistemic |
| D6 cohort gate | ⛔ zero instances of *we / you / young people / this generation / kids / everyone / everybody* |
| D7 AI is not the cure | no machine helps; the only device is a phone held by a shaking arm |
| D8 overhearing not addressing | she says it *to the concrete*, explicitly: *"Not to anybody."* |
| D9 the tape is not redeemed | **"Nothing to review."** She never watches it back. The keeping is the act |
| D10 substitution not subtraction | rhyme is not stripped — it is **concentrated** onto one spine (/æk/) |

**Run-specific:** ⛔ no Colombia material · ⛔ no AI-displacement material · ⛔ nothing about an AI improving.

## 6. THE FLAT TEST (Sunna's word-channel gate)

Said out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face:

- *"Everything hurts in a boring sort."* → survives. It is **simple**, not merely short.
- *"Nothing to review."* → survives, and it is the funniest line in the song.
- *"Somebody's arm is not built for that."* → survives.
- *"Say it like a fact. Leave it at that."* → survives.

⭐ **THE FREE CHANNEL:** all the complexity is in the horn figure, the closed walking bass, the tiring hat
against an on-grid kick, and the gang chant. **The words stay plain and continue the story instead of
interpreting it.** A listener who takes only the surface receives a whole song: *a girl keeps trying a trick
in a car park until it gets dark, and her mate's arm gets tired.* That is complete. Everything else is free.
