# STEP 09 — SOUND DESIGN · PAIR 6 · **TAKE A HUNDRED**

---

## 1. THE BED — power-pop / sixties girl-group over a jungle break

**172 BPM · G major · vocal in half-time against the break.**

The break is Nia Archives-lineage jungle: chopped amen, ride-forward, rolling. Over it sits a **1963 girl-group
record** — handclaps on the backbeat, tambourine, a major-key three-note guitar figure, and stacked shoutable
harmony. ⛔ **Not a club record with a guitar on it.** The girl-group layer is not pastiche and not irony:
**stacked girl-group harmony is a sixty-year-old technology for turning one voice into many**, and this song
needed exactly that before it knew the genre existed.

⛔ **NOT fuzz-bass punk. NOT rage.** The 2026-08-06 census (punk 80 · fuzz 133 vs ska 4 · two-tone 0) is the
failure this assignment exists to prevent. Aggression is a colour, not a bed. This one is **light, fast and
stackable.**

---

## 2. ⭐ THE HUM-TEST FIGURE — the hook with the vocal muted

**Three-note descending guitar figure** — *"gently sloping melodic contour"* — clean-ish, bright-ish, single
coil, played with a plectrum you can hear. **D5 → B4 → G4.** Doubled by a mono-centred bass that plays the
first two notes and ⭐ **drops the third.**

> **The riff has a hole in it, and the hole is exactly where the vocal crack lands.**

✅ Bar one, before any word. ✅ After the last word. ✅ Hummable alone. ⛔ Not a pad.

---

## 3. ⭐ THE STACK — how the composite is made audible

The **harmony count is the take count.** Never a choir; always **doubles of the same close, dry voice.**

| Section | Voices | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 / Chorus 1 | **1** | bone dry, close, centre |
| Verse 2 / Chorus 2 | **2** | second double misaligned **15–35 ms**, detuned **±6 cents** |
| Hinge | 2 → 4 | the split — two voices separate and **the gap is audible** |
| Verse 3 | 4 → 6 | stacking thirds, still narrow, still dry |
| Chorus stack | **4 → 8 → 8** | maximum stack; ⛔ **stays narrow, never widens** |
| Last Take | **1** | everything falls off. One throat. |

⛔ **HARD RULE — the anti-generic clause.** Stacked = **the same singer, copied, misaligned, detuned, narrow,
unpolished, audibly cracking.** The generator's default for "sixties girl-group harmony" is a wide, sweet,
pitch-corrected choir, and that default would destroy the song three ways at once: it breaks Sunna's
never-sung-pretty / no-choir rule, it turns a hundred takes of **one body** into a hundred **people** (D6),
and it makes the stack sound *intentional* — which deletes the emotion, because the whole feeling depends on
nobody having meant to build this.

---

## 4. ⭐ THE ENFORCED SECOND — built, not described

An **ascending synth runs a five-beat cycle against the break's four** — *"subtle polymetric overlap of the
ascending synth"* — so it walks out of phase and re-syncs on a long cycle.

**On the re-sync:** ⛔ everything mutes. **One handclap, dead centre, bone dry. Every stacked voice on one
word: KEEP. One beat.** Hard cut in, hard cut out, no reverb tail, no crossfade.

> ⭐ A hundred separate streaks reading as one sky for exactly one instant — **and then the lag re-opens and
> they scatter again.** The alignment is not a resolution. It is a coincidence that everything was pointed
> the same way once.

---

## 5. ⭐ THE FIXED LAG — sung, never a plug-in

A **written second vocal part, one bar behind, start to finish.** Same words, same person, permanently late.
It sings **through** the chorus, **through** the hinge, and it is ⛔ **still open at the end.**

Motivated by the situation (a concrete stairwell throws your voice back at you late) so the listener never
has to register it as a technique. ⛔ **No delay unit, no slapback, no ping-pong.** If it is a plug-in, it is
one voice with an effect. It has to be **two takes of a person**, because that is the subject.

**Counter-melody that argues:** in the hinge, the lag voice **climbs to the note the lead keeps missing —
and lands it.** The lead still doesn't. Two timelines, one of which is better, and it changes nothing.

---

## 6. ⭐ THE WOLF — one lane, never resolves, never mentioned

**The throat.** The vocal chain drifts *sandblasted* across the entire track: progressively drier, grittier,
more air, more edge, more break-up at the top. **It never recovers and it never comes back down.** Running
alongside it, the ascending synth's filter **only ever opens.**

⛔ Neither is referenced in the lyric. The listener feels the voice being spent without being told.

---

## 7. PERCUSSION & TEXTURE

- **The break:** chopped amen, ride-forward, rolling, dry. Ghost notes intact. Human drag behind the grid.
- ⭐ *"Rattling assortment"* — tambourine, shaker and one dry vibraslap riding the break in the verses.
- **Handclaps:** backbeat, real hands, room-close. ⭐ **Named "exposed": the exposed handclap** — hard centre,
  no reverb, louder than is wise. *(Acoustic grammar 3 — name one texture exposed and let the generator
  relocate it.)*
- ⭐ **Sub: WITHHELD entirely until the hinge.** No sub-bass at all in Verse 1, Chorus 1, Verse 2, Chorus 2 or
  The Lag. It lands on *"A hundred"* **as the event.** Mono-centred, no stereo sub.
- ⛔ No supersaw wall. No white-noise riser into any drop. No airhorn. No generic trap hats.

---

## 8. LEVEL-STATES — hard discrete, ⛔ no crossfades

| State | Sections | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Riff, Riff-out | break + guitar figure + exposed handclap, **no sub** |
| **B** | Verses, The Lag | break thins to amen ride; rattling assortment; riff continues; **no sub** |
| **C** | Choruses | full; handclaps doubled; stacked voices; ascending synth begins its five-cycle |
| **D** | Hinge | ⭐ **sub arrives**; counter-melody enters; vocal splits |
| **E** | Enforced Second | ⛔ **one clap, one word, one beat.** Everything else muted |
| **F** | Last Take, out | one voice, riff, tape saturation; ascending synth **gone**. ⭐ **Warm, never bright.** |

Every transition is an **instant switch on a bar line.** No risers, no filter sweeps, no fades.

---

## 9. VOCAL SPEC

**Female. Alto-to-mezzo, chest-dominant, talky.** Sing-speak, rhythmic, playground taunt one bar and a flat
threat the next. Dynamic mic, close, **breaths left in and not de-essed**, gentle 3–5 dB shelf at 8 kHz.

⛔ **NO transparent pitch correction. NO warm alto. NO choir. NO crystalline anything. NEVER sung-pretty.**
⭐ **The cracks stay in.** They are the subject; a clean take is a different song.

- **Chant range G3–D4** — shoutable at volume while out of breath.
- **The reach that cracks: G5**, hit from below, ⛔ **never landed, not even at the end.**

---

## 10. MIX

- Bass **mono-centred**, sub mono, no stereo low end.
- Vocal stack **narrow** — ⛔ never widens into the chorus. *(The 2026-08 render ledger has stereo narrowing
  into a climax as a technique; here it is the instruction, because a hundred of one person is not wide.)*
- Crowd bed: none. **She is alone in a stairwell.** Room tone only.
- Plate: short, 0.9–1.4 s, on the handclaps only. ⛔ Never a hall on the voice.
- Master **−14 LUFS integrated, ≥10 dB crest.** Weight, not loudness.
- **End warm:** tape saturation, top rolled off, the bright synth gone before the last bar.

## 13. DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK

**What this prompt actually produces, first 5 seconds:** a chopped amen break already at full level with no
intro, a dry exposed handclap hard in the centre, a three-note guitar figure sloping down over it, and
**no low end at all**. At bar five a close, flat, talky female voice enters on the beat, unpretty and
unprocessed; one bar later a second identical voice starts the same line and never catches up.

**⛔ The one way this renders generic:** the generator takes "sixties girl-group" and delivers **polished wide
sweet stacked vocals — a choir** — smoothing exactly the thing the song is made of.

**REPAIR, applied once:** (a) the style field specifies *doubles of one voice, misaligned and detuned, narrow,
never widening, cracks left in*; (b) the EXCLUDE field names *choir, gospel choir, angelic, polished stacked
vocals, pitch-corrected harmony, wide reverb-drenched vocals, multiple different singers, children's choir*;
(c) the lyric itself says ***"a hundred of me"*** so the semantics fight the default even if the tokens slip.
✅ Applied in step 10.
