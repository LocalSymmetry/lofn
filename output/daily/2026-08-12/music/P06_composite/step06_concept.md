**Run:** `2026-08-12_genz-worst-i-will-ever-be` · **Voice:** SUNNA (Pantheon, second seat)

## 0. THE JOB, STATED PLAINLY

Every other pair in this run **applies** THE COMPOSITE as a form rule. Mine is **about** it. That is not a
promotion, it is a trap: the pair that is about the form rule is the pair most likely to write an essay with
handclaps on it. So the ruling for this pair, before any line is written:

> ⭐ **Yours must be the most PHYSICAL and least conceptual of the six.**
> The listener feels the stacking **in the chest**. Nobody recognises a metaphor. Nobody is told anything.

Sunna's version of that instruction, which is the same instruction: *depth lives in the SITUATION, never in
the SENTENCE.* So the job is **not** "write about a composite." The job is **find one body doing one physical
thing badly, a hundred times, in one room, and let the room be a closed circle.**

---

## 1. ⭐ THE NAMED EMOTION — **STACKSTARE**

*(Named here per brief. ⛔ Never appears in the prompt text, never appears in the lyric.)*

> **STACKSTARE** *(n.)* — the flat-footed lurch when a hundred separate misses stop being a **count** and
> become a **volume**. You were keeping score. It turns out you were building. It is not pride: pride needs
> a good one, and there is no good one. It is not shame either — shame needs an audience and there isn't one.
> It is the specific vertigo of meeting **a thing you made without meaning to make it**, assembled entirely
> out of evidence you were bad.

Why not a generic emotion: "pride," "triumph," "nostalgia" and "awe" all require the pile to be **redeemed**
by an outcome. STACKSTARE has no outcome. The last attempt is exactly as bad as the first. What changed is
the *viewing distance*, and the vertigo is that changing the viewing distance changed the object.

Taxonomy anchors (`EMOTION_TAXONOMY.md`, for the EMO headers): **Disorientation · Revelation · Delight ·
Defiance · Amazement · Frustration · Glee · Stupefaction.**

---

## 2. ⭐ THE ONE DEVICE — four words

> ### **"THE SAME TAKE, STACKED."**

One device. Not six. It runs on two channels at once, which is why it is still *one* device:

- **In the lyric:** the same physical move recurs, each time failed *differently*, never corrected.
- **In the music:** each recurrence **adds a voice**. The harmony count *is* the take count. One becomes two
  becomes many. ⭐ **Stacked girl-group harmony is literally the composite made audible.**

⛔ **The last one is NOT fixed.** She still cracks on the last one. That is the rule this song cannot fudge,
and it is D9 keeping its teeth: *the tape is not redeemed — the keeping is the act.*

---

## 3. THE SITUATION (Golden Move rule 1 — stand somewhere real)

**Where is the body standing?** A concrete stairwell, some hour that isn't a number. She is there because the
reverb is free. Phone wedged in the handrail. She is going for **one note, on one word**, and it cracks.

**What could hurt it here?** Her throat. It is the instrument and she is spending it. Every take is paid for
out of the same account, and the account is audibly emptying — the voice gets grittier across the song and
never recovers. *(⭐ That is THE WOLF: it never resolves and it is never mentioned in the lyric.)*

**Why a stairwell and not a bedroom:** ⭐ a stairwell is a **loop that returns to its own start** — you can
walk it all the way around and arrive at the same landing. **The stairwell is the little-planet projection.**
The horizon curved into a closed circle is not a metaphor here, it is the floor plan. I never say so.

## 4. ⭐ THE THREE COMPOSITE RULES — where each one is actually BUILT

| Rule | Where it lives (lyric or form — never only the spec) |
|---|---|
| **1. STACK** — one figure recurs ≥3×, each a failed/partial version, **last not fixed** | ⭐ **LYRIC + FORM.** The figure is *"went for the top and missed it."* It recurs in every verse, missing a different way each time (split it · hit it wrong · came in under · ran out of air). **Verse stanzas double: 2 lines → 4 lines → 8 lines.** The harmony doubles with them. Final chorus: still cracking. |
| **2. CLOSED GROUND** — bottom register loops back to its own start | ⭐ **FORM + SITUATION.** A four-bar bass figure that ends on the note it began on — you cannot hear the seam. The stairwell is the same loop in architecture. The last bar of the song is the first bar of the song. |
| **3. ONE RADIANT** — everything converges on one syllable | ⭐⭐ **LYRIC.** The radiant is **"KEEP."** One syllable, hard consonant both ends, shoutable out of breath. Every failed line **rebounds into it**. A hundred meteors point at one spot; a hundred misses point at *keep*. And it is the run's own D9 compressed to four letters: she does not fix the take, **she keeps it.** |

## 5. ⭐ SOUND / RETURN (primary channel) — **REBOUND RHYME ON BEAT 3**

The rhyme does **not** wait for the end of the line. It lands **early — on the third stress** — and the line
keeps going past it into a one-word tail. The tail is always the radiant.

```
Went for the top and I MISSED it.  KEEP.
Went for it flat and I KISSED it.  KEEP.
         (rhyme on stress 3) ──┘    └── tail = the radiant
```

Two effects, both physical, neither requiring the listener to notice anything:
1. The line **trips forward** — it feels like an attempt that overshoots, which is the subject.
2. Each stanza adds another rebound, so the hits **accumulate off the beat**. Meaning is in the mouth.

**L21 THE RETURN — what returns:** the rhyme pairs return (missed/kissed, split it/hit it), the tail returns
every single line, the chorus returns **byte-identical**, and the riff returns before the first word and
after the last. Nothing is stripped, so no debt is owed.

---

## 6. THE HINGE — **THE DOUBLE** · and the one sung numeral

At the hinge three things happen on the same beat:
1. The vocal **doubles** and the room hears the gap open (two voices, split, not unison).
2. ⭐ **The sub arrives** — withheld for the entire opening, it lands here as the event *(acoustic grammar 4)*.
3. ⭐ The one sung numeral lands: **"a hundred"** — spelled in words, **responded to, never recited.**

```
Scroll it back. A hundred. A hundred of me.
Same reach, same crack, same wall.
One at a time it's a mistake.
All at once it's a sky.
```

That is the whole idea and it is four plain lines long. It passes **THE FLAT TEST** — say it to a face with no
music and it survives. It does not explain the form rule; it reports what is on the screen. And it does not
repair anything: she still cannot hit the note after it.

⭐ **D6 DEFENCE, BUILT INTO THE LINE:** *"a hundred of me."* My pair is the most exposed to the cohort gate,
because "all the attempts at once" pulls hard toward a plural of *people*. The lyric forecloses it in the
hinge itself. **A hundred takes, one body.** In production: **stacked doubles of the same voice — never a
choir, never other singers.** That is D6 and Sunna's own no-choir ban satisfied by the same instruction.

---

## 7. THE TWO FLAIRS — both structural, neither decorative

**⭐ THE FIXED LAG** *(two continuous voices, one permanently behind by a set interval — SUNG, not a plug-in)*
A real second vocal part sings the same lines **one bar behind**, start to finish. Two takes of one person,
offset, running together. **That is the composite as a duet.** The stairwell echo is the situation that
motivates it; the arrangement makes it a written part, never a delay unit.

**⭐ THE ENFORCED SECOND** *(a moment the arrangement insists is happening at the same instant for everyone)*
An ascending synth runs a **five**-beat cycle against the break's **four** — *"subtle polymetric overlap of
the ascending synth"* — so it slides out of phase and re-syncs on a long cycle. **When it re-syncs, the
arrangement stops everything on that one beat: one clap, every stacked voice, one word — KEEP.** The lag
closes for exactly one second. Then it slides apart again.

> ⭐⭐ **THAT is a hundred separate streaks reading as one sky for one instant — built into the music, not
> explained in a lyric.** And the lag coming back afterwards is the last one not being fixed.

---

## 8. THE HUM TEST — the figure that IS the hook

⛔ A pad is not a riff. The instrumental hook is a **three-note descending guitar figure** — *"gently sloping
melodic contour"* — doubled by a bass that **drops the third note.** Guitar plays three, bass plays two.

⭐ **The riff has a hole in it, and the hole is where the vocal crack goes.** Present in bar one before any
word, present after the last word. Hummable with the vocal muted. Sunna's own test case — *the exact moment
the bassline drops the third note and everyone knows it before it happens* — is the mechanism, not a nod.

**Harmony as a required instrument:** stacked thirds on the chant; a counter-melody that **argues** with the
lead (it goes UP where she goes down, which is the note she keeps failing to reach, sung by the double who
gets it — and she still doesn't). One moment where two voices split and the room hears the gap open.

## 9. THE MESSAGE · THE RULING ON TENSE · THE POSTURE

**"This is the worst I will ever be"** — a **DECISION, not a PREDICTION**, spoken **from the floor,
mid-failure.** She is in a stairwell with a shot throat on take one hundred and it still cracks. She has no
evidence. She says it to nobody. We overhear it. ✅ D8.

⭐ **THE POSTURE — threat and comfort in the same breath, and the song never picks:**

> **"There's no last one."**

Four syllables. Read as comfort: *I don't have to make this one count.* Read as a threat: *there is no
version of me you get to stop watching.* It is native to the situation (a last **take**), so it never becomes
an aphorism, and it picks neither reading — **the song does not adjudicate.** That is the run's thesis and
this pair carries it in three words.

⛔ Checked against the FAILS list: nothing has resolved · she does not know the outcome · nothing is offered
outward as comfort · there is no adult in the room. Present tense throughout (D5).

## 10. GENRE BED & THE ANGER AUDIT

**POWER-POP / SIXTIES GIRL-GROUP OVER A JUNGLE BREAK.** Handclaps, tambourine, major-key hooks, harmonies
she can shout, riding a Nia Archives-lineage break at ~165–174 BPM with the vocal in half-time.

⛔ **NOT fuzz-bass punk.** The 2026-08-06 run rendered Sunna as *rage* by defaulting, and **rage is the
personality failing** — her closed register is unembarrassed delight. This bed is light, fast and
**stackable**, and stackability is the whole point: girl-group harmony is a technology for making one voice
into many. It was built for this song sixty years before the song existed.

**Tempo departure, declared:** Sunna's YAML default is 124–132 BPM. The assigned bed is jungle-based and
demands 165–174. Her file says *"whatever the gear-change wants."* Declared deliberately, not drifted into.

## 16. DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK — *name the one way this would render generic*

**Predicted render (first 5s):** break already running at full, dry tambourine and an exposed handclap hard
in the centre, three-note guitar figure sloping down over it, no sub at all. A close, talky female vocal
enters flat and unpretty on the beat, and a second identical voice starts one bar later and never catches up.

⛔ **The one way this renders generic:** the generator hears "sixties girl-group harmony" and delivers a
**polished, wide, sweet stacked-vocal pad** — a choir. That would kill the song three ways at once: it
breaks Sunna's ⛔ no-choir/never-sung-pretty rule, it converts a hundred takes of one person into a hundred
*people* (**D6**), and it makes the stack sound *intentional* — which removes the entire emotion, because
STACKSTARE requires that nobody meant to build this.

**REPAIR APPLIED, once:** the stack is specified as **doubles of the same close, dry, cracking voice,
deliberately misaligned 15–35 ms and detuned ±6 cents**, hard-panned narrow, never widening; the EXCLUDE
field names *choir, gospel choir, angelic, polished stacked vocals, pitch-corrected harmony, children's
choir, multiple different singers*; and the lyric itself says **"a hundred of me"** so the semantic frame
fights the generator's default. ✅ Applied in steps 09/10.
