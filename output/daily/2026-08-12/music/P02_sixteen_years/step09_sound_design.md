# STEP 09 — SOUND DESIGN · PAIR 2 "SIXTEEN YEARS"
**Title:** CHECK THAT IT TOOK · LOFN-PRIME · INDIGNATION
**174 BPM** (verses felt half-time ≈87) · **D minor** · aggressive bed **1 of 2** in the six-song set

---

## 1. THE GENRE BED — GLITCH-BAROQUE × BREAKCORE

Two halves that are the same object seen from either end:

**BAROQUE** supplies the **ground** — a real four-bar descending tetrachord (`D–C–B♭–A`), a
**harpsichord** playing actual two-voice counterpoint against the vocal, and ornament that is
*structural* rather than decorative. This is not "baroque flavour"; it is a **passacaglia** with an
amen break on top.

**BREAKCORE** supplies the **weather** — chopped amen at 174, hits landing off the grid, drum edits
that behave like a room being rearranged mid-sentence.

**Why this bed and not any other for this concept:** breakcore is the only popular form built
entirely out of **a loop being cut into and surviving it.** The ground never modulates and the break
never stops assaulting it, and neither one wins. That is a person and a procedure in the same room
for sixteen years.

⭐ **Two deeply-described genre-specific signature styles** *(personality override rule)*:

**SIGNATURE 1 — THE SANDBLASTED HARPSICHORD.** A close-mic'd harpsichord doubling the vocal line in
unison, then running a real baroque ornament against it — but the instrument is **sandblasted**:
its attack transients kept sharp while the sustain is worn away with fine grit, so every pluck
arrives whole and then dissolves like a surface that has been hit with sand for years. It reads as
antique and damaged at once. It is the only instrument that gets to be beautiful.

**SIGNATURE 2 — THE PROCEDURE ENGINE.** The percussion under the verses is not a kit. It is a
**rattling assortment** of darkroom objects — tray-edge clatter, tongs on enamel, a vibraslap, the
snap of paper coming off wet glass — quantised hard onto sixteenths and treated as the hi-hat line,
so the *room doing its job* is the groove. It has no low end at all, which is why the room sounds
floorless until the sub arrives.

## 3. ⭐ THE SIX-POINT ACOUSTIC GRAMMAR *(n=147, render-derived — applied, not quoted)*

**1 · Hard discrete level-states, no crossfades.**
Seven named states (**S1–S7**, step 07 §7). Every transition is a **hard cut on a downbeat**.
⛔ No risers, no filter sweeps into sections, no reverb tails bridging a cut, no crossfades anywhere.
The prompt says *"hard cuts between fixed loudness states"* explicitly so the generator cannot
smooth them (**THE GRAIN LAW**: this runs *with* the generator — cuts survive, long fades get sanded).

**2 · Full level from bar one — no intro.**
Bar one is **Chorus A at full tilt, mid-phrase, on an em-dash.** ⛔ No count-in, no ambience, no
build. The duration grammar (**hook at zero**) and this rule are the same instruction.

**3 · ⭐ Name one texture "exposed" and let the generator relocate it.**
The **exposed sandblasted harpsichord** in the bridge. It is named *exposed* in the prompt and in
the section header, and where the generator chooses to put it is left alone. *(Ledger note: this
tag reliably migrates. Wherever it lands, it lands on the only section with no drums, so it cannot
land badly.)*

**4 · ⭐ Withhold the sub from the opening; let it arrive as the event.**
⛔ **Nothing below 80 Hz exists for the first half of the song** — states S1–S4 are floorless by
construction. The **mono 30–60 Hz Somatic Bass enters once, at 2:05, on the word after "sixteen
years"**, and never leaves. This is the single largest gesture in the record and it is the emotional
event delivered physically rather than lyrically: **the room grows a floor at the exact moment the
fault leaves her body.** She does not sing about feeling lighter. The listener's chest gets a bottom.

**5 · End WARM, not bright.**
Outro **S7**: ground bass alone, tape saturation, air above 8 kHz rolled off, the gently sloping
melodic contour descending to the radiant. ⛔ No bright cymbal decay, no shimmer reverb, no rising
final chord. It **stops on a downbeat** — no fade.

**6 · Mono-centre the bass.**
Sub and ground bass **mono, dead centre, below 120 Hz**. The breaks and the rattling assortment are
wide; the harpsichord sits centre-left; doubles go wide in Chorus D. ⛔ Nothing non-musical is
hard-panned (a smoothing trigger).

## 4. ⭐ THE TWO ASSIGNED FLAIRS

### FLAIR 1 — **THE FLAT LIST**
*A procedural, unexalted voice reading steps while something enormous happens.*

- The verse vocal is **deadpan, unlifted, and metrically identical every pass** — no swell into the
  last line, no catch in the voice, no ad-lib. It is a person reading a laminated card.
- ⭐ **It is reinforced musically by the bold choice: the verse melody never leaves one note.** A
  flat register delivered on a flat pitch. The affect and the pitch contour are the same fact.
- The enormous thing — a whole afternoon's work turning to grey paper — happens **in the room, never
  in the delivery.** The list does not react to it. **The chorus reacts; the list keeps counting.**
- Mix instruction: the verse vocal is **dry and close, no reverb**, so it sounds like it is being
  read *to nobody in particular* rather than performed (**D8** in the mix, not just the lyric).

### FLAIR 2 — **BIT-DEPTH COLLAPSE** ⭐ *(inverted — this is the pair's deviation and it is deliberate)*
*Hi-fi to 2-bit, dramatising a loss of certainty.*

The house use of this device dramatises a **wound**. Here it dramatises **the opposite**, and the
inversion is the point:

> **The certainty being destroyed is a false certainty.** For the whole first half the record is
> hi-fi, immaculate, controlled — *"perfect paper, perfect count"* — and that immaculateness is the
> lie. The collapse at 2:05 is **one bar of 2-bit**, hard in and hard out, and what comes out the
> far side is **warmer, simpler, and lower** than what went in.

So the bit-depth collapse here is **not a wound. It is the relief.** The song loses its resolution
and gains a floor in the same bar. That is *exculpature* rendered in sample words instead of
adjectives — and it is the one place where a Somatic objection is answered **in the form**, not in
the production spec, exactly as **L22** requires.

## 5. THE BOLD CHOICE, AS A MIX INSTRUCTION *(and it is not a glitch)*

> **The verse melody is one repeated note. The ground moves under it.**

Rendered instruction: *the verse rides a single unmoving note like a countdown while a descending
four-chord ground walks underneath, so the same syllable is consonant, then sweet, then wrong, then
home — and the singer never changes what she is doing.*

Phrased that way it runs **with** the generator (single-pitch chant lines and a walking ground are
both things a model does willingly) rather than against it. And because it is written into the lyric
as flat, monosyllabic, hammered lines, it survives even if the model bends the pitch a little —
**the flatness is in the words as well as the notes**, which is the redundancy L22 asks for.

## 8. DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK *(one capped adversarial pass)*

**What this prompt would actually produce:** the first five seconds are a chopped amen break at full
level with a hammered, close, dry female voice already mid-phrase over a harpsichord — no intro,
noticeably **thin at the bottom**. Verses drop to half-time with the drums replaced by clattering
found percussion and a vocal that sits on one note. At roughly two minutes everything craters into
one bar of 2-bit and comes back with a deep mono sub that was not there before, and the last minute
is warmer and lower than the first.

**⚠️ Name the one way this would render generic:**
> The realistic failure is the generator hearing "baroque" and "female vocal" and delivering
> **pretty** — a harpsichord arpeggio under a smooth topline, the flat one-note verse "corrected"
> into a melody, and the withheld sub quietly filled in from bar one so nothing arrives at 2:05.
> That would leave a competent art-pop track with no event in it.

**Self-repair applied (once):**
1. The style prompt now says the sub is **absent** for the first half **in those words**, plus
   *"nothing below 80 Hz until it arrives"* — an absence stated as an instruction, not implied by
   a description of the later arrival.
2. **"one repeated note"** and **"the singer never changes what she is doing"** are stated in the
   style prompt *and* written into the lyric as flat monosyllabic hammered lines, so the flatness
   survives even if the melody is prettified.
3. **"no crossfades, hard cuts between fixed loudness states"** is stated explicitly, because
   smoothing is this generator's default failure and the record is built out of discontinuities.
4. The EXCLUDE field carries *lush pads · smooth legato topline · orchestral swell · gradual build ·
   sub bass from the intro* — the four specific ways "pretty" would eat this song.
