## ⭐⭐ THE STRUCTURAL SHOWCASE — HOW THE TRACE-BACK IS **FELT**, NEVER **REMARKED**

This pair is the run's structural showcase, so the labour is hidden in four places where no listener will
ever say *"that was clever"*:

1. **The verses scatter with no connective tissue** — November, a dog, a van with a flat, a fox on the bins.
   Nothing joins them. That is the composite.
2. ⭐ **V1's verses walk backwards through the year** (November → September → August → July → June → May →
   April → March → February → January → the point). A song that counts backwards is an entirely ordinary
   song. Nobody remarks on it. **It is also, exactly, the trace-back.**
3. ⭐⭐ **The radiant lives in the BASS.** Every one of the four hook figures scatters and lands on the
   **same note every bar.** A bassline that keeps coming home to the tonic is the most unremarkable thing in
   pop music — which is why it can carry the whole idea for free. *Hide the labour, keep the labour.*
4. **Nothing in any lyric announces the device.** No line says *everything traces back*. The lines fly
   apart, then one plain sentence lands late, and the click happens in the listener, not on the page.

⛔ **Devices considered and CUT under THE REMARK TEST:** a harpsichord playing the hook in retrograde at the
seam (a listener notices a backwards tune, and noticing is the toll); a lyric that names the composite; a
final chorus that repeats the radiant three times "to make sure." All three would have announced the
labour. **The radiant is stated once, plainly, late, and never explained afterwards.**

## ⛔ THE INVERSION — MY WORLD IS MODERN, THE PERIOD ELEMENT INTRUDES ONCE

This was the pair's highest risk: *"baroque chamber-pop at wall-of-sound density"* pulls hard toward a
1966 period costume — harpsichord, timpani, castanets and a sepia mix from bar one. **That is a repair, not
a delivery.**

**The world of all four records is a modern one:** 2020s widescreen indie chamber-pop — close-mic'd live kit
dragging behind the grid, a walking DI electric bass front-of-mix, clean guitar chopping, upright piano,
massed handclaps, a female lead in full chest with breath left in, stacked doubles detuned and misaligned.
**The wall-of-sound part is a DENSITY TECHNIQUE — every part doubled, blended, mono-forward, glued —
not a period.** *(⚠️ Density only. The producer associated with it is not named in any field, per the
step-11 refusal of `Wall_Of_Sound_Spector_Layering`; the credit goes to the players and singers who made the
sound — see `## DEDICATION`.)*
