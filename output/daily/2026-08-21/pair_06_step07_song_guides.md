## PRE-DRAFT — THE TWO QUESTIONS, ANSWERED BEFORE ANY GUIDE

**Q1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?**
At a low workbench in a walled yard, standing not sitting, sleeves pushed back. On the bench: lengths of cut cane in three stages of dryness, a short knife with a worn handle, a bowl of water, and a small charcoal fire kept low with a steel rod resting in it — the rod is what bores the holes, not the knife. The floor is swept because splinters are the hazard. Behind her, a plastered wall about a metre thick; through it, a wedding — a drum machine, a bassline, a PA slightly overdriven, and every so often a reed line she recognises because she made the reed. The yard is lit by a waxing gibbous at sixty-five percent, which is bright enough to work by, so the oil lamp stays unlit. Her senses register: scorched cane (the boring smells like burnt sugar), the click of a dry length against a wet one, the bass arriving through the bench-top into her forearms before it arrives in her ears, and the specific dead sound a piece makes when it has already split and has not shown it yet.

**Q2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?**
Four things, in ascending order, and **none of them resolve**:
1. **The knife.** A gouging cut that skids goes into the web of the thumb. This is the mundane danger and it must be the first one the song notices, because a body that has never been at risk is a position, not a body.
2. **The fire.** A rod that is too hot cracks the cane along the grain instead of boring it — the instrument is destroyed by the operation that makes it. The making and the breaking are the same gesture at different temperatures (Flair #3: *the same gesture performed with and without knowledge*).
3. **The material's own clock.** The cane cracks in a season regardless of skill. She breaks several a year and has stopped counting. There is no version of this where the object survives, and that is **not a tragedy, it is the specification.**
4. ⭐ **The held fear, unresolved:** *the light is arriving, not staying.* The moon is at sixty-five percent and climbing, the cane in the shade is a month from being an instrument, her hands are older than they were, and every one of those is on its way somewhere without consulting her. The tune next door is doing fine without her name attached to it — **it survived, and nobody is coming for it.** The song does not comfort her about this and does not let the listener off it either.

**⛔ R-e, restated as an operation for every guide below:** the narrator's astonishment budget is **zero**. Every wonder-carrying image must be delivered in the register of a working note. If a line could be said by a visitor, cut it and give it to the arrangement.

## THE SIX GUIDES

### GUIDE 1 — *The Bored Miracle* (from Facet 1: Emotional Arc & Authenticity)

**Storytelling techniques (two, named):** (a) **Free indirect discourse held at working distance** — narrate in third person so close to her hands that her judgements arrive as facts about objects, never as opinions about life; the reader infers the feeling from what she measures. (b) **The withheld reaction shot** — the extraordinary event (a tune older than the road being played over a drum machine at a party) is reported through its *side effects* on the bench: the water moving in the bowl, the jar rattling, a piece of cane rolling. The camera never turns round.

**Mood and emotional arc:** open in **absorption braided with ennui** — the flat, pleasant, fully occupied attention of skilled repetitive work, smelling of scorched sugar. Move at the hinge into **fascination shadowed by impatience** (something out there is using her object and doing it wrong and loudly and it is working). Land, past the midpoint, on **composure with dread underneath** — she is not upset, and that is the frightening part. Never let the arc pass through consolation. The AWE arrives in the listener as a delayed reaction, roughly at the third chorus, and the singer must be facing the other way when it does.

**Instrumentation, texture and production for this arc:** keep the emotional information in the **breath**, not the melody — audible inhalations placed as a percussion layer, so the human effort is a rhythmic element rather than an expressive one. The lead voice is heavily Auto-Tuned into an alien timbre and must be mixed **dry and close, dead centre**, because the coldness of the processing is the character's flatness and reverb would read as feeling. The one warm object in the field is the reed.

**Structure and dynamics:** run 3:40–4:20. The energy curve is **flat-then-close**, not quiet-then-loud: the third chorus gets nearer rather than bigger. Do not build. Do not swell. Power lands as a decision, staged intimately.

**Rhyme, line lengths and poetic devices per section:** see the SCAFFOLD TABLE below — this guide's binding contribution is that **the tercets must never end on the emotionally loaded word.** Put the feeling in position two of the line and a tool in position three. Devices: **litotes** (the understatement that names the size of the thing by refusing it) and **homoioteleuton** (like-sounding endings across the tercet without true rhyme).

**Three hook implementations:** (1) the four-syllable chorus **spoken-sung on the beat by the Auto-Tuned lead with the reed answering in the gap** — call and response across the seam; (2) the same four-syllable lines **doubled an octave down by a sine-sub** so the hook is felt in the chest as well as heard, with the vocal left un-doubled; (3) the hook **stated first by the flute alone in the intro**, wordless, so that when the words arrive the listener has already learned the shape — the melody is a return before its first appearance.

**Central aspect of the concept:** competence that does not rescue anyone. **Emotional tone signals:** flat, occupied, unhurried, faintly irritated by interruption. **Vocal techniques:** one breath per tercet, ends of lines allowed to run out of air, no belting anywhere in the song.

### GUIDE 2 — *One Wooden Thing* (from Facets 2 and 3: Vocal & Instrumental Synergy, Production Integrity)

**Storytelling techniques (two, named):** (a) **Ekphrasis of a working object** — describe the instrument the way a maker describes stock, by tolerance and defect, never by beauty. (b) **Reported sound as evidence** — what comes through the wall is quoted as sound, never interpreted; the listener does the interpreting.

**Mood and emotional arc:** a sustained **fascination/absorption** that never becomes reverence. The production carries the whole awe budget so the lyric does not have to.

**Instrumentation, texture, and the acoustic declaration:** ⭐ **exactly one acoustic source exists in this record: the cane reed flute.** Everything else is electric and machine-sequenced by declaration, stated positively in the style field — a deep funky bassline with finger noise and round low mids, a drum-machine kit with a dry clipped snare, cosmic synth pads with a slow filter drift, hand claps as a programmed layer. ⛔ **Do not add a second traditional instrument.** No oud, no frame drum, no double-reed, no plucked lute of any kind: four idioms applied competently is the signature of a dead arrangement, and the second one deletes the first one's meaning. The interplay to specify is **reed above, voice behind**: the flute holds the top of the field and enters ahead of the beat; the vocal sits a step behind it, glassy and quantized, and the two are never in unison for more than a phrase.

**Production and effects:** modern, dry, close and clean, so that the reed's breath noise reads as a defect against an otherwise machine-perfect surface. Room information belongs to the reed alone — a short tight ambience on the flute and nothing else, so the one wooden object is also the only object with a room. The wedding through the wall is a **low-passed, plastered-over** bass and kick bleed that shares a rhythmic layer with the drum machine, so the listener cannot always tell which room a drum is in.

**Structure and dynamics:** the first five seconds are **reed alone, unaccompanied**, breathy and unmetered. The machine world arrives underneath it rather than replacing it. At the end, stop dead — a hard cut, never a fade.

**Rhyme, line lengths and devices:** the four-syllable chorus lines must be **monosyllable-dominant** so they can be spoken by a listener with no melodic memory. Devices: **consonance** on hard stops (cut, knife, crack, keep) and **anaphora** across chorus lines.

**Three hook implementations:** (1) the hook's last word left **unprocessed** — one dry unpitched syllable among the tuned ones, so the human is audible exactly once per chorus; (2) the hook arriving with the **bass dropping to a single held root** so the four short lines sit in a hole cut for them; (3) the hook **answered by a hand clap on the off-syllable**, teaching the listener where to join by the second occurrence.

**Musical style and effects:** Mixolydian read as colour, not label — a flat seventh in the flute line and in the bass turnaround, a static tonic centre underneath. **Instrumentation and tone:** cane, charcoal, dry plastic, cold synth glass.

### GUIDE 3 — *The Seam At The Junction* (from Facet 4: Originality & Fusion)

**Storytelling techniques (two, named):** (a) **Metonymy of labour** — the pile by the door stands for the year; the jar of water stands for the fire; never explain either. (b) **The unshown event** (Panel B's Pye-Corner-seat contribution) — describe the room around the wedding and never the wedding itself.

**⭐ The binding instruction of this guide — WHAT THE REED DOES THAT NOTHING ELSE CAN.** The seam is not "a flute over a beat". In this record the reed is **the only element that can bend pitch and the only element that is not on the grid.** The voice is pitch-locked by processing; the bass, kit, claps and pads are sequenced. Therefore: place the seam at the **one junction that carries the meaning** — the entry into the third chorus. There, the reed arrives **ahead of the bar the machine is counting to**, and the machine follows it instead of the reverse. That is the whole thesis of the arrangement, expressed as a timing relationship rather than as a sentiment, and it is the only place in the song where the modern world defers.

**Mood and arc:** curiosity and unease, held. The seam should feel like a small mechanical wrongness the listener cannot name on first pass.

**Instrumentation and production:** give the reed a **portamento scoop into two of its phrases** and forbid the same gesture to every other element; give the synth pads a slow filter drift that is smooth and machine-even so the contrast reads. Sidechain the pads to the kick so the modern world visibly obeys a clock the flute ignores.

**Structure and dynamics:** the seam is **one junction, not a texture** — the flute must be absent or subordinate for at least one full section before it, so its lead at the junction is an event rather than a habit.

**Rhyme, lines, devices:** at the seam, the lyric line should **break its own scansion by one syllable** so the words limp with the groove. Devices: **enjambment** across the tercet's second and third lines, and **polyptoton** (cut / cutting / cut-back) to make the same act appear in three grammatical positions.

**Three hook implementations:** (1) the flute states the hook's four-note shape **a beat early** and the vocal completes it on time — the hook is split across the seam; (2) the hook sung with the flute in a **flat-seventh unison that drifts sharp** by the fourth line; (3) the hook's final line delivered by the flute alone with the voice absent, so the listener supplies the missing words.

**Special effects and innovations:** one **pitch-bend on the last flute note of the periapsis**, downward, uncorrected, while everything else holds equal temperament.

### GUIDE 4 — *The Bench, The Wall, The Pile* (from Facet 5: Narrative Clarity & Imagery)

**Storytelling techniques (two, named):** (a) **The object inventory that has a verb** — every named object must be being handled, not catalogued; a list of things has no verb and this run bans it explicitly. (b) **Overheard speech through a partition** — the only voice other than hers in the song arrives through a wall and is not addressed to anyone in the room.

**⛔ Legibility target, non-negotiable:** a stranger must be able to say after one listen — *a woman makes reed flutes and breaks them, and there is a wedding next door.* Therefore the lyric must **plainly name**, at least once and early: cane, a knife, holes, fire, a flute, a wall, a wedding. Metaphor is welcome on top of that surface and never instead of it.

**Mood and arc:** plain, physical, unhurried. Imagery density: at least one concrete sensory image per two lines, weighted to smell and touch because sight is the cheapest sense in a song.

**⛔ Register bans for every writer downstream:** the words *heritage, ancient, timeless, preservation* and any construction meaning *millennia-old* are forbidden in every lyric and every prompt in this pair — **the maker would not use those words**, and using them is the tourist register this pair exists to refuse. Likewise ⛔ **no collective pronoun anywhere** (delete *we, us, our, everyone*; if a line collapses without one, it was demography with a melody), ⛔ **no second-person address** (nobody is spoken to; comfort arrives by proximity only), and ⛔ **the hook is never addressed to the reed.**

**Instrumentation and production for legibility:** keep the vocal intelligible under the Auto-Tune by writing **monosyllables at the ends of chorus lines** and putting the processing's most extreme artefacts on sustained vowels rather than on consonants.

**Structure:** the subject appears in the first tercet. The wedding appears no later than the second verse. The pile by the door returns at the end as an object, not as a symbol.

**Rhyme, lines and devices:** long-breath tercets of **9–11 syllables**, loose terminal assonance rather than strict rhyme, against a chorus of **four-syllable lines** — the shape is deliberately the inverse of the neighbouring pair's, and the contrast between the long breath and the short landing is this song's prosody. Devices: **asyndeton** in the working passages (clauses stacked without conjunctions, the rhythm of a practised sequence) and **caesura** mid-line in the hinge tercet.

**Three hook implementations:** (1) the hook as a **maxim of the trade** overheard rather than declared; (2) the hook **whispered under the sung version** in a second take, one word out of sync; (3) the hook printed on the beat with **no pickup**, landing cold, so it reads as a fact rather than a chorus.

**Narrative and thematic seed:** one working night, one bench, one wall, one moon. First person or close third; ⛔ never collective. **Lyric structure:** verse tercets carry the events, the chorus carries the trade.

### GUIDE 5 — *Periapsis And The Limp* (from Facets 6 and 7: Dynamic Progression, Rhythm & Harmony)

**Storytelling techniques (two, named):** (a) **Chiastic staging** — the elements of the opening return at the close in reverse order, so the ear registers a closed path without being told about one. (b) **The interrupted return** — the third chorus is cut short by the verse that follows it rather than ending on its own terms.

**⭐ THE PERIAPSIS, SPECIFIED AS THREE MEASURABLE CLAIMS** (a Step-10 critic must be able to point at all three):
1. **Early by a stated amount** — chorus 3 arrives roughly six bars before the ear expects it, because the approach that preceded choruses 1 and 2 is cut, not because the tempo changes. The tempo never changes: 88 BPM throughout, steady, no rubato.
2. **Closer, by a stated mix move** — the lead vocal comes forward roughly 3 dB with a shorter, tighter ambience and the pads step back; nothing gets louder overall. Proximity, not volume.
3. **Exits at the same speed** — it is shorter than the other choruses and it hands straight to verse 3 with no tag, no repeat and no landing. **An orbit does not stay to be admired.**

**⭐ THE KINETIC DEFECT:** the drum machine's hat drags a sixteenth on the last beat of every fourth bar and never corrects; the reed sits a sixteenth ahead of the downbeat and never corrects. The bass stays perfectly in time between them. This asymmetry is written into the style field as an audible groove behaviour, not as a production instruction.

**Harmony:** Mixolydian with a static tonic bass centre and a flat-seventh chord that arrives on the turnaround of every chorus. The flat seventh should feel like a room the song keeps walking back into, never like a modulation.

**⭐ SUBSTITUTION, NOT SUBTRACTION — binding on every arrangement decision:** where an element leaves, something audible must be exactly where it was. If the kick drops out of a chorus line, a knife-tap or a hand clap occupies its slot. ⛔ No long interrupting silence, no dependence on stereo width, no hollow mix — those smooth away in the renderer. **Terminal silence is permitted and is this pair's ending.**

**Structure and dynamics:** run-time 3:40–4:20, sections: reed alone (0:00–0:05) → verse 1 → chorus → verse 2 → chorus → **periapsis chorus** → verse 3 → terminal close, hard cut.

**Devices:** **epizeuxis** inside the chorus (immediate repetition of a single short line) and **antimetabole** at the periapsis (two words swapping positions between the second and third chorus).

**Three hook implementations:** (1) the periapsis hook entering **over the tail of the previous verse's last word**; (2) the hook with the kick removed and a **wooden knock on the downbeat** in its place; (3) the hook's last line **stopped one syllable short**, the flute finishing it.

### GUIDE 6 — ⭐ WILD CARD — *The Working Window* (cross-disciplinary: hot glass on the pipe)

**The import.** Glassblowing has no undo and no pause: the material is workable only while it is above a temperature, so the entire craft is organised around a **window that is closing while you use it**. The maker does not experience this as tragedy; it is simply the clock the job runs on, and a glassblower who stopped to marvel at it would lose the piece. **Bring that clock to this song.** Reed-making has the same shape — cane is workable wet, the rod is right for a few seconds, the moon is up now and rising, the wedding is happening tonight. The song's tempo is the working window, and the reason nothing in this piece is allowed to swell is that **there is no time in the process for a swell.**

**Cross-disciplinary techniques to translate into sound:** (a) **The gather and the marver** — a glassmaker gathers, rolls the mass flat on a steel table to make it even, then works it. Musically: let each verse *gather* material and let the chorus be the flat even roll that makes it usable — the chorus is the marver, which is why it is four syllables and completely regular. (b) **The punty scar** — every blown piece carries the mark where it was broken off the rod, and finishers grind it but never remove it. Musically: leave one audible join in the record — the point where the reed's take and the machine's grid do not agree — and do not tidy it. That scar is where the piece was attached to the process that made it.

**Poetic devices this guide contributes:** **zeugma** (one verb governing a tool and a body part in the same line) and **diacope** (a word repeated with one or two words in between, which is exactly the shape of the mutating refrain).

**Emotional tone signals:** the specific calm of a person operating inside a closing window, which reads to an outsider as serenity and to the worker as arithmetic.

**Key sequence to attempt:** a passage where the flute plays the same four-note figure three times while the arrangement underneath changes what that figure *means* — first as an introduction, then as a call, then as the thing the machine follows. Nothing in the figure changes. Everything about its position does. ⭐ **That is the orbit, executed in sound rather than in the lyric**, and it is the wildcard's actual proposal.

**Three hook implementations:** (1) hook stated over a **single held bass root** while the kit rests, so the working window is audible as an empty table; (2) hook doubled by the flute a **sixth above**, an interval the Auto-Tuned voice cannot produce naturally; (3) hook delivered once with the processing **bypassed for the last word only** — the punty scar, in the vocal.
