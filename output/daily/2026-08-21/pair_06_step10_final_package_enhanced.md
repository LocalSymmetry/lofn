## 3a. ⭐ The correction I had to make to my own correction — recorded because it is the finding

**My first fix was right about the symptom and wrong about the cure, and the measurement caught it, not my ear.**

I found that V1 and V3 answered the pair's one sung fact with the same construction, and that *the tune is a stranger* appeared near-verbatim in V2 and V3. Both findings hold. My first replacements were `bright enough to sight the bore by, and she does.` and `light to work by,`. They read well and they scanned, so I measured them — and the within-pair figure moved the **wrong way**, V3/V4 lyric token 0.293 → 0.315.

The diagnosis took one query. V4 contains `bore` **eight times** — *and bore it through* is in every one of its choruses — and V4's own verse 3 reads *"Out in the yard it is bright enough to work."* I had deleted a V1/V3 collision at the hinge and installed a fresh V3/V4 collision on `bright · enough · bore · work`, in the very song whose vocabulary is the craft. **I traded one collision for another and would have shipped it as an improvement.**

Second pass, chosen against a measured token census rather than by ear:
- `light to work by,` → **`light to see by,`** — `see` already appears three times in V3's own verse 2 (*she can see how it runs · where it lies · where it splits*), and **zero** times in V4. The chorus now gathers its own verse's verb instead of borrowing its sibling's, which is what a chorus is supposed to do. Cost: zero new shared tokens.
- `bright enough to sight the bore by,` → **`a light she can line a hole by,`** — drops `bright`, `enough` and `bore`; `line` occurs nowhere in the pair; the long-I chain is stronger (*sky · five · light · line · by*), and *she can* echoes verse 2's triple *she can see*.

Result: worst lyric 4-gram 0.026 → **0.021**, V3/V4 lyric token 0.315 → **0.298**.

⚠️ **Two things this is evidence for.** First, *the judge is the instrument, not the surgeon* is not a caution about the previous tier — **it applied to me, on my own best find, one edit later.** Second, a Jaccard number that stays inside its cap can still be reporting a real craft defect; 0.315 was never going to fail a gate, and the phrase collision underneath it was exactly the failure the gate exists to proxy. **The cap is not the question. The shared phrase is the question.**
