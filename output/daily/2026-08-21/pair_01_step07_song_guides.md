# PAIR 01 — STEP 07 · SONG GUIDES
## `daily-music-2026-08-21` · **C-18 · THE CLEAN CUT** · six guides · **guides DIRECT, they do not draft**

---

## ⛔ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — ANSWERED FIRST, CONCRETELY

**Where is the body standing?**
On a rubber anti-fatigue mat in front of a hydraulic paper guillotine, in a low-ceilinged workroom on a
night shift. Her left hip is against a pallet of hardbacks; her right hand is on the clamp lever, her left
forearm flat on the steel bed, squaring the block. A tube light in the ceiling row ticks on its ballast.
The air is hot glue, paper dust and warm metal. To her left the roller feed takes the loose pages away; to
her right a wheeled bin fills with sheared spine strips. There is nobody else on the floor.

**What could hurt it here?**
The blade, first and obviously — the clamp comes down on her forearm's side of the bed, and she puts her
hand in there anyway, every cycle, all night. But the blade is not the hazard the song is about. The hazard
is that **correct procedure IS the danger**: she has been good at this long enough that she has stopped
reading the covers, and she remembers stopping. Nothing prevented it. Nobody told her to. The count on the
board went up either way. She is not being crushed; she is being *correctly employed*, and the thing that
could hurt her is that the job has a place for exactly as much of her as it uses.

> Both answers are places and stakes, not moods. Every guide below is written from this mat.

## GUIDE 1 · **V1 — THE CLEAN CUT** *(facet 1 — Procedural Authority)*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *Procedural verse* — the shift told in the order it happens, verbs only,
no commentary, the way a trade is described by somebody who has it; (b) *Epistrophe on a work-sentence* —
the same three-line prediction returns unchanged so that the listener joins on its second occurrence.
**Mood & emotional arc:** open in composure with the vigilance of a person who is watching a blade;
mid-song a steadiness that reads as competence and only later as habit; at the hinge, cognitive dissonance
— the flat shock of an instruction that names a year — and out into a quiet moral outrage that never raises
its voice. Sensory anchor: hot glue, ballast tick, cold hands in a warm room.
**Instrumentation & texture:** real upright piano, close and woody, left hand bouncing a two-beat gospel
stride (LOFN-PRIME's *Piano Bounce* pulled out of the club and set on an acoustic instrument); wire brushes
on a coated head; **the guillotine thump takes the kick's grid position** — substitution, never a gap. Room
tone kept as a rhythmic layer, not as ambience. ONE gasba reed entry at the hinge, high and bent, and it
never returns. Frames used: *Jazz — percussive hits fracturing the groove*, *Field recording — rhythmic
sparks weaving through the mix*, *Piano — rumbling beats*. Genres: Indie Swing Revival · Melodic Jazz ·
Progressive Chamber Pop.
**Production & effects:** a small tiled room, short boxy tail, tracked live. Voice a hand's width from a
ribbon mic, dead centre, conversational volume, breath audible between clauses. Pedal creak and page
rustle stay in. Every literal "no X" belongs in the exclude field, never in the style field.
**Structure & dynamics:** Intro / Verse / Refrain / Verse / Refrain / Turn / Verse (= Verse 1, word for
word) / Refrain / Tag. ~4:00. C minor lifting to E-flat major at the Turn and staying there. **The
arrangement never acknowledges the confirmed prediction** — when the spine tears as called, no fill, no
cymbal, no lift.
**Rhyme & line logic:** iambic tetrameter, hard caesura mid-line, strict ABAB quatrains. Refrain 8/8/7 —
the short third line is the only irregularity in the whole song. Refrain rhyme AAA.
**Poetic devices:** anaphora on `I` + `The`; consonance on hard stops (clamp, block, clock, crack, kick).
**Hook techniques:** (1) sung flat at conversational volume with the piano dropping to left hand alone, so
the room is audible under it; (2) second pass identical, brushes entering on the same syllable, which is
what makes it joinable; (3) final pass with the piano now in the relative major underneath the identical
words — the only change in the record, and it is harmonic, not lyrical.
**Central aspect:** competence as the load-bearing fact. **Emotional tone signal:** on time, correct, quiet.
⛔ Thesis may not land in the final refrain.

## GUIDE 2 · **V2 — THE OFFCUT BIN** *(facet 4 — The Ordinary Miracle, Held At Arm's Length)*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *Synecdoche worked to exhaustion* — the whole practice told only through
what is in the bin, never through what left on the rollers; (b) *A rule stated and broken in the same
breath*, which is this variation's hook grammar and is nothing like V1's paired predictions.
**Mood & emotional arc:** begin in detachment (a bin is a bin), pass through unease as the hours thin, and
arrive at the hinge in a plain recognition that the bin has got older. No lament anywhere. The strip with
part of a word on it is looked at because looking is a compulsion, not because it is precious.
**Instrumentation & texture:** the same Guillotine Gospel band, staged lower and sparser — piano mostly in
the left hand, brushes swinging while the piano stays straight so the backbeat sits *between* the two
(this variation's kinetic defect). Bin-lid clank doubles the guillotine thump on alternate bars. Frames:
*Drums — granular echoes*, *Field recording — rhythmic sparks*, *Medieval — microtonal echoes* (the reed).
Genres: Soft Dub Poetry · Lo-Fi Mazurka · Melodic Jazz.
**Production & effects:** more floor in the mix — caster wheels, the bin's hollow metal, a wheeled cart
crossing the room from left of the mic to behind it. Voice still close, still conversational.
**Structure & dynamics:** Verse / Pre / Refrain / Verse / Pre / Refrain / Turn / Verse (= Verse 1) / Pre /
Refrain / Tag. ~3:40. B-flat minor to D-flat major at the Turn. **Three pre-choruses, all different** —
the approach changes while the refrain does not; that is this song's motion.
**Rhyme & line logic:** ABAB tetrameter verses; pre-choruses ABAB; refrain 8/8/7 with the first and third
lines ending on the *same word* (epanalepsis), which is a different rhyme logic from every other variation.
**Poetic devices:** epanalepsis in the refrain; asyndeton through the bin inventory.
**Hook techniques:** (1) refrain entered with no lift at all, straight off the pre-chorus's last consonant;
(2) the third line's short syllable left as a hole the brushes fill; (3) last pass with the bin-lid clank
landing on the missing syllable.
**Key sequences:** the cart crossing the room, twice, at the same tempo, is the song's only travel.
⛔ No list of titles. One partial word, once. Thesis may not land in the final refrain.

## GUIDE 3 · **V3 — THE NEW PERSON** *(facet 2 — The Confirmed Prediction, Unacknowledged)*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *Reported instruction* — everything she taught is in the past tense and in
the third person, so nobody is ever spoken to; (b) *Anticlimax as structure* — the error arrives, is
absorbed, and the count goes up.
**Mood & emotional arc:** steadfastness giving way to a very quiet dismay; the kid is not a villain and is
not stupid, the kid is *fast*, and the count rewards fast. At the hinge, the ordinary realisation that the
old stock is the point and the kid cannot smell it. No rescue, no scolding.
**Instrumentation & texture:** the piano's left hand **loses its bounce and squares up** across verse two —
the groove gets more even as the kid gets quicker, which is this variation's kinetic defect running in
reverse. Brushes swap to stick-butt on the rim for the kid's sections. The thump stays exactly on the
kick's grid position. Frames: *Percussion — quivering runs*, *Jazz — percussive hits*, *Arabic — squelchy
motifs* (the reed's bend). Genres: Indie Swing Revival · Garage Soft Rock · Progressive Chamber Pop.
**Production & effects:** a chalk stroke on a board in the first seconds, dry and toothy; a clamp ratchet;
then the thump. Second voice never appears — the kid is only ever heard about.
**Structure & dynamics:** Verse / Refrain / Aside / Verse / Refrain / Turn / Verse (= Verse 1) / Refrain /
Tag. ~4:05. D minor to F major at the Turn. The **Aside** is four lines of reported teaching over a single
rim tick where the thump was — substitution, not subtraction.
**Rhyme & line logic:** ABAB tetrameter; refrain 8/8/7 rhyming A-B-A on true rhymes (call / down / all).
**Poetic devices:** epizeuxis (`It comes away. It comes apart.`); polyptoton on *call / called / calling*
used sparingly.
**Hook techniques:** (1) the refrain's second line lands on the same beat the blade does; (2) the short
third line is delivered a shade early, then the band arrives correctly, which is the whole ache of the
song in one bar; (3) final pass, piano in the relative major, delivery unchanged.
**Narrative element:** the wrong cut takes the margin with the spine, the words come off thin, and
**nobody comes** — there is no bell for that. ⛔ Thesis may not land in the final refrain.

## GUIDE 4 · **V4 — THE ONE SHE KEPT** *(facet 5 — The Same Words, A Different Object)*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) *The object that changes rooms* — a green board travels from crate to coat
to kitchen shelf and the song does not say whether that helped; (b) *Refusal of the reveal* — she never
opens it, and the not-opening is an action, not a symbol.
**Mood & emotional arc:** open in plain work, move through a small illicit warmth in the yard, and end
unresolved — the kettle, the grey window, the book on the shelf still shut. Contemplation, never sentiment.
**Instrumentation & texture:** brushes with a slight swing, piano with more sustain pedal than the other
three (the kitchen has a longer tail than the workroom, and the mix moves once, at the Turn, to say so).
Kinetic defect: **the thump lands a sixteenth early in every fourth bar** and is never corrected. Frames:
*Neo Bossa brush pulse*, *Piano — rumbling beats colliding*, *Field recording — rhythmic sparks*. Genres:
Neo Bossa Nova · Melodic Jazz · Soft Dub Poetry.
**Production & effects:** first seconds are a crate lid prised up, dust, a cough, then the thump. At home:
kettle, a window, a shorter and softer room. Return to the workroom for the third verse **with the workroom
reverb exactly as it was** — the only thing that has changed is what the listener knows.
**Structure & dynamics:** Verse / Refrain / Verse / Refrain / Turn / Verse (= Verse 1) / Refrain / Outro.
~3:50. G minor to B-flat major at the Turn. The outro is six lines and is the longest tag of the four.
**Rhyme & line logic:** ABAB tetrameter; refrain 8/8/7 with an A-B-B′ shape ending on a pararhyme
(coat / lot) — the only slant-rhyme hook in the pair.
**Poetic devices:** antanaclasis on *keep* (keep the block / keep it clean / kept it); enjambment forbidden
— every line closes, because the caesura is the pair's whole prosody.
**Hook techniques:** (1) sung almost under the breath the first time, as if she has just decided; (2)
second time with the brushes finally entering behind it; (3) third time unchanged while the piano sits in
the relative major and the coat is already empty.
**Emotional tone signal:** she is not confessing. She is telling nobody, in a car park, in the frost.
⛔ Thesis may not land in the final refrain.

## GUIDE 5 · **THE SHARED ARRANGEMENT — "GUILLOTINE GOSPEL"** *(facet 3 — Plosive Spine On A Steady Pulse)*

**The operation, in one verb each:** the piano *bounces*; the brushes *drag*; the thump *replaces*; the
reed *enters once*; the room *stays*. Nothing here is described as haunted, and nothing is achieved by
taking something away.
**Tempo and body:** 92 BPM, four-four, one tetrameter line per bar — a walking, working patter, close to
speech, so hard consonants carry the pulse and the singer never has to push volume to be legible.
**The substitution law:** where the kick would be, a flat paper-guillotine thump. Where a fill would be at
the moment the prediction comes true, **nothing added** — the band keeps its footing. Terminal silence is
allowed; an interrupting silence is not; stereo width carries no meaning in any of the four.
**Positive acoustic declaration** (style field): real upright piano with pedal noise kept, wire brushes on
a coated head, tracked live in one small tiled room, ballast hum and page rustle left in the mix, voice a
hand's width from a ribbon mic. **Every literal "no X" lives in the exclude field.**
**The seam, and there is only one:** modern world = a small acoustic workroom band. Intruding material =
ONE gasba reed line at the hinge. The seam is placed where the year lands, and **the subject is doing
something across it** — she is turning a book front-side up to read a date. N = 1.
**Vocal spec (all four):** woman in her late thirties, plain northern English alto, dry, plosive-forward,
straight tone, breath audible between clauses, no ornament. She discovers at the Turn; everywhere else she
reports. Astonishment is the listener's job.
**Mix arc:** verses flat and even; refrain lifts **only in the piano**; the Turn is the only key change and
the only new timbre; the final refrain is identical to the first except for the harmony under it.

## GUIDE 6 · **WILDCARD — THE RETAINED BLOCK** *(cross-disciplinary provocation)*

Borrowed from a discipline that destroys in order to read and has two centuries of ethics attached:
**the block is kept and the slice is spent.** In diagnostic sectioning the specimen is embedded and
archived and only a micrometre ribbon is consumed, so the question can be asked again in six years.
Here that is inverted — the slice is kept and the block is destroyed — and the inversion is discharged in
this pair **as form and only as form**: the song returns to its opening verse word for word, and what has
been consumed in between is the listener's ability to hear those words as neutral.

**Direction, not draft:** nothing in any of the four lyrics may point at the return. No line may say the
words mean something else now. No section may be titled to advertise it. The device works because it is
never mentioned; the moment it is described it becomes a trick with an explanation attached.

**Second borrowing, for texture only:** overwritten manuscripts are read by imaging, without cutting,
because the erased layer is still physically there. Use that as a *sound* — the room tone under the band is
the layer that never left — and never as a line. Frames to reach for: *Field recording — rhythmic sparks
weaving through the mix*, *Drums — granular echoes, warm tremors*.

**Falsifiable test for this guide:** play the third verse to somebody who has not heard the first. If they
can tell it is a repeat *from the words alone*, the guide has failed and the fault is a lyric that pointed.

**Self-critique, one line:** four guides written from the same mat risk four songs with the same gait, so
the differentiation is carried deliberately by *four different kinetic defects and four different refrain
rhyme logics* rather than by four different subjects — if the four still walk alike at step 10, the fault
will be here, not in the drafting.
