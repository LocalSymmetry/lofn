## ⛔ 0. THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — answered concretely before any guide is written
*(`04_metaprompt.md` §7: every AWE song answers both. A guide that cannot answer them with **a place** and
**a stake** is not ready to direct anything.)*

### **Q1 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?**
**On a folding chair in a gallery room, with his back to a fire door and his face to a wall.**
Parquet under the chair legs, laid in a herringbone that clicks differently under a stranger's shoe than
under his own. A cast-iron radiator on the left-hand wall that ticks four or five times when the heat comes
up and then holds. Two rope stanchions in front of him, the near one's clip gone soft so the rope hangs a
finger lower than the far one and he re-clips it out of habit, not need. On the wall in front of him: a
**pale flat patch of paint**, cleaner than the wall around it, four small filled holes at its corners. In
his breast pocket, a pen and a folded log sheet. **He can hear the room's emptiness as a longer decay** —
without a body in front of the wall, his own shoe comes back to him a half-beat late.

### **Q2 — WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?**
**It survived and nobody is coming for it** *(Axis 5, assigned)*. The clean fear in this song is not the
theft. It is that **the return does not repair anything, and everybody behaves as though it does.** The
thing comes back after two years, is re-hung a few centimetres off the old holes, gets new glass and a fresh
coat over the pale patch, and the room is **declared fine** — by people who never sat in it. Nobody asks him
what the room sounded like while it was empty; he is the only instrument that measured it, and the
measurement is thrown away with the drop cloth. **The two years remain in the room and now there is nothing
on the wall to point at.** He is not owed anything, nobody wronged him, and that is precisely the shape of
the injury: *the offence is abdication, not loss.*

⭐ **AWE here is terror-adjacent, not domestic.** The awe is that a body kept doing a correct thing with no
object at the end of it for two years. The terror is that this is survivable — that a person can be
*maintained* the way a file is maintained, and nobody promised to keep paying for it.

### 🎼 GUIDE 1 — **THE STANDING BODY, FIRST** *(facet 1 · leads V1)*

**The direction.** Buy the emotional authority with hardware before anyone is asked to feel anything: the
first thirty seconds must give the ear a floor, a radiator, a rope clip and a man's own shoe coming back to
him. Direct the singer to **report inventory and discover meaning only by accident** — she is describing a
shift, not delivering a verdict, and the moment she sounds moved by her own material the song becomes a
lecture about a job instead of the job.

- **Storytelling techniques (2).** ⑴ **Objective correlative** — the emotional state is never named; it is
  handed to a piece of hardware (a clip that has gone soft, a log sheet folded to fit a pocket) and the
  listener does the arithmetic. ⑵ **The Sebald refusal** — the narrator declines to confirm anything she did
  not touch: what happened to the picture is reported as furniture and rumour, never as event.
- **Mood & emotional arc.** Open in **vigilant solitude** (the alertness of someone whose whole job is
  noticing nothing happen), pass through **devotion without an object**, drop at the midpoint into **flat
  disapproval** — the temperature of someone reading a memo about their own room — and resolve into a
  **transcendence that still carries the disapproval inside it.** No section is loud. Nothing here mourns
  prettily; the register is *urgency*, not grief.
- **Instrumentation & texture.** Felted Rhodes carrying the harmonic figure with a slow warm tremolo;
  palm-muted electric guitar on the offbeats, damped with the heel of the hand so each note is a click with
  a pitch inside it; brushed snare on a coated head, played in circles, not strokes; upright bass buzzing
  lightly against the frets on the low notes. Neo-baroque continuo — suspensions, 4–3 resolutions, a passing
  third under the bass — **enters only inside the chorus and is silent everywhere else.** Nothing is doubled
  for size; size comes from the room.
- **Production & effects.** One plate reverb, opened wide from the first bar, describing **a hall
  substantially deeper than the four players standing in it**, so the space is permanently slightly wrong
  for the size of the sound. The lead vocal sits **dry and forward, close-miked at a hand's distance**,
  breath audible, while the room behind her stays enormous — the singer is in the room the guard is in, and
  the reverb is the room he hears when it is empty. Mono-safe throughout: nothing load-bearing is placed on
  stereo width, because width smooths away in the render.
- **Structure & dynamics.** Target **3:45–4:15** at 84 BPM. Sixteen short sections. Verse economy **8 → 4 →
  2**, byte-identical chorus three times, the substitution at the midpoint, a synthesis tag at the end. The
  dynamic arc is a single slow lean, not a build: the chorus does not get louder, it gets **harmonically
  thicker**, and that is the only rise the song owns.
- **Rhyming scheme, by section.** V1 (8): free-verse with a **vowel ladder** as the spine — `ah` for the
  first three stressed positions, `eh` for the next three, `ee` for the last two — and consonance rather
  than end-rhyme holding the line ends. Pre-chorus (4): `A B A B` slant only. Chorus (8): loose `x A x A x x
  B x`, the anaphoric tagline occupying the unrhymed slots. Post-chorus (4): monorhyme on the ladder's top
  vowel. Rupture (8): couplets that **refuse to close** — the second line of each pair lands on a consonant,
  not a rhyme. Section B (8): `A A B C C B`. Verse 3 (2): unrhymed, ladder cut off at `eh`. Tag (8): `A B B
  A` twice, the envelope closing on the same vowel it opened on.
- **Poetic devices (≥2).** **Anaphora** (the four-syllable chant), **consonance** on hard stops (`clip,
  clasp, latch, patch, kept`), **caesura** inside lines to force the walking pace, **polyptoton** on the
  verb *keep* (keeps / kept / keeping) so the Golden Seed's distinction — being kept versus being heard —
  is carried by grammar and never stated.
- **Hook techniques (3).** ⑴ **The four-syllable chant, unaccompanied on its first appearance** — the band
  drops to brushes and voice for the first statement, so the phrase is learnable before it is decorated; on
  every later chorus the continuo thickens beneath the *identical* phrase. ⑵ **The chant as a room, not a
  line** — a single low harmony a fifth below, tracked once, no stack, so it reads as a second person in the
  room rather than a produced effect. ⑶ **The chant landing on the downbeat the brushed snare is dragging
  behind** — the voice arrives on time and the kit arrives a sixteenth late, every time, so the hook feels
  like it is being followed rather than accompanied.
- **Narrative & thematic seed.** *A man is paid to stand in front of a rectangle of wall.* First person
  singular is available; collective person is forbidden outright. English only, plain register, no
  art-history vocabulary of any kind.
- **Central aspect of the concept.** Competence performed on nothing.
- **Emotional tone signals.** Alert, unhurried, unimpressed by its own material.
- **Vocal techniques.** Female low alto, conversational; two quirks — a slight downward scoop into stressed
  `ah` vowels, and a habit of ending phrases on breath rather than tone.

### 🎼 GUIDE 2 — **THE WALL CANNOT RECEIVE** *(facet 2 · leads V4)*

**The direction.** This is the guide that keeps the whole pair legal. The single most natural sentence this
concept produces is a prayer addressed to the missing thing, and that sentence is **banned** — it is the
benchmark archetype's addressee shape and the run rotated it out by name. Direct the writer to **establish
non-reception inside the room, in the room's own vocabulary, before the first chorus lands**, so that when
the hook arrives the listener already knows it is falling on plaster. Nobody is spoken to at any point;
comfort arrives by proximity only.

- **Storytelling techniques (2).** ⑴ **Reported speech of the object, unconfirmed** — the wall, the hooks,
  the rope and the log book are quoted, and the narrator neither endorses nor denies them; the quotation is
  the evidence and the refusal to confirm is the honesty. ⑵ **Free indirect style** — the narrator drifts
  into the guard's idiom for a line at a time without announcing the shift, so the listener is *inside* a
  head that has stopped finding this strange.
- **Mood & emotional arc.** Fascination that never tips into astonishment, then **incredulity** at the
  midpoint — not shock, the flatter thing that happens when a sentence is said out loud and turns out to be
  absurd — then **disapproval**, then a composure that is not resolution.
- **Instrumentation & texture.** Same quartet; here the muted guitar takes the quoted lines, answering each
  vocal phrase with a two-note figure that never varies, like a fixture repeating itself. Brushes go to the
  rim under quotations. The continuo's suspensions land specifically **under the unconfirmed sentence**, so
  the harmony sounds like agreement the narrator is not giving.
- **Production & effects.** Quotations are printed **a touch drier and a touch closer** than the narration —
  the same voice, moved forward six inches, no filter, no telephone effect, no double. A quoted line must
  never be *processed*: processing would make it a device, and the point is that it is testimony.
- **Structure & dynamics.** Target **3:40–4:05**. The chorus is built as **quotation ×3 interleaved with
  narratorial refusal**, so the hook's own grammar performs the ruling. No pre-chorus anywhere in this
  variation; instead two short **[Aside]** sections where the narrator says what she did not do.
- **Rhyming scheme, by section.** Quoted lines are held **byte-identical** and therefore unrhymed by
  definition; the narrator's replies rhyme with each other and never with the quotation — `x A x A x A B B`
  across the chorus — so the wall and the room are audibly in different rhyme systems. V1 (8):
  `A A B / C C B / D D` with the ladder overriding at the stressed positions. V2 (4): `A B B A`. Asides:
  free verse, two lines each. Verse 3 (2): unrhymed. Tag: `A A B B` with the final B left open.
- **Poetic devices (≥2).** **Epizeuxis** on the quoted sentence, **antanaclasis** on the verb *take* (taken /
  take a message / take the hours), **asyndeton** in the narrator's refusals, **homoioteleuton** across the
  reply lines.
- **Hook techniques (3).** ⑴ **The quotation sung flat and the reply sung with contour** — one performance
  choice separates the two speakers with no production trick. ⑵ **The continuo entering only under the
  quotation**, so the arrangement appears to believe the wall and the narrator does not. ⑶ **The final
  chorus placing the narrator's reply first**, before the quotation, without changing a single word of
  either — order as the only variable in an otherwise identical block. *(⛔ Applies to V4's internal
  reply/quote ordering only — the chorus text itself remains byte-identical.)*
- **Narrative & thematic seed.** *A fixture is quoted saying something reassuring, and the person who works
  in front of it declines to agree.*
- **Key sequences.** The two-note guitar answer under every quotation; the suspension that resolves late.
- **Special effects & innovations.** Zero processing on the quoted voice — the restraint is the effect.
- **Lyric structure.** Quotation lines are load-bearing and immutable; everything else may move.

### 🎼 GUIDE 3 — **THE SUBSTITUTION IS AUDIBLE AT THE SEAM** *(facet 3 · leads V2)*

**The direction.** State the operation, do not describe a mood: **at the midpoint the felted Rhodes hands
its exact figure to a dry upright piano recorded closer — same bar, same notes, same voicing, no gap, no
crossfade, no filter sweep, no drop, no half-time.** The room does not get quieter; it gets *differently
occupied*. Everything else in the arrangement continues undisturbed through the seam, which is what makes
the swap audible: the listener hears a chair change hands under a groove that never blinked.

- **Storytelling techniques (2).** ⑴ **The cue that implies a scene it never shows** — the arrangement
  reports a change of custody and the lyric never narrates one. ⑵ **Enumerative procession with verbs** —
  the room's hours told as a sequence of small actions, never as an inventory. *(A list of things has no
  verb; a list of ACTIONS is a shift.)*
- **Mood & emotional arc.** Reverent attention → a rising, almost pleasurable competence → the seam →
  frustration expressed as **flatness**, the sound of somebody reading a notice — then a synthesis that
  keeps the flatness in it.
- **Instrumentation & texture.** Before the seam, the Rhodes is felted, tremolo slow, sustain long, and it
  is the only chordal instrument. After the seam, the **upright piano is dry, close, damper-noisy and
  slightly out at the top octave**, playing the identical figure with the identical fingering. Bass, brushes
  and muted guitar do not change at all across the seam — no fill, no cymbal, no marker. The absence of a
  transition marker is the whole instruction.
- **Production & effects.** The plate stays open at the same setting through the swap, so the *room* is
  proven constant while the *furniture* changes. The upright is closer to the microphone than the Rhodes
  was, which makes it sound like it moved toward the listener rather than replaced anything — that is the
  intended illusion and it should not be corrected in the mix.
- **Structure & dynamics.** Target **3:50–4:20**. Fourteen sections, **no pre-chorus and no post-chorus** in
  this variation; instead a two-line **[Refrain]** after each chorus and one instrumental-cued **[Turn]**.
  The seam falls at roughly 55–58% of the run time, past the midpoint, which is where the turn belongs.
- **Rhyming scheme, by section.** V1 (8): a **serial cascade** — every line begins a new clause in a single
  sentence, rhyme suppressed entirely, the ladder carrying the return; the stanza closes on an unstressed
  syllable so the chorus can lift. Chorus (8): one continuous grammatical period across eight lines, rhymed
  `x x A x A x B B` so the rhyme only appears at the two structural joints. Refrain (2): identical vowel,
  no rhyme. V2 (4): `A B A B`, the only fully-rhymed stanza in the song — it should feel like the room
  briefly getting organised. Rupture (8): `A A A B` twice, the monorhyme grinding. Verse 3 (2): unrhymed.
  Coda: free.
- **Poetic devices (≥2).** **Polysyndeton** in the cascade, **mesodiplosis** on the connective, **zeugma**
  where one verb governs a physical object and an interval of time, **anadiplosis** across the seam so the
  last word before the swap is the first word after it.
- **Hook techniques (3).** ⑴ **The cascade sung in one breath per two lines**, so the hook is identifiable
  by *breathing pattern* before it is identifiable by words. ⑵ **The refrain delivered on the rim**, brushes
  only, two lines, a phrase short enough that a stranger joins it on its second appearance. ⑶ **The chorus's
  final joint doubled at the octave below by the upright bass**, bowed, once — the only bowed note in the
  song.
- **Narrative & thematic seed.** *A room does its hours with nothing in it, and one of the things it does is
  a man.*
- **Central aspect of the concept.** Maintenance without an object.
- **Musical style & effects.** Neo-soul swing under a chamber-pop harmonic frame; the kinetic defect below.
- **Rhythm & melody.** ⭐ **THE KINETIC DEFECT:** the brushed backbeat **drags roughly a sixteenth behind the
  Rhodes and never corrects.** It is not a swing setting and it is not humanisation — it is a limp, present
  from bar one, unresolved at the end. The groove leans the whole way through.

### 🎼 GUIDE 4 — **THE LADDER CLIMBS AND THE STANZA RUNS OUT** *(facet 4 · the sound axis, binding on all four)*

**The direction.** This is the pair's mandatory sound/return obligation and it is a **positive** one — an
addition, not a subtraction. Across each verse the stressed vowels climb **ah → eh → ee**, and the ladder
**resets at every shortening.** Because the verses shorten 8 → 4 → 2, the ladder reaches the top in Verse 1,
reaches the middle in Verse 2, and **is cut off before it can start climbing in Verse 3.** Direct the
lyricist to build word lists *first* and lines *second*; a ladder assembled after the fact will land on
unstressed syllables and become invisible.

- **Storytelling techniques (2).** ⑴ **Prosody as plot** — the listener feels the song running out of room
  before being told anything has run out. ⑵ **Diminishing triad** — three tellings of one shift, each
  shorter than the last, the third too short to finish a thought.
- **Mood & emotional arc.** The ladder does the emotional work: `ah` sits in the chest and is where the
  furniture lives; `eh` is the middle of the face and is where the hours live; `ee` is bright, forward and
  slightly thin, and is where the hands and the going-home live. A stanza that reaches `ee` has completed a
  day. Verse 3 never completes one.
- **Instrumentation & texture.** As the ladder climbs, the arrangement should *not* climb with it — the
  band stays flat so the vowel is the only rise. Under Verse 3, the muted guitar drops its offbeat and plays
  the downbeat only, so the truncation has a rhythmic echo.
- **Production & effects.** No de-essing on the `ee` positions; let the top be slightly bright and slightly
  thin. Keep the vocal compression light enough that the chest-vowels stay heavier than the face-vowels —
  the ladder must survive the mix, and a heavy limiter flattens exactly this.
- **Structure & dynamics.** Verse lengths are non-negotiable: **8, then 4, then 2.** Any variation that
  wants more room takes it from a chorus-adjacent section, never from a verse.
- **Rhyming scheme.** End-rhyme is loose and **secondary by design**; the ladder is the spine. **Rhyme debt:
  not owed** — the two declared returns are the ladder and the byte-identical chorus. The exact-repeated-line
  ratio must nonetheless land above the house return floor, which three identical chorus blocks and a
  recurring short refrain deliver structurally.
- **Poetic devices (≥2).** **Assonance** as architecture rather than ornament; **homoioteleuton** at the
  ladder's rungs; **epanalepsis** in whichever variation uses the envelope chorus; **tmesis** avoided
  entirely — it would break the stress map.
- **Hook techniques (3).** ⑴ **Place the chorus's own tagline on the ladder's bottom rung** (`ah`), so the
  hook is the most chest-resonant phrase in the song and therefore the easiest to sing along to badly, which
  is the correct way to be sung along to. ⑵ **Put one full ladder inside a single chorus line** — three
  stressed vowels, three body-parts of the same physical action — so the device is audible even to a
  listener who never hears the verses again. ⑶ **Withhold `ee` from the last verse and give it back in the
  tag**, so the return of a vowel is felt as an event.
- **Lyric structure.** Mean sung line length is capped hard: **under seven and a half words**, and the
  ladder is what makes that comfortable — short stressed hardware nouns, caesura inside the line.

### 🎼 GUIDE 5 — **TWO YEARS ARRIVES AS AN ANSWER** *(facet 5 · leads V3)*

**The direction.** Exactly **one** number is sung in this song and it is **two years**, spelled in words,
once, at the emotional hinge, **inside somebody's reply** — never announced, never recited, never counted
up to. Direct the writer to build the hinge as a small exchange: something is said in the room, the number
is the answer, and **the room immediately contradicts the arithmetic.** Every other quantity the research
knows stays weather in the brief and never reaches a sung line. ⛔ **No other number appears anywhere in any
sung line of any of the four songs** — not hours, not bolts, not a time of day, not "half".

- **Storytelling techniques (2).** ⑴ **The recontextualising fact** — a plain quantity lands in an ordinary
  scene and re-prices everything the listener has heard so far. ⑵ **Antimetabole of value** — what the number
  measures and what it fails to measure are put in the same sentence with the terms reversed.
- **Mood & emotional arc.** Steady attention → the reply → a beat of **cognitive dissonance** (the number is
  correct and the body disagrees with it) → disapproval → a composure that does not resolve. **The narrator
  is not astonished.** The astonishment is the listener's job.
- **Instrumentation & texture.** Under the reply, the arrangement thins to brushes and bass for two bars —
  ⛔ **not** to silence, and ⛔ **not** with a filter. When the figure returns it returns on the upright, not
  the Rhodes, because the hinge and the seam are the same moment in this variation.
- **Production & effects.** The reply is sung at the same distance as everything else. No lift, no
  reverb-throw, no stereo spread, no double — the line is dangerous enough without help, and production
  emphasis would turn testimony into a slogan.
- **Structure & dynamics.** Target **3:35–4:00**. Twelve sections, **no intro** — the song starts cold on
  Verse 1, first person, mid-thought. One **[Bridge]** immediately before the rupture, which is the only
  place in the pair where a bridge exists.
- **Rhyming scheme, by section.** V1 (8): couplets that keep failing to close — `A A' B B' C C' D D'`, every
  second rhyme a slant one, so the stanza sounds like a person correcting themselves. Chorus (8):
  **epanalepsis** — first line and last line identical, no repetition between them, internal rhyme `x A x A
  x x B x`. V2 (4): `A B C B`. Bridge (4): monorhyme. Rupture (8): unrhymed, hard consonant landings.
  Verse 3 (2): unrhymed, ladder cut at `eh`. Outro tag (6): `A B A B C C`.
- **Poetic devices (≥2).** **Epanalepsis** (the envelope chorus), **litotes** at the hinge — the reply
  understates and the understatement is the wound — **internal rhyme** inside the reply line only, and
  **polyptoton** on *keep*.
- **Hook techniques (3).** ⑴ **The envelope chorus** — because the first and last lines are identical and
  nothing between them repeats, the hook is felt as a *return* rather than a *chant*, which is a different
  and quieter kind of adoptable. ⑵ **The hinge line delivered on the exhale of the previous phrase**, no
  breath taken, so the number arrives as part of an answer rather than as a new sentence. ⑶ **The chorus's
  last statement placed over the upright piano instead of the Rhodes** — identical words, identical melody,
  different furniture, and nothing said about it.
- **Narrative & thematic seed.** *The day the frame came back full, and the hands felt nothing.*
- **Emotional tone signals.** Flat, exact, and unrelieved by the good news.
- **Narrative elements.** First person throughout; the guard speaks. ⛔ Nobody is addressed, no collective
  pronoun exists, and the missing thing is never spoken to.

### 🃏 GUIDE 6 — **WILD CARD: THE RETAINED BLOCK, PLAYED AS A ROOM** *(cross-disciplinary)*

**The direction.** Borrow from a discipline that destroys in order to read, and let its ethic set the
arrangement's rule rather than the lyric's content. **Consume the minimum; retain the source.** Applied to
this pair: **nothing in the song is ever removed — every element that leaves is replaced in the same bar by
something audible in the same place.** Direct the arranger to treat every subtraction request as a
substitution request, and to keep a "block" — the harmonic figure — intact from first bar to last while the
"slices" (which instrument holds it, how close the microphone is, whether the continuo is present) are what
get spent.

- **Storytelling techniques (2).** ⑴ **Ekphrasis inverted** — instead of describing an image, describe
  everything the image was hanging in front of, and let the absence be the only thing with an outline.
  ⑵ **The palimpsest read** — a fresh coat of paint over the pale patch does not remove the patch; direct
  the writer to find the physical equivalents (filled holes, a shade that does not match at the edge, a
  floor worn in a shape).
- **Mood & emotional arc.** Neo-Baroque luminism as a *lighting* instruction rather than a genre: one warm
  source from the side, one cooler source from behind, and the subject reading as human-scaled in the
  middle. The arc is chiaroscuro, not crescendo — the song gets **deeper**, never bigger.
- **Instrumentation & texture.** Architecture as arrangement: the plate reverb draws a hall the quartet
  cannot fill, and the song never tries to fill it. If a part must exit, **something takes its chair in the
  same bar** — the Rhodes hands off to the upright, the brushes hand off to the rim, the muted guitar hands
  its offbeat to the bass. There is no hollow anywhere and there is no bar of nothing.
- **Production & effects.** Recorded as one room, one take feel, players audible as bodies: damper noise,
  brush texture on a coated head, a chair, a breath, fret buzz. ⭐ **The positive acoustic declaration is the
  style field's job**; every literal *no* lives in the exclude field, because the style field reads
  negations as positive tokens and will cheerfully deliver the thing being refused.
- **Structure & dynamics.** Terminal silence is permitted and here it is *used*: the last sound is the room
  closing, not a fade. ⛔ Interrupting silence is forbidden — it smooths away in the render and its absence
  is unfalsifiable.
- **Rhyming scheme.** The wild card's contribution to scheme is a prohibition and a debt: no variation may
  reuse another's chorus grammar (anaphoric chant / serial cascade / epanaleptic envelope / quotation and
  refusal), and any variation dropping a device must name the device that takes its chair.
- **Poetic devices (≥2).** **Chiasmus** across the seam (what the first half puts on the wall, the second
  half puts on the floor), **synecdoche** — hands, sleeve, clip, patch standing for the whole shift — and
  **metonymy** for the missing thing, which is never named directly in any variation.
- **Hook techniques (3).** ⑴ **Hook as maintenance** — the phrase returns because returning is what the
  character does, and it is never developed, ornamented or "paid off". ⑵ **The identical copy** — the third
  chorus is byte-identical to the first and the arrangement under it is byte-identical too; only the
  listener has changed, and that difference is the entire device. ⑶ **A last statement of the hook with the
  band already leaving the room**, brushes still going, so the hook is the thing that outlasts the
  arrangement rather than the thing the arrangement builds to.
- **Special effects & innovations.** ⭐ The one true innovation available to this pair is **a change of
  custody with no transition marker.** Every generator instinct is to announce a midpoint change; the entire
  bet is that refusing the announcement makes the change *land harder*, and that bet is falsifiable on the
  first render.
- **Narrative & thematic seed.** *The block is retained and the slice is spent* — as a form, never as a line.
