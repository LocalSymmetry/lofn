## 3. Panel / Critic Deliberation Log

### 3.1 — Essence, in one sentence, and the two best ways the medium carries it
**Essence:** *A woman is destroying a plant correctly at a bench, on purpose, for the fourth time this month, while the thing she is making is already being played badly and very loudly by somebody else through a wall.*
**Medium leverage 1 — the seam is a timing relationship, not a timbre.** The reed is the only element that bends pitch and the only one off the grid; at the third chorus the machine follows it instead of the reverse. The meaning is carried by which element defers.
**Medium leverage 2 — the Auto-Tune is the singer's body.** Alien, glassy, pitch-locked. Her flatness about the miracle is audible in the processing before a single word is understood, which is how the song can be bored while the listener is not.

### 3.2 — The two critics, and why these two
- **THE LUTHIER (role seat, Configuration 3)** — reed and woodwind making; the seat that produced *"the difference is not destruction versus preservation, it is whether anybody was holding it."* Chosen because this pair is the only one in the run where that distinction is literally the subject, and because a luthier can tell a working note from a tourist's note in one line.
- **⚠️ THE SCARCITY RETURNER (after Sianne Ngai) — Devil's Advocate / Hyper-Skeptic, Configuration 2** — the seat that vetoed the run's most quotable thesis on the grounds that a portable sentence is admired and not felt. Chosen because this pair's material is exactly the material that produces beautiful cards, and because she has already caught this pair once at Step 09.

### 3.3 — Critique in the critics' voices, per refined package

**V1 · Most Of The Cane Is Wrong**
- **LUTHIER:** this is a maker's song and the details are right — the good week that nobody buys, the arm that learned where to stop by ruining a stack, the bend-until-it-talks test. **V2 · The Wedding Takes It Louder**

**V3 · Sixty-Five Percent And Rising**

### 3.6 — Synthesis of the bottom two (Step 6), which is where the real work went
