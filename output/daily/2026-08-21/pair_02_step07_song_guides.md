# ⛔ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — ANSWERED BEFORE ANY GUIDE

### Q1 · WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**In a kitchen, at night, in a town nobody outside it has heard of, under a swarm of shallow shocks.** Not
"in a disaster" — in a *room*, with the fridge running. Her feet are on cold tile that has been laid over a
concrete slab; **one hand is flat on the front edge of the middle shelf**, fingers over the lip, the way you
hold a rail on a bus. Within arm's reach: a tin of loose beads on a low sill, a kettle still too hot to
lift, jars of rice and salt moved down two shelves over the past weeks, a stack of plates lying flat on the
tile because that is where they now live, a tea towel over her forearm, a hairline crack running out of the
top corner of the doorframe, and a doorframe with pencil marks on it. Down the road, a wedding is running
late and the band has not stopped. Her daughter is in the next room with the door not quite shut.
**The senses register, in this order:** the dog next door, then the soles of her feet, then the tin lids,
then the glass, then the tall tin leaning. That order is the instrument she owns.

### Q2 · WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**⭐ The floor is a lid.** There is a room under this room and it is not still. The shallow shocks — the ones
that nobody files — arrive **up** through the soles before they arrive **across**, and nearness, not size, is
what takes a jar off a shelf. She has been calling the near ones *small* because they are near, and she has
had the naming backwards the whole time. **Second hazard, and it is the one she cannot see:** the child in
the next room is learning her composure. The girl has the shelf order by heart, does not look up when the
glass goes, and has started keeping a tally of her own in blue pen, low on the doorframe, under her
mother's. **What is actually at risk is not the house. The house has a crack it can carry.**

*A guide that cannot answer both with a place and a stake is not ready to direct. Both are answered above,
and every guide below is scored against them.*

# GUIDE 1 · **THE SHELF ORDER READS** *(facet 1 · angle: SHELF COUNT)*

1. **Storytelling technique A — the procedural inventory with a verb.** Tell the method the way a person
   tells a route: object, then object, then what it means. Never list; each object must *do* something.
2. **Storytelling technique B — free indirect competence.** The narrator explains nothing. She reports what
   answered and reaches the size without ever saying "I am judging the magnitude."
3. **Mood & arc.** Open in `Composure` (flat, professional). Hold it through `Vigilance`. Past the midpoint
   move to `Trepidation` located **only in the body** — a hand that keeps shaking after it comes off the
   wood. Close in `Determination` pointed the wrong way: she puts the plates back **up**.
4. **The turn (mandatory, past the midpoint).** Verse 3. Opening stance: *I can size these; I am not
   frightened.* Complication: *I taught the child to be unfrightened, and now I want her frightened.*
   ⛔ Never state that. Stage it as an **action**: plates going back to the high shelf so they will have
   something to say.
5. **Instrumentation.** Floor tom, dry, hit with the flat of the hand, no cymbals. Tin-lid ticks as a
   field-recorded spark woven into the mix. Rhodes on one held Dorian chord, warm tremors. Electric bass —
   round, deep, funky, **entering late** and never on the first repeat of anything.
6. **The single intrusion (SKOP-01, N=1).** One squeezebox/accordion figure, four notes, carried in from a
   wedding down the road. It is the only non-modern element. Nothing else may be historical.
7. **Production.** Small tiled room; dry, close, mono-centred; the only ambience is what the tiles give
   back. Delay kept dead centre. **State the acoustic positively in the style field; every literal "no" goes
   to the exclude field.**
8. **Structure & dynamics.** Intro (bass absent) / V1 / Chant / V2 / Chant / Bridge / V3 / Chant ×2. Target
   3:20–3:50. Static throughout; the only dynamic events are the bass arriving and the plate stack landing.
9. **Verse form (never varied).** Couplets: **a 7-syllable line answered by a 4-syllable line, every time.**
10. **Rhyme logic.** ⭐ **Internal rhyme chained across the caesura, never at the line end.** The rhyme sits
    inside the 7-line and hands its sound to the 4-line answer. End-rhyme is incidental and unplanned.
    ⛔ **Rhyme debt: not owed** — internal rhyme is an addition; the byte-identical chant is the return.
11. **Bridge form.** 4-syllable lines **only**, unanswered — the answer-half of the couplet, orphaned.
12. **Poetic devices (≥2).** *Polyptoton* (turns/turn, wait/weight) and *epizeuxis* ("The hand. The hand.").
13. **Hook option A.** The chant enters through a wall, thin, doubled an octave up, unaccompanied.
14. **Hook option B.** The chant lands on the substituted downbeat, plate stack under every second syllable.
15. **Hook option C.** Final pair of chants: the mother's voice joins on the second occurrence, a fraction
    under, and never corrects.
16. **Return geometry — BAR scale (this pair's alone).** The four-note squeezebox figure moves **one scale
    degree** on every repeat and is never corrected; **no other element adjusts to it.**
17. **Substituted downbeat (Flair 8).** Where the backbeat was, a stack of plates settles a sixteenth late.
18. **Survivable wound (Flair 13).** The hairline crack out of the doorframe corner. The house can carry it.
    The question the song refuses to ask aloud is whether the child can.
19. **The chant a room can hold (Flair 10).** Five syllables, byte-identical, started by the daughter.
20. **The one sung fact.** ***ten kilometres down***, at the hinge, **responded to** by converting the depth
    into a distance she has walked. ⛔ No second number anywhere.
21. **Legibility.** A stranger retells it in one sentence: *a mother counts earthquakes by which shelf
    rattles.* Say plainly what is happening. Do not say what it means.
22. **Emotional tone signal.** Professional tenderness toward a thing she is processing.
23. **Vocal technique.** Late-thirties plain low alto, unornamented, clipped consonants, lands a fraction
    early on the short lines, breath audible, no vibrato.
24. **Key sequence.** The bass arrives under the second chant and the room does not get bigger — only lower.

# GUIDE 2 · **THE FIGURE THAT GOES WRONG AND IS NOT FIXED** *(facet 2 · angle: TEN KILOMETRES)*

1. **Storytelling technique A — the technical explanation as intimacy.** A person explaining how a thing
   arrives is a person telling you what they lie awake with. Keep the physics in the soles of the feet.
2. **Storytelling technique B — antanaclasis as an argument.** One word said twice, meaning two things, is
   the whole reversal: *just* as dismissal, then *just* as measurement.
3. **Mood & arc.** `Contemplation` → `Apprehension` → `Reverence` (terror-adjacent, unresolved). No relief.
4. **The turn.** The far big shake barely moved the tall tin; the near small one took the jar clean off.
   The naming has been backwards. Stage it as a correction of vocabulary, not as a revelation.
5. **The hinge.** ***Just ten kilometres down*** answered by *just* used the other way. The fact is
   **responded to**, never recited.
6. **Instrumentation.** Floor tom alone for the first eight bars, no lids. Bass enters latest of the four
   songs — under the bridge. Rhodes replaced here by a low held electric-piano cluster, pulsating,
   suspending time. Squeezebox figure as before, walking off the note and continuing to walk.
7. **Production.** Face-down proximity: the vocal should sound like it is being sung with a cheek on tile.
   Mono-centred sub. Positive declaration: every consonant audible, room tone limited to what tile gives.
8. **Structure.** Intro (floor tom alone) / V1 / Chant / V2 / Chant / Bridge / V3 / Chant ×2 / Coda
   (a cappella chant, then **terminal** silence — terminal survives the renderer; interrupting does not).
9. **Verse form.** The pair's couplet, unvaried: 7 answered by 4.
10. **Bridge form.** ⭐ **Five-syllable lines only** — the chant's meter has colonised the song. The
    daughter's measure is now the measure of the whole bridge, and nobody remarks on it.
11. **Rhyme logic.** Internal chain across the caesura; the /oʊl/ and /aʊn/ families (roll, soles, down,
    ground) carry the arrival of a wave.
12. **Poetic devices.** *Antanaclasis* (just/just) and *chiasmus* (up before across / across after up).
13. **Hook option A.** Chant delivered as a child's direction-of-travel rhyme, flat, no melisma.
14. **Hook option B.** Chant doubled by the squeezebox a step wrong, so the sung line and the played line
    disagree and neither yields.
15. **Hook option C.** Final coda chant unaccompanied, one voice, stopping mid-decay into terminal silence.
16. **Held fear (Axis 5).** **The floor is a lid.** Name it plainly once, in the first verse, and never
    explain it.
17. **Survivable wound.** Inverted: the mother lies face down on the child's floor so the shock reaches her
    body a beat sooner. She knows it is no help. She does it anyway.
18. **Substituted downbeat.** Here the plate stack is replaced by a knuckle on the underside of a table —
    same job, different wood, so the pair's two strongest songs do not share a sound.
19. **Legibility.** *A woman explains how a shallow earthquake arrives, and realises the small ones are the
    near ones.*
20. **Vocal.** Same singer, staged lower and closer, more breath, occasional near-spoken line ends.
21. **Central aspect.** Depth as nearness. The number is a distance, not a statistic.
22. **Special effects.** A sub-bass swell that rises **before** the floor tom rather than with it — the P
    arrival before the S arrival, never named as such.

# GUIDE 3 · **THE SUBSTITUTION IS AUDIBLE** *(facet 3 · angle: BETWEEN)*

1. **Storytelling technique A — the interval as the subject.** The song is the wait after, not the shock.
   Nothing happens and everything is decided.
2. **Storytelling technique B — the chore as the load-bearing line.** A domestic instruction carries the
   emotional weight because nobody is allowed to say the emotional thing.
3. **Mood & arc.** `Suspense` → `Equanimity` (false) → `Recognition`. End on an action, not a feeling.
4. **The turn.** She has been calling the near ones small; and she puts the plates back up **because the
   child asked**, not because it is safe. Being managed by her own daughter is the wound.
5. **Instrumentation.** Brushed floor tom. Kettle-metal ticking as it cools, used as the clock. Bass enters
   under the second chant and stays under the tile. Chamber-pop upright piano, felt-muted, one chord.
6. **Production.** Dry, close, mono-centred; the plate stack is the loudest wooden object in the mix.
7. **Structure.** Intro / V1 / Chant / V2 / Chant / Bridge / **Instrumental** (squeezebox alone, a step
   further wrong) / V3 / Chant ×2. The instrumental is where a bridge-thesis would have gone; it is
   wordless on purpose.
8. **Bridge form.** ⭐ **Inverted couplets** — the 4-syllable answer arrives **before** its 7-syllable
   question. The reply precedes the question for the whole bridge, and is never explained.
9. **Rhyme logic.** Internal chain, /uːl/ and /aɪl/ families (cool, tool, tile, while).
10. **Poetic devices.** *Anaphora* on a two-word phrase, and *asyndeton* through the verse-3 reversal.
11. **Hook option A.** The chant sung as an instruction, not a plea — flat, bored, absolutely certain.
12. **Hook option B.** Chant over the plate stack only, no bass, so the substitution is naked.
13. **Hook option C.** Chant with the kettle tick as the only other pitched element.
14. **Special Flair 8, primary here.** Where the backbeat was: plate stack, a sixteenth late, dry, wooden.
15. **Special Flair 13.** The crack over the door has grown **a fingernail** — the wound measured by a body
    part, never by a number.
16. **The one sung fact.** ***ten kilometres down***, opening verse 3, answered by *not far down*.
17. **Legibility.** *A woman waits out the interval after a shock and decides whether the plates go back up.*
18. **Emotional tone signal.** The competence is real and it is not exculpation (Panel C: the worker is
    good at it).
19. **Narrative elements.** Nobody is addressed. Comfort arrives only by proximity: she sits on the floor
    beside the child and marks the frame with the same pen.
20. **Lyric structure.** Verses 20 / 16 / 20 lines — deliberately unequal; length is not complexity.
21. **Vocal.** Slightly warmer than Guide 1, still dry, still unornamented, with one held vowel per verse.

# GUIDE 4 · **THE COMPOSURE THAT IS NEVER NAMED** *(facet 4 · angle: THE ONES BELOW THE NEWS)*

1. **Storytelling technique A — the tally as a character.** Pencil marks on a doorframe: her child's height
   and the shocks in the same wood. The frame keeps both and does not distinguish them.
2. **Storytelling technique B — reported speech without confirmation** (Flair 14). The girl is quoted; the
   narrator never verifies her.
3. **Mood & arc.** `Solitude` → `Tenderness` → `Shock` (quiet, domestic) → `Consolation` withheld.
4. **The turn.** A new mark appears low on the frame, crooked, in blue pen. The child has been keeping her
   own count, at night, and has more of them.
5. **⛔ The forbidden section is doing real work here.** This is the song whose thesis is most portable
   (*composure is what gets inherited*). It may not appear in the bridge and may not appear as a line
   anywhere. The bridge is pure observation: what the girl does, and does not do.
6. **Bridge form.** ⭐ **7-syllable lines only** — all question-halves, never answered. Anaphora on a
   two-word negative phrase. The withheld 4-syllable answers are the point and are never supplied.
7. **Instrumentation.** Indie-dub weight: bass round and late, delay tails kept dead centre and short.
   Floor tom with a folded cloth on the head. Squeezebox figure heard through a wall, not in the room.
8. **Production.** Mono-centred dub echo — a tail, never a width. Positive declaration: the delay is a
   repeat of the source, close and dry, not a space.
9. **Structure.** Intro (**the chant alone**, a young voice, unaccompanied — the daughter starts it, which
   is the pair's law made structural) / V1 / Chant / V2 / Chant / Bridge / V3 / Chant ×2 / Outro
   (squeezebox alone, still wrong, then terminal silence).
10. **Rhyme logic.** Internal chain, /ɪk/ and /ʊd/ families (tick, thick, wood, good).
11. **Poetic devices.** *Anaphora* and *antanaclasis* on "big enough".
12. **Hook option A.** The chant sung by the young voice first, alone, no accompaniment at all.
13. **Hook option B.** The chant with the dog next door underneath it.
14. **Hook option C.** Final chants with the mother a fraction flat and staying there (Flair 11 as texture,
    not as this pair's device).
15. **The one sung fact.** ***ten kilometres down***, in the **bridge**, read flat off a screen by the girl,
    **responded to** by her going straight back to humming. The fact is not the thesis; the humming is the
    response.
16. **Held fear.** It survived and nobody is coming for it — ten shocks in a day, none of them anybody's
    headline. ⛔ **No news-reader register.** Absence of news is the subject; a broadcast voice is banned.
17. **Legibility.** *A woman keeps a pencil tally of unreported earthquakes on the same doorframe as her
    daughter's height marks.*
18. **Cohort gate.** Zero collective pronouns. On this subject *we* is the song refusing to name who.
19. **Lyric structure.** Verses 18 / 18 / 22 lines. The final verse is longest because the turn needs room
    to be *done*, not announced.
20. **Emotional tone signal.** The offence is abdication, not loss. No villain. Nothing here is anybody's
    fault and that is the horror.

# GUIDE 5 · **THE CHANT AS THE ARCHITECTURE** *(facet 5 · chant-first construction)*

1. **Storytelling technique A — build the song from the hook outward.** Write the five syllables first;
   every verse is a place the chant could be overheard from.
2. **Storytelling technique B — the overheard, never the addressed.** The listener is in the hallway.
3. **Mood & arc.** `Curiosity` → `Absorption` → `Trepidation`, with the chant unchanged throughout — the
   meaning moves and the phrase does not (this is the run's **identical copy that is a different object**).
4. **Hook engineering.** Five syllables. Byte-identical. Joinable on the second occurrence. Started by the
   daughter. Containing no number and no collective pronoun. Not a prayer, not addressed to anything.
5. **Test.** If a panelist cannot hum it back after one read, rebuild it. This is a floor, not a target.
6. **Placement.** Four chant appearances minimum, at least one unaccompanied, at least one over the plate
   stack alone.
7. **Instrumentation.** Whatever is minimum. The chant must survive being the only thing in the room.
8. **Structure.** The pair spine, with an added unaccompanied appearance somewhere the listener does not
   expect it.
9. **Rhyme logic.** The chant does not rhyme with anything. It returns instead.
10. **Poetic devices.** *Epizeuxis* by repetition of the whole phrase; *diacope* around it in the verses.
11. **Special Flair 10, primary.** A chant a room can hold.
12. **Merge note for step 10.** This guide is deliberately thin as a standalone song and rich as a donor —
    it is built to be synthesised into Guide 3's angle, contributing the chant-first architecture and the
    unaccompanied appearance.

# GUIDE 6 · **WILDCARD — THE INCREMENT BORER** *(cross-disciplinary: dendrochronology → a doorframe)*

1. **The import.** From the ICB's BRIDGE configuration, the dendrochronologist's seat: *a core is drilled
   out of a living tree to read its years; the tree closes over the wound; the core does not go back; you
   take the core from the specimen that can afford it, and that judgement is the job.*
2. **Translation to this kitchen.** The doorframe is the specimen. The pencil marks are the core. Soft wood
   takes a line and keeps it. **The frame has gone out of true and the door still shuts.**
3. **Storytelling technique A — the object that records without being asked.** Wood as an instrument that
   was never installed on purpose.
4. **Storytelling technique B — the reading, not the ruin.** Do not describe damage. Describe *what can be
   read off* the damage, by a person with a pencil.
5. **Mood & arc.** `Fascination` → `Tenderness` → `Self-doubt`, and the self-doubt is about the judgement:
   was this the specimen that could afford it?
6. **Instrumentation.** The knock of a shoulder against a swollen door as a percussion element. A pencil on
   wood as a texture. Otherwise the pair's palette, unchanged.
7. **Production.** Very close on the wood sounds; the door is the second-loudest object in the mix.
8. **Structure.** Pair spine; the bridge is the door sticking and giving.
9. **Rhyme logic.** Internal chain, /uːd/ and /eɪm/ families (wood, good, frame, same).
10. **Poetic devices.** *Metonymy* (the frame for the family) and *polyptoton* (mark / marked / marking).
11. **Hook options.** As Guide 4; this guide's job is the doorframe, not the hook.
12. **Held fear.** The wound is survivable **for the frame**. The song never says who else is being cored.
13. **Merge note for step 10.** Built as a donor for Guide 4's angle — it supplies the doorframe, the blue
    pen, the out-of-true frame that still shuts, and the ethical question underneath the tally.
14. **⛔ Discipline check.** Only **one** intruding material is permitted per song. The borer is a *reading
    method*, not a second sonic world; it may not bring an instrument with it.
