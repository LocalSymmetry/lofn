## 0. THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS — ANSWERED CONCRETELY, BEFORE ANY GUIDE

### **Where is the body standing?**
At a preparation bench, late, in a windowless room off a corridor whose lights run on a timer. She has a split rock, one half in each hand — the **part** and the **counterpart**, the fossil and its negative. Chalk dust is in the seam of her palm and dried into the lines of her thumb. A rock hammer is set down where she put it, still warm from the hand. The anglepoise is dropped low and pushed off to the left so the light rakes across the surface at a hard angle: that is how you make a low-relief thing show at all. On the bench: a foam tray, a dental pick, a sable brush, a jar of consolidant, a card with a locality and an age written on it in somebody else's handwriting. Under everything, a mains hum from the floor that has not stopped since she came in. **She is good at this and she is paid by the tray.**

### **What could hurt her here?**
Not the rock. **The thing in it is the part that never survives, and it survived, and there is no reason for it.** The soft tissue was the disposable component — designed to rot in a week in warm mud — and the calcite column that was built to last came apart exactly as built. Flair #12 runs backwards here: *the consumable is what is left*. So the fear is two-handed and neither hand resolves: **it survived and nobody is coming for it** (the corridor lighting is on a timer; the cabinet temperature is maintained by nobody in particular; the drawer will be opened by somebody who finds it ordinary), and — the sharper one — **you are the copy and you know it**: what she is holding is an impression, a print of a print, and the survivor is not the animal. She is the warmest object currently touching a thing that has not been warm since the sea it lived in closed. *This is stated here so no song has to state it.*

**Legibility contract for all four songs:** a stranger retells it as *"someone opens a rock and finds the soft part of an animal that died four hundred and fifty million years ago."* The subject is plain in the lyric. The engine is not.

## 1. STANDING DIRECTION FOR ALL SIX GUIDES *(the operations, stated once)*

- **The floor operation:** sub-bass is the room's floor — present from the first bar, unarriving, never a drop or a swell. Where a kick would be, a **dry wooden knock** sits in exactly that place. Substitution, never subtraction; the quiet is *low*, never *absent*; terminal silence only.
- **The material operation:** an acoustic viola is genuinely in the room — rosin drag, fingerboard knock, bow hair on gut — and its figure is **data-moshed as material, played not applied.** Its loop length disagrees with the bar (fifteen-sixteenth, seven, nine or five against four-four, one choice per song) so the grid keeps missing itself. That disagreement is the kinetic defect and it is the reason the piece cannot settle into wash.
- **Flair #7, structurally:** every return of that figure arrives **one generation further down** — bit depth drops, the copy of the copy, melody still legible. The degradation must be pointable, not a master-bus filter.
- **The return geometry (PHRASE scale, this pair's alone):** one held vowel, same pitch class, at **bar four, bar twelve, bar twenty-eight**, longer each time. The ladder generates its own intervals: strophes of three, five and eight lines land their ends at exactly those bars. Under each held vowel the pad drops to floor-only so the vowel is an **event**, not a pad note.
- **The mouth operation:** monosyllabic in the strophes, polysyllabic in the returning strophe. The single sanctioned breach is the sung fact itself, which is the only long word allowed into monosyllabic terrain and is therefore the hinge, audible as a change of jaw.
- **Never cadences.** Modal throughout; the final chord stays open; no section resolves onto its tonic. There is no chorus and no adoptable hook by assignment — the returning strophe is the memorable object and it is memorable by recurrence, not singability.
- **Forbidden section: the opening verse.** Nothing thesis-shaped lands there.
- **Bans in force:** no second sung numeral, no collective pronoun, no address to the listener, no *miracle / gift / against all odds*, no dependence on stereo width, and no made thing describing its own condition except V3's single line.

## 2. GUIDE 1 — against facet **F1 · ORDINARY HANDS, TERRIBLE OBJECT**

**Storytelling techniques:** *procedural realism* (the tradition of the work-shift narrative, where competence is the characterisation and no one explains their job to the reader) and *the withheld reaction* (the camera stays on the hands through the moment the face would have been the shot). **Mood and emotional arc:** open in unhurried absorption — the room-temperature attention of somebody eleven hours into being good at something — pass through a vigilance that never becomes alarm, and end in a composure that is *worse* than alarm because it does not change anything. Smell of acetone and cold stone; grit that makes a small sound against the bench when she brushes it off. **Instrumentation and texture:** viola with the bow noise left in, upright piano with the felt lifted so the hammers click, a fabric-muted hand drum, and the wooden knock keeping the pulse where a kick would sit; cosmic pad far under, granular echoes at the edges. Layer the voice a hand's width in front of everything, dry, with the room arriving behind rather than around. **Production and effects:** a small hard room, close mics, almost no tail; the reverb that exists belongs to the corridor, not the bench. **Structure and dynamics:** four and a half to five minutes; the ladder rises three, five, eight without a single lift, the returning strophe enters at the point a chorus is expected and does something else entirely. **Rhyme scheme:** free verse, zero end-rhyme, all sections — the debt is discharged by the held vowel and the returning strophe, never by a repair. **Poetic devices:** *epizeuxis* (a plain phrase said twice with nothing added, which is what people actually do) and *asyndeton* (clauses laid flat with the conjunctions removed, so competence reads as competence). **Hook techniques — three ways to make the memorable object stand out:** (a) the returning strophe enters with a **hard-tuned octave double** exactly where a chorus lift would be, so the ear gets its expected event and the event is a machine-flattened voice; (b) the viola drops a generation at the same bar so the return arrives visibly worn; (c) the last two lines of the return are sung with the pad muted to floor-only, so the long words land on nothing but sub. **Narrative seed:** a shift, correctly executed, over an object that should not exist; third person, present tense, no address. **Vocal techniques:** low alto, straight tone, breath audible, consonants slightly ahead of the beat. **Emotional tone signals:** absorption, unease, tenderness, numbness — never wonder announced.

## 3. GUIDE 2 — against facet **F2 · THE FLOOR IS PRESENT, THE FIGURE IS AN EVENT**

**Storytelling techniques:** *the room around the event* (describe the surroundings and never the moment itself — the cue that implies a scene it never shows) and *reported speech of the object* (what the card, the label, the tray say, quoted without a narrator confirming them). **Mood and emotional arc:** begin in flat attention, thicken into suspense that is purely textural, arrive at a detachment that has more dread in it than the suspense did. Think of raking light as a sound: everything is lit from the side, so only the ridges speak. **Instrumentation and texture:** the floor first — a continuous sub that behaves like weather rather than an event, never swelling, never dropping out; on top of it pulsating string clusters, deliberately mis-looped; granular echoes and warm tremors from a struck metal tray; a tin lid tapped with a pencil; cosmic pad written as a slowly rotating cluster rather than a chord. **Production and effects:** commit the disagreement in the arrangement, not the mix — the loop-length conflict must survive a mono fold, because width will not survive the renderer. Every removal is a substitution: when the pad leaves, the wooden knock is exactly where it was. **Structure and dynamics:** the piece never gets louder; it gets *denser and then thinner in the same place*, which is the only dynamic available to a song with no drop. **Rhyme scheme:** free verse throughout; the return is the only repetition and it is exact. **Poetic devices:** *anaphora* on a plain existential ("there is… there is… there is…") so the room accumulates without a verb of judgement, and *polysyndeton* in one late section, where the county-sized list refuses to stop. **Hook techniques:** (a) make the held vowel the only sustained pitch in the whole arrangement so it cannot be mistaken for pad; (b) drop everything but the sub underneath it, so the vowel is heard against a floor and not a bed; (c) let the third and longest held vowel overlap the entry of the returning strophe by one bar, so the two returns cross. **Narrative seed:** the building goes on around a thing that has just happened in it and registers nowhere. **Special effects and innovations:** each generation-down of the figure is achieved as an audible re-record — a photocopy of a photocopy — not as a filter sweep.

## 4. GUIDE 3 — against facet **F3 · THE DEBT IS PAID IN AUDIBLE OBJECTS**

**Storytelling techniques:** *the refrain that is not a refrain* (a block that returns byte-identical while everything around it has changed meaning) and *the ladder* (each stanza strictly longer than the last, so the listener feels the breath extending without being told). **Mood and emotional arc:** begin curious and small-scale, widen into fascination, and let the widening itself become the apprehension — three, then five, then eight lines, then a block of long words that will not be scanned for a rhyme because there is not one. **Instrumentation and texture:** the mouth is the instrument this guide is really about — monosyllabic lines want a bench-like accompaniment (knock, pick, click, mute) and the polysyllabic return wants sustained bowed material underneath so the long words have something to lie on. **Production and effects:** the return's hard-tuned double is doubling only, never replacing; underneath it the natural voice stays audible so the flatten reads as *a second copy*, not an effect. **Structure and dynamics:** three, five, eight — returning strophe — three, five, eight — returning strophe, and nothing else may take chorus position. Runtime four to five minutes. **Rhyme scheme:** end-rhyme stripped in every section of every variation. **Replacement, declared:** (1) one held vowel, same pitch class, at bars four, twelve and twenty-eight, lengthening each time; (2) the byte-identical returning strophe in chorus position. Nothing else discharges the debt and neither is optional. **Poetic devices:** *homoioteleuton* inside the polysyllabic return (the long words rhyme by suffix and only by suffix — *-ation, -ing, -ally* — which is a return of sound with no end-rhyme in it) and *diacope* across the ladder (a plain word interrupted and resumed). **Hook techniques:** (a) give the held vowel a real monosyllable from the scene so it is a word being held rather than a vocalise; (b) keep the same pitch class for all three, so the third one is recognised before it is understood; (c) place the third one so it arrives on the seam between the ladder and the return. **Lyric structure:** short heavy lines, mostly spondaic, in the strophes; long unstressed-heavy lines in the return; the jaw must audibly change shape at the seam.

## 5. GUIDE 4 — against facet **F4 · REACHABLE ONLY BY HAVING GONE THROUGH IT**

**Storytelling techniques:** *the uncertain narrator* (a voice that reports and declines to conclude, set against material that is begging for a conclusion) and *the deferred object* (the thing the song is about is named plainly and early, and what it *means* is never named at all). **Mood and emotional arc:** open in flat curiosity with no stance; pass the midpoint into a genuine turn where the arrival of a number reorganises the room; end somewhere the opening could not have predicted and does not summarise. **Instrumentation and texture:** the arrangement carries the argument the lyric refuses to make — the figure returns worn, the floor never changes, and by the last return the same block of words is sitting on a demonstrably more damaged accompaniment. That contradiction is the thesis and it is not written down. **Production and effects:** resist any production gesture that reads as commentary — no swell on the important line, no sudden dryness at the reveal. The important line gets the same treatment as the unimportant ones; that is the whole trick. **Structure and dynamics:** the thesis is forbidden from the opening verse, so the opening verse is pure procedure: hammer set down, chalk, lamp, grit, floor hum. The turn lands past the midpoint, in the section immediately after the first returning strophe. **Rhyme scheme:** free verse; no scheme anywhere; the return is exact. **Poetic devices:** *litotes* (the response to the enormous fact is understatement — *not much*, *right, then*) and *chiasmus* used once, quietly, so the sentence turns back on itself without announcing it. **Hook techniques:** (a) place the number so it lands mid-strophe, not at a section head, and let the next line be a physical response instead of a comment; (b) make the response an action the body performs (a jaw left open, a word not written down); (c) let the final return of the strophe be the only "answer" the song offers, and let it be word-for-word the answer it already gave before anything happened. **Narrative and thematic seed:** competence meeting an object that competence has no procedure for. **Central aspect:** what returns is identical and what it means is not — and nobody in the song says so.

## 6. GUIDE 5 — against facet **F5 · THE NUMBER IS ANSWERED AND THE VOICE DOES NOT PITY ITSELF**

**Storytelling techniques:** *understatement as characterisation* (the size of the fact is carried entirely by how small the reaction is) and *the overheard shift* (nobody is addressed; the listener is beside the bench, not in front of it). **Mood and emotional arc:** curiosity → absorption → one clean spike of incredulity that is over in a bar → equanimity that refuses to become peace. **Instrumentation and texture:** at the hinge, nothing rises. The one permitted change is *subtractive-by-substitution*: the pad steps out and the wooden knock and the sub are exactly where it was, so the number lands into a room that got harder rather than bigger. **Production and effects:** keep the number in the same vocal plane as everything else — no reverb bloom, no double, no pause in front of it. **Structure and dynamics:** the number is sung once, spelled entirely in words, at the emotional hinge, and the line after it is a response, never an explanation. In V1 the hinge is the interlude past the midpoint; in V2 it is the sixth strophe, where the number is the only polysyllabic thing in monosyllabic terrain; in V3 it is the fifth strophe, read off an old tag in someone else's handwriting; in V4 it is the sixth strophe, at county distance. **Rhyme scheme:** free verse. **Poetic devices:** *antanaclasis* on *soft* (the material property and the judgement, the same word doing two jobs) and *epistrophe* on a flat closing phrase that ends adjacent sections identically. **Hook techniques:** (a) let the response be an involuntary body fact rather than a sentiment; (b) let a second response be something she does *not* do (does not set the card down) — an action, not an absence; (c) let the third be a swear-word-shaped hole she declines to write in the notes. **Self-pity gate, operationally:** the singer may describe her condition **as a person with hands in a place**; she may never describe her condition as a made thing. **V3 alone** may cross that line **exactly once**, and the composition of V3 is the refusal that follows — the refusal must be physical (tools, margins, the lamp), never a second sentence about the self. Self-pity leaks as tone: watch for slowing down after the crossing, for any line that asks to be believed, and for the pad thickening in sympathy. **Emotional tone signals:** revelation without volume; numbness that is a working state, not a wound.

## 7. GUIDE 6 — **WILD CARD** · the palimpsest and the part/counterpart

**Cross-disciplinary import:** multispectral manuscript recovery — text scraped off and written over is still physically present under the new layer, and is read *without cutting anything*. Bring that operation into sound. **The compositional import is literal:** when a fossil-bearing rock is split you get two faces, the **part** and the **counterpart** — the thing and its negative, identical information, opposite relief. Write the arrangement as part and counterpart: for one section, a figure; for its return, the same figure with its contour inverted (rising intervals falling, accents on the offbeats the original left empty), the *mould* of what was just played. It is a return that is recognisable and not a repetition, and it belongs to the same family as the held vowel without duplicating it. **From painting:** raking light — a low, hard, side-on key that makes only ridges visible and hides everything flat. Its audio equivalent is a narrow-band excitation that makes only the transients speak; use it on the string figure so that at the third generation down, only its edges survive and the sustained part has gone. **From architecture:** a service corridor — long, lit on a timer, nobody in it — as the only reverberant space in the record, used once. **Mood and arc:** a curiosity that is genuinely scientific rather than reverent; the seam where it turns is the moment the negative is understood to carry as much as the positive. **Instrumentation and texture:** add one instrument that exists nowhere else in the pair — a bowed metal tray, struck once and bowed thereafter — as the counterpart's voice. **Rhyme scheme:** free verse; the counterpart section is the only place a *sound* may return without words returning. **Poetic devices:** *antimetabole*, used once and never explained, and *synaesthesia* linking the raking lamp to the narrow-band figure. **Hook technique:** let the counterpart figure appear early enough to be forgotten and return late enough to be recognised without being placed — the listener should feel the return before locating it. **Risk to manage:** this is the guide most likely to produce cleverness. It is bound by F4 — if the part/counterpart idea can be stated as a sentence in the lyric, it has failed; it must be in the arrangement or nowhere.
