## C. ⭐ THE DEVIATION CLAUSE — named in advance

**The disobedience I expect:** at the substitution, the generator will not honour *no gap*. The upright piano
will most likely arrive **a beat or two early**, overlapping the last Rhodes chord instead of taking its
place cleanly at the bar line.

**And I would keep it.** For that overlap the two instruments are briefly **the same instrument** — which is
exactly Flair #6, the identical copy that is a different object, arriving in the arrangement instead of in
the lyric. A deviation that performs the song's subject is raw material, not a defect. *(Judge the result,
not the distance from intent.)*

**The disobedience I would NOT keep, and it is a different one:** a crash cymbal, a riser, or half a bar of
air at the seam. Those convert a **substitution** into a **transition**, which deletes the device outright
and takes ruling 4 with it. Both are already refused in every exclude field.

**Second-order, recorded because it will probably happen:** the brushed sixteenth drag will be quantised
into a generically laid-back groove. That is a texture loss rather than a structural one — **worth one more
take, never worth rejecting a take that is otherwise right.**
