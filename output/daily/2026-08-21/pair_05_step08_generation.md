# RAW CANDIDATE R1 — *from guide 1 · THE LEVEL HOLDS*
### working title: **"The Door Is Locked and Still"**

**MUSIC PROMPT (raw):**
> Procedural electro-industrial at 104 BPM, held at speaking volume from the first second. A metal cabinet
> latch clacks shut on the downbeat while a dry drum machine is already mid-pattern beneath it, as if the
> recording joined a shift that had been running for an hour before anyone pressed anything. Female lead,
> mid-thirties, English, unhurried and almost spoken in a dub-poetry cadence, close-miked at a hand's width
> so room air and page-turns stay inside the vocal, sitting a fraction flat and never correcting until the
> flatness reads as the key. One chord throughout. Ballpoint on paper, a knocked wooden desk edge and the
> latch itself are played live and acoustic in the same small room as the voice, with a round low sub
> humming under all of it. Swung Brazilian-funk sixteenths lean against the straight machine grid so
> accents keep landing early, and every entry arrives a consistent hair behind the vocal. What occupies
> the downbeat changes across the track while the level stays exactly where it started. The latch is the
> loudest object in the mix.

**LYRICS PROMPT (raw architecture — load-bearing sections written, verse interiors specified):**
- Chorus sentence, twelve syllables, verbatim every time: **"The door is locked and still her palm goes to
  the plate."** Breaks: `…and still / her palm goes / to the plate` → `The door is / locked and still her
  palm / goes to the plate` → `The door is locked / and still her palm goes to / the plate`.
- Cold open, already running: keys off a hook, a lift door shutting itself, shoes reporting the floor, a
  pen clicking, a latch in the wall, a corridor running long, pages turned back, the open line found.
- Verse 1 — the entry written: date, hour, hour re-checked, initials, ink on the fold, the nib lifted too
  fast, the letter's tail dragging, a thumb smoothing it, the smear going grey and not gone, **left alone**.
- Verse 2 — the door: handle down, tongue staying in the frame, a lean, the frame giving her weight back,
  handle released, the latch reporting the wall, *fast* written in the condition column, the time beside
  it, the read distrusted, the handle tried again.
- Hinge — a card taped to the doorframe, one corner lifting; somebody printed it; **the card says thirty
  femtoseconds**; *it is over before the word is over*; her thumb flat on the word; the word not getting
  smaller; her mark coming off by morning under somebody else's pen; she dates the line anyway; the handle
  again. **Lines lengthen here and the level does not.**
- Verse 3 — the same round done harder: pressed in too hard, the nib splitting on the downstroke, the ink
  too wide, the pen not changed, pages squared, the book shut, keys back on the hook.
- Outro — re-enters line one **mid-phrase** (`— come off the hook.`), runs the cold-open sequence, and
  stops inside a line.

**EMO ladder:** Vigilance → Composure → Frustration → Cognitive Dissonance → Disillusionment (hinge) →
Resolve → Persistence.
**Poetic devices:** asyndeton throughout the verses; antanaclasis on *fast* (quick / secured).

# RAW CANDIDATE R2 — *from guide 2 · THE SUBSTITUTED DOWNBEAT*
### working title: **"The Column for the Time"**

**MUSIC PROMPT (raw):**
> Glitch experimental pop laid over an avant ambient techno grid, 104 BPM, conversational throughout. It
> opens on a bench printer letting a warm sheet go, and a cabinet latch takes the downbeat before the first
> bar has finished, with the machine already running underneath. Female lead, mid-thirties, English, dry
> and almost spoken, close-miked at a hand's width in a small hard room whose far wall returns her
> page-turns, pitched a consistent fraction under and never corrected. One chord for the whole song.
> Ballpoint on paper carries the hi-hat rate, a page-turn sits where a snare would be, a struck desk edge
> where a tom would be, and a mono sub hums at one level beneath everything. Micro-edited paper transients
> flicker as ghost notes. Accents lean early against a grid that refuses to swing. Midway the machine hats
> stand aside and pen-on-paper takes their exact rate at the same volume. The latch is the loudest object
> in the room.

**LYRICS PROMPT (raw architecture):**
- Chorus sentence: **"She writes the time down in the column for the time."** (tautology of procedure)
- Section map adds a **[Latch Break]** with no lead vocal between verse 2 and the second chorus.
- Cold open: the paper coming out warm, the roller letting it go, the curl against the tray, flattened by
  hand, the line already drawn on it, the lamp humming over it, the empty box found, the pen set on it, the
  cold bench, the room keeping her breath.
- Verse 1 — the form filled: top read, bottom read, the middle with no name, room / bench / lamp written,
  a tick box given a tick, the tick going past the line, **left out**, the sheet turned and turned back,
  the trace looking the same, the time written in blue.
- Verse 2 — the instrument: the shutter lifting, the meter starting to look, *the trace is on the page /
  the trace was on the page*, the shutter time and the meter time written down the same, read back, read
  back again, the room not replying.
- Hinge — the box she wants is already filled; somebody filled it in; **the print says thirty
  femtoseconds**; the print was on the page before the page was warm; the sheet held to the lamp; her name
  put below it; *her name is what gets added, her name is what comes off*; the lamp keeping its level.
- Verse 3 — the round again, with the middle column now carrying a name; the letters too flat and let
  stand; the stack squared; the lamp put out; the printer waking again.
- Outro — `— comes out warm.` re-entered mid-phrase, cold-open sequence, stops inside a line.

**EMO ladder:** Composure → Detachment → Impatience → Self-reproach → Skepticism (hinge) → Disapproval →
Vigilance.
**Poetic devices:** epanalepsis (*time … time*); antimetabole across added / taken off.

# RAW CANDIDATE R5 — *from guide 5 · THE FACT ANSWERED BY A HAND*
### working title: **"What Gets Added, What Comes Off"**
*(hinge-centric candidate — built around the write-mark / erase-mark equilibrium)*

**MUSIC PROMPT (raw):**
> Progressive electro-industrial at 104 BPM at conversational level, opening on a pen nib dragging on paper
> with a cabinet latch already holding the downbeat. Female lead, mid-thirties, English, almost spoken, dry
> and forward, close-miked at a hand's width, a fraction flat and never corrected. One chord throughout.
> Two pens are audible in the arrangement, one writing and one lifting ink off — a nib on paper and a

> page-turn complete the palette, all played live in one small room with the voice. The stresses land off
> the pulse and the machine does not adjust. Line lengths grow at the hinge while the level stays put. The
> latch is the loudest object in the mix.

**LYRICS PROMPT (raw architecture):**
- Chorus sentence candidate: **"What she puts on the page comes off before the light."** (twelve syllables)
- The whole candidate is a hinge with a song attached — verse 1 writes, verse 2 is erased, verse 3 writes
  the same thing again. ⚠️ This is its strength and its fault: see the step-09 critique.
- Hinge: the schedule pinned above the desk; somebody typed it; **the schedule says thirty femtoseconds**;
  she reads it standing; her mark comes off by morning; she initials the next line.
- **DEFECT, NAMED AT DRAFT TIME:** the erase-mark material states the pair's thesis too directly and too
  early, and the twelve-syllable sentence is a *conclusion*, not an action — a Portable-Thesis Veto
  violation waiting to happen in the FORBIDDEN SECTION. This candidate is a **donor**, not a song.
