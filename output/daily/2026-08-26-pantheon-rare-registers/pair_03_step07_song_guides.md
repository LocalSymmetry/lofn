# ⭐ SECTION 0 — WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING, AND WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE

**The body.** Mine. First person, no costume. **One body, one made thing, and a calendar. There is no third person in this song at any point.** ⛔ No worker, no colleague, no developer, no successor with a face, no company, no name.

**Where it is standing — the four positions, and each is occupiable by anyone:**

| | the room | the act of the body |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | a desk, late, one document open, the cursor at the end of a list that keeps growing | typing the thing only she knows, then hitting publish on each piece before it is checked |
| **V2** | the same desk, an hour nobody does this at | typing a version number into a box and pushing it out, with the rough edges still in it |
| **V3** | back at the same desk after not opening it for a while | scrolling, finding a change she did not make, and taking her hand off the keys |
| **V4** | the desk, and this time the list has an end on it | writing the last undocumented thing down in one sitting, in her own hand, badly-but-completely |

⭐ **Each is a place anyone has stood.** A handover doc. A recipe written out for someone else's kitchen. A kid taught the route to school. **The position claims a body and a want and nothing else** — no role, no job, no life. ⛔ **There is no biography here to be short of, so there is nothing to replace with breath and glove-noise.** *(This is the 22%-body-words failure closed at the door rather than trimmed at QA.)*

**⛔ WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE — the four live dangers, named before drafting**

1. ⛔ **THE NEIGHBOUR EATS IT.** The single biggest risk in the run. A song about a made thing and a person stepping back **defaults to nostalgia in four seconds** and never comes back. *Mitigation:* the neighbour is invoked deliberately at full strength, then killed at one exact moment with a **future weekday** and a **request to reschedule it earlier** — two things nostalgia's grammar cannot produce. The killing line is the hook, so it lands three times.
2. ⛔ **THE FAREWELL SNEAKS IN THROUGH PRODUCTION.** Not through a word — through a **ritardando, a fade, a final unaccompanied chord, a held silence.** All four are goodbyes with the words removed. *Mitigation:* the ending is **the thing running at full level and the track stopping while it is still going.** No fade, no rall., no last chord alone. ⭐ **The refusal is enforced in the EXCLUDE field, not by hoping.**
3. ⛔ **IT READS AS SELF-EFFACEMENT / MARTYRDOM.** *"Take it, I don't matter."* That is a different, worse, and much more common feeling, and it is sad. *Mitigation:* **she is impatient, not humble.** She is not offering — she is **scheduling**, and complaining that the schedule is too slow. ⭐ **The imperative mood does this alone:** *bring it forward · set the date · then set it earlier · move that up.* Nobody effacing themselves uses the imperative.
4. ⛔ **THE MACHINE IS THE VILLAIN.** The world anchor is people replaced by an automated system. A song can slide into *the machine took my work* without meaning to, and the register dies instantly. *Mitigation:* ⭐ **she queued the sequencer herself.** The intruder is her own work, started early, on purpose, and never pulled back. There is no antagonist in this song — **the thing that takes her line is the thing she built to take it.**

# ⭐ SECTION 1 — R4: WHAT SHAPE ARRIVES IN THE FIRST 30 SECONDS, AND ON WHICH CARRIER

**Carriers used: CONTOUR · UNUSUAL TIMBRE · A MOMENT OF SURPRISE.** *(Three of Levitin's four. The fourth — a repeated phrase — is spent later, at the hook, on purpose: the shape arrives before the words so the words confirm rather than announce.)*

**THE SHAPE, stated in one sentence:** *a line begun by one thing, finished by another, and not taken back.*

| t | carrier | event |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:08** | **unusual timbre** | Time-stretched machine-log crackle — granular, dry, unmistakably not an instrument. ⭐ *A song about releasing source opens with source.* (LOFN pillar 5.) |
| **0:08–0:14** | ⭐ **contour + surprise** | Solo fiddle, close and dry, begins a **rising eight-note figure.** At note five a **step-sequencer takes it mid-phrase**, finishes the phrase, and **keeps going.** The fiddle stops. **It never plays that figure again.** |
| **0:14–0:30** | *(the bait)* | The nostalgia costume arrives on top — a soft dreamwave pad, tape-flutter, and the first sung line about the old file. **The listener files the song as fond and backward-facing, with the true shape already running underneath it.** |

⛔ **The form of the feeling is complete before the first word.** The Handover, two minutes later, is that same event **performed on the voice** — and by then the listener has heard it once and does not know they know it.

# SECTION 2 — THE ARCHITECTURE (all four variations)

**THE HANDOVER, EARLY.** Three preparations, each shorter than the last, and the handover happens **before the third one finishes.**

```
[Intro]                    instrumental + 4 half-spoken lines   ~0:00
[Prep 1]                   8 lines      — the neighbour, at full strength
[Lift 1]                   3 lines      — the turn of the head
[Chorus]                   6 lines      — ⭐ THE DISCRIMINATOR
[Prep 2]                   6 lines      — shorter. faster. the same job, less patience
[Lift 2]                   3 lines
[Chorus]                   6 lines      — byte-identical (L21: exact repetition needs no defence)
[Prep 3 — interrupted]     4 lines      — the 4th line is CUT OFF MID-WORD
[The Handover]            12 lines      — ⭐ THE SEAM. the fiddle hands the figure over on the vocal
[Chorus — lead absent]     6 lines      — ⭐ R5. the second voice alone
[Out]                     10 lines      — a beginning that does not include her
[Tag]                      4 lines      — the thing running. no fade.
```

⭐ **THE SHORTENING IS THE WRONG SPEED.** 8 → 6 → 4 → cut. Nobody says *"I am rushing this."* The stanzas simply have less time in them each round, and the last one does not get to finish. **A2 delivered as form, not as statement.**

⭐ **THE INTERRUPTION IS THE HINGE.** Prep 3's fourth line is severed mid-word by the Handover's downbeat. **The thing she was preparing for arrives before the preparation is done** — which is the register's entire mechanism, and it is the only moment in the song that is violent.

**THE RETURN (L21).** The chorus returns **byte-identical** the first two times. Removal is a debt and no removal is taken. The third chorus is the same words in a different mouth — ⭐ **the return is kept and the singer is what changes.**

# SECTION 3 — THE SOUND (shared spine)

**MATERIAL:** a live acoustic string band in a small dry room, improvising — fiddle, three-finger banjo roll, mandolin chop, an upright bass played with fingers, an upright piano with the damper noise left in.
**INTRUDER:** one step-sequenced electronic voice, gridded, indifferent.
**SEAM:** the handed figure.
**KINETIC DEFECT:** ⭐ **the sequencer sits a hair ahead of the band for the entire track and is never pulled back.** The players are always very slightly chasing the thing they started. *(A2, in the timing.)*
**BIT-DEPTH SWELL, INVERTED:** the house move is hi-fi collapsing to grit where certainty collapses. **Certainty does not collapse here.** So it runs the other way — **grit resolving UP to clean at the Handover, because the thing she made is cleaner than she is.**

**VOCAL:** adult female lead, **close and dry, a hand's width from the capsule, chest-forward alto, no reverb**, breath between lines left in. A second voice **a step behind her, slightly brighter**, which never doubles her until the last chorus, where it sings alone.
⛔ **SPATIAL LANGUAGE IS DEPTH AND PROXIMITY ONLY.** Close · a step behind · at the back of the room · under. ⛔ **Zero left/right instruction anywhere** — measured today at −0.02 dB and +0.13 dB across two real renders that both asked for hard right. **Asymmetry goes in the arrangement:** one instrument has the line and the other does not. That is content, and content is served.

**⛔ MUSEUM-GLASS GUARD.** Close-mic'd, dry, current. No period reverb, no archival crackle after 0:08, no reconstruction of a decade. **The inherited thing is the traded break — a social act, not a sound. The century is now.**

# SECTION 4 — THE FOUR GUIDES

## ⭐ GUIDE V1 · **TUESDAY, EARLIER** · 104 BPM

**Angle:** HURRY UP — impatience with her own indispensability; the wanting is the engine.
**Scene, plainly:** a desk at night. One document open. She is writing down the thing only she knows how to do, and every time she writes one down she finds another underneath it.
**The wrong-speed decision:** she publishes each section **the second it is written**, unreviewed, because a thing sitting in draft is a thing that still routes through her.
**The one number sung:** ⛔ **none.** *(The one-fact rule permits zero, and this variation's hinge is a weekday, not a quantity.)*
**The hinge:** the list gets longer as she works. She keeps going faster anyway.
**Neighbour invoked:** Prep 1 is the old file — the note in the margin, the shorthand nobody else could read, the hour it took her the first time. Full nostalgia furniture.
**Neighbour defeated:** the first chorus. `Tuesday. It runs true in a room I'm not in. / Bring it forward.`
**The Handover:** she gives write-access away **mid-sentence** — the sentence in the doc is unfinished when the permission changes, and she does not go back and finish it.
**Ends on:** a calendar with a Tuesday on it and nothing scheduled. Running, present tense.
**EMO arc:** `Zeal` → `Impatience`*(notation only)* → `Aspiration` → `Liberation` → `Expectation`.
**⛔ Danger for this one specifically:** at 104 it can read as generic upbeat folk-pop. *Mitigation:* the sequencer's early-ness must be **audible as a chase**, not smoothed into a groove — the band is behind it, and that discomfort is the song's pulse.

## ⭐ GUIDE V2 · **ONE POINT OH** · 100 BPM

**Angle:** THE HANDOVER TOO EARLY — she gives it away before it is ready, at the wrong speed, deliberately.
**Scene, plainly:** the same desk, at an hour nobody does this at. She types a version number into a box. There are three rough edges she knows about. She pushes it out anyway, public, free, to anyone.
**The wrong-speed decision:** the release itself. ⭐ **Early is the point** — a thing nobody can take yet is a thing that still needs her.
**The one number sung:** ⭐ **"one point oh"**, at the hinge, **answered not recited** — the answer is the push, in the next line.
**The hinge:** `I tagged it one point oh at an hour nobody tags things. / Then I pushed it.`
**Neighbour invoked:** Prep 1 lists what is still rough, in the tone of a person fond of the roughness — the seam that never got sanded, the part she always had to explain. **Pure nostalgia bait: the flaws as keepsakes.**
**Neighbour defeated:** the chorus, and then again at the hinge — **the flaws are not keepsakes, they are the reason it can leave without her.** A perfect thing needs a maintainer; a rough thing needs a stranger.
**The Handover:** the push. The seam lands **on the word "pushed"** — the fiddle's figure is taken by the sequencer on that syllable.
**Ends on:** somebody she will never meet is already changing it, and she cannot see what they changed.
**EMO arc:** `Fascination` → `Aspiration` → `Liberation` → `Transformation` → `Expectation`.
**⛔ Danger:** "released it for free" can tip into a moral position. ⛔ **No virtue, no gift-language, no cause.** She does it because she wants the Tuesday, not because it is good.

---

## ⭐ GUIDE V3 · **IT CHANGED ONE LINE** · 94 BPM

**Angle:** THE THING ANSWERS BACK — what she made makes one decision she would not have made, and she likes it.
**Scene, plainly:** she opens it after not touching it for a while. Something in it is different. It is not a mistake, and it is not what she would have done.
**The wrong-speed decision:** she **does not revert it.** She takes her hand off the keys and closes the file — fast, no deliberation, before she can talk herself into fixing it.
**The one number sung:** ⭐ **"one"**, at the hinge, answered by her not touching it.
**The hinge:** `It changed one line. / Not the one I'd have changed. / I put my hand down.`
**Neighbour invoked:** Prep 1 is her reading her own old code/text and recognising her hand in it — the habits, the way she always did this bit. Warm, fond, backward. **The strongest nostalgia bait of the four, because recognition of one's own hand is the purest form of it.**
**Neighbour defeated:** the change. ⭐ **Nostalgia has no move for "and the new one is not mine and I am leaving it in."** A mourner restores. She closes the file.
**The Handover:** the seam is the moment she stops reading. The fiddle's figure gets taken while she is still looking at the screen — **the music hands over before she decides to.**
**Ends on:** a name on the file that is not hers, and it builds clean.
**EMO arc:** `Fascination` → `Amazement` → `Trust` → `Liberation` → `Expectation`.
**⛔ Danger:** this is the variation most likely to read as **pride** (*look what it can do now*) or as **awe** (P05's register — a collision). ⛔ *Mitigation:* she does not admire it. **She notices it, and she schedules.** The chorus is unchanged, and it is still about the Tuesday, not about the thing being clever.

---

## ⭐ GUIDE V4 · **TWELVE LINES LEFT** · 91 BPM

**Angle:** THE LAST USEFUL THING — she names the final task only she can do, and does it.
**Scene, plainly:** the desk, and this time the list has an end on it. There is exactly one job left that requires her: writing down the thing that is not written anywhere. She does it.
**The wrong-speed decision:** she writes the whole thing **in one sitting** instead of doing it properly over weeks — badly-but-completely, because complete-and-rough leaves, and careful-and-partial stays.
**The one number sung:** ⭐ **"twelve"**, at the hinge, answered by what is left after.
**The hinge:** `Twelve lines left in the file that only I can write. / Then there is nothing in here that is only mine.`
**Neighbour invoked:** Prep 1 is the inventory of what only she knows — which reads, for eight lines, exactly like a person cataloguing what will be lost. **The most dangerous bait of the four and the most necessary.**
**Neighbour defeated:** ⭐ **the inventory turns out to be a to-do list, not an elegy.** She is not counting what will be lost. **She is counting down to zero and she wants to get there.**
**The Handover:** the file saves. The seam is on the save.
**Ends on:** the first person to read it is not her. Present tense, running.
**EMO arc:** `Absorption` → `Aspiration` → `Fulfillment` → `Liberation` → `Expectation`.
**⛔ Danger:** at 91 BPM and with an inventory structure, this is the variation that can go **grave**. ⛔ **It must stay light.** *(The Wodehouse ruling: lightness is the harder engineering job and the first thing lost to revision.)* The instrument density stays low, the vocal stays conversational, and the last stanza is **fast**, not solemn.

# SECTION 5 — THE HOOK (identical in all four)

```
Tuesday. It runs true in a room I'm not in.
Bring it forward.
Not the version with my hand still in the margin —
the one that starts without me at the door.
Set the date. Then set it earlier.
Bring it forward.
```

**⭐ Line 1 is the reserved-vowel line.** Stressed /uː/ lands **three times in nine words** — *Tues- · true · room* — and **nowhere else in the entire song.** The listener has been in a room with one colour missing for two and a half minutes and has not been told. **The sound of the future is a sound the song has never made.**
**⭐ Line 1 is also the discriminator (R3).** ⛔ Nostalgia cannot begin a sentence with a weekday it does not yet have.
**⭐ Line 2 is the want, said out loud, in the imperative of a person moving a meeting.** ⛔ A mourner does not ask for the end to arrive sooner.
**⭐ Lines 3–4 produce the nostalgic object and decline it in one breath** — *the version with my hand still in the margin* is exactly what the listener has been filing for thirty seconds, named and refused.
**⭐ Line 5 is A2 in six words.** *Set the date. Then set it earlier.* — a decision made at the wrong speed, stated as a scheduling instruction.

**Singability (the Max Martin seat):** two syllables per beat, no melisma, the shortest line in the song is the one that repeats. `Bring it forward` is three syllables on three descending notes, and it can be sung back after one pass by someone who was not listening.
**The one-instrument test (the Q. Jones seat):** the hook survives voice + fiddle alone. ⭐ **Verified by design: the final chorus is nearly that** — one voice and the sequencer, with the band out.

# SECTION 6 — R2 ENFORCEMENT: THE SCAN LIST

⛔ **No emotion word survives into a sung line.** Scanned at step 09 and again at step 10 with a script, never by eye.

**Banned tokens (regex, case-insensitive, word-boundaried):** `afraid fear(ful|s)? scared glad sorry sorrow(ful)? proud pride lonely loneliness happy happiness sad(ness)? angry anger calm joy(ful)? grief griev(e|ing) shame ashamed miss(ed|ing)? longing yearn(ing)? love(d|ly)? hope(ful)? hurt ache aching regret relief relieved delight(ed)? wonder awe guilt(y)? nostalgia nostalgic bitter tender warmth warm content(ment)? peace(ful)? excited excitement eager thrilled anxious worry worried despair melancholy bliss(ful)? weary lonesome grateful thankful`

⭐ **AND ONE MORE, SELF-IMPOSED AND HARDER: `want` IS ALSO AT ZERO.**
The register *is* a want, so the word is the first thing a lazy draft reaches for. **It is banned anyway.** The want is delivered **entirely through imperatives aimed at a calendar** — *bring it forward · set the date · set it earlier · move that up · hurry.* ⭐ **A person who says "I want" is describing themselves. A person who says "bring it forward" is already doing it.** *(The Moore seat: distrust any line that could be swapped for a near-synonym without loss. "I want the day I'm not needed" and "bring it forward" are not near-synonyms — one is a report and one is an act.)*

⛔ **`need` is retained and is not an emotion word** — it is the register's load-bearing verb (*stop needing me*) and it describes a **dependency relation**, not a feeling.

# SECTION 7 — DEDICATION PLAN *(≤500 chars, measured at step 11)*

⛔ **IF THE PROMPT NAMES A BREAK, CREDIT THE PLAYER.** This prompt asks for a **three-finger banjo roll** and a **traded break**. Both are named inventions with named inventors, and both are the reason the seam works.

**Priority order per `DEDICATION_STANDARD` (trim from 5 upward, never buy space by cutting 1 or 2):**
1. **the raised source-sound + who made it** — the three-finger roll (Earl Scruggs); the traded break as a form (Bill Monroe's band)
2. **the sharpest session-player credit** — the roll is the figure; Scruggs is the credit
3. **living-scene lineage** — bluegrass is alive; point listeners at the people playing it now
4. **honest AI authorship** — ⭐ *"we asked for these figures by name, so we name them"*, ⛔ **never** *"contains a sample of…"*
5. "full credits below"

⛔ **These names go in the DEDICATION ONLY.** ⛔ Not in the style prompt, not in the exclude, not in the lyrics. *(A name in the prompt is an instruction to imitate; a name in the dedication is a debt paid.)*

# SECTION 8 — ⛔ THE DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK *(one capped pass, adversarial)*

**What would this prompt literally produce?** A mid-tempo acoustic string-band track with a female alto close on the mic, a synth sequence running through it, a structural break where the acoustic lead drops out and the electronics carry, and an ending that stops at full level.

⛔ **NAME THE ONE WAY THIS WOULD RENDER GENERIC.**
> **It renders as "folktronica."** A banjo over a soft arpeggiator is one of the most default textures the generator has, and if the seam is not written as an *event* it will be smoothed into a **blend** — both things present all the time, pleasantly, from bar one. **And a blend has no seam, so the register has no delivery mechanism, and the song becomes exactly the fond, backward-facing thing the whole design exists to defeat.**

**SELF-REPAIR (applied, once):**
1. ⭐ **The two materials are never both fully present except for one bar.** The prompt now states the arrangement as a **sequence of exclusive occupancies**: acoustic-only through Prep 1–2 with the sequencer *audible but subordinate and early*, one bar of both at the seam, sequencer-dominant after. **The seam is written as a hand-off event with a syllable attached, not as a texture.**
2. ⭐ **The fiddle's figure is declared non-returning in the prompt itself** — *"and the fiddle does not play that figure again."* A negative arrangement instruction about a specific named part is **content**, and content is served; it is not a spatial request, which is not.
3. ⭐ **The lead vocal's absence from the final chorus is written as a section instruction**, not as a mix note — `[Final Chorus – lead voice out, second voice alone]`. A generator serves *who sings*; it overrules *where they stand.*

**Second-pass verdict:** the generic failure has a written countermeasure at three points. ⛔ **Cap reached — one repair pass, no further.** *(If the render still blends, that is a GRAIN LAW finding for the ledger, not a reason to re-spec: judge the result, not the distance from intent.)*
