## GUIDE 1 · V1 — **"THE BACK WALL GOES FIRST"**
### facet anchor F1 · *the reading, not the pulse* · **129 BPM · F minor**

**Storytelling techniques.** *(i) The unreliable forecast* — the narrator's entire authority is a claim
about the near future, and the text never audits it; *(ii) synecdoche as census* — the room is never
described as a crowd, only as parts (a rail, a post, a wall, coats on a floor), so the listener assembles
the mass themselves and it becomes theirs. The song begins in a floodlit, sweating, low-ceilinged basement
and never leaves it: condensation on a ceiling at 11:40, tape in an X under her boots, a cable with just
enough slack. The emotional arc runs **forward-leaning readiness → declared certainty → an admitted
fallibility that does not reduce the certainty → the call, unresolved** — never triumph, never doubt as a
mood, and never a landing. Instrumentally, one **inherited body** only: a **call answered from another
register**, the soul-lineage horn punctuation stripped of its horn section and translated into a two-note
synth-brass stab that asks in the upper mid and answers itself a fourth lower, inside one bar; everything
carrying it is now — clipped electronic kit, muscular synthetic FM sub locked mono, candy-crisp programmed
claps sitting **8 ms late** while the sub stays exactly on the grid, granular vocal dust in the gaps, and
**no live kit, no horn section, no guitar anywhere.** Production is dry, close and bright with a short
glossy plate under the stack, a room-mic bed high-passed and mixed well under instinct, and **hard digital
silence used as the only transition device** — the record's whole punctuation grammar is *stop*, never
*rise*. Structure: **CALL-BEFORE-THE-MOVE**, Read (8, bare) / Call / Verse / Call / Chorus / Verse / Call /
Chorus / The Half-Second (4, unpaired, shortest) / Call-home, run-time about **3:05**; the arrangement
thickens **by voices only** — one lead becomes a six-wide stack at the second chorus — while the low end
refuses to move, and the two beats where a drop would go are **silent and then repeat the figure
unresolved.**

- **Rhyme / line logic.** End-rhyme kept, loose and forward-leaning; verse scheme **A B A C / D D E E**
  with the E-couplet arriving one line early so the stanza always finishes ahead of its own phrase. Chorus:
  **anaphoric, unrhymed, four repetitions of one clause**. Calls do not rhyme at all — they answer.
- **Poetic devices.** ⭐ **Epistrophe** (*"I would put money on that. / I have lost money."*) and
  ⭐ **anaphora** (*"The back wall goes…"* ×4, entering earlier in its phrase each time). Plus
  **asyndeton** in the Read — eight declaratives, no conjunctions.
- **Three hook implementations.** (1) *The lead alone, conversational volume, dead centre, no vibrato,*
  while the stack behind her goes full — the biggest line of the song delivered smallest; (2) *the room
  answering in the parenthesis* — a low crowd unison on **(Not yet.)**, high-passed at 180 Hz, arriving one
  beat before the bar that should contain it, **once, uncorrected**; (3) *the figure alone*, no vocal, both
  notes, over the silence where the drop is not.
- **R5 · the vocal decision she'd have to be talked into.** ⭐ **The hook, first time, sung flat-toned and
  un-belted at conversational volume.** Her instinct is full chest and land it; being asked to *under*-sing
  the title line is against everything in her. It is exactly right here: **a person reading a room does not
  shout the reading.**
- **Narrative & perspective.** First person, hers, present tense, no invented person anywhere; other bodies
  appear only as positions and objects. **One number sung:** *"Nine times out of ten"*, at the hinge,
  **answered** by *"and I'm still stood here for the other one."*
- **Lit from across (LAW 3).** The wash off the back of the room comes at her, sideways to her forward
  motion into the song; one held note is the five-second exposure.
- **Consequence class:** **vocal / harmonic pickup.** **Invariant layer:** the sub, all song.

## GUIDE 2 · V2 — **"YOU GO ON THE NEXT ONE"**
### facet anchor F5 · *the free channel, and the funny* · **124 BPM · D major**

**Storytelling techniques.** *(i) Comic inventory* — the scene is built from an absurd, affectionate list
of things that have already happened without her (a dog asleep at the barrier since the doors, a kid in a
yellow coat doing a whole song facing backwards, a bank of speakers aimed at an empty field);
*(ii) insistence as character* — the plot is one sentence said four times, and the drama is entirely in
whether she softens it. She does not. The arc runs **daylight appetite → a claim → the claim not
happening → the claim repeated, unchanged, with a laugh in the middle of it → the claim eroding down to a
single word** — and it is **funny**, on purpose, which is the hardest engineering in this pair. The
inherited body is a single **offbeat relation**: the answer lands on the *and*, never on the beat, the
upstroke logic of the rocksteady lineage kept as a **relationship** and translated into a clipped
electronic chord-stab — ⛔ **no organ bubble, no skank guitar, no spring echo, no walking upright bass, no
period kit.** The chassis is loud and current: a dry electronic snare cut with granular transient edits, a
wavetable bass with a slowly moving formant, a **micro-sampled crowd fragment used as percussion**, hard
comping and a bright parallel double on the lead, and a **spectral restart** at the turn. Production is
deliberately unglamorous in the low mids and immaculate on top — outdoor daylight, no reverb wash, air at
8–12 kHz, one dry clap that is allowed to be the only thing in the room for a bar. Structure:
Read (8) / Call / Verse (8) / Call / Chorus (8) / Verse (8) / Call / Chorus / The Half-Second (4) /
Call-home (8, **collapsing**), about **3:10**; the event's consequence is a **percussion-grid mutation** —
the grid changes underneath and the sub does not move.

- **Rhyme / line logic.** Loose end-rhyme, forward-leaning, deliberately **imperfect**: slant rhyme in the
  verses (*bottle / doors / backwards / hours* held together by assonance, not by ending), and the chorus
  unrhymed so the repeated clause carries all the return.
- **Poetic devices.** ⭐ **Diacope** (*"I'm saying it again… I'm saying it again"*) and ⭐ **a collapsing
  refrain** — the Call-home loses one word per line until the hook is a single syllable (*"You."*).
- **Three hook implementations.** (1) *Chest voice, full, on the offbeat stab, room answering the question
  **(When?)** that never gets answered*; (2) *the hook over the crowd-fragment percussion only*, no kit;
  (3) *the hook spoken once by the lead at half tempo* under the sung version — a stacked-time double.
- **R5 · the vocal decision she'd have to be talked into.** ⭐ **An audible laugh, kept in the take,
  MID-LINE.** Her own doctrine says *no laugh at the end of her own good line* — and I am keeping this
  because **mid-line a laugh is a breath event, not a hedge**: it does not undercut the claim, it proves
  she is still in her body while making it. ⚠️ **This is a disagreement with my own written rule and it is
  recorded, not resolved.**
- **Narrative & perspective.** First person, her own wants, unembarrassed: *"I want it anyway. I want it
  out loud."* That is **HOPE in its working form — appetite pointed at the future**, and nothing in the
  song asks permission for it. **One number sung:** *"the moon's at ninety-nine"*, at the hinge,
  **answered** by *"and I'm going to call it full because it's going to be. / That's not the same as being
  right."*
- **Lit from across (LAW 3).** A low four-o'clock sun crossing the deck sideways; the exposure is the bar
  where the grid stops and one line stands in the daylight with nothing behind it.
- **THE FACE TEST — owned here.** ⭐ Per the Wodehouse ruling *(the arm chooses; P02 is the run's primary
  owner)*, **V2 is this pair's funny song**, and it is funny by construction rather than by garnish: the
  dog moves before the people do, and that is the only confirmation anyone gets.
- **Consequence class:** **percussion-grid mutation.** **Invariant layer:** the wavetable sub.

## GUIDE 3 · V3 — **"ONE MORE OF US IN HERE"**
### facet anchor F1 + F3 · *the reading, found not made* · **127 BPM · B♭ minor**

**Storytelling techniques.** *(i) The census by object* — eight opening lines that name only what a body is
holding or where it is resting (a cup at chest height held like it is heavier than it is, a boot on a rail
the rail is taking, a phone face down on a ledge, *which is a decision*), so a whole floor is built without
a single person being invented; *(ii) the two-hander inside a mass* — the entire event is two people
looking at each other for twelve seconds and doing nothing with them. The arc runs **observation → the
suspicion that she is not the only reader → confirmation from one other person → and the deliberate refusal
to convert that into the room's move.** This is the variation where the discriminator is at its sharpest,
because the confirmation she receives is about **the reading**, never about **the arrival**: *"You saw it
too. / Then it isn't me."* The inherited body is **melodic forward bass motion** — the funk-lineage habit
of a bass that is its own song, kept as a *verb* and given to a synthetic sub that walks up two steps and
back inside one bar, doubled by a mid-range synth bass; ⛔ **no live rhythm section, no horns, no organ.**
The chassis: a low-passed electronic kick, a rim-click on the upbeat, **no hi-hat grid at all**, a
pitch-shaped vocal double at +6 cents misaligned by 15–35 ms, a short glossy plate at 0.7 s, and a room-mic
bed high-passed at 180 Hz and mixed 10–14 dB under instinct. Production leans on **absence**: half the
imaginary light rig is dead, so the record is mixed as though only one side of the room is lit, and the
space that a hi-hat would occupy is left empty for the whole song. Structure: Read (8) / Call / Verse (8) /
Call / Chorus (8) / Verse (8) / Call / Chorus / The Half-Second (4) / Call-home (8, **unison splitting on
the last word**), about **3:15**.

- **Rhyme / line logic.** Internal rhyme carries the verses (*early / decided / decided / together*),
  end-rhyme deliberately loose; the Read is unrhymed and purely nominal. **The two-note bass figure answers
  itself under every call**, so the ear learns the prediction's sound before its words.
- **Poetic devices.** ⭐ **Antimetabole** (*"She hasn't moved. She's decided. / I haven't moved. I've
  decided."*) and ⭐ **epizeuxis** (*"Not me. Not only me. / Not only me."*).
- **Three hook implementations.** (1) *Two adult female voices in strict unison — same note, no third —
  with the answer* **(I know.)** *arriving from the second voice alone*; (2) *the hook carried by the bass
  figure with no vocal at all* across the mid-song call; (3) *the hook whispered under the sung version*
  by the second voice, one beat early, uncorrected.
- **R5 · the vocal decision she'd have to be talked into.** ⭐ **The whole second half sung in strict
  UNISON with the second voice — no thirds, no harmony stack** — in a personality whose own pillar 2 makes
  stacked harmony *a required instrument*. Refusing it here is the point: **two people who have read the
  same thing are on the same note, not in harmony.** The one required moment where two voices split and
  the room hears the gap open is saved for **the final word only.** ⚠️ Recorded disagreement.
- **Narrative & perspective.** First person, hers, and she is **not on stage** — she is a body on somebody
  else's floor, which is the most occupiable position in the pair. **One number sung:** *"Twelve seconds"*,
  at the hinge, **answered** by *"and neither of us did anything with them."*
  *(Flag, declared not hidden: the hook contains the counter "one more," which is a census, not a recited
  fact. The one-fact rule is FLAG-only; the declared fact is the twelve seconds.)*
- **Lit from across (LAW 3).** One side of the rig only; the light crosses the floor sideways and lands on
  every face at the same angle. The exposure is the look.
- **Consequence class:** **offbeat timbre / cadence change** — the answer moves from the reed to the second
  voice. **Invariant layer:** the mono-locked sub, which never changes register.

## GUIDE 4 · V4 — **"ANY SECOND NOW, NOT NOW"**
### facet anchor F3 · *the figure, and the arrival withheld* · **137 BPM · G minor**

**Storytelling techniques.** *(i) Suspended syntax* — the eight-line Read is **one sentence broken across
eight lines and never completed**, ending on the word *"before"*, so the song's form is the half-second
before the sentence lands; *(ii) deixis as suspense* — almost every line points at something that has not
happened yet (*a hand going up at the far end and it hasn't gone up*), which is grammatically impossible
and exactly what the register feels like. The arc runs **inside the gap → the gap described as habitable →
the instruction to look away from her → the gap holding → one note arriving on the last beat and nothing
else.** The inherited body is **negative space**: the modal-lineage instruction to play what is not there,
kept as a relationship — **the second note of the figure is a rest for the entire song** and lands only on
the final beat. The chassis is the most aggressively current of the four: clipped electronic drums
architected to **thin rather than build**, a synthetic sub that is flat and never rises, granular vocal
dust, micro-sampling, a **spectral restart that opens the top end only**, and hard digital silence as a
structural element rather than an effect — ⛔ **no riser, no crash, no supersaw, no drop, ever.** Production
is bright, dry and slightly airless on purpose in the mid-band so the spectral opening at the last chorus
reads as a room getting taller rather than louder; the sustained spectral wash and the hard-quantised
clipped hit are the two poles and the withheld note is the single seam. Structure: Read (8, enjambed) /
Call / Verse (8) / Call / Chorus (8) / Verse (**10**) / Call / Chorus / The Half-Second (4) / Call-home (8),
about **3:20**.

- **Rhyme / line logic.** Almost no end-rhyme; the return is carried by **anaphora and negation**
  (*"Still not. Still not."*), and by a repeated *"Hold it."* that shortens each time it appears. The
  Half-Second is a four-line anaphoric growth that breaks mid-word.
- **Poetic devices.** ⭐ **Enjambment as architecture** (the Read is one unfinished sentence) and ⭐
  **antithesis in the hook** (*"Any second now. (Not now.)"* — the call and its own refusal in one line).
- **Three hook implementations.** (1) *Full chest, on the beat, with the parenthetical* **(Not now.)**
  *arriving one beat early and never corrected*; (2) *the hook over hard digital silence*, unaccompanied,
  twice; (3) *the hook with the answer note of the figure withheld* — the listener supplies it and is
  wrong.
- **R5 · the vocal decision she'd have to be talked into.** ⭐ **The final line sung on an INHALE —
  ingressive phonation, the whole last word drawn in rather than pushed out.** Her doctrine is full chest,
  no hedging, no lowering the voice to prove seriousness; this is not a lowering, it is a **physically
  reversed** delivery, and it makes the last half-second audible as a thing being *taken in* rather than
  performed. It is also R4's unusual-timbre insurance at the very end of the song. ⚠️ Recorded
  disagreement.
- **Narrative & perspective.** First person, hers, and the one moment that defeats excitement is an
  **instruction**: *"Look at their hands, not mine. / Mine are on a microphone. / Theirs are the ones about
  to decide."* **One number sung:** *"Half a second"*, at the hinge, **answered** by *"longer than a beat at
  this speed, and I've checked"* — a fact that is only **true at 137 BPM**, which is why this variation
  alone leaves Sunna's measured home band.
- **Lit from across (LAW 3).** A green exit sign over a door is the only light reaching the far end; the
  mirror ball is never switched on. The exposure is the two beats of silence.
- **Consequence class:** **spectrum / restart.** **Invariant layer:** the sub, flat and unrising, all song.

## GUIDE 5 · CROSS-VARIATION — **THE FREE CHANNEL, THE FLAT TEST, AND THE ROOM'S OWNERSHIP**
### facet anchor F5 · *applies to all four*

**Storytelling technique.** *Plain speech as the hardest surface.* Every hook in this pair is a **call and
its own answer in one line** — *"The back wall goes. (Not yet.)"* · *"You go on the next one. (When?)"* ·
*"There's one more of us in here. (I know.)"* · *"Any second now. (Not now.)"* — four to eight syllables a
half, no interval a normal throat can't reach at volume while out of breath, and every one of them
survives being said flatly, with no music, to a person's face. **The complexity is in the music and the
feeling is in the words**, and none of these songs asks anybody to decode anything before they are allowed
in: a listener who takes only the surface receives a complete song about a room that hasn't gone yet.
Instrumentally this facet is enforced by **THE HUM TEST** — in all four variations the two-note figure
exists **before the first line and after the last** and is recognisable with the vocal muted, and in none
of them is a pad allowed to stand in for it. Production carries the ownership: the parenthetical answer is
a **room** answer in every variation — low crowd unison, high-passed at 180 Hz, bussed to a short glossy
plate, mixed 10–14 dB below instinct — and in each song it arrives **one beat before the bar that should
contain it, once, and is never corrected.** ⭐ **I do not run THE REMARK TEST here.** A device a listener
notices and enjoys is not a toll; a device that makes them work before they are let in is. **The
disagreement with the centre's doctrine is recorded and not resolved.**

- **Poetic devices across the pair.** ⭐ **Anaphora** (the call, four times, entering earlier in its phrase
  each time — the song getting ahead of itself is a *formal* fact, not a described one) and ⭐ **antithesis
  in every hook** (the claim and the withholding in one breath).
- **Return, spent not stripped.** Choruses return **byte-identical** in all four; that needs no defence and
  no note. What varies is the *ending grammar*: V1 **breaks off mid-phrase**, V2 **erodes word by word**,
  V3 **splits from unison on one word**, V4 **withholds and then lands a single note**.
- **The face test.** Somewhere in these four, a face has to move. **It moves in V2**, at the dog.

## GUIDE 6 · WILDCARD — **A DAY THE GROUND DID NOTHING**
### the cross-disciplinary guide

**Storytelling techniques.** *(i) Stillness with one thing moving inside it* — the day this run was made
had an **empty significant-quake feed**, a quiet sun, a **99% moon that is not full until tomorrow** and a
5.9-foot swell on a **12.1-second** period; the pattern to take is not the events but the **shape**:
nothing is happening, and one thing inside the nothing is in motion. That is a half-second before a room
agrees, drawn at planetary scale. *(ii) The five-second exposure* — from the aurora over Skógafoss: a
**vertical falling thing lit by horizontal light that is not its own**, held open long enough to register.
Every variation obeys this as *form*: the singer is lit by something she did not make, the light runs
**across** her direction of travel, and one moment is held long enough to expose. The wildcard permission
this guide grants is **architectural, not decorative** — the cross-disciplinary import is **photographic
exposure time used as a song structure**: in V4 the entire piece is one exposure and the shutter closes on
the last beat; in V1 the exposure is a single held note over the silence where a drop is not; in V3 it is
twelve seconds of two people looking; in V2 it is one dry clap alone in daylight. Production-wise this
means the four records are permitted to be **quiet where the genre is loud**, because a long exposure needs
a still camera, and the thing moving inside the frame has to be the only thing moving.

- **The oblique strategy in force:** ⭐ **"Use `unqualified' people."** Taken literally and gladly: **the
  people whose bodies carry this song are unqualified to be in it** — a stranger with a cup, a boot on a
  rail, a dog at a barrier, a kid facing backwards — and **none of them is invented into a life.** They are
  qualified by being in the room. That is the whole credential.
- ⛔ **What this guide may NOT be used to smuggle in.** No invented worker, no role, no job, no biography.
  No second idea in the frame: **N = 1** per song, one dominant material, one intruder, one seam.
- **The seam per variation.** V1 the bar where one voice becomes the stack · V2 the line where the grid
  stops · V3 the look across the floor · V4 the withheld second note. **The subject is doing something
  across every one of them:** pointing, insisting, raising a cup, telling the room to turn round.
