## 3. Panel / Critic Deliberation Log

### Step 1 — the essence, in one sentence
> **A body stands under enormous borrowed light, produces exactly the response the light deserves, and owns no instrument that reports whether the response is hers — so she says both halves out loud and refuses to choose between them.**

### Two ways to leverage the medium
1. **DURATION AS THE ONLY ARGUMENT.** Six byte-identical returns over an emptying frame do something prose cannot: they let the listener stop resisting a state instead of being told about one. Nothing may be added to hold attention, because the holding IS the delivery.
2. **THE FUSION WINDOW AS THE SUBJECT.** Ninety milliseconds is inside the interval where the ear fuses two events into one and cannot locate the join. The song's central instrument is therefore not a timbre but a *perceptual failure the listener has in their own head while listening* — the register, running on the audience's hardware rather than described to them.

Jones).** He owns the condition attached to this pair's repetition grant and he is the only seat whose standing question — *does the song survive one voice and one instrument* — can fail this pair outright. ### Step 3 — critique, in their voices

**V1 — “A Light On The Front Of Me”**
*Q. Jones:* “The refrain stands up. `I am making the face a person makes` is a hand on the table — you can play this on one string and a voice and it holds. And the reach is the best thing in the pair: a woman asking for a little lamp on her chest that comes on when the feeling is hers, and looking down at nothing. That's a song. My complaint is `I am saying it to a wall` — that's a stand-in for a line and you know it.”
*Björk:* “It is the least softened of the four. The five-second fact is answered rather than recited, and the answer is the sharp part — the exposure proves the light was there, not that it was hers. Keep the awkwardness of `I am five seconds of holding still`. It is a strange sentence and strangeness is the point.”

**V4 — “Until My Hands Went Stiff”**
*Q. Jones:* “This one moves, which none of the others do, and the footfall earns its place because it is the only motion in a drumless record. `So I stay till my hands go stiff. That, I can check.` — that is the whole register in eleven words and it costs her something. But `So I stay. So the ground stays still.` is a cute parallel doing a job a plain line would do better.”
*Björk:* “The danger here is that acting-anyway reads as courage. It doesn't, because she never claims the act meant anything — she keeps the cold instead of the answer. Do not let anyone add a line that redeems it.”

**V3 — “Say It Before I Do”**
*Q. Jones:* “The flat unison is the right idea and it is genuinely uncomfortable — two voices agreeing with no interval sounds *wrong* in a way a listener feels before they understand. The rigged test is excellent. But the second person is warm, and warmth is a companion, and a companion turns this into a song about being alone together. That is a different, more common song.”
*Björk:* “Then make the other body an instrument rather than a friend. It only takes a verb.”

**V2 — “Twice Is Not Two Things”**
*Q. Jones:* “Structurally the purest of the four — a song about repetition built out of repetition — and the one I trust least. It is the slowest, it has the least motion, and its reach is the most abstract: `I cannot tell habit from answer` is a proposition, not an event. This is where your pretty-slow-wash risk actually lives.”
*Björk:* “It needs one thing a body does, and it needs it to be small and specific and slightly stupid, the way real self-observation is.”
