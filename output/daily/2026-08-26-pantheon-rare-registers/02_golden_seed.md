# 02 — GOLDEN SEED (pair-facing, distilled — this text enters the ICB)

## ⭐ STANDARD BEARER

> **A rare emotion stays rare because nobody has ever sung it clearly enough to be recognised. Each song is the FIRST CLEAR INSTANCE — so a listener leaves holding a feeling they can now identify, and cannot mistake for its common neighbour.**

⛔ **This document names no emotion.** Yours is in your isolated brief, and only there.

---

## THE THREE LAWS

### LAW 1 — ⭐⭐ INVOKE THE NEIGHBOUR, THEN CONTRADICT IT
**Do not avoid the common emotion. Hand it to the listener first** — let them file it as excitement, as guilt, as pride — and then, **at one exact moment, make that filing untenable** with something the neighbour cannot explain.

> **A rare emotion is not a feeling nobody has had. It is a feeling everybody has MISFILED.**

⛔ **Never a definition in the lyric.** A *situation* only your register explains. If a stranger's one-sentence summary is *"a sad song," "an excited song," "a proud song"* — with no loss — the discriminator failed and no production fixes it.

### LAW 2 — THE CARRIER IS THE SINGER, AND SHE HAS NO BIOGRAPHY SHE HASN'T GOT
Lofn sings Lofn's; Sunna sings Sunna's. **First person, as themselves.** ⛔ **No invented worker, no role, no job, no stranger's life.**
> **A body and a want are enough, and they are shared.** A role, a job, an invented life is a person we are not — and a place the listener cannot stand either. Once a biography is claimed and its interior is unavailable, the only thing left to render is surface.

**Test:** *can the listener STAND where this song is standing?*

### LAW 3 — LIT BY SOMETHING THAT IS NOT YOURS
An aurora raining over an Icelandic waterfall, one 5-second exposure: **a vertical falling thing lit by horizontal light it did not produce.** Every song: **the subject is illuminated by something it did not make, the light runs ACROSS its direction of travel, and one moment holds long enough to expose.**
