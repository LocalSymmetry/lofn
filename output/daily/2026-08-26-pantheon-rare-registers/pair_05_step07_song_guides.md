## 3. Panel / Critic Deliberation Log

**THE DELIVERY (after Nina Simone) — opening position.**
"The delivery *is* the specificity. I will not accept a feeling that lives in the arrangement. Give me one vocal decision per song that the singer would argue against, and make it the thing the song is about — not an effect placed near it."

**THE NEGATIVE SPACE (after Miles Davis) — removal.**
"Every element is preventing the listener from hearing something. In a song built on repetition, the elements you keep are the ones the ear stops noticing by pass three. So take one away at every pass. And name the obvious thing this kind of song always has that yours will not have — that is your refused element, and it is not the silence, it is the **decision**."

**THE OFFBEAT RELATION (after Tony Allen) — the pulse.**
"A feeling arrives through the body's relation to a pulse, never through a chord's name. You have no drums. Then the doubled return is your pulse relation: ninety milliseconds is a *body unit* — it is inside the window where the ear fuses two events into one and cannot say where the join is. That is not an effect. That is the subject, at the level of the nervous system."

**THE KEPT BODY (after Arthur Russell) — the chassis.**
"You are keeping one inherited motion — a single bowed string over a held drone, which is a very old body — and you are putting a current electronic band across it once. Do not let the old body become a costume. The zither is not folklore here; it is a source that can be doubled, which is why it is in the room."

**THE DURATION (after Radigue) — the grant, and its terms.**
"I hold that five of the six songs in this run are too short to deliver a state honestly, and I was overruled on five and granted one. This is the one. The listener arrives at the register because they have been in it long enough to stop resisting it — which means **nothing may be added to hold their attention.** If you get nervous at refrain four and add a layer, you have converted my grant into a normal song with a long chorus."

**THE ANTI-ATMOSPHERE (Medium HYPER-SKEPTIC / Devil's Advocate, after Q. Jones) — dissent, and it is the sharpest objection this pair received.**
"Then you have written a stall. Four constraints — the repetition grant, the byte-identical refrain, the thinning fields, the one-instrument condition — each improve this song and *together* they make its single required event very hard to place. Show me where the event is, or take the grant back."

**Resolution — what changed because of the Devil's Advocate:**

He is right that the slots are all spoken for, and the answer is that **the event does not need a new slot — it needs an existing slot to change while staying byte-identical.** Three changes were made to this step's guides because of him:

1. **THE SEAM IS REFRAIN FIVE, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING THAT EVER CHANGES.** The words are identical to the other five. What changes is that the **doubled return closes to zero** — for the length of the hook line the source and its copy arrive at the same instant, so the pair sounds *single and is not* — and one wide electronic band crosses the drone at a rate that does not match it. The join becomes invisible exactly where she is looking for it. That is the moment of surprise, it costs no new section, and it is the register rather than a decoration on it.
2. **THE FOURTH FIELD BECAME THE REACH.** Rather than adding a section for the verification attempt, the thinning was made to *land on it*: eight lines of world, six, four, and then two lines that are nothing but the small failed check. The last thing left when the room is empty is the instrument that does not work. This is what the named liability asked for — short, physical, slightly humiliating — and it needed no slot of its own.
3. **THE ONE-INSTRUMENT TEST WAS RUN, AND THE FIRST ANSWER WAS NO.** In the first draft the refrain was seven declarative sentences and it did not survive voice-plus-drone; only the last two lines did. The refrain was rebuilt as **one falling three-note cell said seven times with different words**, which is a chant and survives by construction. Recorded here rather than in a later step because the objection was raised here.
