# V1 · refined — **"The Back Wall Goes First"** · 129 BPM · F minor
### lens: THE HORN PLAYER WHO IS ALWAYS LATE

**Her note:** *"You wrote me out of the hook. The answer is the whole reason a horn exists — you play four
notes into a hole somebody else left. And I don't stay for the outro. Nobody ever heard me finish."*

### EDITS (minimal, additive)
1. ⭐ **The hook gains its answering half, inside the line:**
   `The back wall goes first.` → **`The back wall goes. (Not yet.)`**
   The call and its own refusal now occupy one breath. **A3 is now in the words as well as the figure**, and
   the room has a part. The full-length form `The back wall goes first.` survives inside the chorus as the
   chanted line, so nothing is lost.
2. ⭐ **The parenthetical answer arrives one beat before the bar that should contain it, once, and is never
   corrected.** *(This is the pair's sonic device, and it is now a vocal event rather than a chart note.)*
3. ⭐ **The Call-home breaks off mid-phrase** — `The back wall—` — and the record stops there. **She leaves
   before the outro.** The refused element is now structural at the last possible instant.
4. **Call 3 gains the interruption** — `The back wall— (Not yet.)` — so the fourth entry is *earlier than
   its own first word*.
5. `Give me the count.` kept: the horn player never gets one.

### R5 · TALKED-INTO VOCAL DECISION
⭐ **The hook, first appearance, sung flat-toned and un-belted at conversational volume, dead centre, no
vibrato — while the six-wide stack behind her goes full.** *Sunna's instinct is full chest and land it;
being asked to under-sing the title line is against everything in her.* **A person reading a room does not
shout the reading.** It is also the seam: one voice / the whole stack, and she is doing something across it
— she points.

# V2 · refined — **"You Go On The Next One"** · 124 BPM · D major
### lens: THE 5AM REGULAR

**Her note:** *"I've never asked you for anything. That's the test. If I move for it in daylight, on grass,
with a dog asleep at the barrier, it works. And the funniest thing in your song is that you keep saying it
after it didn't happen — so stop tidying that up."*

### EDITS (minimal, additive)
1. ⭐ **Hook gains its answering half:** `You go on the next one.` → **`You go on the next one. (When?)`**
   — and ⛔ **the question is never answered anywhere in the song.** The room holds an open question,
   which is precisely the register.
2. ⭐ **The laugh goes into the take, MID-LINE**, on *"I'm saying it again, I've got a whole other verse of
   this."* ⚠️ *Sunna's own written rule: no laugh at the end of her own good line.* **Mid-line it is a
   breath event, not a hedge** — it does not undercut the claim, it proves she is still in her body while
   making it. **Disagreement with my own doctrine, recorded, not resolved.**
3. ⭐ **The Call-home now ERODES** — one word lost per line until the hook is a single syllable:
   `You go on the next one. (When?) / You go on the next one. / You go on the next. / You go on. / You go. /
   You.` **A collapsing refrain**, and it is the pair's second distinct ending grammar.
4. ⭐ **The dog gets paid off.** `The dog has moved. The dog is up.` — the **only** confirmation anyone in
   the song receives, and it is not about the people. *A joke that is also the discriminator.*
5. The vocal now sits on the offbeat with the stab in Call 2 and Call 3 *(THE OFFBEAT RELATION's note at
   step 08)*.

### R5 · TALKED-INTO VOCAL DECISION
⭐ **An audible laugh, kept in the take, mid-line, uncorrected.** See edit 2.

### REFINED MUSIC PROMPT
> Modern offbeat electronic pop, 124 BPM, D major. The two-note figure rises and refuses to land in the

### lens: THE SOUND ENGINEER WITH ONE GOOD EAR

**Her note:** *"You've written a song about two people finding each other and you've mixed it in thirds.

### EDITS (minimal, subtractive — the only subtractive pass in this step)
1. ⭐⭐ **THE THIRDS COME OFF.** Chorus 2, Call 3, The Half-Second and the Call-home go to **strict unison,
   two adult female voices on the same note**, pitch-shaped double at +6 cents and misaligned 15–35 ms so
   the smear stays in. ⛔ This overrides Sunna pillar 2's *stacked harmony as a required instrument*, and
   it is the right call: **two people who have read the same thing are on the same note, not in harmony.**
2. ⭐ **The one required moment where two voices split and the room hears the gap open is saved for the
   FINAL WORD.** `There's one more of us in / here.` — the split happens on *here*, and the record ends.
   **Third distinct ending grammar: unison → split.**
3. ⭐ **Hook gains its answering half, and the answer comes from the OTHER PERSON:**
   `There's one more of us in here. (I know.)` — ⭐ **the only confirmation in the entire pair, and it
   confirms the READING, never the arrival.** This is the sharpest instance of the discriminator in the
   four.
4. `Raise it if you've got it. Don't raise it if you haven't.` kept whole — it is the hook's honest
   invitation and it does not beg.
5. Call 3 gains the interruption: `There's one more— (I know.)`

# V4 · refined — **"Any Second Now, Not Now"** · 137 BPM · G minor
### lens: THE PERCUSSIONIST WHO WON'T USE A CLICK

**Her note:** *"You've got a note you're not playing for three minutes. Good. Don't count it in when it
comes. Let it turn up. And your first sentence finishes — why? The whole song is a sentence that hasn't
finished."*

### EDITS (minimal, one of them a deletion)
1. ⭐⭐ **THE READ'S SENTENCE IS BROKEN.** `…and I've got about the length of a breath / before it happens.`
   → **`…and I've got about the length of a breath / before`** — the full stop is deleted and the eight-line
   opening never completes. **The form of the song is now the half-second before the sentence lands.**
2. ⭐ **Hook gains its answering half inside one line:** `Any second now.` → **`Any second now. (Not now.)`**
   — the claim and its own refusal in one breath, which is the two-note figure sung.
3. ⭐ **The withheld answer note lands on the FINAL BEAT, uncounted.** `Any second / now.` — the second
   note of the figure, absent for the entire record, arrives once, alone, and the record ends on it.
   **Fourth distinct ending grammar: withheld, then landed.** ⛔ *This is not a drop:* nothing else moves,
   the sub does not change, and the room's decision is still never shown.
4. **The Half-Second's fourth line breaks mid-phrase**: `And still not yet.` → `And—`
5. `Turn round.` kept — the instruction to the room to look at itself is the most Sunna line in the pair.

### R5 · TALKED-INTO VOCAL DECISION
⭐ **The final line sung on an INHALE — ingressive phonation, drawn in rather than pushed out.** Her
doctrine is full chest, no hedging, and she will not lower her voice to prove seriousness. **This is not a
lowering; it is a reversal**, and it makes the last half-second audible as something being *taken in*. It
is also R4's unusual-timbre insurance placed at the very end. ⚠️ Recorded.

### REFINED MUSIC PROMPT
> Modern suspended electronic pop, 137 BPM, G minor, built so that nothing lands. Clipped electronic drums
> thin instead of build; a synthetic sub sits flat and never rises; granular vocal dust and micro-sampled
> punctuation fill the bars a fill would take; a spectral restart opens the top end only. Adult female lead,
> full chest, close and bright, breath audible, spoken under at the turn, and the very last line drawn in on
> an inhale rather than pushed out. Hard digital silence works as a structural element rather than an
> effect: two full beats at fourteen seconds, mid-line, before a sentence has finished. The two-note figure
> keeps its answer note withheld for the whole record and plays it once, alone, on the final beat, with
> nothing counting it in and nothing else moving underneath it.

### REFINED TITLE
**Any Second Now, Not Now**
