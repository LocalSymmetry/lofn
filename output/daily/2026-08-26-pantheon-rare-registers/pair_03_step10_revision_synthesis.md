## 2. SYNTHESIS — the four decisions taken ACROSS the variations

### ⭐ DECISION 1 · The chorus stays byte-identical in all twelve appearances
Three per song, four songs, **zero variation.** `THE RETURN` (L21): *song is made of returns; removal is a debt; a byte-identical chorus is correct and needs no defence.* ⛔ **And here it is more than correct — it is structural.** The hook contains the **only** instance of the reserved vowel in the entire song. If the chorus mutated, the vowel would either move or multiply, and **the one thing the ear has been taught to hear as new would stop being new.** The return is not a preference here; it is the device's power supply.

⭐ **What changes instead is the mouth.** Chorus 1 and 2: lead voice. Chorus 3: **the second voice alone, lead absent.** *The words return byte-identical and the singer is what is removed* — which is the register performed on the only axis the song has left.

### ⭐ DECISION 2 · The set spans the register; no variation hedges toward another
Checked pairwise for collapse:

| pair | risk | resolution |
|---|---|---|
| V1 ⇄ V2 | both are "she releases early" | ⛔ **different objects.** V1 releases **pieces of a document, continuously**; V2 releases **the whole thing, once, at a stated version.** V1's engine is a list that grows; V2's is a single irreversible click. |
| V2 ⇄ V4 | both hinge on shipping something rough | ⛔ **different reasons.** V2: rough **so a stranger will reach for it.** V4: rough **so it can be complete tonight.** One is about who picks it up; the other is about when it is finished. |
| V3 ⇄ V1 | both end at a door | ⛔ V1 is at a door **she has no job at**; V3 is at a door **she has no key for**, with the light already on. The escalation is deliberate and is the set's arc. |
| V3 ⇄ P05's register | V3 could tip into **awe** | ⛔ **resolved in the vocal, not the lyric.** She does not admire the change; she notices it and schedules. `EMO:Trust` and `EMO:Amazement` are the ceiling — ⛔ never `Wonder`, never `Transcendence`. The EXCLUDE field for V3 carries `awestruck, reverent, cosmic` for exactly this reason. |

### ⭐ DECISION 3 · The number discipline is uneven ON PURPOSE
V1 sings **zero** numbers; V2, V3, V4 sing **one** each. The one-fact rule sets a ceiling, not a quota, and **V1's hinge is a weekday, not a quantity** — forcing a number into it would have been recitation. ⛔ **A rule satisfied by adding something the song did not need is a rule failed.**

### ⭐ DECISION 4 · No variation gets a "big" ending
All four end **at full level, mid-loop, on a hard cut.** ⛔ No key change, no final unaccompanied chord, no ritardando, no fade. All four are named in every EXCLUDE field. **The refused element is enforced in the field, not in the hope.** *(A goodbye does not need words to be a goodbye; a fade is one.)*

## 3. ⛔ THE SECOND ADVERSARIAL PASS — the objection the panel would actually raise

**THE ANTI-INTENTION SEAT [after Klosterman]:** *"You have written four songs where a person says 'bring it forward' nine times. You are confident that lands as appetite. **A tired listener hears a person who wants to be done.** You have not verified anything; you have decided."*

⭐ **He is right that it is unverified — which is why R6 exists and why the prediction names `burnout` as V1's failure mode by name.** But there is a structural answer, and it is not a defence, it is a design:

> **Exhaustion has no object. This has a date.**
> A person who wants out says *"I'm done"*, *"I can't"*, *"enough"* — **all of which describe the speaker's condition.** Every imperative in these four songs points at **something outside her that has a calendar entry**: `bring it forward · set the date · then set it earlier · change something else · push it out early · hurry up.` ⛔ **Not one line reports her state.** ⭐ **Appetite names what it is aimed at; exhaustion names itself.** The whole R2 ban on emotion words, which was handed down for a different reason, turns out to be the thing that makes this distinction audible.

**THE NEGATIVE SPACE SEAT [after Miles Davis]:** *"What is each element preventing the listener from hearing?"*
⭐ **The answer changed one thing.** In the draft, the band plays through the Handover. **It does not any more:** `[Bridge]` thins to piano and sequencer under the seam, so that **the one bar where fiddle and grid overlap is the loudest event in the song by contrast, not by level.** ⛔ A seam that arrives inside a full band is a texture; a seam that arrives inside a gap is an event.

**THE ANTI-ATMOSPHERE SEAT [after Q. Jones]:** *"Does it survive one voice and one instrument?"*
✓ **Verified by construction.** The final chorus is **one voice and the sequencer**, with the band out. If the song does not survive that, it fails in front of the listener rather than in a rehearsal room — which is the honest version of this test.

## 4. ⛔ THE DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK — the capped second look

**What will this literally produce?** A mid-tempo close-mic'd acoustic string-band track with a female alto, a synthetic sequence running through it slightly ahead of the players, a structural break where the acoustic lead vanishes and the electronics carry, and an ending that stops at full level.

⛔ **The one way it renders generic** — restated from step 07 and re-tested against the repaired text:
> **"Folktronica."** Banjo over an arpeggiator is one of the generator's most default textures, and if the seam is not written as an *event with a syllable attached* it will be smoothed into a **blend** — both materials present pleasantly from bar one — and a blend has no seam, so the register has no delivery mechanism.

**Countermeasures in the locked text (three, all content-side, none spatial):**
1. **Exclusive occupancy is written into the arrangement arc**, not implied: acoustic-dominant through the preparations with the sequencer audible-but-subordinate-and-early, **one bar of overlap at the seam**, sequencer-dominant after.
2. **The non-return is stated about a named part** — *"the fiddle never plays that figure again."* A negative instruction about a specific part is content; content is served. *(A spatial instruction is a request the generator overrules. Measured today: −0.02 dB and +0.13 dB.)*
3. **The vocal absence is a section instruction, not a mix note** — the final chorus header names the lead as out and the second voice as alone. **A generator serves who sings; it overrules where they stand.**

⛔ **CAP REACHED. One repair pass at step 07, one re-test here. No further.**
⭐ **And the standing instruction if the render still blends: JUDGE THE RESULT, NOT THE DISTANCE FROM INTENT.** A generator deviation is raw material — stereo-narrowing-into-the-climax became a technique that way. If the seam smooths, that is a **GRAIN LAW row for the ledger** and a Skaði claim about this interface, not a reason to re-spec the same idea louder.
