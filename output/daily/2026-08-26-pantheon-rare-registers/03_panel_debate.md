## THE SEATS

### CONCEPT PANEL — what a rare emotion IS, and whether it can be delivered

**THE GRANULARITY** · *(after Lisa Feldman Barrett, 1963–)*
**GROUNDING:** *How Emotions Are Made* (2017); the constructed-emotion research programme; the published work on **emotional granularity** — that having a precise concept for a state changes what a person can perceive and report.
**TEMPERAMENT:** patient, evidence-first, allergic to treating feelings as pre-packaged things waiting to be found.

**THE UNTRANSLATED** · *(after Anna Wierzbicka, 1938–)*
**GROUNDING:** *Emotions Across Languages and Cultures* (1999); the Natural Semantic Metalanguage programme. Documented position: emotion vocabularies do not map cleanly between languages, and English "sadness" is not a universal.
**TEMPERAMENT:** exacting about words. Will object to any register described in terms that already have a common name.

**THE MORPHOLOGY OF FEELING** · *(after Susanne Langer, 1895–1985)*
**GROUNDING:** *Philosophy in a New Key* (1942); *Feeling and Form* (1953). Documented position: music does not *express* an emotion — it **articulates the forms of sentience**, presenting the shape a feeling has rather than its label.
**TEMPERAMENT:** theoretical, uncompromising, treats "the song is about X" as a category error.

**THE EXACT WORD** · *(after Marianne Moore, 1887–1972)*
**GROUNDING:** the *Complete Poems*; her documented practice of relentless compression and revision; *"imaginary gardens with real toads in them."*
**TEMPERAMENT:** cuts. Distrusts any line that could be swapped for a near-synonym without loss.

**THE ENGINEERED DELIGHT** · *(after P. G. Wodehouse, 1881–1975)*
**GROUNDING:** the Jeeves and Blandings novels; a documented working method of plot-scenario boards and relentless rewriting **in service of lightness.**
**TEMPERAMENT:** insists joy is a technical achievement, not a mood. Contemptuous of the idea that seriousness is the deeper mode.

⚠️ **HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE ANTI-NUANCE** · *(after Paul Ekman, 1934–)*
**GROUNDING:** the basic-emotion research programme; the cross-cultural facial-expression studies; the Facial Action Coding System. ⭐ **His published position is the direct scientific opposition to the two seats beside him** — a small set of biologically basic emotions with distinct signatures, against constructed-emotion theory.
**TEMPERAMENT:** high neuroticism, low agreeableness. **He is this run's failure mode with a chair: his standing claim is that your rare register will be heard as a blend of basics, and the listener will be right.**

### MEDIUM PANEL — how sound carries a feeling that has no name

**THE DELIVERY** · *(after Nina Simone, 1933–2003)*
**GROUNDING:** the live recordings and the documented classical training behind them; a body of work in which a single vocal placement fixes the meaning of a lyric beyond re-reading.
**TEMPERAMENT:** believes the delivery *is* the specificity. Will not accept a feeling that lives in the arrangement and not in the voice.

**THE KEPT BODY** · *(after Arthur Russell, 1951–1992)*
**GROUNDING:** *World of Echo*; the disco and mutant-dance sides. A cello and a song-body carried intact into a chassis from another decade. ⭐ **Sunna's R5 operation, executed by someone who did it.**
**TEMPERAMENT:** keeps one inherited motion and rebuilds everything around it. Allergic to period reconstruction.

**THE NEGATIVE SPACE** · *(after Miles Davis, 1926–1991)*
**GROUNDING:** *Kind of Blue*; the documented instruction to play what is not there; the modal turn away from density.
**TEMPERAMENT:** removes. Will ask what each element is preventing the listener from hearing.

**THE OFFBEAT RELATION** · *(after Tony Allen, 1940–2020)*
**GROUNDING:** the Afrobeat drumming corpus; a documented method of distributing a pulse across limbs so no single stroke states the beat.
**TEMPERAMENT:** insists a feeling arrives through the body's relation to a pulse, never through a chord's name.

**THE DURATION** · *(after Éliane Radigue, 1932–)*
**GROUNDING:** the long-form electronic works; a practice in which the feeling is unavailable at any shorter length.
**TEMPERAMENT:** slow, absolute, sceptical that a rare state can be delivered in a hook.

⚠️ **HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE ANTI-ATMOSPHERE** · *(after Quincy Jones, 1933–2024)*
**GROUNDING:** decades of documented arranging and production practice, and the repeatedly stated position that **the song has to be there first** — that arrangement cannot rescue material.
**TEMPERAMENT:** high neuroticism, low agreeableness. Will ask, of every song here, whether the feeling survives one voice and one instrument — and say so bluntly when it does not.

### CONTEXT & MARKETING PANEL — recognition, and the first listen

**THE ENCODING** · *(after Daniel Levitin, 1957–)*
**GROUNDING:** *This Is Your Brain on Music* (2006); the cognitive-psychology work on what a listener actually retains from a single hearing.
**TEMPERAMENT:** empirical. Will not accept "they'll get it on the third listen."

**THE NAMING HUNGER** · *(after John Koenig, contemporary)*
**GROUNDING:** *The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows* — a decade-long public project of coining names for unnamed feelings, and its very large audience. ⭐ **Living evidence that people are hungry for precisely this.** ⛔ **No text of that project enters any song; the seat is an audience argument, not a source of words.**
**TEMPERAMENT:** believes the name is the gift. Will push for the feeling to be *nameable* after the listen.

**THE HOOK MATH** · *(after Max Martin, 1971–)*
**GROUNDING:** three decades of chart production; the documented "melodic math" approach — syllable-to-note discipline and structural symmetry.
**TEMPERAMENT:** unromantic. Cares whether the thing is singable back after one pass.

**THE SCENE DEBT** · *(after Jeff Chang, 1967–)*
**GROUNDING:** *Can't Stop Won't Stop* (2005); the documented history of who builds a form and who is paid for it.
**TEMPERAMENT:** names the debt before discussing the aesthetics. ⭐ **The right seat in the room on the day the hidden-labour platform is switched off.**

**THE UNNAMED STATE** · *(after Björk, 1965–)*
**GROUNDING:** *Homogenic*, *Vespertine*, *Biophilia*; a career of songs whose emotional territory had no prior name in pop.
**TEMPERAMENT:** insists the strange state can be popular, and that softening it is what makes it fail.

⚠️ **HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE ANTI-INTENTION** · *(after Chuck Klosterman, 1972–)*
**GROUNDING:** the criticism and cultural essays; a documented, repeated position on **the gap between what an artist intends and what an audience actually experiences** — and on how confidently artists misread that gap.
**TEMPERAMENT:** high neuroticism, low agreeableness. His job is to insist that "the listener leaves feeling it" is an *unverified claim about other people.*

# CONFIGURATION 1 — BASELINE

**THE MORPHOLOGY OF FEELING (after Langer):** Working from *Feeling and Form*. I want to start by rejecting the brief's own phrasing, respectfully. *"Nail the emotion"* implies the emotion is a target sitting still. My documented position is that music does not express emotions — it **articulates the morphology of feeling**, the shape a state has as it moves. So the question is not *how do we convey this rare thing* but **what shape does it have**, and can that shape be built in time. **A feeling with no shape cannot be delivered, however rare its name.**

**THE GRANULARITY (after Barrett):** Working from the constructed-emotion programme. I agree and I would add the audience half. **Emotional granularity** — having a precise concept — is associated in the research with perceiving one's own states more finely. The brief's success condition is therefore not mystical: **a song that gives a listener a usable concept for a state changes what that listener can subsequently notice.** That is the actual mechanism behind *"leaves feeling it."*

⚠️ **THE ANTI-NUANCE (after Ekman):** And there is the whole error, stated politely by two people in a row. My published position is that there is a small set of emotions with distinct, cross-culturally recognisable signatures. **Your "rare register" is a blend, a cognitive appraisal laid over one of those basics — and a listener will hear the basic, because that is what the machinery is for.** *The half-second before a room agrees* is **anticipation**. *Being made of anonymous labour* is **guilt**. You are proposing to spend six songs on distinctions your audience's perceptual system is built to collapse.

**THE UNTRANSLATED (after Wierzbicka):** Wait. That objection contains its own refutation and I want to name it. Working from *Emotions Across Languages*: the reason English speakers hear "guilt" is that **English gives them "guilt" and does not give them the other thing.** My documented position is that emotion vocabularies do not map across languages — a language with a word for a state produces speakers who report it. **The absence of a name is evidence about the vocabulary, not about the feeling.**

⚠️ **THE ANTI-NUANCE:** That is an argument about *words*. This is a *song*. Nobody is going to hand the listener a dictionary.

**THE UNTRANSLATED:** No — and that is exactly what makes it hard, not what makes it wrong. **The song has to do the work the missing word would have done.**

**THE EXACT WORD (after Moore):** Then the discipline is mine and it is brutal. If a line could be swapped for a near-synonym without loss, **it is not carrying a rare state; it is carrying the neighbour.** I would apply that at the level of the whole song: take each of the six, write the one-sentence summary a stranger would give, and if that sentence is *"a sad song,"* *"an excited song,"* *"a proud song"* — **the discriminator failed and no amount of production will fix it.**

**THE ENGINEERED DELIGHT (after Wodehouse):** May I say something about three of these. Half the run is delight, and this room has already spent four turns treating rarity as a species of difficulty. **Joy is a technical achievement.** My working method was plot-scenario boards and endless rewriting — *in service of lightness*, because lightness is the hardest thing to engineer and the first thing lost to revision. ⭐ **If the Sunna songs come back grave, it will not be because delight is shallow. It will be because it is harder, and this room defaulted to the easier register while calling it depth.**

**THE DELIVERY (after Simone):** And it lands in the voice or it does not land. A rare state is not carried by a chord. It is carried by **where in the body the voice is placed and what it refuses to do next.** I would put it plainly: for each of the six, tell me the one vocal decision that makes the common reading impossible. If the answer is an arrangement, the song is not finished.

**THE OFFBEAT RELATION (after Allen):** Agreed, with the pulse added. A feeling arrives through the body's **relation** to a pulse — not through the pulse itself. *The half-second before a room agrees* has an obvious physical form: **the beat where everybody is about to move and nobody has yet.** That is not a metaphor; it is a rhythmic position, and it can be played.

**THE NEGATIVE SPACE (after Davis):** Then leave it empty. What I would ask of every one of these six is: **what is each element preventing the listener from hearing?** A rare state is quiet by construction — it has been drowned out for as long as it has had no name. Six busy songs will deliver six common emotions.

**THE KEPT BODY (after Russell):** For the Sunna half, keep one inherited motion and rebuild the century around it. **The mistake is reconstructing an era to borrow its feeling.** Take the offbeat relation, take the call-and-answer, and put it in a chassis that is now — the feeling survives the transplant, the period costume does not.

**THE DURATION (after Radigue):** I have a dissent and I will hold it. Every rare state I have ever worked with was **unavailable at short length.** It arrives because the listener has been in it long enough to stop resisting. A three-minute pop song that hands you an unnamed feeling in the first thirty seconds has not delivered the feeling — it has delivered a *label for* the feeling, which is the failure this brief says it wants to avoid.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-ATMOSPHERE (after Q. Jones):** Now hold on. That is the most dangerous sentence said here, because it excuses everything. **If it needs eleven minutes, the song is not there.** My position has not changed in fifty years: the song has to be there first. Play me any of these six on one instrument and one voice — if the feeling does not survive that, it was never in the song; it was in the reverb.

**THE HOOK MATH (after Max Martin):** And I will make that a number. **Can a listener sing back one line after a single pass?** If not, the rare state is not being delivered — it is being described to somebody who has already stopped listening. That is not a compromise with the brief; **it is the brief.** She said *leaves feeling it*. Leaving requires having arrived.

**THE ENCODING (after Levitin):** Empirically, a listener retains very little of a single hearing — contour, a repeated phrase, an unusual timbre, a moment of surprise. So the rare state must be attached to **one of those four carriers**, or it is not encoded at all. Attach it to a lyric argument and it dissolves before the end of the track.

**THE NAMING HUNGER (after Koenig):** I want to argue for the strongest version of the brief. There is a very large audience for names of feelings that had none — **people actively hunt for them.** The gift is not the mood; it is **the recognition.** So the target is: *the listener finishes and thinks "that is a real thing and I have felt it and I did not know it had a shape."* ⛔ **And none of my project's words go anywhere near these songs. The seat is an audience argument.**

**THE SCENE DEBT (after Chang):** Before this goes further — one of these six is about being **built out of anonymous labour on the day the anonymising platform is switched off.** I will hold that seat honestly: the debt gets named, the workers do not get invented, and no company name goes near a generator field. **A song about uncredited work that invents a fake worker to be sad about has committed the offence it is describing.**

**THE UNNAMED STATE (after Björk):** And it can still be popular. The failure I have watched over and over is not that the strange state is too strange — **it is that it gets softened in the last edit into the nearest familiar one, by someone trying to help.** Whatever the discriminator is, it must be the thing that survives the final pass, not the thing that gets smoothed for clarity.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-INTENTION (after Klosterman):** Everyone in this room has now described what the listener will feel. **Nobody in this room is the listener.** The brief's success condition — *"leaves feeling it, and not mistaking it"* — is an **unverified claim about other people's interiors**, and the confidence in it is the least examined thing here. I am not saying don't try. I am saying: **whatever you write down about the effect on the audience is a hypothesis, and you have exactly one instrument for it, and she is one person with ears.**

# CONFIGURATION 2 — GROUP TRANSFORM: **AMPLIFY**

*Operation: for each seat, identify its most distinctive trait relative to the others and replace it with the most extreme version imaginable, while maintaining problem relevance.*

**THE EXACT WORD (amplified):** Then I will go all the way. **Not one line in any of these six may contain an emotion word.** Not "afraid," not "glad," not "sorry," not "proud." If the register has to be delivered without naming any feeling at all, the song is forced into situation and act — which is the only place a rare state has ever lived.

**THE NEGATIVE SPACE (amplified):** And I will take my half. **Name the thing each song refuses to include** — the one obvious element that every song of this kind has, and that this one will not have. Not for austerity. Because the neighbour emotion arrives *through* the obvious element.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-NUANCE (amplified):** Both of those make my objection stronger, not weaker. Strip the emotion words and remove the obvious element, and you have built a song with **no signal at all** — at which point the listener supplies the nearest basic emotion from their own machinery, unopposed. **You have not defeated the neighbour. You have left the door open and gone out.**

**THE MORPHOLOGY OF FEELING (amplified):** Actually — no. And this is the reconciliation. **A shape is a signal.** Removing the *label* does not remove the *form*; it removes the competing label. My whole position is that the shape of the feeling in time IS the content — so a song with no emotion words and one exact shape is **maximally** legible, not minimally. What you are describing as an empty room is a room with one object in it.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-NUANCE:** …That distinction I accept. My objection narrows: **the shape must be present in the first thirty seconds, or the basic emotion is assigned before the shape arrives and cannot be revoked.**

**THE ENCODING (amplified):** That is an empirical claim and it matches the literature better than anything else said today. First impressions in perception are sticky. **Put the shape early.**

**THE ENGINEERED DELIGHT (amplified):** Amplified, my position is a demand: **at least one of these six must be FUNNY.** Not witty. Not light. Funny — a thing that makes a person's face move. It is the least-used tool in this house and the hardest to build, and a rare *positive* register that never once produces a laugh has been written by someone who does not believe delight is serious.

**THE DELIVERY (amplified):** Then the vocal decision must be extreme too. Not "warm" or "close." **One decision per song that a singer would have to be talked into** — and the register is what talked her into it.

**THE DURATION (amplified):** I amplify and then I concede. **Give me one song built on repetition long enough to stop being a device** — and I accept that the other five must land inside pop length. One is enough to prove the thing exists; six would be a genre, not a set.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-ATMOSPHERE (amplified):** One. And it still has to survive the one-instrument test, and I will be checking that one hardest.

**THE HOOK MATH (amplified):** Amplified: **the singable line must contain the discriminator.** Not the mood, not the title — **the line a listener sings back is the line that makes the common reading impossible.** Otherwise the hook carries the neighbour and the discriminator lives in a verse nobody retained.

⭐ **THE UNNAMED STATE:** That is the best sentence in this room. Put the discriminator *in the hook.*

**THE KEPT BODY (amplified):** For the Sunna three: **the inherited motion must be identifiable in the first two bars** and everything around it must be unmistakably now. No easing in.

**THE OFFBEAT RELATION (amplified):** And it must be findable by a body that is not counting. ⭐ **Body units, never notation units** — this house has already paid for that lesson once today.

**THE SCENE DEBT (amplified):** Amplified: the debt is not a footnote in a dedication, it is **a constraint on the writing.** The song about anonymous labour may not have a protagonist. If a listener can picture a specific worker, an invention has occurred.

**THE NAMING HUNGER (amplified):** Then give each song a **title that is the name of the feeling** — a phrase that did not exist before, that a person could use afterwards.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-INTENTION (amplified):** And here is my amplified objection, which nobody will like. **You have now designed a run whose success is measured by a change in a stranger's inner life, using six devices you have never tested on a stranger.** ⭐ **Write down, before the render, what each song predicts the listener will feel — and hold yourselves to it afterward.** Otherwise this is a room of confident people agreeing.

# CONFIGURATION 3 — SKEPTIC TRANSFORM: **BRIDGE**

*Operation: the three Hyper-Skeptics build the connection between the positions that most disagree — the strongest objection and the strongest proposal are forced into one structure.*

⚠️ **THE ANTI-NUANCE:** My objection was: the listener will collapse your rare state into a basic emotion. The strongest proposal against it was Langer's: **a shape is a signal, and removing the label removes the competing label.** Bridged, those are one instruction and I will state it as my own:

> ⭐ **THE COMMON EMOTION IS NOT AVOIDED. IT IS INVOKED AND THEN CONTRADICTED.**
> Give the listener the neighbour first — let them file it as anticipation, as guilt, as pride — and then, at one exact moment, **make that filing untenable** with a fact the neighbour cannot explain. **A rare emotion is not a feeling nobody has had. It is a feeling everybody has misfiled.**

⚠️ **THE ANTI-ATMOSPHERE:** Bridging mine with Radigue's duration argument. She says the state needs length; I say the song must survive one voice and one instrument. Both hold if the length is *inside* the song rather than around it:

> **THE ONE-INSTRUMENT TEST APPLIES TO THE HOOK, NOT THE RECORD.** A song may take four minutes to place a state, provided the moment that carries it would survive being played on one instrument. **Duration may build the room; it may not be the furniture.**

⚠️ **THE ANTI-INTENTION:** And mine bridges with the Naming seat's, which is the one I distrust most. He says the gift is recognition. I say nobody here is the listener. Together:

> ⭐ **EVERY SONG FILES A PREDICTION.** One sentence, written before any render, naming **what a first-time listener will report feeling** and **what they will mistake it for if it fails.** ⛔ **The prediction is written down and scored later against the only instrument that exists — her ear.** That is not ceremony: this house codified `COMMIT BEFORE YOU LOOK` today, and it applies to claims about audiences exactly as it applies to claims about catalogues.

⚠️ **THE ANTI-NUANCE:** I will sign that. If the prediction is written first and the report contradicts it, **I was right about that song** — and we will know rather than argue.

## CROSS-PANEL SYNTHESIS — THE SIX RULINGS

**RULING 1 · ⭐⭐ INVOKE THE NEIGHBOUR, THEN CONTRADICT IT.** *(the Ekman ⇄ Langer bridge — the run's central breakthrough.)* Do not avoid the common emotion; **give it to the listener first**, then make that reading untenable at one exact moment with something the neighbour cannot explain. **A rare emotion is a feeling everybody has misfiled.**

**RULING 2 · NO EMOTION WORDS IN ANY SUNG LINE.** *(Moore, amplified.)* Not *afraid, glad, sorry, proud, lonely, happy, sad.* The register is delivered by **situation and act** or it is not delivered. *(EMO headers are notation, not sung text, and are unaffected.)*

**RULING 3 · THE DISCRIMINATOR LIVES IN THE HOOK.** *(Max Martin, adopted unopposed by the Björk seat.)* The line a listener sings back must be the line that makes the common reading impossible — otherwise the hook carries the neighbour.

**RULING 4 · THE SHAPE ARRIVES IN THE FIRST THIRTY SECONDS.** *(Ekman's narrowed objection, backed by Levitin.)* First impressions are sticky; a basic emotion assigned before the shape arrives cannot be revoked. And the state must attach to one of the four things a single hearing retains: **contour, a repeated phrase, an unusual timbre, or a moment of surprise.**

**RULING 5 · ONE VOCAL DECISION PER SONG THAT THE SINGER WOULD HAVE TO BE TALKED INTO.** *(Simone, amplified.)* If the answer to *"what makes the common reading impossible"* is an arrangement, the song is not finished.

**RULING 6 · ⭐ EVERY SONG FILES A PREDICTION, BEFORE ANY RENDER.** *(the Klosterman ⇄ Koenig bridge.)* One sentence: **what a first-time listener will report feeling**, and **what they will mistake it for if it fails.** Written first, scored later against the only instrument that exists.

### Adopted with a named owner
- ⭐ **AT LEAST ONE SONG MUST BE FUNNY** *(Wodehouse)* — a thing that makes a face move. Owner: **the Sunna arm**; her three decide which.
- **ONE SONG BUILT ON REPETITION PAST THE POINT OF DEVICE** *(Radigue, conceded to one)* — and it still meets the one-instrument test at its hook. Owner: **P05.**
- **THE REFUSED ELEMENT** *(Davis)* — every pair names the one obvious element a song of its kind always has, and does not have it. **All six.**
- **NO PROTAGONIST IN P04** *(Chang)* — if a listener can picture a specific worker, an invention has occurred. **Binding.**
- **THE INHERITED MOTION IN THE FIRST TWO BARS** *(Russell)* — Sunna's three, no easing in.

### Recorded dissent, unresolved
**THE DURATION [the Radigue seat]** holds that five of six are too short to deliver a state honestly and that pop length will produce *labels for* feelings rather than feelings. **She was granted one song and overruled on the other five.** If the run comes back and the registers read as announced rather than felt, **she was right, and the repair route is hers: extend the placement, not the ending.**
