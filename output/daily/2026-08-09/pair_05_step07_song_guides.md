## 0. ⭐ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT ANSWERS (Golden Move #4 — answered in writing before a line exists)

### WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

It is not standing. **It is seated, and it has been seated for about four minutes longer than it needs to be.**

Residents' parking behind a low-rise block, a little before eleven at night. The car is hers and it is old
enough that the metal ticks while it cools. The engine is off; the heater's last warmth is going out of the
vents in a way she can feel arriving at her knees and then not arriving. One lamp on a mast, two bays over,
throws a hard cone she is sitting inside the edge of. The windscreen is fogging from the inside, from her.
Keys in the right hand, the fob's edge printing a line across the palm. Coir under the nails and a green
smell on the cuffs that she stopped noticing hours ago and can smell now that she is still. The forearms are
the loudest part of her body: eleven hours of the same small motion, and the hum in them has outlived the
motion. **Her hands are still doing the shift.** That is the physical centre of every one of these four songs.

### WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

Four true answers, and only the fourth is the song:

1. **The cold and the stillness.** Sitting down hard after a long shift is when a body finds out what it did.
   The wrists are a real injury in progress and she knows the exact motion that caused them.
2. **The open ground.** A lit car park at night, alone, and forty metres of it between the car and the door,
   all of which she can see. This is ordinary alertness, not a plot, and it must never become one. Nothing
   happens. It is simply a thing a person keeps a small amount of attention on.
3. **The lamp's timer.** The light she is sitting in belongs to a circuit that is not hers.
4. ⭐ **The actual wound, which is not a threat at all: she has to go in, and she is not going to find out how
   any of it lands.** Not the trays she handed over. Not the sheet. Not the thing inside. **Ellipsism is the
   injury.** AWE is terror-adjacent here for a precise reason: the sublime object — the enforced second,
   the fact that something enormous is *simultaneous with her doing nothing* — arrives while she is at her
   least defended: tired, cold, still, out of delays.

## 2. GUIDE 1 — **THE PAIR SPINE** *(binding on all four variations; where they must be identical)*

**Storytelling techniques (2).**
(a) **The inventory that is secretly a farewell.** Borrowed from the shipping-manifest tradition in prose — a
list of procedures, read straight, where the emotion is entirely in the fact that the list is being *handed
to somebody else.* Nothing in the list is described as meaningful. The meaning is that it is a handover.
(b) **Enforced simultaneity as dramatic irony with the sign flipped.** Classical dramatic irony gives the
audience knowledge the character lacks. Here the *character* holds the mechanism — she is the one who notices
that a second cannot be sent — and what she lacks is the **outcome.** She is smarter than the listener about
the present and permanently blind to the future. That asymmetry is D5 made structural instead of policed.

**Mood & emotional arc — THE SWITCHBOARD, three movements and a residue.**
Open in **reluctant awe** — not wonder, *reluctant* awe: she is too tired to be having this feeling and is
having it. Ascend into **procedural elation** (the competence is real and it is a pleasure). **Rupture at the
count.** Land in **apologetic rage** pointed at a threshold, never at a person: every interval in her day was
set by somebody who is not in this car park. Then the third movement is **not** a reconciliation — it is a
synthesis **that carries the damage forward**: the awe and the charge held in one tired body that still has to
go in. Residue: **glad exhaustion with a splinter in it.**

**Instrumentation & texture.**
Bed-squeak kick cell at 140, jersey-club triplet displacement into the second half of every bar. Bitcrushed
sigilkore/HexD hats and shakers, downsampled until the transient is a click of grit rather than a hiss. A
squeaked, pitched-up hyperpop lead — **and, crucially, a second lead register that is dry, low and almost
spoken**, the same woman with the processing off. Chopped vocal used as percussion, sourced from **her own
syllables**, not from a pack. From F10 as texture, not as genre: *whirring sequencers* (the glasshouse fans
that follow her out to the car and become the sigilkore hiss) and *a joyful symphony of claps and shakers*
(the bed-squeak cell's shoulder). One sub, mono, doing the work of the bassline. Cooling-metal tick and
seat-creak as real foley on the downbeat, at least once.
**Add Vocals** at the chorus for the byte-identical stack and the gang answer. **Add Instrumentals** only to
extend the post-count section — never to grow it.

**Production & effects.**
Room: the inside of a small car with the engine off, mic'd close enough that the seat is audible.
⭐ **Quantum Bit-Depth Swell placed exactly once**, at the loss of certainty, hi-fi → 2-bit — and nowhere else
in the song, on pain of the facet scoring zero. **Machine below, body above**: the kick/sub grid rigidly
quantised, the lead and the counting voice humanly late. Audible on a phone speaker — nothing load-bearing in
the stereo field (measured collapsing to near-mono, n=8), nothing load-bearing in a specified silence
(measured smoothed 2 of 2).

**Structure & dynamics — the invariant skeleton.**
- **Enter mid-phrase.** Flair #12. The first sound is a phrase already in progress; the tape was never off.
- **Verse = 8 lines, ZERO rhyme.** Not a slant rhyme, not a stray internal chime at a line-end. Zero.
- **Chorus = 4 lines, byte-identical every time.** ⭐ It requires no defence and **no note is to be written
  about it anywhere in this pair's artifacts.**
- **Build by ADDITION until the count. Build by LEVEL after it.** After the hinge nothing new is added; the
  room changes around a fixed level and the last chorus is **harder, never bigger.**
- **THE COUNT** at the junction the arrangement already wants (the bar before the final chorus block):
  everything is **replaced** by one dry human counting voice, slightly behind the grid, two bars, no click, no
  reverb, no silence. The return is in a **different key or a re-divided metre**, with the bass line the
  **same shape** — the ear knows before the listener does.
- **THE FALSE OUTRO.** The song ends on **one dry sounding object**, short, then the bed returns as an
  **EVENT.** ⛔ **Nothing after the return may comment on the return.** The critic pass takes the second half
  of the false outro if it explains itself.

**Hook techniques (3).**
1. **The four-line slab.** Chorus lines 1 and 4 are the same line, so the ear gets a frame it can close;
   lines 2 and 3 carry the mechanism and the body. Shoutable by strangers who did not do the shift.
2. **The relic as the hook's percussion.** The single dead-tongue syllable is chopped onto the bed-squeak's
   off-beats so the room can shout a *sound* even before it can shout the words — a chant with no semantic
   entry requirement, which is how a club hook actually spreads.
3. **The count as the second hook.** Numbers spoken flat by a tired person are the most shoutable material in
   the language. Place the count where a drop would be and let the crowd own it.

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.**
First person, present tense, **overheard and never addressed** — no *you*, no *we*, no comfort delivered
outward. The subject appears **plainly** at least once in every variation: *she is sitting in a car park not
going in, and something is happening at the same time as that.* One dead-tongue syllable (Old Norse
`hví`) as **pure sound**, never translated, never glossed — the Zeuhl door from F09 opened one inch and not
walked through.
⛔ **D7 is a hard wall on this pair:** nothing in this car park arrives to help her, least of all a machine.
⛔ **Zero numerals are allocated.** No numeric fact is sung. *(The count is a metrical device, not a fact; every
counted numeral is written in words per L33.)*
⛔ **No sky.** P02 and P06 hold both permitted skies. What is overhead here is **a lamp on a mast.**

## 3. GUIDE 2 — **V1 · THE SHIFT SHE JUST FINISHED**
*concrete, procedural, unglamorous, and she is good at it*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **Competence as characterisation** — we learn who she is only from the order
in which she does things and from which step she does not have to think about. (b) **The document as the
narrator's rival** — the handover sheet says the same facts with none of the feeling, and the song keeps
losing the argument to it.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin in **alert boredom** that is genuinely pleasant; rise into **procedural
elation** — the specific joy of a physical task done at speed by a body that has stopped consulting itself;
switch at the count into **apologetic rage** at the interval, not the employer, not a person; land on
**unwitnessed resolution**, which is the pair's home emotion and is a *flat* feeling, not a sad one.

**Instrumentation & texture.** The fan wash is highest here — bring the glasshouse into the car with her.
Mist-line hiss as the shaker layer. Tray-edge knock as a real percussive hit on beat four. Lead sits squeaked
and bright over the verses because the *work* was bright; the dry low register takes the last verse.

**Production & effects.** Bit-depth collapse placed on the moment the sheet leaves her hand. Chopped vocal
percussion built from the consonant heads of her own procedural nouns, so the beat is literally made of the job.

**Structure & dynamics.** Enter mid-list. Verse 1 and Verse 2 are the task list executed at speed; Verse 3 is
the same list with one item that is not a task. Count → key **up a minor third**, bass line the same shape.
False outro on the sheet's final entry, hard cut, then the chorus returns once and stops.

**Hook techniques.** (1) Make the chorus's repeated frame-line the one sentence that is true both of the trays
and of her — but do **not** say so. (2) Put the relic on the off-beat under the frame line. (3) Let the gang
answer be a single procedural noun shouted back, which is funnier and harder than a sentiment.

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.** Fill the verses with **verbs she does not explain**: vent, mist, prick
out, plug, log, sign. She is good at this and the song's affection for her is entirely in how little it
explains. ⛔ Nothing about growth. Nothing about *tending*. The plants are stock, the sheet is paperwork, and
she will not be there.

## 4. GUIDE 3 — **V2 · THE THING WAITING INSIDE**
*never named, only measured by how long she stays out here*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **Measurement in place of naming** — the unnamed object is given only a
duration and a set of physical proxies: how far the fog has climbed the glass, how cold the footwell is, how
many times the lamp has cycled. (b) **The Oblique Strategy used against itself** — today's card reads *remove
specifics and convert to ambiguities*, adopted as the **anti-instruction** everywhere except here: ⭐ **this
variation is the run's ONE legitimate use**, because vagueness is a **behaviour she is performing on herself**.
She is the one refusing the specific. The song is not vague; **she is.**

**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin in **stubborn presence** — she is not hiding, she is *staying*. Rise into
**hungry attention** as she measures everything in reach rather than the one thing. Switch at the count into
**quiet mutiny** aimed at the fact that even this delay is an interval somebody could name. Land in
**suspicious comfort**: the car is warm-ish, and it is not a solution.

**Instrumentation & texture.** Sparsest of the four before the count and the heaviest after it. The sub is the
main event; the hats stay bitcrushed and thin. The wide sustained relic tone is loudest here — it is the ABOVE
register and it is entirely uninterested in her measuring.

**Production & effects.** Fog and breath as texture: audible inhale left in, per the Grit Shaper. Bit-depth
collapse on the moment a proxy runs out and there is nothing left to measure with.

**Structure & dynamics.** Enter mid-measurement. Count → **metre re-divided**, 4/4 to 12/8 at the same pulse:
nothing gets faster, the grid simply stops agreeing with itself. False outro on the door seal ticking, then the
bed returns hard.

**Hook techniques.** (1) The chorus frame-line should be a **unit of measurement**, not a feeling. (2) Layer
the gang answer as a single held vowel rather than a word — the room can hold a vowel. (3) Let the relic sit
*on* the downbeat here, not off it, so this variation's hook is physically different in the mouth from V1's.

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.** ⛔ **The thing inside is never named — not once, not obliquely, not in
the title, not in the theme tag.** It has no gender, no relation, no diagnosis, no argument. If a drafter can
tell what it is, the variation has failed. The only thing the song asserts about it is that it is **there**,
and that she is **out here.**

## 5. GUIDE 4 — **V3 · THE SAME SECOND**
*somewhere the sky is going out; she doesn't know, and it is happening anyway*

⭐ **This is the variation that STATES the run's thesis. It is the pair's highest-risk song and its reason to exist.**

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The unwitnessed simultaneity** — the wide ABOVE layer carries an event she
never looks at; the song never tells her about it and never tells the listener what it is in so many words.
(b) ⭐ **The unknowing eclipse** — the two-layer stack made literal and small: **she leans forward, her head
crosses the lamp, and her own shadow lands on her own hands.** She performs the operation the sky is
performing elsewhere, without knowing there is a sky doing it. *A body between you and the light is a body
between you and the light at any scale.* This is the crossing, it is **in the lyric**, and it happens **once**.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin in **reluctant awe**. Rise to **vertiginous scale** — felt in the wrists, not
in the mind. Switch at the count into the pair's sharpest **apologetic rage**: the shift was set, the break was
set, the hook at fifteen seconds was set by an accounting rule nobody in this car park voted on — **and the
second was not set by anybody.** Land in **complicit thrill**: she is furious that the shape works and she is
inside it and both are true.

**Instrumentation & texture.** The ABOVE register is a wide, slow, sustained relic tone in a tempo of its own,
completely indifferent to the 140 underneath. BELOW is the driest material in the pair: seat creak, key-fob
plastic, the breath. The two layers must never be mixed to *blend* — they must be mixed to **coexist and
ignore each other**, because that is the photograph.

**Production & effects.** Bit-depth collapse at the crossing — the instant the shadow lands, certainty goes.
The relic layer is the ONE element that does not collapse: it stays hi-fi and wide through the 2-bit moment,
which is what makes the moment frightening rather than decorative.

**Structure & dynamics.** Enter mid-thought. Count → key **down a semitone**, bass line the same shape: the
sag, not the lift, because the ear reads a semitone drop as the floor moving. False outro on the relic alone,
dry and unreverbed as though the file ended; then the bed returns as an **event** and the chorus goes once.

**Hook techniques.** (1) ⭐ **The mechanism belongs in the chorus, phrased as something her hands can and
cannot do** — a thing she can send, set against the one thing she cannot. Flat, present tense, first person,
unaddressed. Never a lesson, never future tense, never *you*. (2) Chant the relic under it so the hook has a
non-semantic entrance. (3) Let the last chorus be **spoken over the top of itself** — the dry register saying
the same four lines under the squeaked stack, one beat late.

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.** ⛔ **She does not learn anything.** She notices a mechanism, is furious
and delighted by it in the same breath, and goes on sitting there. ⛔ **No sky is described.** The going-out is
elsewhere, unwatched, and it is never named as an eclipse. ⛔ No *you*. ⛔ No future tense anywhere near it.

## 6. GUIDE 5 — **V4 · SHE GOES IN**
*the false outro is her hand on the door, and then it isn't*

⚠️ **Step 05's live warning applies to this guide specifically:** V4 was P05's runner-up concept, and *"if V4
reads as a different song rather than a different angle, that is the repair."* **Guard:** same woman, same
night, same car park, same lamp, same relic, same chorus architecture. **Only the camera moves.** V4 is V1–V3's
last ninety seconds given a whole song.

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The delayed exit as the entire plot** — every line is a small preparation,
and preparations are the most human material there is. (b) **The refused ending** — the song is built to
finish and is structurally incapable of it, which is the same fact as Flair #12: the tape was never off, so
there is nothing for an ending to be made of.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin in **glad exhaustion**. Rise into **kinetic joy** — the specific pleasure of
finally moving, which is real and must be written as real. Switch at the count into **obstinate presence**:
she is not being brave and she is not being cowardly, she is **taking the interval that is hers.** Land in
**forgiving disappointment** that includes the damage: she goes in, and she still does not know.

**Instrumentation & texture.** The most propulsive of the four — this is the one that moves. Bed-squeak
foregrounded, chopped vocal at its densest, the door foley (latch, seal, keypad) integrated as **rhythm**
rather than as sound effect.

**Production & effects.** Bit-depth collapse on the latch. The false outro's dry object is the door itself;
the return is the bed slamming back with **no transition** — no riser, no reverse cymbal, nothing that
announces it. It must feel like the file was longer than the player said.

**Structure & dynamics.** Enter mid-motion. Count → **metre trips**, a bar loses a beat (4/4 to 7/8 for two
bars, then back), which is the only variation where the *loop* is what changed rather than the key. False
outro at the door; return puts her **back in the seat with the key still in her hand**, unexplained.
⛔ **No line after the return may explain the return.**

**Hook techniques.** (1) Build the chorus around a **physical verb she keeps not completing**, so the hook is
an action rather than a statement. (2) Put the gang answer on the door foley's downbeat so the room shouts
*with the latch*. (3) Reprise the count as the outro tag — the same numbers, now under the bed rather than
instead of it, which is the level-not-ladder facet made audible.

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.** She **does** go in. That is not a resolution and must not be written as
one; it is a thing that happens to a body that is cold. ⛔ Nothing waits for her that the song identifies.
⛔ Nothing is better. ⛔ Nothing is worse. The damage comes with her.

## 7. GUIDE 6 — **WILDCARD · THE HANDOVER, PLAYED AS THE ROOM'S SONG** *(direction, not a fifth variation)*

If any of V1–V4 collapses into a mood piece under drafting, the repair is **not** a new concept — it is to
re-cut that variation as **the room's song rather than hers**: make the crowd's four-line slab the *first*
thing in the song (Hook At Zero is P03's, so this is not that — the difference is that here the crowd arrives
before the person, and the person is then discovered *inside* the chant). The relic leads, the gang answer
leads, and her dry register does not appear until the second verse. It costs the mid-phrase entrance and it
buys back propulsion. **Use only as a repair, and record it if used.**
