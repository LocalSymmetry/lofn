## 1 · ⭐ THE STRUCTURAL FIX — a `[Turn]` section, in all four, at the same place

The raw pass had a real defect: **V2 had a `[Break]` section the other three lacked**, which made V2 read as a
different *song* rather than a different *angle* — a §8 REJECT criterion if it survived — and **V1's and V3's
turns existed only in the direction, not in the text.** One fix closes both.

**All four now carry a two-line `[Turn]`, placed identically:**

`… Chorus 2 → **[Turn]** → [HINGE — register handoff] → Final Chorus → Scroll 3 → Outro`

⭐ **Placement matters and was checked:** the Turn goes *before* the hinge, so the hinge still lands in **the bar
before the final chorus** — the one junction a pop arrangement always permits a discontinuity, and the only
place a substitution survives. Moving the Turn after the hinge would have pushed the crossing off its junction.

| | the Turn, verbatim | why it is a turn and not a summary |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | *"The lines go past faster than I can read one. / So I stop reading and hold the frame."* + `*scroll blur*` | She was reading them for two-thirds of the song. **She stops.** The chat does not change; her relation to it does — and she does not say what that means. |
| **V2** | *"My arm goes down on its own. / The stream keeps going from the gravel."* + `*phone hits gravel*` | The body fails, and **nothing else does.** The song's whole premise — that she is holding this up — is withdrawn, and the machine is indifferent. |
| **V3** | *"The person I am watching is watching a screen too. / I put my chin back on my knees."* + `*duvet shifts*` | The frame recedes one level. She is not watching an event; she is watching **somebody watching**, and she is one of the five hundred doing that. The second line refuses to react. |
| **V4** | *"The corner goes red and the light is still coming. / I stop filming. I keep it up anyway."* + `*low battery chime*` | Two clocks, and **the wrong one is running out.** ⛔ The comparison is staged, never stated. And she keeps the arm up for a phone that has stopped recording, which is the most honest thing in the pair. |

**Turn test applied to each:** *does the song, past the midpoint, contradict or complicate its opening stance?*
V1 opened *"I am holding this up for the whole thing"* and the Turn is her stopping doing the other thing she was
doing. V2 opened with a shoulder and the Turn is the shoulder winning. V3 opened *"I am watching somebody else
hold their arm up"* and the Turn discovers that somebody else is doing exactly what she is. V4 opened *"There is
enough in it"* and the Turn is the enough running out while the sky has not started. ✅ **Four turns, four
different mechanisms, all past the midpoint, none of them a moral.**

## 2 · THE NINE CHANGES — every edit, with the voice that forced it

### CHANGE 3 — two of four approach openers rewritten *(grammatical monoculture)*

| | was | is |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | *"I am holding this up for the whole thing."* | **unchanged** — it is the pair's thesis line and it earns the slot |
| V2 | *"I am putting my arm up now and leaving it there."* | ⭐ **"The shoulder goes first, not the hand." / "Nobody says that."** |

**THE FIRESTARTER (after Powers):** *"Say the thing flatly, once, and then spend the song not taking it back —

### CHANGE 4 — ⛔ **D5 repair.** V4's opener was future-tense in feel
*"I am going to make this last until the end of it"* is a narrator with an outcome in view. Replaced (Change 3)
with a present-tense assertion **that immediately undercuts itself** — *"There is enough in it. There is
probably enough in it."* Same information, no future, and the doubt is now audible in the second line rather
than explained later. **This is the comic dread the V4 angle was assigned and the raw pass had not found it.**

### CHANGE 5 — attribution decay repaired in V1 Scroll 2
V1 couplet 8 had lost its frame while its neighbours kept theirs — a hole, not a decay.
**was:** *"the flight gets in tonight / she writes from the taxi, always"*
**is:** ⭐ *"a stranger says the flight gets in tonight / says she writes from the taxi, always"*
The frames now thin **monotonically**: full in Scroll 1, light in Scroll 2, **absent in Scroll 3**, which is the
whole point — the acceleration is the chat outrunning the ear's ability to see who is talking.

### CHANGE 6 — ⛔ **numeral sweep.** Three stray numerals removed
The one-fact rule allocates this pair exactly **"five hundred."** Three casual numerals had crept into chat
lines where a scanner would rightly flag them:

| where | was | is | new rhyme pair |
|---|---|---|---|
| V3 couplet 6 | *"says a **third** of the street came out"* | ⭐ *"says the **word** went round the street"* | birds / word |
| V4 couplet 5 | *"says an **hour** ago, the whole street"* | ⭐ *"says the **shower** went cold mid-wash"* | power / shower |
| V4 couplet 8 | *"types it **third** time, gets it"* | ⭐ *"types it again, **hears** nothing back"* | word / hears |

⭐ All three replacements are **better**, not merely legal: *the word went round the street* is a warmer and more
human observation than a fraction; *the shower went cold mid-wash* is the domestic specific the Angst
Cartographer asked for; *hears nothing back* is the unanswered check appearing in somebody else's life.

### CHANGE 7 — V1 Scroll 3 couplet 13 rewritten *(voice collision)*
**was:** *"so cold out, hands gone / I hold the rail, sorry"* — in an unattributed scroll this read as **her**,
and she had already said *"The rail is right there. I don't lean."*
**is:** *"it's cold out, hands gone / I'll hold on a bit longer"* — unmistakably a stranger, and it keeps the
rebound (cold / hold) intact.

### CHANGE 8 — line-length trim across all four approaches *(`mean_words_per_line ≤ 7.5`)*
Six approach lines ran nine to eleven words. All trimmed or split. The two that were **not** trimmed are V1's
opener and V3's opener, because they are the two lines a stranger needs in order to retell the song after one
listen (the legibility rule), and one long line does not move a mean across eighty-plus.

### CHANGE 9 — rebound-position audit across all sixty-four couplets
**THE GRIT SHAPER (after Perry):** *"You have a device that lives at a fixed place in the bar and you let it
wander. A device that wanders is a coincidence."*
**Rule applied:** the rhyming syllable sits between **45% and 72%** of the line's word count — beat 3 of a 4/4
bar with the tolerance a sung line actually has at 174 BPM. Lines that placed it at word 1–2 of a six-word line
were re-ordered so the rhyme falls after the caesura. **No line-end rhyme was introduced to fix a position** —
that would pay the debt with the exact coin the pair is forbidden.

## 3 · ⛔ THE D6 COHORT GATE — line-by-line sweep at step 09

Every sung line in all four variations was tested by deleting *we / us / our / everyone / everybody / young
people / this generation / kids / people* and asking whether the line still stands.

**Lines killed at step 09: 2.** Both were mine, both were in draft material that did not reach the step-08 file,
and both are recorded because the gate is only meaningful if it fires:

1. ⛔ **V3 couplet 16, drafted as** *"the host is crying / most of us are"* — delete *us* and the line is gone.
   It was demography with a melody, and it was also the warm ending the Hardcore Elder is standing watch

2. ⛔ **V4 couplet 16, drafted as** *"somebody says good night / somebody says should we stay"* — delete *we* and
   it collapses. **Replaced** with *"somebody says night, then leaves / somebody says right, then stays."*

**Surviving inspections, recorded rather than waved through:**
- V3 couplet 10, *"asks if they paid for the drone"* — **kept.** *They* is referential (whoever made the stream),
  not a cohort claim; the deletion test does not apply to a definite third party.
- V2 couplet 10, *"says the park is full anyway"* — **kept.** A park is a place, not a demographic.
- V3 couplet 6, *"says the word went round the street"* — **kept.** A street is a place.
- Nowhere in the four songs does the word *we*, *us*, *our*, *everyone*, *everybody*, *kids*, *generation* or
  *young* appear. The mass is always rendered as **an enumerable object** — the room, the box, the scroll, five
  hundred names — because a room is a thing and a generation is not.

## 4 · THE ARTIST PASS PROPER — flair injected, no new scene content

### V1 · **Five Hundred in the Room**
**THE HALF-PIPE WITNESS (after Hawk)** and **THE DIGITAL REBELLION SOCIOLOGIST (after boyd)** hold this one.
The sixteen were re-read one at a time against *what does this one want, and are they going to get it?* All
sixteen have a want. **None gets one, and not one of them is written as an idiot.** The kid who asks how long
it lasts and then asks whether his song got played is the pair's whole thesis in two lines: **the second
question is the real one and nobody answers either.** No line was made cleverer; two were made plainer.

### V2 · **The Arm at Full Extension**
**THE MACHINE-GUN DRUMMER (after Barker)** and **THE GRIT SHAPER (after Perry)**. The arrangement note added
here and carried to step 10: **the break plays a fill one bar early, once, immediately before the Turn** — the
grid breaking in the same bar the body does. And the vocal take specified for the Turn is **the one where she
runs out of air a syllable early, not punched in.** Couplet 16 — *"it's fine now, that's better / the line is
dead straight"* — is left exactly as drafted. It is the cruellest line in the pair: the picture improves the
moment the phone is lying on gravel pointing at nothing, and **she never replies.**

### V3 · **Somebody Else's Phone**
**THE ANGST CARTOGRAPHER (after Rodrigo)** and **THE MINIMALIST ATMOSPHERIST (after O'Connell)**. Couplet 5 —
her own line in the chat — was tested for whether it should be marked, and it is **not** marked: no tag, no
change of voice, no arrangement lift, and the fifteen lines around it treat it identically. ⭐ **Nobody in the
song notices that one of the marginal voices is the singer.** That is the point and it is never pointed at.
The hinge check is the sharpest of the four — *"I scroll back for my line"* — and the outcome is never stated,
never lamented, never winked at.

### V4 · **Low Power Mode**
**THE NEON CHEERLEADER (after Tillie)** and **THE RELENTLESS OPTIMIST (after Victoria)** insisted this one keep
its teeth: comic dread is a **bright** emotion, and V4 is the funniest song in the pair without a single joke
in it. **Defence of the one deliberate rule-break, as required:** couplet 16 carries **two voices in one unit**,
the only place in sixty-four couplets where that happens. It is kept. The couplet architecture is *one person
per unit* because the room is being enumerated by the person hosting it — and by couplet 16 **her screen has
been black for eight lines.** The unit stops being hers to allocate. One leaves, one stays, and neither mentions
her. If a reviewer wants it reverted, the revert is *"somebody says night, then stays / types right, then types
nothing more"* and it is measurably weaker.

**Self-critique.** The change I am least sure of is **Change 3's rewrite of V2's opener.** *"The shoulder goes
first, not the hand. / Nobody says that."* is a true sentence about her own body discovered in real time, which
is what D1 permits — but it is one degree from being **a piece of advice**, and this pair's specific ban is
that the flat voice must not become the wise one. It survives because the knowledge is worthless: it helps
nobody, it does not change what she does, and the very next line is her locking the elbow anyway. If step 10 or
QA reads it as wisdom, the repair is to cut *"Nobody says that"* and leave the observation orphaned.

**Second, and it is a limitation rather than a defect.** Change 9's rebound audit was applied by rule and by
ear. The four soundcraft numbers reported at step 10 **cannot verify it** — `strict_end_rhyme()` reads
line-final words only and is structurally blind to a device that lives inside the bar. So the pair's central
sound device ships **unmeasured by the harness**, and the only honest thing to do is say so plainly rather than
report a number that means something else. ⛔ **No end-rhyme was added anywhere to make a floor look better.**
