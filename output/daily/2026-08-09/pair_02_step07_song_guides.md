## GUIDE 1 — **DIMENSION** *(facet P02-a, weight 1.00 · the emotion gate)*

**Storytelling technique A — the survey.** Write the emotion as **measurement work**, the way somebody paces out a room. The singer takes the size of her own vantage with instruments she has on her: a thumb, a hand, the width of a stranger's shoulders, the distance from a gate to a hedge. **Technique B — the misplaced scale.** State the near thing and the far thing in the same flat sentence with no signalling word between them. The shock is arithmetic, not adjective.
**Mood arc.** Begin in **eagerness** with the tiniest hairline of **apprehension** in the vowels; pass through **fond irritation** at the middle; land in **wonder that is not comfort** — the awe of a correct number. ⛔ Never *humility*. Humility apologises; **dimension does not.**
**Instrumentation.** Give the dimension line **the least support in the song** — kit and bass and one guitar, no pad, no harmony — so the ear hears a body saying a fact. Then let the band answer it, not her.
**Production.** Dry vocal, close, one plate return at the tail so it sounds like a field and not a booth. Live-to-tape saturation on the room mics; no vocal tuning that removes the flat edge of a plain belt.
**Structure.** ⭐ **The dimension move must land at least ONCE away from the hinge** — inside an ordinary verse where nothing is riding on it. A pair that performs its emotion only at the climax has performed a trick, not an emotion.
**Rhyme & devices.** Verses: **hard end-stopped quatrains, full rhyme, no enjambment ever.** Devices required: **polyptoton** on a size word (*the size of a hill / the size of the sky*), and **antimetabole or near-chiasmus** on a pair of objects, once per song, to make the two registers audibly swap places.
**Hook technique.** The dimension line is spoken-sung on one note, then the band lands on the following downbeat as a whole-band unison — the arrangement agreeing with the measurement.
**Thematic seed.** *A person finds out exactly how big she is by finding out exactly how much of the sky she can cover with her hand.* First person, present tense, overheard.

## GUIDE 2 — **THE CLOSED LID, HELD OPEN** *(facets P02-b + P02-h · Flair #5, the live engine)*

**Storytelling A — the withheld verdict.** The song's central variable is a weather outcome and **the song never delivers it.** Not "it cleared," not "it never cleared." The last sung bar of every variation leaves the lid **present tense and unmoved**. **B — the antagonist made of water.** Give the cloud the grammar of a body: it *has not agreed*, it *has not moved*, it *has a shape*, it *fits the hill exactly*. ⛔ **Never describe it beautifully. Never describe the sun at all, and never say what the corona would have looked like** — that is a counterfactual and it fails present tense.
**Mood arc.** Anticipation → fond irritation → **obstinate presence.** The comfort in this song is that she is still standing there; the song does **not** say that this is good.
**Instrumentation.** The lid is the **ABOVE register** and gets a wide, slow, unhurried sustain — organ pad, low guitar drone, one high bell tone — that is **indifferent to the beat** and never resolves its suspension.
**Production.** Keep the ABOVE layer **quiet and constant**; do not automate it upward with the arrangement. The lid is not dramatic. It is just there, at the same level, all song. ⭐ **The one place it changes is the hinge, where it is replaced, not removed** (D10).
**Structure.** The unresolved variable must be re-opened **at least three times**: once in the first third, once at the turn, once in the outro. Anti-tourism gate: **every sensory noun must be buyable at a petrol station or findable in a wet field.** No country. No local colour. No postcard.
**Rhyme & devices.** **Anaphora** on the lid's grammar across a stanza; **asyndeton** in the ordinary-object lists (drop the *ands* — objects arrive faster than a sentence).
**Hook technique.** The lid image belongs in **chorus line 3**, in every variation, so it returns byte-identical six to nine times without ever being explained.
**Thematic seed.** *The obstruction is not a metaphor. It is a lid, at a height, with an edge, and it is not going anywhere while she watches.*

## GUIDE 3 — **THE CHANT AND THE ENFORCED SECOND** *(facets P02-c + P02-k · A1 · Flairs #10 and #1)*

**Storytelling A — the room-holder.** ⭐ 3–5 syllables, **byte-identical on every return, ≥4 returns, no variation and no apology.** **B — the qualification test:** the chant must be shoutable by **people who did not travel**, so it may not contain the journey, the destination, the sky, the reason, or a number. If a stranger in a pub could shout it cold, it passes; if it needs the song, rewrite it.
**Mood arc.** The chant carries no sadness at any point. It is the same in the first bar and the last. **The song changes around it; the chant does not change.** That immovability is what makes the final return land as cost rather than as comfort.
**Instrumentation.** Gang vocals, six to ten untrained-sounding voices, slightly wide, recorded in a room with the kit bleeding into them. Snare on the downbeat of each chant line; guitars **out** for the first chant, **in** for the last.
**Production.** ⛔ **No stereo trick may be load-bearing** — hard-panned non-musical elements have been measured collapsing to near-mono. The gang's width is a bonus, never the mechanism. Claps and shakers double the chant's rhythm by the third return.
**Structure.** Chant block appears **four times**, byte-identical, as its own section: after chorus 1, after chorus 2, after chorus 3, and at the outro. ⭐ **THE ENFORCED SECOND** is a **named, single, whole-band unison entry** into one of those chants — no pickup, no fill, nobody can be late. Name it per variation; it is structural and is never stated in words.
**Rhyme & devices.** **Epizeuxis is permitted here and nowhere else in this pair** — a chant repeats because chants repeat, and the run's epizeuxis caution is about reaching for it as a *device*, not about writing a gang vocal.
**Hook technique.** Three deployments to choose between per variation: (1) chant naked, voices only, kit stops dead under it; (2) chant against a single held organ chord; (3) chant doubled at the octave by the low unison introduced at the hinge.
**Thematic seed.** *A sentence a field of strangers can say at the same instant without being told to.*

## GUIDE 4 — **THREE CHORUSES, THREE BUILDINGS, AND THE SWAP** *(facets P02-d + P02-e + P02-j · A2 · A3 · D10)*

**Storytelling A — identical words, changed cost.** The four chorus lines are **byte-identical** three times. Everything that changes is arrangement and what it takes out of her. ⭐ **Chorus 3 is not sadder. It is louder and it costs her more.** **B — the crossing as an event in a sentence.** At the bar before chorus 3, **a body from BELOW crosses in front of the thing ABOVE and takes the light out.** This happens **to a body, in a sentence** — a lorry, a stranger's shoulders, a thumb, a walker at dusk. ⛔ A production note alone does not answer it (THE GRAIN LAW, L22).
**Mood arc.** C1 = eagerness with the band half-dressed. C2 = communal, claps arriving, gang entering under her. C3 = **effort**; the belt is at the top of her range and audibly working.
**Instrumentation — the addition ladder** *(P02 builds by ADDITION; it is the run's control case and addition is correct here)*: **C1** kit + bass + one guitar + lead. **C2** + second guitar, claps, shakers, gang under the lead, jangling triangle on the backbeat. **C3** + Hammond organ, low unison doubling, tambourine, and ⭐ **the over-figured counter-melody** — AAA-orchestral / chip-lineage voicing, bell-toned and busier than anything the singer does, doing real harmonic work **she never acknowledges** (facet P02-j: the machine below is more complicated than the body above).
**Production — the substitution, exactly.** At the hinge bar: the **soaring high layer mutes** and a **low unison replaces it in the same bar** — vocal down an octave, doubled by baritone-register guitar and bass. ⛔ **Not a gap. Not a silence. A swap at a junction the arrangement already wanted.** Terminal silence and near-silent openings survive the renderer; specified mid-song voids do not.
**Structure.** Hinge = 4–5 lines: the crossing, **the one numeral spelled out in words**, the body's answer to it, then the downbeat into chorus 3.
**Rhyme & devices.** Chorus **AABB, hard, obvious, unashamed.** Devices: **epistrophe** across the three chorus returns (identical endings are the point), and **diacope** on the lid word inside the hinge.
**Hook technique.** (1) Chorus 3 entered from the low unison so the first syllable is the octave jump back up. (2) Gang doubling only the last two chorus lines in C3. (3) The counter-melody surfacing for exactly one bar between chorus lines and losing again.
**Thematic seed.** *The same four sentences, said three times, by someone who has less left each time and says them louder anyway.*

## GUIDE 5 — **THE ONE NUMERAL, AND THE ARENA IT LANDS IN** *(facets P02-g + P02-i)*

**Storytelling A — respond, never recite.** ⭐ **"a hundred and thirty-eight" — spelled out in words, sung exactly once, at the octave-drop hinge**, and the **next thing sung is a body's answer to it**: a duration she can compare with her own standing, a size she can compare with her own hand. ⛔ **No second numeral anywhere in the sung lines** — not *four*, not *ten*, not *half*, not *twice*, not *a hundred* on its own. ⛔ **DURATION ONLY. No arithmetic, no equation, no comparison to the length of anything that is played on a speaker.** That equation belongs to another pair and writing it here is the essay this run bans. **B — the number arrives low.** It is sung at the bottom of the octave drop, quietly, as a fact she already knew and has just now felt.
**Mood arc.** The bar before it: eagerness at full extension. The bar of it: **clean fear**, one second wide. The bar after: **obstinate presence.**
**Instrumentation.** Under the numeral: bass and floor tom only, plus the low unison that has just replaced the soaring layer. Nothing bright is allowed in that bar.
**Production — the lane gate.** ⭐ **Real drums played hard**, one fill arriving a hair early, ride bleeding into the vocal mic, **recorded live onto tape**, analog saturation on the bus, amp buzz audible between sections, spring reverb. ⛔ **Zero irony anywhere in the delivery.** Phone-speaker test: chorus must land with **vocal and kick alone** on a small transducer.
**Structure.** 96–104 BPM. Runtime 3:20–3:50. No long intro; the first sung line arrives inside the first few bars. Terminal ending, not a fade — terminal silence is the one silence the renderer keeps.
**Rhyme & devices.** **Consonance chain** through the hinge lines binding them to the chorus that follows; **caesura as wound** — one mid-line breath in the hinge that changes what the sentence is about.
**Hook technique.** (1) Numeral on a single sustained note, no vibrato, band absent for its length. (2) Numeral answered by the gang a beat late, once. (3) Numeral spoken-sung against the low unison, the answer belted.
**Thematic seed.** *Somebody says out loud how long a thing lasts, and then says how long she has been standing here, and the two facts do not comfort each other.*

## GUIDE 6 — ⭐ **WILDCARD: THE ARRIVAL SONG THAT ARRIVES AT WEATHER**

**Cross-disciplinary source.** Take the shape from **stadium arrival music and processional brass**, and the *content* from **survey photography** — the discipline of a picture taken to record where the camera was, not what was beautiful. The song is built like a piece of music that expects a moment of triumph, and it is **loaded with the wrong cargo**: a lid, a queue, a folding chair. ⭐ **The inversion is structural and the performance is one hundred percent sincere.** Irony would let the listener off; sincerity keeps them standing in the field.
**Storytelling A — honour every beat.** Leaving, road, gate, mud, count, gang, three choruses, the big one at the end. Do not skip a single beat of the arrival shape. **B — the arrival happens; the thing does not.** She gets everywhere she meant to get. The only thing missing is the event.
**Mood arc.** Eagerness → communal warmth → fond irritation → **wonder with a hard edge**. ⛔ The song never converts the disappointment into a lesson, a consolation, or a friendship. **D1: nobody teaches anybody anything. D5: the singer does not get the moral.**
**Instrumentation.** Let one instrument be **wrong on purpose** and never fixed: a jangling triangle keeping perfect time through the most chaotic bar; claps and shakers arriving at a moment nothing is being celebrated. The joy is real and it is pointed at nothing, and that is the whole feeling.
**Production.** Room ambience over polish, always. If a choice is between clean and *there*, choose **there**.
**Structure & dynamics.** Growth by **addition only**. Nothing is removed until the hinge, and at the hinge nothing is removed either — it is **replaced**.
**Devices.** One **zeugma** joining an object and a feeling with a single verb; one **synecdoche** where a coat, a thumb or a chair stands for the whole person.
**Thematic seed.** *The completion of an arrival with the arrival's object subtracted, sung by somebody who came anyway and is still there.*
