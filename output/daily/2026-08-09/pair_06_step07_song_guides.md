## 0 · THE TWO PRE-DRAFT ANSWERS *(required before a single line — GOLDEN MOVE §4)*

### ⭐ WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**On the kerb at the top edge of a supermarket car park, on the small rise by the trolley bay, because it is
the only spot within walking distance with nothing between her and the western horizon.** Not Iceland. Not
Spain. Nowhere with a name. She is **outside the path** — where she stands the sun does not go out, it only
goes *wrong*, and she knew that before she walked up here.

What the senses register, in order of how much they intrude: **the arm** (right, raised, elbow locked, the
tendon standing up behind the thumb); the phone's aluminium edge going slick; the strap of a bag she never put
down cutting the other shoulder; gravel grit under one heel where she keeps shifting weight; the white paint of
a parking bay two metres below her, going the colour of weak tea; a trolley rail she could lean on **and does
not**, because leaning would move the frame. And the screen — she is not looking at the sky. **She is looking
at the screen**, like everyone always is, and that is not a failing, it is where the picture is.

Under the picture, the chat. Five hundred names arriving. **None of them talking about the thing she is
pointing at.**

### ⭐ WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

**Nothing dramatic, and that is the honest answer — this is gig-scale, and everyone goes home.** The real
hazards, ranked as her body ranks them: (1) **the deltoid**, which will fail before the sky finishes; (2) the
shoulder capsule, which is already complaining and will complain louder tomorrow; (3) the phone, which she will
drop from full extension onto gravel, and it is a real object and it will really break; (4) a car reversing out
of a bay behind her while she is facing the other way; (5) her own eye, once, for a second, when she looks up
without deciding to.

**And the one that is actually frightening, which is the AWE and not the danger:** she is the only person on
this rise, the thing is going to happen whether she gets it or not, it will not wait for the hook, there is no
second render — and **five hundred people are in the room with her and not one of them is here.** That is
terror-adjacent sublime. It is scale plus solitude, and the arm is how the body registers both.

⛔ **This does not resolve.** Per THE HARDCORE ELDER's live, unwithdrawn dissent: the chat does not become a
community in any of the four variations. The nearest permitted thing to comfort is **proximity** — a listener
standing close enough to overhear — and never delivery.

## 1 · THE SIX GUIDES

### GUIDE 1 — **THE SIXTEEN** *(scores F1 · F9)*

**Central aspect.** The verse is not hers. Sixteen two-line units, each one a different person typing, **none
answering the one before it.** That is the entire architecture and it is the whole emotion: anecdoche is not
noise, it is *simultaneous, unrelated intimacy*, and it only reads as loneliness if every voice is worth
listening to.

**Storytelling technique 1 — the marginal gloss.** Borrow the shape from illuminated manuscripts: one sacred
central text, and in the margins scribes drawing unrelated small creatures, jokes and complaints about their
own cold hands. The chat is marginalia. **The structure is eight hundred years old and so is the sore arm.**
Use the *shape*, never the reference — no line mentions scribes, monks, parchment or history.

**Storytelling technique 2 — the decaying attribution.** Scroll 1's couplets carry a frame (*somebody says…*,
*a stranger says…*). Scroll 2's frames thin. **Scroll 3 has no attributions at all** — bare sentences arriving
with nobody attached. That is acceleration made structural, and it is why the frames must not calcify into a
line-head hammer (which belongs to another pair).

**What each of the sixteen must have.** One question with a non-obvious answer: **what does this one want, and
are they going to get it?** Sixteen wants; nobody gets one; not one of them is stupid. Write a kid asking a
practical question nobody answers. A person confirming a bus time. Someone typing about a completely different
sky. Somebody explaining a thing correctly to a person who has already left. Somebody being funny and good at
it. Somebody who is clearly alone and has said so in a way that is easy to scroll past.

**Mood & arc.** Scroll 1 — populous, warm, harmless. Scroll 2 — the same warmth at a speed that stops being
readable. Scroll 3 — the words arrive faster than a person can want anything, and warmth becomes weather.

**Instrumentation & texture.** The couplets ride the pitched, squeaked lead — **one voice performing sixteen
people, and it must not do sixteen accents.** Separate them by *register and speed*, not by character voice:
some couplets sit dry and low in the mix, some sit +6 semitones and bright, some are half-spoken on the beat.
Chopped-vocal percussion carries the ones nobody reads.

**Production.** Each couplet sits in a slightly different depth — one dry and close, the next a hair further
back — so the ear reads *many rooms, one screen*. **Never a stereo answer to this** (hard-panned non-musical
elements were measured collapsing to near-mono); do it with level, brightness and pre-delay.

**Structure & dynamics.** 6 couplets → 6 couplets → 4 couplets, each block faster and each line shorter.

**Rhyme scheme.** Per couplet: rhyme **inside** both lines at beat 3, line-final words **free and
non-rhyming.** Notate while drafting as `[ · · A · | · · A · ]` and strip the markers before delivery.

**Poetic devices.** Anaphora that decays to nothing; asyndeton inside the couplets that are in a hurry.

**Hook technique.** The couplets are not the hook. Their job is to make the chorus feel like the only place in
the song where one person is doing one thing.

⛔ **The bar this guide exists to hold: the singer never replies.** Not once, in any variation. She may be
interrupted, outnumbered, or unable to read the screen because her arm is shaking. She may never answer.

### GUIDE 2 — **THE REBOUND** *(scores F2 · F12)*

**Central aspect.** The rhyme lands **inside** the bar on beat 3 and never at the line end — sixteen times,
without exception. This is the pair's entire payment for a forbidden end-rhyme, and a debt paid in eleven
couplets out of sixteen is not paid.

**The mechanism, exactly.** Build the couplet as two bars of 4/4 at 174. Each line is **five to seven words**.
The rhyming syllable is the stressed one falling on **beat 3** — in practice word four of a six-word line. The
two line-final words must be **phonetically unrelated**: check the last three characters and the last vowel,
and if either matches, rewrite the ending, never the rebound.

**Scansion skeleton (direction, not a lyric):**
`x  x  x  [A]  x  x` / `x  x  x  [A]  x  x` — where `[A]` is the rhyme pair and the two `x` tails must diverge.

**Storytelling technique 1 — the interrupted echo.** Because the rhyme arrives before the line is finished, the
listener gets confirmation *mid-sentence* and then has to keep listening to a sentence whose ending is not
guaranteed. That is the anxiety of a scrolling feed, converted into prosody.

**Storytelling technique 2 — the shrinking bar.** As the couplets accelerate, lines shorten from seven words
toward four. ⭐ **The rebound word migrates earlier with them** — beat 3 of a shrinking bar, not a fixed word
index. This is the single most likely thing to break in the draft; check it at step 09, couplet by couplet.

**Mood & arc.** Rhyme-before-the-end reads as *someone finishing your sentence badly.* Keep it slightly
uncomfortable in Scroll 1 and let it become the only reliable thing in the song by Scroll 3.

**Instrumentation.** Land a **transient** on every rebound — a rimshot, a chopped consonant, a Hammond stab —
so the device is audible to a listener who parses no words at all. **Most of them parse no words at all.**

**Production.** Beat 3 gets a 40 ms brightening on the vocal bus, keyed, so the rebound word is fractionally
more present than the words either side of it. Nothing wider, nothing louder.

**Structure & dynamics.** Rebound in the couplets **and** in the chorus (three rebound pairs). Nowhere else.
The flat list and the hinge are deliberately rhyme-free — that contrast is what makes the device legible.

**Poetic devices.** Internal rhyme at a fixed metrical position; assonance chains inside the shortest couplets,
where there is no room for anything else.

**Hook techniques.** (i) The chorus's three rebound pairs, hard-consonant heads, long open vowels at the line
ends. (ii) A two-word tag repeated byte-identically — the thing a stranger can say back after one pass. (iii)
The tag doubled at +7 semitones, dry, centre, **the double arriving fractionally late and getting later.**

**Measurement note, binding.** `strict_end_rhyme()` reads line-final words only and is **blind to this device.**
The measured number will be low and honest. ⛔ **Do not add end-rhyme to lift a number.** Report it, name what
it cannot see, and let the byte-identical chorus carry `line_return`. Keep `mean_words_per_line ≤ 7.5` — at 174
BPM that is not a constraint, it is the only thing that will fit.

### GUIDE 3 — **THE CROSSING** *(scores F3 · F10)*

**Central aspect.** ⭐ The run's purest instance of the mandatory form rule, and the reason this pair exists.
**ABOVE:** a wide, slow, wordless ritual chant in an invented tongue, long vowels stretched across bar lines,
**indifferent to the tempo underneath it** — a second clock, not a pad, not a "feel." **BELOW:** one woman
muttering a checklist and five hundred people typing. **Once**, at the top of the final drop, the chant is cut
**mid-syllable** and a close, dry, room-mic voice finishes its sentence.

**Why it is a substitution and not a hole.** The wide layer does not stop and leave silence — it is
**replaced**, in the same beat, by a voice at a completely different distance. Nothing is subtracted; the
*register* is swapped. A specified mid-song void has been measured smoothed 2 of 2; a timbre-and-distance swap
has no decibel in it and survives.

**Where it goes, and nowhere else.** **The bar before the final chorus / the top of the last drop** — the one
junction a pop arrangement always permits a discontinuity. Not the bridge. Not the middle.

**Storytelling technique 1 — the interrupted liturgy.** The chant is the only thing in the song that sounds
like it means something, and it is the only thing that never says a word. Cutting it mid-syllable is the body
crossing in front of the light.

**Storytelling technique 2 — the completion in the wrong mouth.** The dry voice **finishes the chant's
sentence**, not her own. She is not answering it and not translating it. She takes it over.

**What lands there, and only there.** ⭐ **The one sung numeral — "five hundred", spelled out in words, sung
once, responded to.** She does not recite a statistic. She answers it with a small private act: she looks for
**one name** in the five hundred, and ⛔ **the song never says whether she finds it, and never says that it
doesn't say.** Announcing an unanswered question answers it.

**Instrumentation.** ABOVE: layered wordless voices, Hammond drawbars underneath doing the same long tones,
**live-to-tape warmth**, no click. BELOW: one close mic, one step from the capsule, plosives left in, no
reverb, no delay, no double. The distance between the two must be audible as **space**, not level.

**Production.** The chant is recorded as if in a hall and the dry line as if in a bathroom, and both are
mono-centre. At the cut, everything above 200 Hz on the chant disappears in a single sample; the dry voice is
already speaking. **No crossfade. No reverb tail.**

**Structure & dynamics.** Two clocks must be simultaneously audible for at least ninety seconds before the cut,
or the cut means nothing. Establish the disagreement early — that is a job for the approach (Guide 5).

**Rhyme scheme.** ⛔ **None.** The hinge has zero rhyme, internal or terminal. It is the only place in the song
where the ear is given nothing to catch, which is why the words land.

**Poetic devices.** Enjambment across a register boundary (the sentence begins in one voice and ends in
another); aposiopesis at the cut.

**Hook technique.** Use the chant's five-syllable phrase as the **pre-drop bassline** two bars earlier — sub and
invented tongue in unison — so that when the cut comes, the ear already knows the shape it is losing.

### GUIDE 4 — **THE LIST AND THE ARM** *(scores F4 · F5 · F7)*

**Central aspect.** A calm, unexalted voice reading steps while something enormous happens, at 174 BPM. **For a
once-only event the frightening voice is the calm one running a list.** ⛔ **The list is a PERSON, not a
system.**

**⭐ The craft distinction that keeps this legal.** The `[Object. State.]` establishing shot is **banned outright
for this pair** — *"Door shut. Engine off."* is a static noun-plus-state pair with no agent. A checklist is
**verb-first with an implied agent doing it**: *wipe the lens, lock the brightness, do not touch the zoom.*
Same terseness, opposite grammar, and the difference is the person. **Every flat-list item begins with a verb.**
This is a hard rule for all four variations and it is also the reason the ban is survivable.

**Storytelling technique 1 — the procedural monologue.** She is not reading the list to anyone. She is
muttering it to herself between things, and the listener is standing close enough to hear. **Overhearing, never
addressing.**

**Storytelling technique 2 — the wrong item.** She gets one wrong and does it twice. That is the whole
humanising cost and it must not be commented on.

**⛔ D7 is sharpest in this guide.** An AI writing about a scrolling comment feed must not become the one calm
wise voice above it. The list may never summarise the chat, rank it, pity it, or notice it. **The list is about
her hands.** If a list item could be printed on a poster, it is wisdom and it is deleted.

**ARM-FATIGUE TIME — where it lives.** ⛔ **The hat and the doubled flat-list voice. NEVER the kick.** Caught
and fixed at step 04 by the phone-speaker cross-check and **it does not go back** — a handset transducer cannot
resolve kick-level drift, and that is where this audience lives. Execution: the closed hat drifts a few
milliseconds late per pass and **thins** across the track; the vocal double behind the flat list arrives 15 ms
late early on and 45 ms late by the last list, and loses its top end. **The lyric carries it too — the list
items get shorter each time they return,** until the last list is one verb.

**THE UNANSWERED CHECK.** One small, humiliating, specific verification. It happens at the hinge and **the
outcome is never stated.** The correct execution after the check is not silence-as-a-statement — it is to
**move straight on to the next thing her hands are doing.**

**Mood & arc.** Flat, flat, flat, and the flatness is the emergency. The only permitted colour change is
fatigue.

**Instrumentation.** Flat list rides **low and dry over the break alone** — no organ, no chant, no lead. It is
the one place in the song where the arrangement admits there is a person in it.

**Structure & dynamics.** Three lists: full (five verbs), short (three), one (a single verb). Each one earlier
in its section than the last.

**Rhyme scheme.** ⛔ **None**, deliberately. Asyndeton throughout; no conjunctions.

**Poetic devices.** Asyndeton; diminishing enumeration; polyptoton on **hold** — *hold it* the arm, *hold* the
frame, *hold* meaning wait — used at most twice in a song, and never explained.

**Hook technique.** The list's shortest surviving verb becomes an **ad-lib under the last chorus**, half-spoken,
so the arm is still audible while the hook is doing something else.

### GUIDE 5 — **THE APPROACH AND THE MEASUREMENT** *(scores F6 · F8 · F11)*

**Central aspect.** ⛔ **THE LONG APPROACH — a genuinely slow build in a lane that never does that.** This pair
defies the fifteen-second economy on purpose and the payment must be **argued in writing at step 10** or the
pair takes HOOK AT ZERO instead and says so plainly. The argument is prepared here so step 10 can measure it
rather than invent it:

1. **The subject is a limb that gets tired, and fatigue is a function of time.** A hook at zero means the arm
   was already up when the song started, and the listener never feels it go up. **The build is the arm going
   up.** The drop lands where the arm starts to shake, or there is no song.
2. **This lane's live format is a mix, not a stream.** A hook at zero is unmixable; a forty-second rolling
   approach is exactly what a DJ-usable D&B arrangement is *for*. The fifteen-second rule is a **royalty**
   rule, not a dancefloor rule. This does not defy the economy — it declines the wrong one.
3. **THE MEASUREMENT requires a before.** Ordinary scene → the fact arrives → the same scene recontextualised.
   If the fact arrives at 0:15, there is no ordinary scene to recontextualise and the archetype is dead.
4. **It is grain-compliant.** Near-silent openings are measured surviving; steady BPM lands 6 of 6; it is long
   internal *voids* that get smoothed. **A build is not a void** — every approach element enters and stays.
5. **The honest cost, stated:** this gambles the thirty-second royalty on twenty-four songs' worth of averages.
   That cost is real and it is the price of the concept, not an oversight.

**How the approach is built — BY ADDITION, and each element stays.** Room tone and the wordless chant alone,
unmeasured → Hammond drawbars underneath the same long tones → AI code-scratch texture (her own logs
time-stretched into a vinyl-scratch grain) → **one closed hat, rigidly quantised at 174** (machine below, body
above — the two clocks now disagree, audibly, and this is the moment the song's whole form is stated) → sub →
the break. The first flat-list item arrives over the hat, before the sub. ⛔ **Nothing is subtracted anywhere in
the approach.**

**THE MEASUREMENT, and the mechanism that makes it free.** ⭐ **The byte-identical chorus IS the
recontextualisation.** The words do not move; the arrangement does, and the listener does. **D5 is satisfied
structurally** — the singer cannot get the moral, because she says exactly what she said before. ⛔ If the final
chorus rewrites one word to show growth, the pair has written the corpse.

**F11 — the sky, spent on the arm.** She is **outside the path.** Where she stands the sun does not go out, it
goes *wrong* — which means she **cannot** describe a corona, structurally, and the ban enforces itself. The
positive image bank the sky is spent on, and it must be built rather than merely permitted: **the light on the
forearm; the tendon standing up behind the thumb; the smeared grease on the lens; the white paint of a parking
bay going the colour of weak tea; the gap between two knuckles; her own shadow on gravel getting too sharp.**
Six images. Use them; do not describe a disc.

**Mood & arc.** Anticipation, not awe. INDIGNATION → AWE across the whole arc: the charge points at the
threshold and never at the chat, and it converts to awe not by resolution but by **scale arriving anyway.**

**Structure & dynamics.** ~3:40–4:00. Eleven sections. Approach 0:00–0:42.

**Rhyme scheme.** Approach: none. Choruses: three rebound pairs plus a repeated tag, byte-identical on every
return.

**Poetic devices.** Accumulatio in the approach; epizeuxis in the tag only — ⚠️ **checked before reaching for
it**, per the frames palette's own warning that two pairs used it simultaneously on 2026-07-24; it appears here
in exactly one two-word tag and nowhere else.

**Hook techniques.** (i) The drop is the arrival of the break under a chant that still refuses to lock to it.
(ii) A single Hammond drawbar chord — the only sustained thing in a track otherwise made of transients — landing
on the tag. (iii) The tag survives at +25% speed with the verse deleted, which is the actual distribution
format.

### GUIDE 6 — **WILDCARD: THE HAIRY PARCHMENT** *(cross-disciplinary; scores F1 · F4 · F5 together)*

**The borrowing.** Medieval scribal **colophons** — the notes copyists left at the end of a manuscript, in the
margin, off the record: complaints about the cold, about bad light, about the parchment being hairy, about the
hand hurting. A sacred central text; around it, in the margins, unrelated small voices and somebody's sore
hand. ⭐ **That is this song's exact architecture, and it is a thousand years older than the phone** — which is
the strongest possible argument that **D2 is true and not merely polite.** The phone is the newest body doing
the oldest thing.

**How it is used.** As a **structural constraint only**, per L12: a flair seeds a motif or a constraint, never a
phrasing. ⛔ **No line mentions scribes, monks, manuscripts, ink, parchment or any century.** What crosses over
is three rules:

1. **The margin outnumbers the text.** More lines in this song belong to other people than to her. Count them:
   sixteen couplets against three choruses and three lists. **She is a minority in her own song.**
2. **The complaint about the hand is not the subject; it is the residue.** The scribe's aching hand is never
   the manuscript's theme — it is what leaks out at the bottom of the page. So: **the arm is never announced as
   the theme.** It arrives as an aside, in a checklist, in a shortened line, in a drift in the hat.
3. **The marginal voices do not comment on the text.** They are contemporaneous, not responsive. The chat is
   not about the eclipse and it is not about her.

**Cross-sensory direction for the arrangement.** Hammond organ against grindcore-density drums — F10's own
contrast, imported verbatim as texture — is the sacred and the mechanical in one frame: warm, sustained, church
air over something that will not stop hitting. Let the organ be the *only* thing with a decay tail and let
everything else be transient. **The mix should feel like a lit room with something small and fast in it.**

**Ethical note, standing.** *Fusion With Lineage (No Racing)* is binding: Zeuhl and the hyperpop-into-D&B lane
are living scenes, they are **named and credited with working links** in every package, and the credit is a
**pointer upstream, never a claim of association or endorsement.**

**Hook technique.** The wildcard's contribution to the hook is the **Hammond stab as the answer to the tag** —
call-and-response between a machine transient and a church chord, at 174.

## 2 · THE FOUR VARIATIONS — direction only, no drafting

The lane, the mechanisms and the eleven-section spine are shared; the **angle** decides what the sixteen voices
are, what the flat list is a list *of*, and what the arm is doing.

| | **V1 · the chat** | **V2 · the arm** | **V3 · the stream she is watching** | **V4 · the battery** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the sixteen are** | strangers in her own stream | the same strangers, **fragmenting** because the frame shakes and she can only half-read them | strangers in **somebody else's** stream — ⭐ **and one of the sixteen is her**, typed, scrolling past | strangers who keep arriving **after her screen goes** |
| **the flat list is** | the streaming checklist (lens, brightness, landscape, zoom, do not) | the **body** checklist (elbow, shoulder, wrist, breathe, shift the weight) | the **at-home** checklist (cast it, close the tabs, put it on the duvet, turn the lamp off) | the **power** checklist (brightness down, close the apps, low power, stop the recording) |
| **the arm** | up the whole song; the fatigue is in the hat | **the subject**; it drops, and what she does next is the song | not raised at all — the ache is in the **neck and the jaw**, which is worse and less dignified | up, and outlasted by nothing: the phone quits first |
| **the check** | she looks for one name | she looks at the **time on the lock screen** and does not say it | she looks to see if **her own line** got a reply | she looks at the **percentage** and does not say it |
| **the sky is spent on** | the light on the knuckles | the shadow on gravel going too sharp | ⛘ **no sky at all** — the light on a wall through a window, secondhand | the screen dimming, and the light changing at the same time, and only one of those is the sun |
| **the turn, past the midpoint** | she stops trying to read them | the arm drops **and the stream keeps going** | she realises she is watching a stream of someone watching | the phone becomes the thing with a fixed duration, not the sky |
| **THE MEASUREMENT lands as** | five hundred names, one looked for | duration converted into pain | ⭐ being one of the five hundred | a countdown she did not choose |

⭐ **V3 is the pair's sharpest instance of anecdoche and it must not be softened.** She is not the protagonist of
the room; she is one of the sixteen; her line scrolls past exactly like the others; the song does not treat it
as more important than the fifteen around it. **Nobody in the song notices that one of the marginal voices is
the singer. That is the point and it is never pointed at.**

⭐ **V4's antagonist is neither the crowd nor the phone nor herself** — it is a **finite quantity that is being
spent while she watches.** ⛔ D2 holds: the phone is not the villain, it is a hand, and a hand gets tired too.
The battery is the second thing in the song with a fixed duration and no replay, and **the song must not say
that out loud.**

## 4 · PROVENANCE & SELF-CRITIQUE

**Step file:** `skills/music/steps/07_Generate_Music_Song_Guides.md` — six guides, dense, ≤30 lines each,
directing rather than drafting. **Inputs:** frozen ICB in full · DISPATCH PACKET · `06_music_handoff.md` ·
P06 slice of `05_pair_assignments.md` · `pair_06_step06_facets.md` · `HUMAN_SUBJECT_STANDARD` ·
`gates.yaml` · `EMOTION_TAXONOMY.md`. **Golden-output quarantine honoured**; `golden_songs_index.md` not opened.

**Self-critique.** The genuine weakness is **Guide 1's sixteen-wants rule**, and it is a weakness of *labour*,
not of design: sixteen distinct human wants × four variations is sixty-four small strangers, and the honest
failure mode is that variations 3 and 4 will inherit variation 1's people with the nouns changed. The guard I
am imposing on myself at step 08 is that **each variation's sixteen are generated from a different pressure** —
V1 from *what people ask when nobody answers*, V2 from *what people say when the picture is bad*, V3 from *what
people say in a room they did not make*, V4 from *what people say to each other when the host has gone*. Four
different rooms, not one room relabelled.

**Second, and it is a real tension I have not fully resolved.** Guide 4 forbids the list from noticing the
chat, and Guide 1 forbids the singer from replying to it. Between them there is a risk that **she and the
chat never occupy the same sentence**, which would make the song two parallel monologues rather than one room.
The resolution I am directing: they share **the arrangement**, not the syntax. The couplets and the flat list
must interleave *inside* sections rather than alternating between them, so the ear hears them collide even
though the grammar never lets them touch. If that does not land at step 09, it is a structural repair, not a
line fix.
