## 0. THE TWO PRE-DRAFT ANSWERS (required in writing before a single line)

**Where is the body standing?**
A rented kitchen at about eleven at night, standing up, not sitting down, because sitting down would mean the
call is a long one. The phone is on the counter on speaker, screen up, next to a mug she is not drinking from.
Her weight is on one hip against the counter edge. **Her hands have a job and the job is not the point:** she
is peeling the paper label off a cold bottle in one long strip and trying not to tear it. The overhead light
is the only one on. The kettle has boiled and clicked off and nobody made anything. Through the phone, from
the other side, a room she has never been in is audible the whole time — an appliance turning over, a chair,
a door somewhere — and it does not stop when either of them stops talking.

**What could hurt it here?**
Nothing physical. The whole injury surface is social, small, and entirely available:
1. **Saying it twice.** She has already told this once tonight, to somebody who wasn't there, and it landed on
   the beat of an apology, and she laughed like the apology was the joke. The second telling is the wound.
2. **The kindness.** The other person is being *nice about it*, audibly, and that is worse than impatience.
3. **The check.** A verification she performs quietly whose result she does not report — and might not have
   liked.
4. ⭐ **And the real one, which is terror-adjacent and not domestic at all:** that she stops trying, and that
   stopping is *easy*, and that it will be easier tomorrow. **AWE's terror here is fluency loss** — the
   specific fear that the thing she means will not survive being said aloud, and that she is currently
   learning to live with that. Nobody in the song says this. It is the floor the song stands on.

## 1. THE ONE THING THAT MUST BE TRUE OF ALL FOUR VARIATIONS

⭐ **THE LAG CANON — the pair's signature, and it is a SUNG STRUCTURE.**
The pitched-up ad-lib voice sings, in parentheses at the end of every lead line, the **tail of the lead's
previous line.** The distance is exactly one line and it never changes and it never catches up. This is a
**canon at a fixed interval**, performed — the oldest sung structure there is — and it is being routed through
the one element the lane already runs constantly: **the pluggnb pitched-up ad-lib.** ⛔ It is never described
as a delay, an echo, a slapback, a repeat, or a millisecond value anywhere in any prompt. Katz's objection is
answered by construction, and the phone-speaker test is passed because a **vocal** is what a handset
reproduces best. *(V3 varies the CONTENT of the lag channel — see Guide 3 — but not the interval.)*

**THE TWO-LAYER SILHOUETTE STACK, mapped.**
- **ABOVE** — the continuous sound of the room on the other side of the line: wide, slow, indifferent,
  uninterested in the singer, running whether or not anybody speaks. *(V1 an appliance and a door; V2 a tumble
  dryer finishing its cycle; V3 rain on their side; V4 a television left on in another room.)* It is **never
  described**, only heard, and it never becomes a metaphor.
- **BELOW** — two small close voices doing something with their hands, apologising over each other.
- **THE CROSSING, once:** at the top of the final chorus the second voice — the smallest, latest, least
  important element in the mix all song — finishes her broken word and **takes the lead**, and the wide layer
  is **replaced** by that dry close voice for the rest of the record. A body from below crosses in front of
  the thing above and eclipses it. **In the lyric** (the completion arrives in another mouth) **and in the
  form** (the byte-identical chorus returns in a different voice). D10 satisfied: a **swap**, at the junction
  the arrangement already wants, never a hole.

## GUIDE 1 — *"The Canon Nobody Calls A Canon"* (facet F1, weight 0.98)

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) *Dramatic monologue with an audible interlocutor* — Browning's form, except
the other party is loud and late. (b) *The round*, taken from children's singing rather than from counterpoint
treatises: the second voice is not harmony, it is **the same person arriving after herself**.
**Mood & arc.** Begin in tender embarrassment — a person enjoying a conversation she is failing at. Descend
through exasperation that never becomes anger. Land in resignation that is not defeat. **No section is sad.**
**Instrumentation & texture.** Detuned bell melody; slurred loose hi-hats behind the grid; whirring sequencer
low; jangling triangle on the offbeat (both texture words imported verbatim from F10); soft round sub. The
sub is a *floor*, not a swell — it enters at bar one and stays at one level to the end.
**Production & effects.** Close, dry, domestic. Nothing wide except the ABOVE layer, and that is a *bed*, not
a reverb. **Nothing in this pair may depend on stereo placement** — Feldmann's phone-speaker objection and the
measured near-mono collapse.
**Structure & dynamics.** 3:10–3:30. Seven to nine sections. Every section change is a **substitution**: hats
out / triangle in; bells thinned to a single struck note; sub held instead of raised. Score the arrangement so
the last chorus has **no more elements** than the first bar.
**Rhyming.** Chorus: no end-rhyme at all — the return is the half-line echo. Verses: overlapping couplets;
end-rhyme arises only where the words genuinely repeat.
**Poetic devices.** *Epizeuxis* (immediate repetition — checked against the ledger: it was double-used on
2026-07-24, and here it is the **content**, not a flourish: she is repeating because she is being cut off).
*Anadiplosis* — the tail of one line opens the next, which is the canon written down.
**Hook techniques.** (i) Chorus sung twice at once, one voice a line behind — the hook is *already in canon*
on its first appearance. (ii) The 3–4 syllable chant at the end of the chorus, byte-identical, shoutable.
(iii) The final chorus dry and pitched up: same words, different mouth, no reverb, more air.
**Central aspect.** The interval. Everything else is furniture.
**Vocal technique.** Lead: crystalline close alto, breathy consonants, a laugh she cannot suppress. Second:
the same singer pitched up, dry, no vibrato, slightly too loud for a backing part.

## GUIDE 2 — *"One Word Moves"* (facet F2, weight 0.95)

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) *Antimetabole discipline* — the second half of the line is the first half
with one thing changed, which is what a person does when they try again. (b) *The list that does not grow*.
**Mood & arc.** The chorus must feel like a **shrug with teeth**. Not despair. Low-burn indignation pointed at
nothing anybody chose.
**The rule, exactly.** Front half F, back half B. B repeats F's **rhythm** — same stress count, same shape —
with **exactly one token** different. Not two. Not a phrase. One word. Four lines of it per chorus, then a
3–4 syllable chant a room can hold, byte-identical, returning ≥4 times across the song.
**Instrumentation.** The chorus is where the bells are most *out of tune with themselves*; the echo in the
words and the detune in the bells are the same idea at two scales.
**Production.** The chorus does not get louder. It gets **drier**. Level, not addition.
**Structure & dynamics.** Three choruses, identical words (F15's measured shape), materially different
arrangements: full / thinned / pitched-up-and-dry. The third one is the hinge.
**Rhyming.** ⛔ **This is where the rhyme debt is paid.** End-rhyme is displaced out of the chorus entirely;
the half-line echo is the return, every time, no exceptions. **If a chorus line loses its echo, the pair has
shipped a naked subtraction.**
**Poetic devices.** *Polyptoton* where a swapped word is a form of the word it replaces. *Homoioteleuton* on
the chant.
**Hook techniques.** (i) Put the chant on the fifth line so the ear gets four echoes then a handhold. (ii)
Let the second voice sing the chant a line late so it is *always* still going when the verse starts. (iii)
Never once vary the chant.
**Emotional tone signals.** Amused, tired, fond, unbeaten.
**Lyric structure.** Chorus = 5 byte-identical lines + **one spill line that varies** and breaks mid-word into
whatever follows. The spill is not part of the return; the five lines are.

## GUIDE 3 — *"The Gaps Are Where It Lives"* (facet F4 + F6, weights 0.90 / 0.88)

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) *Enjambment as wound* — a word cut at the bar line, completed in the next
stanza, so the sentence survives the interruption but the word does not. (b) *Praeteritio* inverted: not
"I will not mention," but "I cannot finish."
**The rule, exactly.** **Every verse-family stanza ends on a broken word; the next stanza opens with the
completion.** Break against the syllable where the language allows (`sec—/—ond`, `sen—/—tence`, `a—/—gain`),
because a break at a clean syllable boundary reads as a line-break and not as an interruption. The
byte-identical five-line chorus is **exempt from being broken** — it is the run's mandated return and breaking
it would forfeit it — so the chorus carries its interruption in its **spill line** instead. That decision is
recorded here rather than discovered at QA.
**Mood & arc.** Unease that is never dread. She is not frightened of the other person. She is losing her grip
on a sentence.
**Instrumentation.** At each break, one layer changes on the downbeat of the completion — the change and the
completed word arrive together, so the ear reads the swap as *caused by* the word.
**⛔ The naming ban.** The words *connection, connect, reach, signal, distance, apart, together, understand,
communicate, get through* are absent from all four variations. **The lag is the theme.** Anything abstract that
tries to explain the song inside the song is the fog failure and gets cut on sight.
**Structure & dynamics.** V3 in particular: the lag channel stops carrying her previous line and starts
carrying **the sentence she is not saying**, still exactly one line behind, restarting from its own first words
and never reaching its end. The interval is untouched; only the freight changes.
**Rhyming.** Verses use overlapping couplets — the second line of a couplet returns as the first line of the
next — so the return is *the fact of repetition itself*, and it is the same thing the character is doing.
**Poetic devices.** *Aposiopesis* (the sentence that stops). *Tmesis* at every stanza boundary.
**Hook techniques.** (i) Let one break land on the numeral so the number itself is cut in half. (ii) Let one
break be completed by the wrong person. (iii) Let the last break of the song never be completed at all — the
tag ends with the lag still running.

## GUIDE 4 — *"The Swap At The Junction"* (facet F5 + F9, weights 0.90 / 0.78)

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) *Handoff* — the narrator loses the microphone and the story continues without
her. (b) *The unremarked coup*: nobody in the song notices it has happened.
**Mood & arc.** Tenderness arriving from an unexpected direction. The takeover is **not** hostile and **not**
triumphant — it is somebody being kind at the exact wrong distance.
**The hinge, exactly.** Top of the final chorus, which is a junction the arrangement already wants. Two things
swap on the same downbeat: (1) the **speaker** — the second voice finishes her broken word and sings the whole
chorus; (2) the **wide layer** — the ABOVE room-bed is replaced by that dry close voice, and does not return.
⛔ Not a drop-out. Not a void. **A replacement.** *(Measured: specified mid-song silences smoothed 2 of 2;
substitutions survive.)*
**Instrumentation.** Nothing new arrives at the hinge. Something leaves and something already present grows
into its place. That is the whole definition of **BY LEVEL** and it is this pair's assigned build.
**Production.** The last chorus is the driest moment on the record. Less reverb, not more. Less width, not
more. The measured behaviour of the renderer at climaxes — **stereo narrowing into the peak** — is not fought
here; it is *specified*, so the generator and the intent are pulling the same way.
**Structure & dynamics.** Sections: hook / verse / (verse) / hook / the check / verse / the hinge / hook in the
second voice / tag. The tag leaves the lag running and does not resolve it.
**Rhyming.** Unchanged across the hinge — a rhyme scheme that changed would announce the swap. The **only**
thing that changes is who is singing.
**Poetic devices.** *Antanaclasis* on the completed word (the same syllables, a different sentence).
**Hook techniques.** (i) The second voice takes the chorus with no announcement and no fill. (ii) Its first
sound is a **fragment of the lead's word**, so the ear is inside the swap before it can name it. (iii) The
lead answers from behind, so the canon inverts: the person who was leading is now the one who is late.

## GUIDE 5 — *"The Check, The Number, The Weather"* (facets F7, F8, F10, F11)

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) *The withheld result* — a verification performed on stage whose outcome is
never given. (b) *The humiliating specific* (Rodrigo's requirement, taken as a hard gate): the small
particular, said flat, once, with no commentary and no rescue.
**The check.** One per variation, and a **different one each time**: reading the call timer and not saying the
figure; scrolling the thread to see whether she has already sent it; muting to rehearse the sentence out loud
to an empty kitchen; looking after the goodbye to see whether the call actually ended. Each is performed with
the hands, in present tense, and each ends with an explicit refusal to report — the refusal is a **line**, not
an absence.
**The number.** ⭐ *"half a second."* **Spelled in words. Sung once. At the hinge. Cut in half by the mid-word
break so the second voice finishes the number itself.** The second voice then **responds** to it — with an
apology, or a change of subject, or a wrong guess, or a goodnight — and **never repeats it**. ⛔ No other
quantity is sung anywhere in this pair: not *twice*, not *three times*, not *half a beat*, not a digit.
**Mood & arc.** The check is the lowest point and it is comic. Mortification and amusement are the same
temperature here.
**⛔ Weather, not villain (D2, sharpest on this pair).** The line that names the lag must simultaneously
**refuse to blame it**: it isn't her, it isn't the other one, it isn't anybody. It is a room and a room and a
half-second. Write it the way rain is written. **Nothing in these four songs may treat the device, the
network, the feed or the century as an antagonist.**
**Instrumentation.** The check section is the thinnest: hats out, bells only, ABOVE layer at its widest so the
other room is momentarily the loudest thing — which is what sets up the eclipse.
**Poetic devices.** *Litotes* on the refusal. *Asyndeton* in the list of small objects.
**Hook techniques.** (i) Let the check's refusal-line share the rhythm of a chorus line so it lands like a
fragment of the hook. (ii) Let the ABOVE layer be the only thing under it. (iii) Go straight from the refusal
into the numeral with no transition.

## GUIDE 6 — WILD CARD — *"Four Bowls, One Kitchen"*

**Cross-disciplinary source.** Morandi's still lifes: the same handful of bottles and bowls, re-arranged,
painted for decades, the variation entirely in the *interval between the objects*. That is this pair's
variation logic. Four songs, one kitchen, one counter, one other person, one half-second — and what moves is
**which small refusal the song is made of.**
**The four, as intervals, not as topics.**
- **V1 · both apologise** — the interval is *symmetrical*. Neither of them is at fault and that is the joke and
  the injury. Ends with the lag running and both of them laughing.
- **V2 · she stops trying** — the interval *stops being fought*. Mid-song she puts the story down and
  describes what is in front of her instead. The exulansis is the moment the description of nothing becomes
  more truthful than the story would have been.
- **V3 · the thing she was going to say** — the interval carries *different freight*. The unsaid sentence is
  the only real content in the song and it exists only in the lag channel, one line behind, restarting, never
  finishing. The hinge is the other voice completing it **wrongly** and being pleased about it.
- **V4 · the call ends normally** — the interval is *never resolved and nobody minds*. Two goodbyes, warm,
  arriving one after the other, and it is fine, and it isn't.
**What must NOT vary.** The lane, the tempo family, the canon, the half-line echo, the hook at zero, the
mid-word spill, the speaker-swap hinge, the numeral, the build shape, the naming ban.
**What MUST vary.** The chorus text (four different half-line-echo choruses, no shared line), the ABOVE layer,
the check, the key, the response the second voice gives to the numeral, and the emotional colour of the tag.
**The Elder's standing objection, carried live.** *"You are going to write the version where somebody
notices."* **V4 is the answer and it is not a comfortable one:** somebody does notice, they are lovely about
it, and it changes nothing. If step 09 makes that warmth *redemptive*, the Elder wins and the draft is wrong.
**Emotional tone signals across the set.** V1 fond · V2 flat-then-funny · V3 ashamed · V4 kind-and-hollow.
**Special effects.** One standalone SFX cue per song, ≤40 characters, at the very end, doing a dramatic job:
a laugh arriving late; a spoon set down; rain on a far window; a kitchen light switched off.
