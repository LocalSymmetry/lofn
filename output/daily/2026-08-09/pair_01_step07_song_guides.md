## ⭐ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT ANSWERS *(GOLDEN MOVE #4 — required in writing before any line exists)*

**1 · WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?**
In three places, in this order, holding the same short clip.
**(i)** On her side on top of an unmade bed with wired headphones in, phone flat on the duvet a hand's width
from her face, the cable pulling at her jaw every time she shifts. **(ii)** Upright on the arm of a sofa in an
otherwise empty room, phone face-up on the cushion, playing out loud through the phone's own speaker, which is
thin and points at the ceiling. **(iii)** Standing in a kitchen with **one other person in it** who is running
a tap, is not looking at the screen, does not speak, and is not company. Her arm is tired by then; the phone
comes down and rests warm against her sternum, still playing.

**2 · WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?**
Not the room. **The ninth watch itself.** She now knows the clip well enough that her mouth arrives at the
words *before* the tape does — and each time that happens, the file gets slightly more familiar and the night
gets slightly further away. **The copy is eating the original, and the eating is being done by her, on
purpose, with her thumb.** That is the terror in this AWE: nothing threatens her, and she is fluently
dismantling the only thing she has by handling it. Second, smaller, and never resolved: the coat on the chair
belongs to nobody she can name, so some part of that night was already not hers while she was inside it.
⛔ *Neither answer may become a sung line. They are load-bearing under the floor, not furniture on it.*

## SIX GUIDES

### GUIDE 1 — **THE CATALOGUE ENGINE** *(facets P01-a, P01-k)*
**Storytelling techniques:** (i) *Ekphrasis without praise* — the whole lyric describes one recorded image, in
the manner of a person describing a photograph to someone who cannot see it, and never once says what it is
worth. (ii) *Accumulatio* — meaning is produced only by the pile, never by a summarising line.
**Mood & arc:** flat absorbed attention → a small snag at the misfit item → attention that has become
mechanical → a snag that does not resolve. No lift anywhere.
**Structure:** irregular list stanzas of **3 / 5 / 3 / 7**, cycled three times, each cycle framed top and tail
by the single-line refrain. The **seven-line block must be heard as too many** — that is the "past comfort" in
LOOP UNTIL IT HURTS, delivered lyrically instead of by a production note.
**Rhyme scheme:** none by end-position. The stanzas are *unrhymed lists* whose only return is the braid vowel
(Guide 3) and the refrain. This is the declared rhyme debt and its declared payment.
**Poetic devices:** asyndeton (strip the *ands*); polyptoton on the verb *keep* / *keeps* / *kept* — used only
about the file's behaviour, never about her intentions.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the refrain sits **alone** in the mix, no answer vocal, no double, until the hinge;
(b) it always lands on the loop's downbeat, never syncopated, so the ear learns its clock; (c) it is short
enough that the loop's fourth bar is empty after it — the emptiness is the hook's second half.
**Narrative:** first person, present tense, one body, no addressee. The clip is never named as "a memory".
**Central aspect:** the list is the plot. **Key sequence:** the seven-line block is where a listener first
notices they are being made to wait. **Lyric structure:** exactly one concrete noun per line; a line carrying
two nouns is a repair.

### GUIDE 2 — **THE CONDITION OF THE TAPE** *(facets P01-b, P01-f — D9 and D5 enforced at the surface)*
**Storytelling techniques:** (i) *The inventory of damage* — every attribute of the clip is a defect or a
neutral fact, in the register of someone reading a fault list aloud. (ii) *Withheld verdict* — the song
possesses an opinion and never states it.
**Mood & arc:** clinical → briefly snagged → clinical again. The emotional information is carried entirely by
**which detail she stops on**, never by an adjective.
**Instrumentation implication:** the vocal must be **low, close and almost spoken**, chest voice, because a
sung-pretty delivery would do the aestheticising the lyric refuses. Breath, lip noise and an audible swallow
are content.
**Production:** the take where **she runs out of air one syllable early stays in, unpunched** (the Grit
Shaper's note). Do not comp the person out of the vocal.
**Structure & dynamics:** no dynamic solution to an emotional problem. If a section feels flat, the fix is a
harder noun, never a louder bar.
**Rhyme scheme:** unrhymed; the *only* permitted sonic return here is consonantal (Guide 3).
**Poetic devices:** litotes (the badness stated as understatement, never as charm); enjambment used **against**
the list — one item spilling into the next line so the ear trips.
**Hook techniques:** (a) place the refrain immediately after the ugliest item, so the return is contaminated
by it; (b) never place it after the misfit — the misfit gets silence; (c) the last word of the refrain is
always the braid vowel.
**Narrative:** ⛔ **BANNED SENTENCES, all four variations:** any line saying the video matters, is all she
has, is precious, or is beautiful; any comparative that upgrades a flaw; any future tense; any *one day*.
**Emotional tone signal:** the flatness is not numbness. It is *attention*. Play it as interest, not damage.

### GUIDE 3 — **THE BRAID IN THE MOUTH** *(facet P01-c — the A1 return device, the rhyme debt's payment)*
**Storytelling techniques:** (i) *Sound-led selection* — objects enter the catalogue partly because their
names carry the braid vowel, which is how a list acquires music. (ii) *Homoioteleuton* against spelling — the
ear hears rhyme where the page shows none.
**The mechanism:** one vowel nucleus is carried across **four or more consecutive line-ends**, spelled
differently every time, at least three times per song. The mouth does not change shape; the words do.
**Braid allocation — one per variation, no reuse:** V1 **/ɪ/** (in · it · still · rim · click · wrist · lit ·
chin · lid) · V2 **/oʊ/** (no · coat · ago · slow · road · hold · over · closed) · V3 **/eɪ/** (tape · same ·
wait · place · grey · late · made · shape) · V4 **/aɪ/** (file · time · size · line · title · drive · quiet ·
behind). ⭐ V4's braid *is* the metadata vocabulary — that is the joke and it should not be explained.
**Instrumentation:** the braid vowel should be doubled by the detuned Rhodes-ish chord's top note where
possible, so the room agrees with her mouth.
**Rhyme scheme per section:** stanzas = braid-only, zero end-rhyme; refrain = braid-terminal; the worse
earlier version deliberately **breaks the braid** (that is part of why it is worse).
**Poetic devices:** assonance chain as the primary structure; consonance on hard stops (t, k, d) so the flat
vowel has edges and does not smear at 80 BPM.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the refrain's final vowel is the braid vowel, held a fraction long; (b) the braid
tightens in the seven-line block — six of seven line-ends carry it; (c) after the break, no braid at all.
**Emotional tone signal:** a braid is a mouth that cannot get off a shape. That is the emotion, made physical.

### GUIDE 4 — **THREE ROOMS, ONE LOOP** *(facets P01-d, P01-h — build BY LEVEL; the Katz condition)*
**Central aspect:** the four-bar loop is **identical from first bar to last.** ⛔ No accretion, no added layer,
no thickening chorus. The build is **which room the loop is playing into.**
**The three acoustics (a SUBSTITUTION per room, never a density change — the Auditor's ruling):**
**(i) headphone-close** — sub against the skull, break dry and dusty, no air; **(ii) small speaker in air** —
the same loop thinned and mid-forward, as if leaking out of a phone lying face-up, room tone audible;
**(iii) hard kitchen** — tile reflection, a tap running under it, the loop unchanged and suddenly public.
**Instrumentation & texture:** heavy dusty break; sub carrying the weight; **one detuned Rhodes-ish chord that
never resolves**; and — imported verbatim from the Bandcamp source as texture, not as genre — **"a breezy
acoustic guitar" used WRONGLY**: dry, too close, no reverb, mic'd like a spoken word, doing a job it was not
built for. It should sound like an instrument that wandered in from a warmer record and cannot get out.
**Arm-fatigue (Flair #4):** something **tires** — the shaker/hat drags a few milliseconds later each pass and
the vocal double thins toward the end. ⛔ **Never the kick** (Feldmann: a phone speaker cannot resolve it).
**Structure & dynamics:** ~3:10–3:30. Level throughout. One break near the end; **no return.**
**Production:** terminal silence survives the renderer; a specified mid-song void does not. Put the emptiness
at the END, never in the middle.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the loop's fourth bar is always slightly empty — the hook lives in that hole;
(b) room change lands exactly on a refrain, never mid-stanza; (c) the kitchen tap is the only new sound
element in the entire song and it is not musical.

### GUIDE 5 — **THE DOUBLE, AND THE WORSE EARLIER VERSION** *(facets P01-e, P01-i — the hinge + Flair #7)*
**The hinge (A2, D10, Source 3's crossing):** at the **top of the final refrain**, the single-tracked lead
**vanishes and the doubled voice is standing in its place** — a substitution at the junction the arrangement
already wants. The two copies drift apart by a fraction of a beat, hang there, then re-fuse on the last word.
**It must live in the lyric too:** by the ninth watch her mouth is **early** — she reaches the words before
the tape does, and for one line she is the recording. **That is the body crossing in front of the light.**
⛔ Not a layer over the single. ⛔ Not a hole. A **replacement.**
**Immediately after:** the one break. Then nothing. The loop does not come back.
**The worse earlier version (Flair #7):** exactly once, mid-song, the refrain appears in the shape it had
before it settled — **longer, more syllables, one redundant word, the braid broken.** Sung once, flatter,
slightly rushed, never returned to and never explained. It should sound like a draft that got recorded.
**Poetic devices:** epanalepsis in the refrain (the line begins and ends on the same small word) so that the
doubled version has an obvious seam to drift on; diacope in the break.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the doubled refrain is *narrower* in the mix than the single was, not wider — the
crossing makes the song smaller, which is the honest direction; (b) the drift is a fraction of a beat, not a
chorus effect; (c) the re-fuse lands on the braid vowel and the break takes it away mid-decay.
**Emotional tone signal:** the hinge should feel like competence, not catharsis. She has got *good* at this.
That is the frightening part and no line may say so.

### GUIDE 6 — **WILDCARD · THE CONDITION REPORT** *(cross-disciplinary: conservation, not music)*
**The borrowed form:** a museum conservator's **condition report** — the document that describes an object
*only* by its damage. *Tear at lower left. Foxing across the upper third. Adhesive residue, previous repair,
not original.* It never says the object is beautiful. It never says why it is worth keeping. **The keeping is
implied by the fact that somebody is filling in the form.** That is D9 solved at the level of genre, and it is
LOFN-PRIME's Eager Archivist arriving in exactly the wrong decade with exactly the right instrument.
**How it lands in the lyric:** the catalogue's register is *inspection*, not reminiscence. Damage is named
without regret. "Previous repair, not original" is the emotional core of **V4** — she cropped it once and the
original is gone, and a conservator's phrase for that is a fact, not a lament.
**How it lands in the sound:** an inspection is done under a bad light, close, with the object flat on a
table. That is the mic position: **close, dry, no reverb, no flattery** — including on the acoustic guitar.
**Cross-sensory direction:** the whole track should feel like a warm object handled with cold hands.
**Storytelling techniques:** (i) *Documentary register borrowed and then broken once* — the misfit item is the
line where the form has no field for what she found; (ii) *The unspoken purpose* — condition reports exist
because somebody intends to keep the thing, and that intention is never written on the form.
**Special effect / innovation:** the only permitted "glitch" is the clip's own audio clipping on a laugh —
LOFN-PRIME's *Glitches Done Right* pillar sourced from the object rather than from a plug-in. ⛔ No
bit-crush here; that pillar belongs to the AMBITIOUS arm this run.
**Risk it takes:** a condition report is a boring document. If the song becomes boring rather than *flat*, the
repair is more concrete objects, not more feeling.

**Self-critique.** Guide 6 is the one carrying real risk. The condition-report register is a genuinely good
solution to D9 — it makes the ban structural instead of policed — but it is also a **cold** register, and this
pair's mode is AWE, terror-adjacent, not clinical detachment. If step 08 takes Guide 6 literally the song
becomes a form being filled in, which is the Hardcore Elder's complaint inverted rather than answered. The
correction I am carrying forward: **the register is inspection; the attention is love; neither is ever named.**
The tell that it has gone wrong is if a listener would call the singer detached rather than absorbed.

Second: the section map has the misfit landing in Catalogue II and the arm-fatigue starting in Catalogue IV,
which means the middle of the song is where both of its structural events sit. That is a real crowding risk
and step 08 may move the misfit later in V3 and V4 specifically — where the misfit *is* the reveal — provided
the seven-line block still reads as too many. Flagged rather than pre-solved.
