## ⭐ REQUIRED OPENING — THE TWO QUESTIONS

### *Where is the body standing?*
> **On both knees, on the low wet flat below the Atlantic City boardwalk, some evening around nineteen
> hundred, with the sun already off the face of the thing he is working on.** Not standing — kneeling, which
> is the working posture and not a devotional one. Left hand flat on the lion's shoulder to steady it, right
> thumb dragging the last ridge into the mane. A flat tin pail of seawater at his knee, the rim gone green
> as old copper. Sand up to the second knuckle. Boardwalk planks about twenty paces behind and above him,
> with shoes on them that stop and go. Wet packed sand under his shins, cold coming up through the trousers.

### *What could hurt it here?*
> **The sea. Eleven feet away and closing, and it is not a metaphor.**
> The plinth he built the lion on is on the low flat because that is where the sand holds, and the low flat
> is the part that goes first. A high wave takes the plinth's corner and the whole east side of the lion
> sloughs at once — five hundred pounds of wet sand going down in the time it takes to stand up. If he is
> leaning across the chest when it goes, the sand takes his forearms with it and the next wave puts him on
> his back in six inches of cold sea with a boardwalk full of people watching.
> **Secondary and real:** the cold. He has been kneeling in wet sand since the light was white, and his
> wrists have quit, and a man who cannot get his hands under him is a man the shorebreak can hold down.
> **The terror is clean, scheduled, arithmetic — and he is not afraid of it.** That is the AWE. He is
> *timing* it. He knows to the minute how long he has, and he spends the last of it on the ridge that will
> not slump.

⛔ **The one thing that cannot hurt him is the loss of the lion.** That is the deal and he took it. Any line
that treats the tide as the wound has misread the song.

## THE THROUGH-LINE (all four variations)

**A man is finishing a mane before the sea arrives, and he is talking to the lion the whole time.**
The lion is *you* from the first line to the last and it never answers. There is no listener in the song and
no room that could hold one. The song ends with him going home and promising to come back before the light
to start another one, which is the whole moral position stated as a schedule.

**The single sentence the pair is written to earn:**
> *"This was the deal. I read it and I took it."*

## THE FORM — long-lined free verse, slow accretion, rhyme every fourth line

**The rhyme chain.** Terminal rhyme lands **only on lines 4, 8, 12, 16 …** Each fourth line rhymes with the
**previous** fourth line. Every time the chain approaches a refrain it is **pulled into the `-ing` family**,
because the refrain's fourth line always ends on **morning**. The effect: the rhyme arrives at a fixed
interval whether or not the sentence is ready for it, and the closer the song gets to the refrain the more
the language bends toward it. **That is a tide, written as prosody.**

**Accretion, not development.** Verses do not build to anything; they *thicken*. Each section adds one more
physical fact to the same evening — the pail, the thumbnail, the sheet of sea, the plinth, the palm-clap —
and drops nothing. By the last section the listener is holding every object at once, which is what a finished
sand lion is.

**The seam, in the form.** At the hinge the long line **collapses for four lines** into short physical
phrases (*"Palm flat. Press. Lift."*), the groove grows hips, and then the long line returns. That is the ONE
JOIN the listener is allowed to notice, and it is placed exactly where the tide is.

**THE RETURN (L21), discharged in writing.** Rhyme is thinned to a quarter density by assignment, so the debt
is paid by repetition: the four-line refrain returns **byte-identical four times** in every variation, and the
anchor line returns inside and outside it. ⛔ Exact refrain repetition requires no defence and none is offered.

## PER-VARIATION GUIDES

### V1 — *The Last Ridge of Your Mane* · **THE HANDS**
Thumb-distance the whole way. The tide never appears in a wide shot; it appears as the room return getting
longer and as one line of arithmetic at the hinge. Warmest of the four. Rhodes-forward, tenor latest.
**Section map:** Wetting Down · Ridges · Refrain · Flash · The Sheet · Refrain · Eleven Feet · Second Flash ·
Packing the Chest · Refrain · Before the Light · Refrain.
**Emotional shape:** absorbed → proud → warm → startled → hushed → calm → hard-joyful → tender → certain.
**Ends on:** a goodnight to a lion, and a promise to start his brother before the light.

### V2 — *Eleven Feet and Closing* · **THE COUNTDOWN**
The distances shrink as the song accretes: a boat length, eleven feet, a hand's width, the width of the ridge
itself, nothing. The most forward groove of the four; the upright is most present here, and the tenor answers
each hinge line like a second man agreeing. **Section map:** Pacing It Out · The Plinth · Refrain · Powder ·
The Sheet Arrives First · Refrain · A Hand's Width · Second Powder · The First Corner · Refrain · Boots On.
**Ends on:** the first corner going, and him still working, and the walk home.

### V3 — *I Pack Your Paw Twice* · **THE PRACTICE**
Not an event — a habit, years wide. He has packed this paw twice every evening of his working life: once for
the shape, once for the hour. Most drifting and modal of the four, slowest accretion, tenor doubled in a low
second voice like the same man agreeing with himself. **Section map:** Once for the Shape · Twice for the
Hour · Refrain · The Crack of Powder · What the Sheet Does · Refrain · Eleven Feet · The Second Crack ·
The Chest Goes Last · Refrain · Tomorrow's Brow.
**Ends on:** tomorrow's lion, already described, already worse-mannered.

### V4 — *A Box Clicked Behind Me* · **THE INTRUSION**
The strangers with the boxes and the flash powder. Two cracks, structurally central, and he never turns
around once. Sharpest seam, briefest and hardest crests, most physical language. ⛔ **Danger line:** this is
the variation closest to the banned thesis. It is safe **only** because the sculptor dismisses the box in the
same breath — the lion is for the tide, not for the man on the boardwalk. **Section map:** Low Flat · The
First Crack · Refrain · Hold Your Face · The Sheet · Refrain · Eleven Feet · The Second Crack · Smoke Going
Sideways · Refrain · Planks.
**Ends on:** the boardwalk emptying for supper while he is still working.
