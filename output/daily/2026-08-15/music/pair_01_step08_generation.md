## THE DERIVATION
The concept is one continuous event with four bodies in it: **the woman**, **her hand**, **the room**,
and **the clock**. Each variation gives the minute to one of them. That is the only axis, and it is
internal to P01 — nothing here is transferable to another pair.

| seat | whose minute | title | feel inside 102 BPM | seam placement | refrain owner at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 THE TWO SECONDS** | the woman deciding | **Hands Kept The Count** | straight, driving, band-forward | dead centre, top of verse two | shared, colliding |
| **V2 THE DEAD FADER** | the hand with nowhere to be | **I Left My Hand On A Dead Fader** | half-time verses, heaviest, slowest-feeling | late — she holds the full band one section longer than is wise | hers, refused by no one |
| **V3 THE RETURN** | the room | **The Back Wall Sang It Back** | double-time clap over a lazy bass, antiphon maximal | early — the cut is almost the first thing that happens | the room's, entirely |
| **V4 THE ARITHMETIC** | the clock in a pair of forearms | **Sweat On The Low Stage** | fastest-feeling, percussion-forward, descarga-led | twice — a second, shorter cut before the last refrain | nobody's; cut off mid-word |

**Diversity beyond nouns:** different section counts, different refrain-ownership trajectories, different
seam placements (centre / late / early / doubled), different lead-vocal registers (leading / interior /
answering / shouting), different instrumental hierarchies (band / bass / hands / percussion).
**Shared, by design and by brief:** AABB couplets, antiphon, the room as second person, the hot ending.

## V1 · **HANDS KEPT THE COUNT** — first draft
*(the woman's minute — two seconds to decide, and she decides before she knows she has)*

**Refrain (the hummable core, tested against F5):**
> Hands — keep the count!
> Hands — keep the count!
> Hold the ground, hold the ground,
> every open palm's a sound.

Six words or fewer, one syllable dominant, no interval wider than a fifth, consonants carrying the
rhythm. Shoutable. Returnable at the wrong pitch, half a beat late, by a stranger, on second hearing.

**Draft verse one (band still up):**
> We were four bars off the turnaround,
> sweat on the neck and the room shut down.
> Front row breathing on my shoes —
> nothing left in here to lose but this.

**Draft across the seam:**
> Same mouth, same word, no help —
> *(one beat of nothing)* — hands, keep the count.

**Note to self:** the phrase that crosses the seam has to be a *sentence*, not a syllable. If the edit
lands mid-word the generator will read it as a glitch and repair it.

## V2 · **I LEFT MY HAND ON A DEAD FADER** — first draft
*(the hand's minute — the control that controls nothing, and the decision made somewhere else)*

**Refrain:**
> Palm on a dead fader, palm on a gap,
> the only arrangement in here is a clap.
> Take the low end out of my chest,
> hand it back hotter than the rest.

**Draft verse:**
> Ten years of learning where the numbers go,
> and the numbers went out with the rest of the show.
> I could move it up, I could move it down,
> it moves like a thing that moved this town.

Register: **interior, but never quiet.** Half-time verses so the body is heavier — this is the
*"downright salacious slow jam"* seat, and the heat is the point: a blackout renders as sweat.

**Note to self:** the fader is the ONLY equipment permitted in this pair. Every other noun must be a
body, a room surface, or an instrument in someone's hands.

## V3 · **THE BACK WALL SANG IT BACK** — first draft
*(the room's minute — the reflection is not a section, it is the second half of the song)*

**Refrain (call / room):**
> Sing it at the back wall —
> *(room)* — sing it at the back wall —
> it comes back flat and late and tall,
> I don't need it in tune, I need it in line.
> Give me the count and the count is mine.

**Draft antiphon block:**
> I said the one — *(they said the one)*
> I said the two — *(they said it too soon)*
> Late is fine, late is fine,
> late and loud is still in time.

The plot from F4 is fully exposed here: ragged → on top of her → **ahead of her.** By the last section
she is the one following, and the last refrain is entirely theirs. **The seam comes early** so the
whole song is lived after it — this is the variation where the cut is the premise, not the event.

## V4 · **SWEAT ON THE LOW STAGE** — first draft
*(the clock's minute — four minutes of forearm, and the fast one goes first)*

**Refrain:**
> Arms up — we are not done!
> Arms up — we are not done!
> Hold the heat, hold the heat,
> we finish this on hands and feet.

**Draft hinge (the ONE sung numeric fact):**
> About four minutes in a pair of arms —
> *(band)* — so play the fast one first.

Responded to, not recited: the number is an instruction, and the answer is a decision. Spelled in words.

**Structure note:** two cuts, not one — a second, shorter butt-cut just before the last refrain, which
is the only place in the pair where the device repeats. Justified because this is the endurance
variation: the point is that it can happen again and the arms are already tired.
