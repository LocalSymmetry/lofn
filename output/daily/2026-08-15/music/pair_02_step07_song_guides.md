## ⭐ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS, ANSWERED CONCRETELY

**WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?**
Ankle-deep in an inlet at two in the morning, on packed gravel that gives way to soft bottom about a metre out. Boots are on the stones behind her, one sock stuffed in each. She is facing the water because the copy of the light lands at her feet and looking down does not cost her anything, and she has been standing still long enough that the water has gone flat around her shins.

**WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?**
Two things, both punctual. **The cold** — bare skin in northern salt water at two in the morning goes numb from the toes up in about fifteen minutes, and numb feet on a soft bottom is how people fall over sideways in half a metre of water. **The tide** — it is coming in behind her, it does not pause while she is looking down, and the gravel bar she walked out along is the first thing it covers. She is not in danger. She is in a situation that becomes danger on a schedule, and the song's clock is that schedule.

## THE FORM SPEC — MIRRORED QUATRAINS, ABBA, CHIASMUS

Every stanza is four lines. **Lines 1 and 4 rhyme; lines 2 and 3 rhyme.** The rhyme scheme is itself a mirror — it closes back on the sound it opened with, which is the same shape as a surface with something under it.

**The chiasmus rule, applied per stanza:** line 3 restates line 2 with its clauses in the opposite order, and line 4 answers line 1 from the far side. Worked example (V3 chorus):

> I put my hand flat on you and you broke,
> I took my hand back up and you were whole,
> you were whole, I took my hand back up — the cold
> went to the wrist, and neither of us spoke.

⭐ **The form is the reflection. So the lyric describes reflection at most ONCE**, and when it does it never uses the word — it is the moment four lights are on the water and one of them is not a thing.

## THE APOSTROPHE — WHO "YOU" IS, AND HOW IT IS PROVED

**"You" is the inlet.** It is established in the **first sung word** of every variation and re-anchored at least once per section with a property only water has (wide, cold, rising, flat, holding, arriving at the shin). The listener is never addressed, never invited, never included. There is **no "we"**, no "us" that contains an audience, and no rhetorical question.

**Residue is policed in three places besides the lyric lines:**
- **EMO fields** name the singer's state only — *steady curiosity, deliberate resolve, clean astonishment, physical alertness, warm stubbornness, bright vigilance, settled contentment.* None addresses anyone.
- **SFX cues** describe events in the inlet — a foot breaking the surface, a room answering, a palm laid flat, an intake of breath on cold air. No crowd, no listener, no "the audience hears".
- **Section names** are drawn from tide and body vocabulary — *Shallows, Waterline, Ghost Return, Slack Water, Calf, Walk Out.* No section is named for an addressee.

## THE BED — B3, AND THE THREE ATTACKS ON THE PAD DEFAULT

**Standing spec:** sixty-eight beats per minute · one low sine holding the floor · a long room return · bowed cello and viola · one harp.

⚠️ **This pair is the run's highest risk for "female vocal over pretty pads."** Three structural defences, all in the prompt text, none left to hope:

1. **THE HARP IS A STRUCK WIRE, NEVER A SWEEP.** Damped with a fingertip (V1), played with a coin (V2), prepared with paper woven through the strings (V3), stopped at the soundboard and plucked near the pins (V4). One note per bar or fewer. Glissando and arpeggio are named in the **EXCLUDE** field, never negated in the style field.
2. **THE ATMOSPHERE IS A RETURN, NOT A SYNTH.** The "pad" the ear reaches for is the **convolution tail of the harp and the voice**. It cannot exist unless something is played — which means it breathes with the performance and cannot smooth into wallpaper.
3. **THE STRINGS ARE BOWED WHERE THE NOISE IS.** Sul ponticello / sul tasto, close enough that the hair-grain and rosin drag are audible texture. A drone with visible friction in it is not a pad.

**And the floor is a beat, not a hum.** The low sine is tuned a hair off the cello so the two beat against each other a couple of times a second. That flutter is the bed's pulse when nothing else is playing, and it is why there is never a long full stop for the generator to smooth.

**Kinetic defect:** the pulse is a heel shifting in shallow water — every third, fourth or fifth stroke lands a sixteenth off. A body standing in cold water does not keep time.

## FOUR GUIDES — ONE PER VARIATION

### V1 · **ANKLE DEEP AT TWO IN THE MORNING** — *the ledger*
The water as a **keeper**. It had the light before she did and it kept the second one. She stands in it, takes a reading, and leaves when the tide reaches the calf. Slowest, most static, **vocal dead centre**. Ghost return = intro + chorus, reversed, left, late. The number lands here.

### V2 · **THE TIDE CAME UP MY SHINS** — *the rising*
The water as an **arriving body**. The whole song is a measurement going up: ankle, shin, knee-adjacent, and the decision she made in advance about where she stops. **Vocal off-centre right, migrating to centre as the water rises.** The low sine glides up one semitone across the song and never comes back down — the tide, made audible, is the only element in the pair that is irreversible.

### V3 · **I PUT MY HAND FLAT ON THE WATER** — *the touch*
The water as a **surface that can be broken and reassembles**. The gesture is the song: hand down, the held light comes apart, hand up, it is whole again, and the cold is at her wrist. **Vocal centre and dry, with its own copy low-left and a beat behind.** The middle drops to the room return alone while the sine keeps walking. This is the variation where the chiasmus is most literal.

### V4 · **MY BREATH KEPT GETTING IN THE WAY** — *the steam*
The water as a **witness to something warm**. Her exhale keeps standing between her eye and the light, and it lasts longer than the light did. **Breath is the percussion** — the upbeat is an intake, and the reverb only lights up when she breathes. Warmest, most amused, closest to laughter. This is where **"steamy"** is doing its most honest work.

## WHAT WOULD MAKE ME REJECT A DRAFT
- Any line that reads as **loneliness, wistfulness or being overlooked.**
- The word **sky** or **current**. The word **dark** in a title.
- A **second** description of reflection.
- The addressee slipping to the listener at any point, including in an EMO field or a section name.
- The harp behaving like decoration; the bed behaving like a pad; a long full stop.
- More than one sung numeric fact, or any numeral written in digits inside a sung line.
