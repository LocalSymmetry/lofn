**Why these four and not others.** Three candidate angles were cut in drafting:

- ❌ *THE TIDE'S POINT OF VIEW* — the sea as narrator. **Cut: it breaks the assigned residue mechanism.**
  P05's discharge is ADDRESS TO THE WORK; a speaking sea gives the song a second voice that can answer, and
  the whole engine is that nothing answers.
- ❌ *THE MORNING AFTER* — the empty flat at dawn. **Cut: it is elegy by staging.** There is no way to shoot

## DRAFT — V1 · *The Last Ridge of Your Mane* (THE HANDS)

**Refrain (drafted first; everything else accretes toward it):**
> Hold your face for me, lion. I have got your face.
> I have had it since the light was still white and useless.
> There is one ridge left in the whole length of your mane —
> the sea can have the rest of you in the morning.

**Draft opening (rhyme lands on line 4: *morning*):**
> Your jaw took the whole afternoon and it was worth it.
> I am wetting you down again, low, from the flat pail.

**Working note.** First draft had *"and everything I know about you I learned at seven this morning"* — a
second numeric fact, and a recited one. Cut on sight per the ONE-FACT RULE. The distance is the only number
that survives in this pair.

**Hinge draft:**

> That is two of you, nose to tail, and a hand's width over.
> It will be gone in about the time it takes to pack a paw,
> so I am going to spend it here, on my knees in the sand.

⭐ The number is **responded to** — *so I am going to spend it here* — never reported.

## DRAFT — V2 · *Eleven Feet and Closing* (THE COUNTDOWN)

**Refrain:**
> Eleven feet and closing, lion, and I am in no hurry.
> You have got my hands until the sand goes soft beneath you.
> Then you get the evening and I get the walk home,
> and neither of us was ever promised a morning.

**Draft — the shrinking measure, one per section:** a boat length → eleven feet → a hand's width → the width
of the ridge → the sand goes soft. ⛔ Only **eleven feet** is a *numeric* fact; the rest are body-measures
and objects, which is how the countdown gets its shape without breaking the one-fact rule.

**Draft — the seam, where the line collapses:**
> Palm flat. Press. Lift. Again. The sound is a wet clap.
> The sea takes the first corner of the plinth and takes it clean.
> I do not look up. I have a chest to finish.

## DRAFT — V3 · *I Pack Your Paw Twice* (THE PRACTICE)

**Refrain:**
> I pack your paw twice — once for the shape and once for the hour.
> Once is for the looking and twice is for the standing up.
> Nobody on this beach has ever paid me for the second one,
> and the second one is the only one I do for you.

**Working note.** "Twice" and "once" are counting words, not numeric *facts* — a numeric fact is a
measurement of the world (eleven feet). The distinction was checked, not assumed. **Eleven feet still appears
exactly once** in this variation, at its own hinge.

**Draft — the habit stated flat, no self-congratulation:**
> This is the four hundredth mane and I could not tell you about the first one.
Cut. *(Second numeric fact; and "four hundredth" is a boast.)* Ships as:
> I could not tell you about the first one, and I have made a great many.

## DRAFT — V4 · *A Box Clicked Behind Me* (THE INTRUSION)

**Refrain:**
> A box clicked behind me and I did not turn around.
> You are not for the man on the planks, you are for the tide.
> Hold your face where I put it, in the last of the light,
> and let him have whatever the powder is giving him.

⛔ **This refrain is the pair's danger point and it is where the ban is enforced, not evaded.** The box is
present, physical, and **dismissed in the same breath**. The lion belongs to the tide. Nothing is said about
being seen, kept, or known.

**Draft — the flash, physically:**
> The powder cracks and for half a second you are white and I am white
> and the whole beach is a dropped plate, ringing —
> hold still, lion. It was only smoke.
