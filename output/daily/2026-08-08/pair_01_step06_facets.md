## §1 — ⭐ D3 DISCHARGED: THE TWO LINES, NAMED BY INTERVAL, BEFORE A WORD OF LYRIC EXISTS

> *This is the Marsalis seat's unwithdrawn condition and a step-06 hard requirement. It is answered here, in notes, before anything is written. It refines the coordinator's assignment (minor third, second A-section, neither bends); it does not reinvent it.*

**TONAL CENTRE: G.** Tempo **78 BPM**, 4/4, one fixed tempo, no key change.

**HER FIVE NOTES — the set the whole song is drawn from:**
> **G · A♭ · C · D · F**

Five notes. A **lowered second** above the tonic (A♭), a **lowered seventh** (F), **no leading tone**, and — the load-bearing absence — **no third at all.** Nothing in this set pulls upward into the tonic; every phrase can only settle sideways or fall. That is the *function* of the mode this pair's tradition supplies — a set of intervals whose whole job is to make a listener feel the absence of something that is gone. ⛔ **The function is used. The label is never written, never sung, and never enters a render field.** *(D9; Gigi seat: "take the function, credit the tradition, never print the word.")*

### LINE 1 — THE HUM ALREADY ON THE REEL
A **single sustained pitch, B♭2**, from an amplifier left switched on in the live room. Present from bar one. It **never moves, never swells, never bends, never stops.** It is the only pitched thing on the last take.

### LINE 2 — THE HUM SHE MAKES WHILE SHE WRITES
A **five-note wordless figure in the same register**, drawn from G–A♭–C–D–F. Shape: **F3 → D3 → C3 → A♭2 → G2**, descending, returning up to C3 to start again. Closed mouth, half-breath, the thing a person does while their hand is busy.

### ⭐ THE INTERVAL, AND WHY THE UNISON IS IMPOSSIBLE
**B♭ is not in her set.** B♭ is the **third her scale does not have** — the one note that would tell a listener whether her tune is major or minor. She never sings it. It is already in the room. She does not hear it.

> **THE CROSSING — second A-section, bar 12, once per song and only once.**
> Her figure steps **C3 → A♭2 → G2** while the amplifier holds **B♭2**.
> She passes from **above** its pitch to **below** its pitch **without ever sounding it**, and comes to rest a **MINOR THIRD BELOW** it (G2 against B♭2).
> **Neither bends. No unison — and no unison is available**, because the note between them is the one already on the reel.

**Entering side by side, easily confused:** both are low, wordless, quiet, in the same octave, and both are in the mix from bar one. For the first pass of the form a listener genuinely cannot say which hum is hers and which is the room's. That confusion is the setup; the crossing is the payment.

**⛔ Executed in the notes and in the lyric, never in the mix** (L22 THE GRAIN LAW). No pan move, no automation, no reverb change carries this. In V2 a person in the lyric mistakes one hum for the other out loud.

**⭐ Accretive, because subtractive specs get smoothed.** At the crossing bar the arrangement **adds** — vibraphone doubles her figure an octave up as it passes under — rather than removing anything. *(2026-08-07 finding: accretive specs survive the generator.)*

**⭐ THE GRAIN-LAW TRANSLATION (the one production decision that decides whether this survives).** In the fiction, Line 1 is an amplifier hum. **In the render field it is specified as a held combo-organ note — tuned, quiet, unchanging.** A generator smooths an untuned drone (step04 bans them by name); it will happily hold an organ note for four minutes. Same sound, same job, written the way the machine will honour.

## §2 — WHAT THE SONG IS, IN ONE BREATH

A woman sits in a van with the doors shut and the engine off at the end of a fourteen-hour day, logging takes. The last thing on the reel was rolled for one reason: to mark where the reel stopped. Nobody played. It is the room — the air coming on, a chair, a held cough — and it is the only thing on the reel with the room in it. **She writes N.G. beside it and closes the book.** She is right to. Anybody would.

**The wince (D11):** the listener has just spent the take *inside* that room, and then watches her write it off — seven times, because the refrain returns.
**The withholding (D1):** she may notice the hum is stuck in her teeth. **She may never find out what it means.** Nobody finds out (D4).
**The register (D2):** competent, mid-task, faintly irritated, going home. ⛔ Not moved. Not sad. **Finishing up.**
