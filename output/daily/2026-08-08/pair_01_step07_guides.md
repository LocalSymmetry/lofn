## GUIDE 1 — **THE GAP** *(facets F1 + F4 · drives V1, V3)*

**Storytelling techniques.** ⭐ **Dramatic irony held to the last bar** — the audience is given a fact the narrator is never given, and the song's only job is to keep her from getting it. Second: **the withheld reveal that never arrives**; every convention in a listener's body says the last A-section is where she understands, and she does not, and the song stops anyway.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin in flat competence — *Composure* with a thread of *Irritation*, the specific weather of somebody at the end of a fourteen-hour day who has four columns left. It does not rise. It does not fall. What moves is the **listener's** emotion: *Absorption* while the room plays, then *Dismay* and *Recognition* the instant the letters land on it. ⛔ Never once available to her.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Vibraphone carries the top with the motor off — no shimmer tremolo, just struck bar and decay. Upright bass in halves, felt more than heard. Brushes on a snare with the snares loose. A washed-out combo organ holds one low note the entire song. A breezy acoustic guitar strums on two and four, close, unamplified.
**Production & effects.** One room the size of a van, doors shut, engine off. Kit audible in the vocal mic on purpose. Traffic outside the panel. ⛔ No hiss, no crackle, no found-recording framing. Warmth is the van's, not a plug-in's.
**Structure & dynamics.** ~4:45. AABA 32-bar stated three times: head, wordless hook, head, wordless hook, final head, tag. The take itself lives in the fourth A-section, where the arrangement thins to organ + brushes and the singer half-speaks. **It thickens by addition at the crossing, never by gain.**
**Rhyming schemes.** A-sections: **ABCBDD** with the last two lines a fixed tag — end-rhyme locked to the long *-ee* of *knee / N.G.* so the wince rhymes with the object. B-sections: **free, unrhymed, deliberately** — the release is where the sound-return stops, which makes the A-section's return audible as return. *(Removal named, and what replaces it named: consonance on hard /k/ — can, columns, keys, locks, clock.)*
**Poetic devices.** **Epistrophe** (every A-section ends on the same two lines) and **polyptoton** (*put down / put back / puts it down*, the same verb doing three different jobs). Plus **asyndeton** in the take description — no conjunctions, the sounds arrive in causal order and nothing joins them.
**Hook techniques.** (1) The five-note closed-mouth hum, unaccompanied for the first two bars so the ear grabs it before the band tells it what it is. (2) The same figure doubled by vibraphone an octave up at the crossing only — one occurrence, so it reads as an event. (3) The hum leaking into the verses as a call-and-response under the written line, in parentheses, so the listener learns it is involuntary before she does.
**Narrative & thematic seed.** First person, present tense, one body, one van, one book. **The listener is the defendant, not the mourner.**
**Central aspect.** The room is built with such care that writing it off feels like an injury — and it isn't one, and that is worse.
**Emotional tone signals.** Unmoved. Tired. Good at this.
**Vocal techniques.** Low female contralto, conversational, half-spoken on the bottom three notes but always pitched. No belt. No vibrato. Consonants soft, ends of lines dropped.

## GUIDE 2 — **THE TWO HUMS** *(facets F2 + F10 · drives every variation; it is the pair's spine)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **Mistaken identity, staged in sound rather than plot** — two things a listener cannot tell apart, then one moment that proves they were never the same. Second: **the false witness**, a character who hears correctly and reports wrongly *(V2: someone says "you've got a hum on that" and she thinks he means her)*.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Neutral to neutral. The crossing carries **no** emotional swell; its whole power is that nothing happens. ⛔ If the arrangement gets bigger there, the pair's argument is deleted.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Line 1 is a **held combo-organ note, low, tuned, unchanging from first bar to last** — quiet enough to be furniture, present enough that its absence would be noticed. Line 2 is the voice, wordless, in the same octave. Vibraphone and guitar stay out of that octave entirely so the two lines have it to themselves.
**Production & effects.** ⛔ **Nothing about this may live in the mix.** No pan, no automation, no filter sweep, no reverb change. If a listener could only hear the crossing by watching a meter, it did not happen. Both hums sit centre, at the same distance, on purpose — ⭐ *two distances in one frame with no depth cue between them.*
**Structure & dynamics.** The crossing is at **bar 12 of the second A-section of the second pass**, once. The first pass exists to make the two indistinguishable; the crossing is the payment; the third pass is after. ⛔ **One crossing. Not two.**
**Rhyming schemes.** The crossing section's rhyme **breaks mid-section** — the couplet that should close does not, and the fixed tag arrives anyway, so return covers the hole. Elsewhere the A-scheme holds.
**Poetic devices.** **Antanaclasis** on *hum* (a sound she makes / a sound a machine makes / a defect an engineer reports) and **epizeuxis** on *same* — *same sort of low, same sort of nothing*.
**Hook techniques.** (1) State the figure before the first word of the song, unaccompanied, so it is established as material and not as decoration. (2) At the crossing, **add** a vibraphone octave rather than removing anything — accretive specs survive the generator, subtractive ones get smoothed. (3) Restate the figure after the last word, alone, with the held organ note still under it and not moving.
**Narrative seed.** The thing already in the room is the note her tune does not contain. She never sings it. She does not hear it. **Nobody tells her.**
**Key sequences.** Her figure descends past the held note and settles a minor third below it and holds; the held note does not acknowledge that anything happened, because nothing did.
**Special effects & innovations.** The unison is not forbidden — it is **unavailable**, because the note is not in her set. A rule the generator cannot break by accident is worth more than a rule it is told not to break.
**Emotional tone signals.** Two things that sound the same and are not, in a van, at night, while somebody does paperwork.

## GUIDE 3 — **THE TRADE NOUN** *(facets F3 + F8 · drives V1, V2)*

**Storytelling techniques.** ⭐ **Total objective correlative** — every feeling in this song is carried by an object or an action and never by an adjective. Second: **procedural narration**, the story told as the sequence of a job being finished, where the job's own order supplies the plot.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Housekeeping. Mid-task. Faintly irritated. ⛔ **Reverence is the failure mode; a hushed opening is this pair's death.** The first line should sound like somebody sitting down, not like somebody beginning a song.
**Instrumentation & texture.** The guitar and brushes do the diction's work: short, dry attacks, nothing sustained, nothing sighing. Bass walks in halves and stops when she stops.
**Production & effects.** Close but not clinical. Breath audible. Pen audible if the room allows it.
**Structure & dynamics.** One clipped line per bar. That is the discipline that produces the mean-words-per-line the doctrine wants, and it is also simply how a tired person talks.
**Rhyming schemes.** A-sections **ABCBDD**; the tag's *N.G. / knee* rhyme is the pair's only sung "clever" moment and it is a shop-talk accident, not a joke.
**Poetic devices.** **Alliteration on hard stops** — *column, can, keys, clock, cough, cases, car park* — carrying the sound-density the doctrine measures. **Anaphora** at the top of the head: *Door shut. Engine off. Book open.*
**Hook techniques.** (1) The vocable hook exists *because* the diction refuses metaphor: with no figurative language available, the only place feeling can go is a wordless sound. (2) The tag's flat statement (*there's nothing on it*) is delivered identically every time, so the seventh delivery lands on a listener who by then disagrees. (3) A shop abbreviation as the hook's payload: two letters, spoken as letters.
**Narrative seed.** ⛔ **No enumeration survives.** The enforceable test is applied to every section: **reorder its lines; if nothing is lost, it is a list and it is rewritten.** The three sounds on the take are causal — the air comes on because it is on a timer, he shifts because it came on, he coughs because he had been holding it since the light went red.
**Lyric structure.** Five moving lines then a three-line fixed tag, per A-section. B-sections five lines, unrhymed, one of them the reason.
**Central aspect.** Nothing in this song is *like* anything. Everything in it *is* something.
**Vocal techniques.** Consonant-first delivery; the singer bites the front of words and lets the ends go, the way people talk when they are writing at the same time.

## GUIDE 4 — **THE VAN** *(facets F7 + F5 · drives V2, V4)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **The interrupted scene** — a second person enters and the task does not stop, so the dialogue has to happen over the top of the writing. Second: **the room as the second character**, given entrances and exits but never a feeling.
**Mood & emotional arc.** *Ennui* over *Composure*, one thread of *Warmth* when somebody comes to the door and one of *Impatience* when they will not go. ⛔ Not lonely. She likes them fine. She wants to finish.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Vibraphone with the fan off, upright bass, brushes with the snares loose, a **breezy acoustic guitar**, and the washed-out combo organ holding its one note. The whole band the size of the inside of a van.
**Production & effects.** ⭐ Room, not booth. **Bleed named and welcomed** — kit in the vocal mic, vocal in the room mics. **Entries by instrument, not by grid**: somebody comes in a beat late because they heard a cue, not because a bar started. One continuous take implied: no per-section re-amping, no section-boundary reverb changes. Traffic outside; when the door opens, the outside gets louder for four bars and then does not.
**Structure & dynamics.** ~4:30–5:00. Builds once, settles back. ⛔ No riser, no drop, no build-into-final-chorus by level. It gets bigger by **addition** and smaller by **subtraction of players**, never by gain.
**Rhyming schemes.** Same A-scheme; the visitor's lines fall **outside** the rhyme so his speech sounds like speech against her sung column.
**Poetic devices.** **Zeugma** (*he holds the door and the whole conversation*) — used once and only once, because this diction does not decorate. **Consonance** on /k/ and /t/ throughout the van's furniture.
**Hook techniques.** (1) Have the visitor hear the hook and misattribute it — the hook becomes a plot event. (2) Let the hook stop mid-figure when she is interrupted and resume from the same note afterwards. (3) End the section with the hook alone through the closing door.
**Narrative seed.** Two people, one of them working. The conversation is ordinary and warm and she keeps writing through it, and neither of them is rude.
**Musical style & effects.** Small, soft, close walls; nothing shiny; nothing wide.
**Instrumentation & tone.** ⭐ *Smoke without a fire* — the sound of a space that has been used all day. ⭐ *The hour the light goes flat* — no contrast anywhere, and nothing dramatic about that.
**Emotional tone signals.** The end of a shift, with company, which makes it longer.

## GUIDE 5 — **THE RETURN** *(facets F6 + F9 · drives V3, V4)*

**Storytelling techniques.** ⭐ **The refrain as verdict** — the same three lines, byte-identical, arriving seven times, so the *listener's* relationship to them changes while the singer's does not. Second: **the completed gesture as an ending**, borrowed from stage practice: the curtain falls on an action finished, not on a question answered.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Level throughout. The return is the only development, and it develops in the audience.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Each return of the tag brings **one more instrument than the last** and never more volume — the accretive shape the generator honours. By the seventh, everyone is playing, and it is not loud.
**Production & effects.** The held organ note is what makes the returns feel like the same place. It never moves; therefore the room never moves; therefore return is legible.
**Structure & dynamics.** The final A-section carries the **completed physical act**: the pen goes back in the spine, the book shuts, it goes in the door pocket, she gets out. ⛔ **The song does not end with the object being kept, and no last line hints that anyone will ever know.** The gesture finishes; the judgement stays open.
**Rhyming schemes.** ⭐ The fixed tag is **byte-identical and requires no defence and no note.** It is not pre-emptively mutated. It is not justified. Elsewhere, a strict end-rhyme return inside a four-line window, carried by short words with hard endings.
**Poetic devices.** **Epistrophe** (the tag) and **anadiplosis** across the A/B seam — the B-section opens on the word the A-section closed on.
**Hook techniques.** (1) State the wordless hook twice as a full section so it earns the word "hook." (2) Thread it into the A-sections as parenthetical answers so it is present far more often than it is announced. (3) ⭐ **Make the words carry the return too** — the tag must clear the return floors *lexically*, with the hum removed, or the song has satisfied an instrument rather than a doctrine.
**Narrative seed.** The same act, done again, correctly, and then again.
**Lyric structure.** 7 A-sections × 8 lines · 2 B-sections × 5 lines · 2 hook sections × 4 vocable lines · a 4-line outro. **Target 80–95 sung lines**, inside the 78–110 band and away from both boundaries.
**Special innovations.** The tag's second line is the wince and its third line is the title. Both return unchanged. ⭐ **A byte-identical refrain is correct craft.**

## GUIDE 6 — ⭐ **WILD-CARD: THE HANDWRITING** *(cross-disciplinary · drives V1 primarily, and the meter of all four)*

**The cross-disciplinary import.** The song's meter is taken not from a musical form but from **penmanship under fatigue**: the physical rhythm of filling a ruled column at the end of a long day. Pen down, three or four strokes, lift, move right, pen down. That is a **short-short-short-lift** figure, and it is the phrase-length of every line in this song. A tired hand writes in shorter bursts and rests longer between them — so the lines get slightly shorter as the song proceeds while the rests get slightly longer, and the tempo never changes. The listener feels the day's length without being told it.
**A second import, from the visual arts.** A ledger page is a **grid with one column that is mostly the same mark**. Look at any working log and the eye goes straight to the repeated entry. That is the visual fact this song's refrain is built from: repetition is not monotony on a page like that, it is **the shape of the document**, and the one row that is different is only legible because of it. ⭐ **But the run's own rule holds: the list is the subject and never the form.** The song does not become a ledger. It borrows the ledger's *rhythm* and refuses its *shape*.
**Mood & emotional arc.** The handwriting's own arc: neat at the top of the page, still legible at the bottom, faster near the end because she can see the end.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Brushes take the penmanship figure directly — three strokes and a lift, on the snare head. The upright bass plays only where the hand rests. Vibraphone marks the line-ends. ⭐ *The crossing is the only bar where the hand keeps writing and the music does something else.*
**Production & effects.** If the room allows it, the pen is audible. If it does not, the brushes are the pen and nobody needs to be told.
**Structure & dynamics.** Unchanged AABA; the wild-card is a **meter** decision, not a form decision, so it costs the pair nothing structurally and is measurable in the words-per-line figure.
**Rhyming schemes.** Short lines force **more line-ends per minute**, which raises the rhyme-return density without adding a single ornament. The doctrine's floors are met by the *hand*, not by decoration.
**Poetic devices.** **Caesura** inside almost every line — *Door shut. Engine off.* — and **homoioteleuton** on the -ing of the working verbs (*writing, logging, holding, sitting*), which is what a shift sounds like from inside.
**Hook techniques.** (1) The hum is what a hand-busy mouth does; keep it strictly at the pitch a person hums at when they are not performing. (2) Let one hummed phrase be cut off mid-figure by a line-end, exactly as humming is cut off by concentration. (3) The final hum continues after the last written word, because the hand has stopped and the mouth has not.
**Narrative seed.** ⭐ **The song is the handwriting.** Not a song about a woman writing — the *rhythm* of the writing, with words on it.
**Central aspect.** A form imported from the body of the task rather than from the genre. If it works, the pair gets its meter for free and the doctrine's floors come with it.
**Self-critique of this wild-card.** It is the riskiest of the six, and its risk is specific: **penmanship rhythm is invisible to a text gate and probably invisible to the generator too.** A model reading the prompt will honour "short clipped lines" and will not honour "the hand rests here." What survives is the *line lengths*, which is real but is the cheap half. **Recorded so a render audit knows what to listen for and what to expect to be missing.**
