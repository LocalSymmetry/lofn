## G1 · THE MOUTH THAT DOES NOT SING
**Covers F1, F2.**

**Storytelling techniques.** Two, both borrowed from outside song. The first is the **ballad burden** as the tradition actually built it — a fixed block of words that returns between stanzas and belongs to nobody in the story; here it is dragged one step further, because the burden is not the singer's and is not even in her century. The second is the **procedural monologue** of stage naturalism: a person talking through a task they have done a thousand times, in which the emotional content is entirely in what is *not* said. **Mood & emotional arc:** it begins in Composure with a thread of Ennui — a body at the end of a shift, ladder-cold hands, light going flat — descends through Impatience at the middle stanzas, and arrives at Numbness, not sorrow. The listener's arc is the inverse and the two never touch. **Instrumentation & texture:** the burden must be surrounded by air, not by pad. When it enters, the melody stops and only the wire tone and the plate remain under it; nothing harmonises with speech, ever. **Production & effects:** recorded outdoors in the same open air as everything else; the burden sits a half step left of centre so the ear can place it as *another room* without a hard pan. **Structure & dynamics:** the burden's first entry is partial and its later entries are complete — the audience learns the form by hearing it fill in, the way you learn a form by filling one in. **Rhyming schemes:** the burden's twelve lines rhyme in adjacent pairs on *identical word-endings* (`time/time`, `tin/tin`, `off/off`, `put/put`) — a form-filler's rhyme, the rhyme of a rubber stamp, never a lyric rhyme. Sung stanzas take a loose ABCB with an **epistrophe**: every stanza closes on the same line. **Poetic devices:** epistrophe (the returning stanza-final line) and **antimetabole** in the burden's spine — *"It is not a thing you mean. / It is a thing you put."* **Hook techniques:** (i) the phrase **"Nothing further"** is spoken and never sung, in any song, by anyone — the hook is a hole in the melody; (ii) the burden's arrival is announced by the melody *stopping*, so the ear reaches for the next sung line and gets speech; (iii) the burden's last entry runs directly into the next entry with no stanza between, so the listener notices the sung side has run out. **Narrative & thematic:** first person present, singular, unnamed. **Central aspect of the concept:** the words repeat because the words are what you say when you finish — **not because anything connected.** **Vocal techniques:** guttural, level, unpitched, deliberate, throat-forward; audible dentals; no rise at line ends. **Emotional tone signals:** the burden is faintly bored and it is the least moved thing in the song.

⛔ **F-C, written into the guide, not left as advice.** The two voices may **never share a referent.** The sung side has a mast, a field, a wire, a ledger on a ledge. The spoken side has a stair, a strip light, a tin, a coat. Neither mentions the other's objects. **Neither mentions the other.** The later mouth has not read anything, is not looking for anything, and is finishing an unrelated shift. **Nobody is vindicated and nobody is told.**

## G2 · TWO METALS, ONE BAND
**Covers F3.**

**Storytelling techniques.** **Dramatic irony delivered by acoustics** — the audience hears a relationship between two sounds that the character hears only as a nuisance — and **Chekhov's object refused**: the plate is placed early, described precisely, and when it finally does the significant thing, the character declines to notice. **Mood & emotional arc:** unchanged from first second to last, on purpose; the only thing that moves is the plate's pitch. **Instrumentation & texture:** the whole song lives in one low band around **A2**. A steel guy-wire, wind-driven, holds A2 without wavering — bowed metal, a real pitch with slow beating in it, **never an untuned drone**. Against it, a steel plate struck with a screwdriver shank enters a **semitone above at B♭2**; the two beat, and for the opening minute a listener cannot separate them. A somatic bass sits under the plain melody, felt more than heard, and never plays the crossing note. Brushed tom or snare lands a hair off the wire's pulse so nothing ever squares to a grid — the kinetic defect is in the *timing*, not the volume. **Production & effects:** open air, no walls, decays ending at the object; the wind is a continuous musical presence and **not a bed**. Nothing is added at the crossing — the crossing is a pitch event, not a mix event. **Structure & dynamics:** the plate steps down once per stanza. B♭2 through the first two stanzas; **A2 for exactly one strike at the third stanza; G♯2 from the next strike onward.** It never returns to B♭2. **Rhyming schemes:** the crossing stanza is the shortest-lined in the song — four lines, five to eight words each, no rhyme at all except the returning final line. The rhyme thins where the pitch matters. **Poetic devices:** **polyptoton** at the crossing (*"The plate rings what the wire rings"*) and **caesura** — the crossing line is broken by a full stop mid-line so the two clauses sit either side of a gap. **Hook techniques:** (i) the struck plate itself is the hook, because it is the only element that changes; (ii) the beating between the two metals is a slow tremolo the ear tries to resolve and cannot; (iii) at the crossing the plate is struck **twice** — once on the note, once below it — so the ear gets the answer immediately and is denied the pause it wants. **Narrative & thematic:** she works against a pitch all day and it is a fact about her afternoon, not about her life. **Key sequences:** stanza three, four lines, one strike on A2 between them. **Musical style and effects:** tuned steel, bowed low metal, real wind, somatic bass, brushed kit off-grid.

⛔ **L22, obeyed in the specification.** Both crossing elements are **tuned and musical**; neither is a drone and neither is hard-panned. The wire has a pitch and beats. The plate has a pitch and moves. **A generator asked for two tuned low metals will render two tuned low metals; a generator asked for "wind" and "a drone" renders ambience and smooths it.**

## G3 · THE REGISTER OF SOMEONE FINISHING UP
**Covers F4, F5.**

**Storytelling techniques.** **Free indirect competence** — the narration is exactly as intelligent as the worker and no more, so the listener's extra knowledge has nowhere to hide except in the listener — and **the withheld reaction**, in which the emotionally loaded fact is stated in the same tone as the tool inventory. **Mood & emotional arc:** Composure and Ennui, with Apathy (Detachment) as the floor and one thread of Impatience where the pen is cold. It does not resolve, brighten or darken; the singer leaves in the same state she arrived in. The **listener's** arc is Dismay arriving at Recognition, and it is never available to her. **Instrumentation & texture:** the plain melody is genuinely plain — stepwise, small compass, mostly conjunct, nothing above a fifth from the tonic — because an ornamented line in this register reads as feeling. The octave-down self-double thickens the voice without adding sentiment. **Production & effects:** the voice is close but the room is huge and open; breath is audible before every phrase because the body is doing something. No compression that flatters. **Structure & dynamics:** one level of loudness from first second to last. Nothing swells at the last stanza. **Rhyming schemes:** stanza-internal rhyme is deliberately weak — the returning stanza-final line carries the sung side's return, and inside the stanzas the ear gets consonant repetition rather than end rhyme, so the language sounds like speech and measures as craft. **Poetic devices:** **asyndeton** throughout — clauses set down beside one another with no conjunctions, the grammar of a job sheet — and **anaphora** in the closing sequence, where three lines begin *"The wire…"*, *"The light…"*, *"Down the…"* **Hook techniques:** (i) the returning stanza-final line, said four times in four different physical situations so the words stay the same and the meaning does not; (ii) the two-line fourth stanza, which is the shortest and flattest thing in the song and lands hardest because everything around it has been longer; (iii) **the wince**, delivered as a compliment to a form: *"Short line. Correct line."* — the singer approving of her own paperwork while the listener hears what has just been closed. **Narrative & thematic:** ⭐ **the setting-aside is a skill.** She is good at this. She is not a fool, she is not a victim, and the song does not scold her, pity her, or catch her out. **Emotional tone signals:** faintly irritated at the cold, not at the job. **Central aspect of the concept:** *"nothing further"* is **accurate** — the song agrees with her, and that is the wince.

## G4 · FURNITURE, NOT RELIC
**Covers F6.**

**Storytelling techniques.** **Deliberate anti-elegy** — every place the form invites a lament, a measurement is inserted instead — and **the inventory refused**, in which a room full of nameable old things is described only by the parts a working hand touches. **Mood & emotional arc:** flat light with no contrast, which is a *colour* rather than a *mood*; the emotional weight comes from duration, not from timbre. **Instrumentation & texture:** ⛔ nothing in the palette may be a media artefact. **No tape hiss, no vinyl crackle, no cassette warble, no old-radio filter, no telephone EQ, no "found recording" framing, no sepia treatment.** Analogue warmth here is a property of the *field* — a real space with real air in it — and is never a texture laid on top. **Production & effects:** if it sounds like it was recovered, it is wrong; it must sound like it is happening. **Structure & dynamics:** no ritardando, no fade-out, no final swell — the song stops when the job stops. **Rhyming schemes:** unchanged; the anti-nostalgia work is lexical, not prosodic. **Poetic devices:** **metonymy of the working hand** (the pen, the tin, the shank, the ladder stand in for the whole trade) and **litotes** in the burden's closing — *"Then home. Nothing further."* **Hook techniques:** (i) the book's years of daily entries stated once, in one flat observation, and never returned to; (ii) the plate described by its manufacture — *stamped, not painted* — which is a fact about tooling, not about age; (iii) the pad, which is the only thing that will still be there and is not consoling in the slightest. **Narrative & thematic:** ⭐ **unspent, not sepia.** The feeling is not *the past was better*. It is **there was a future in that book and nobody took it — and nobody is going to.** The singer says this herself, flatly, in the second stanza of one variation: *"Nobody has asked for this book. / Nobody is going to."* That is the anti-nostalgia clause and the vindication ban in the same breath, said by the person it is about, without pathos. **Special effects and innovations:** none. **The absence of a production trick is the point of this guide.**

## G5 · SHRINK AND ACCRETE
**Covers F7, F8.**

**Storytelling techniques.** **Diminishing stanza** as a formal argument — the sung account of the job gets shorter because there is less of the job left — and **the relay that never happens**, in which a second voice takes over the song's real estate without ever taking over its story. **Mood & emotional arc:** the emotional temperature is constant; only the *ratio* changes. **Instrumentation & texture:** because the sung side subtracts, everything else must add — the strikes come closer together, the octave-down double stays once it arrives, and the somatic bass enters late and does not leave. **Nothing is ever removed to create an effect.** **Production & effects:** no section-boundary reverb changes, no filter sweeps, no risers into anything. **Structure & dynamics:** stanzas of **8, 6, 4, 2** lines. Between them a burden that arrives partial and fills in: five lines, then eight, then twelve, then twelve, then twelve. **Total sung material per section goes up, never down**, and the last physical event in the song is the largest and most concrete. ⚠️ **The predicted failure is that a generator reads a shrinking sung line-count as a fade**; the answer is that the terminal section is the loudest concrete act in the piece and the plate is still being struck under it. **Rhyming schemes:** the four stanzas hold ABCB loosely; the two-line stanza is a single closed couplet ending on the returning line. **Poetic devices:** **diacope** (a word returning after intervening material — *plate … plate*, *note … note*) and **epizeuxis** in the burden's terminal clause. **Hook techniques:** (i) the moment the sung side runs out and two burdens run back to back; (ii) the two-line stanza, arriving after the longest burden, which reads as a person interrupting a form to say one true thing; (iii) the last struck plate, unaccompanied. **Narrative & thematic:** ⛔ **no enumeration anywhere.** The stanzas are four positions in one continuous physical job — arrival and ladder; the book and the writing; the fixings and the crossing; the last screw — and **they cannot be reordered without breaking the sequence of a task.** That is the F-A test, applied by construction rather than by inspection. **Key sequences:** the third stanza, where the shortest sung block and the first complete burden sit either side of the crossing. **Lyric structure:** short declarative lines, five to eight words, hard stops, ±2 syllables inside any section.

## G6 · WILD CARD · THE PAPERWEIGHT
**Covers F9, plus the cross-disciplinary limb the step file asks for.**

**Cross-disciplinary source.** Not music: **the engineering drawing.** A general-arrangement drawing is a portrait of a thing drawn entirely in dimensions, and the only feeling in it is in what the draughtsman chose to dimension. Everything else — colour, weather, intention, whether anyone will ever build it — is outside the frame and is not missing, because a drawing has no place to put it. **That is the song's grammar.** The second source is **architectural**: a concrete pad is a building that consists entirely of its foundation, and it outlasts everything bolted to it while meaning nothing at all. **Storytelling techniques.** **Dimensioning instead of describing** (chest height; the last page; the width of a ruled box) and **the last image as a use, not a symbol.** **Mood & emotional arc:** unchanged; the wildcard is a *method*, not a mood. **Instrumentation & texture:** one new thing this guide licenses — the **paper** itself, close-miked: a nib dragging, a page turning, a hardback spine closing. It is percussion and it is tuned to nothing, which is why it belongs to the hand and not to the crossing. **Production & effects:** paper close, field wide, and the two distances audible in one frame with no depth cue between them. **Structure & dynamics:** unchanged. **Rhyming schemes and the measured return — the whole point of this guide:** `vault/gates.yaml` binds `rhyme_return ≥ 0.30`, `line_return ≥ 0.20`, `alliteration_per_100w ≥ 11.0`, `mean_words_per_line ≤ 7.5`. **Removal of rhyme is a debt.** This pair pays it in three currencies: the burden's stamped adjacent rhymes; the stanza-final epistrophe; and a consonant field built out of trade nouns that alliterate naturally — *plate, pin, post, box, book, ballast, bolt, ledge, lid, lamp, latch, track, tin, tray*. ⭐ **And because the return vehicle is a spoken burden, the measurement is reported twice** — once for the whole field and once with every burden line removed — because the instrument cannot tell *"the song returns"* from *"one block of speech repeats."* **A byte-identical burden is correct craft, needs no defence and gets none here.** **Hook techniques:** (i) the book put down on the plate **so the wind does not take it** — a paperweight, not a memorial, and the last physical act in the song; (ii) the pen warmed in a hand before it will write; (iii) the chain going onto the gate post, which is the sound of a field being closed and not of anything ending. **Narrative & thematic:** ⛔ **the last image must be a use.** The moment the book on the plate reads as a gesture rather than as a solution to wind, the pair has become reverent and it is a repair.

## §1 — MEDIUM PANEL REVIEW *(the supplied seats, with genuine dissent)*

**THE HARD SECTION (after Brenneck):** One room, everyone hearing everyone, and here the room is a field — **which is the hardest version, because a field gives you nothing back.** Good. Just do not let anyone add a tail to make it feel like a place. **The wind is the room.**

**THE GLOBAL TRIO (after Speer), one objection:** Everybody in these guides is talking about the worker. **The plate has been on that box for decades and is completely indifferent to coming off.** There is a warmth available in the object's indifference and G4 nearly finds it and then flinches into the pad. → **Partly taken.** Folded into V3, where the pad is the thing that does not care and is stated as a fact of concrete. Not taken further, because warmth toward the object is one step from elegy for the object, which is D10.

**⚠️ THE MAXIMALIST (after Kamasi), still dissenting:** G5 is the only guide that earns its restraint, because it adds. G3 and G4 are both *lists of things not to do.* **A guide made of prohibitions produces a cautious song.** → **Conceded, and it is a real defect of G4.** G4 is a ban list wearing a paragraph. Its one positive instruction — *the last image must be a use* — is doing all the work.

**THE BIG-BAND TRANSLATOR (after Gershon):** The chart is legible: A2 held, B♭2 struck, A2 once, G♯2 after. **Anyone could play this in a room this afternoon.** That is the test I care about and it passes.

**THE SMALL ROOM (after Boilen):** Could it be sung at a desk, unamplified? — **The stanzas, yes.** A person could say these lines at a desk and you would understand the whole situation in thirty seconds: a plate, a book, a hand, a field. **The burden needs a second person, which is one more than a desk.** That is a genuine cost of the form and I am recording it rather than arguing it away.
