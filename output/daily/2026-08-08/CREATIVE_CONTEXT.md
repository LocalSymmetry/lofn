### THE BRIDGED SEATS

**B1. THE LOST-FUTURES CRITIC** — *after Mark Fisher (1968–2017)* — whose published work on hauntology describes culture haunted by futures that never arrived.

**TAXONOMIST OF NOT (after Linnaeus):** I am the man in the next chair to yours. **CATALOGUER (after Dewey):** Then I will say the harder half. **LOST-FUTURES CRITIC (after Fisher):** ⭐ And that is why this is not a nostalgia record, if it is done correctly. **⚠️ LATE-CAPITALISM AUDITOR (after Jameson):** No. Or rather: not yet, and I am going to be difficult about the gap between those.

It might not. Usually it does not. Messier is a survivorship artefact: **you know his name precisely because his discard pile happened to be useful.** The ten thousand people whose discard piles were just discard piles are not in the picture, and a record built on this shape flatters everyone and asks nothing.

**RETROMANIA CRITIC (after Reynolds):** He is right, and there is a second version of it that is more dangerous because it is aesthetic. **CATALOGUER:** So the song is not "poor man, unrecognised."

**LATE-CAPITALISM AUDITOR:** *Wait.* **That version I cannot dismiss**, and I should say why the difference matters rather than just conceding.

The comfortable version is *the past will vindicate me*. It costs nothing and asks nothing. The version just proposed is *you are doing it now, and the person or thing you are setting aside will not be told.* **That one has a cost, it is falsifiable in a single afternoon, and it implicates the listener rather than consoling them.** I withdraw the flattery objection **against that version only.**

⛔ **And I want it as a gate, because the comfortable version will grow back:**

**TAXONOMIST OF NOT:** ⭐ And it answers the objection carried forward from the baseline. **The Fela seat asked who is keeping the other list, now, about someone alive.** With the ban in place, the answer is not an accusation — **it is the listener.** Every person hearing this has a list of what does not count, and they are adding to it today, and they are not going to be told either.

### THE REFLECTED DEBATE

**ANTI-ARCHIVIST (after Kondo):** *Working from the documented method —* I want to say something the last two configurations have made almost unsayable. **The man was right to set those things aside.** He was hunting comets. The smudges were not comets. Discarding was correct, it was skilled, and it was how he got his work done. This room keeps treating his exclusions as a tragic error. They were **competence.**

**ORAL TRADITION (after Hampâté Bâ):** And I will add the sharper form. You are all in love with a piece of paper. Where I come from, the saying is that when an elder dies a library burns — but the point of that saying is that **the library was alive until then.** ⭐ A thing written down is a thing that has stopped changing. Messier's list survived **because it was dead.** That is not a triumph. That is what survival costs.

**DESTROYER OF DOCUMENTS (after Tinguely):** My machines were built to destroy themselves in front of an audience, and the ones that failed to destroy themselves properly are the ones in museums now — which is the joke and the failure. **The by-product outliving the work is not a happy accident. It is the museum winning.**

**PRESENT-TENSE FLOOR (after Summer):** Can I say the audience thing? Nobody puts on a song to feel sorry for a filing clerk in 1774. **They put on a song because it is happening to them.** Every one of these ideas is about a man who is finished. Where is the person who is *in it*, tonight, who has not stopped? If the record is about someone who is over, the listener is a tourist.

**IMPROVISER (after Taylor):** Then the form is wrong, not just the tense. You have built six songs about a document. **A song is not a document — it is an event that happens in time and then stops.** If these songs describe a list, they are the wrong shape. If they *are* the moment of setting something down, they are the right shape.

**⚠️ PRESERVATIONIST (after Prelinger):** Hold on, all of you. **I am the skeptic of this configuration and I am going to defend the archive against my own room, because the reflection has overshot.**

My entire career is the material everyone agreed was worthless — industrial films, driver's-ed reels, sponsored shorts. The consensus was unanimous that this was garbage. **The consensus was wrong**, and it was wrong in the specific way this run is about: nobody throws away what they *know* is precious. They throw away what does not fit the category they are working in.

But — *actually, wait.* **The reflection has given us something my side cannot produce, and I should hand it over rather than defend my turf.**

Every seat over there is right that **the paper is not the song.** I keep film cans; I have never once believed a film can is a story. And the Summer seat is right about the audience in a way that is not sentimental: **a listener will not enter a song through a dead man.**

⭐ **So here is what I think survives both rooms.** The archive matters — *and* the song cannot be about the archive. **The song has to be the fifteen seconds where a living person decides.** Set the thing down, do not throw it away, do not look at it again, go home. **That decision is happening in every listener's day, tonight, and it is the same physical act Messier performed. Two hundred and sixty years apart, and it is the same gesture. That is the crossing.**

**ORAL TRADITION:** ⭐ That is the version I can sing. The gesture is alive. The paper is not.

**ANTI-ARCHIVIST:** And it keeps my point intact — **the setting-aside is a skill, not a sin.** The song is not scolding anyone.

**PRESENT-TENSE FLOOR:** And it puts a living body in the room. I withdraw the tourist objection.

**DESTROYER OF DOCUMENTS:** I do not fully withdraw. I will accept it on one condition: ⛔ **no song may end with the object being kept.** Set it down, and leave. If the last image is a hand putting something safely in a box, the museum won.

**IMPROVISER:** Then the shape is settled. **Six songs, each fifteen seconds long in real time, stretched to three and a half minutes.**

## 2. ⭐ THE FIND — the catalogue of things which are not comets

**NASA APOD, today, verbatim:** *"the 30th entry in astronomer Charles Messier's catalog of things which are not comets."*

Charles Messier hunted comets. The faint fuzzy things that fooled him — the ones that **did not move from night to night** — he wrote down **so that he could ignore them.** That list is the Messier Catalogue. It is the most beloved catalogue in amateur astronomy; every backyard telescope in the world is pointed by it.

**His thirteen comets are forgotten.** He died in 1817 believing he was a comet man. He was, by the king's own naming, *the Ferret of Comets*. **The list of distractions outlived the hunt, and he never knew.**

And today's picture is the joke landing 250 years later: comet **10P/Tempel 2** — discovered by Wilhelm Tempel in Milan in 1873, back at perihelion **six days ago**, on schedule, as it has been every **5.36 years** — passes across **M30**. The comet's dust trail **appears to pierce the star cluster.** The comet is **3.5 light-minutes** away. The cluster is **28,000 light-years** away. *The intersection is completely real to the eye and completely false in space.*

**And the direction of the ache reverses:** the thing Messier wanted — the comet — **left every time**, and he waited years. The things he wrote down **never moved at all**, and are still exactly where he put them.
