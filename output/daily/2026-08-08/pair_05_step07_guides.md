## GUIDE 1 — **THE ROOM, AND THE HAND THAT IS BUSY** *(serves F1, F6)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) *Continuous stage business* — the Chekhovian trick of letting a character do an unremarkable task straight through while the meaning happens beside it; the audience watches the hands and receives the story in the gaps. (ii) *Causal chaining (anadiplosis of action)* — each act is produced by the one before it, so the sequence has a grain and cannot be shuffled.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin in flat, contented competence; stay there. No descent, no ascent. If anything moves, it is the *listener's* footing, not the singer's. **The Hour The Light Goes Flat** is the colour: late, even, no contrast, nothing dramatic, a lamp on one side of a rented room.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Brushes on a dry ride; upright bass in whole notes; nylon-string guitar picking single notes at arm's length; a low shaker; **Modal Tenor Searching** between the phrases, never landing. Everything at conversation volume. Track as a band in one room — bleed named and welcome, entries made by ear rather than by count.
**Production & effects.** Room mics open wide; the kettle, the chairs and the bag are allowed into the take. ⛔ No section-boundary reverb moves, no re-amping, no risers. ⛔ No tape hiss, no vinyl crackle, no "found recording" — analogue warmth is a property of the **room**, never a texture laid on top *(D10)*.
**Structure & dynamics.** 3:45–4:15. Instrumental intro ~20s → falling voice alone ~45s → climbing voice alone ~45s → falling voice alone, briefly → **both, simultaneous, for the rest** → outro. **The second minute thickens by ADDITION** — a new player arrives, nothing gets louder.
**Rhyming schemes.** Falling strophe: paired end-rhymes on a **close front vowel** at a distance of two lines (AA BB inside its own stream). Climbing strophe: paired end-rhymes on an **open back vowel**, same spacing. ⭐ Because the streams interleave, the two rhyme chains never touch — the ear hears two rhyme systems running past each other.
**Poetic devices.** *Anadiplosis* (the last object of one line begins the next act), *epizeuxis* (a word said twice because the hand did it twice), heavy *alliteration* on plosives to keep the line from drifting to prose.
**Hook techniques.** (1) The falling five-note descent itself, hummable and unresolved; (2) the repeated short line spoken as a physical fact rather than a slogan; (3) ⭐ the one-word refrain landing in a new position each time so the ear leans in to check what it meant.
**Narrative & thematic.** First person, present tense, both voices. **Central aspect of the concept:** the body's routine competence. **Emotional tone signals:** ease, fondness, dispatch. **Special innovation:** the causal lock — the packing cannot be reordered, so it is a chain and not an inventory *(F-A, enforced)*.
**Devil's advocate, logged.** THE MAXIMALIST (after Kamasi Washington): *"You are building a small grey song and calling the greyness integrity."* Answered by the flair **Two Distances In One Frame** — the size is the gap between two women in one room, not the arrangement. His condition stands: if the gap is inaudible in the render, the objection is live again.

## GUIDE 2 — **THE TWO LINES** *(serves F2, F7 — the counterpoint guide)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) *Overlapping dialogue*, the stage convention where two people talk across each other and the audience does the sorting — the effort of sorting **is** the experience. (ii) *Aposiopesis* — breaking off before the end of a sentence, again and again, until the unfinished shape becomes the character.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Two contentments that do not know about each other. Neither voice is trying to be heard over the other; they are simply both going.
**The two lines, as composed** *(from step 06 §2; not re-derived here)*. **LINE ONE**: mezzo, **D Dorian**, descending **A4→D4**, 5–7 syllables, downbeat entry, close-front end-vowels. **LINE TWO**: mezzo, same mode, ascending **C4→G4**, 9–11 syllables, upbeat entry half a bar early, open-back end-vowels. **Entry interval: a MAJOR SECOND (D4 against C4)** — they beat.
⭐ **THE CROSSING:** both arrive on **E4**, the **second degree of the mode**, in **unison**, on the word **"second"**, one falling through it and one rising through it, for **one beat**, and **neither adjusts.** They end a **perfect fourth** apart. **The Crossing That Changes Nothing.**
**Instrumentation & texture.** Nothing may double either vocal line. The tenor sax occupies the space **between** phrases so the ear never gets a third thing to hold onto in the crossing bar. Bass in long notes so the harmony never tells the listener which voice is "right."
**Production & effects.** ⛔ The crossing is **NOT** executed in the mix. No panning trick, no level move, no reverb change at the seam. It is in the notes and in the words *(L22 THE GRAIN LAW: a Somatic objection answered in the production spec is not answered)*. The only production instruction is: **do not fix it.**
**Structure & dynamics.** Separate → separate → simultaneous → simultaneous → **crossing** → simultaneous, diverging → one voice alone. **One crossing per song. Not two** *(L38, N=1)*.
**Rhyming schemes.** Two independent chains, described in Guide 1. The climbing line frequently breaks on a function word — *"…and the —", "…I'm —"* — which is both true to interrupted speech and a real return mechanism.
**Poetic devices.** *Antanaclasis* (the same word, two meanings, in one bar), *asyndeton* (clauses stacked without conjunctions as the second voice runs out of breath).
**Hook techniques.** (1) The one shared syllable, unforgettable precisely because it is the only one; (2) the climbing line's repeated unfinished question, which the listener finishes and the singer never does; (3) the falling line's four-word declarative, byte-identical every time.
**Narrative & thematic.** **Key sequence:** the bar in which both voices are on E4. **Vocal technique:** two mezzos matched on age, accent and weight so a listener must work out who is who. **Emotional tone signals:** neither voice competes; both are relaxed.
**Devil's advocate, logged.** THE TRADITION CUSTODIAN (after Marsalis): *"Show me the two lines, named by interval, before you write any words."* Discharged at step 06 §2, in advance, in writing. His suspicion is **not** withdrawn and does not need to be.

## GUIDE 3 — **THE WORD THAT MOVES** *(serves F3 — the return vehicle)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) *Dramatic irony delivered by vocabulary* — the audience holds a meaning of a word that the speaker does not; (ii) *the innocent repetition*, where a phrase accrues weight only because the situation around it changes.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Utterly level. The word must never be leaned on, italicised, held longer, or sung with more air the fourth time than the first. ⛔ **If the singer performs the word, the song has told her, and D1 is broken.**
**The four positions.** *the second one* (the spare, the smaller piece of black glass) · *in a second* (Trish, waiting) · *give it a second* (Trish, hopeful) · ⭐ **second driver** (the box on a hand-copied page, with her name under it). The first three are ordinary. The fourth is the wound, and it is delivered in the flattest possible voice.
**Instrumentation & texture.** On every occurrence the accompaniment does exactly what it was already doing. ⛔ No hit, no stop, no cymbal, no held chord, no space cleared. **Modern Antiquity**: the arrangement is indifferent to the most important word in the song, the way rooms are.
**Production & effects.** No automation on the word. If the mix loves it, cut the love.
**Structure & dynamics.** Positions distributed across the song, never adjacent; the simultaneous occurrence **once only**, in the crossing bar, roughly two-thirds through.
**Rhyming schemes.** *second* ends on an unstressed syllable and refuses to rhyme, which is exactly right — the return vehicle is **positional**, not phonic. The phonic return is carried by the two vowel chains, measured separately.
**Poetic devices.** *Antanaclasis* (the pivot), *polyptoton* where the ordinal and the unit stand within a bar of each other, *diacope* on the surrounding short line.
**Hook techniques.** (1) Land the word on a strong beat in a weak sentence, so the ear catches it and the meaning does not; (2) sing it as the first word of an interrupted clause so the listener supplies the rest and is wrong; (3) ⭐ have the *other* voice use it as pure filler at the same instant — the meaning is destroyed by an ordinary politeness and that is the sound of the whole song.
**Narrative & thematic.** **Central aspect:** a life summarised in a position, not a word. **Lyric structure:** the word is never defined, never repeated for emphasis, never at the end of a section.
**Devil's advocate, logged.** THE CINEMA CARRIER (after Jarmusch): *"The moment a song tells me what it means about attention, I stop attending."* Enforced: there is **no line anywhere** that comments on the word, the order of the names, or what either means.

## GUIDE 4 — **THE HAPPINESS THAT COSTS THE LISTENER** *(serves F4, F5 — register)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) *Withheld interiority* — the narrator reports only what her hands and eyes do, so the listener supplies a feeling she does not have; (ii) *the innocent third party*, whose kindness is the instrument of the injury and who is never blamed.
**Mood & emotional arc.** From the taxonomy: **Composure + Contentment**, with **Warmth** on the second voice and **Anticipation** where the night turns. ⛔ Not Sorrow, not Nostalgia, not Sentimentality, not Melancholy. **She is fine.** She is looking forward to tomorrow the way a person looks forward to a good day.
⚠️ **F-B, restated:** the run's locked register is *slightly irritated*; **this singer is tender instead, declared and allowed.** Her composure is happiness, not boredom. She satisfies the binding half of the axis — **unmoved by the thing the listener is moved by** — completely. ⛔ **Do not add irritation to comply.**
**The wince, per variation, named in advance.** V1: *"is that new, or have you had it —"*, asked twice, never finished, never answered. V2: Trish says there will be another one and **she agrees, immediately and sincerely.** V3: ⭐ **she reads down the page past her own name to get to the time.** V4: the other woman is asleep in a minute and she lies awake, alone in a shared room, **and calls it the good bit.**
**Instrumentation & texture.** Warm and unhurried throughout; ⛔ nothing minor-key-signals grief. **Tej-warmth is available but not required** — if the room sounds fond, the wince has further to fall.
**Production & effects.** ⛔ No production cue may mark the wince. If the arrangement flinches, the listener stops flinching.
**Structure & dynamics.** The wince sits inside the ordinary flow of a verse, never at a section boundary, never after a rest.
**Rhyming schemes.** At the wince, keep the rhyme chain running exactly as before. A broken rhyme at that point would be the song pointing.
**Poetic devices.** *Litotes* (the largest thing said smallest), *epistrophe* on the contentment refrain.
**Hook techniques.** (1) A four-word declarative of happiness, repeated byte-identically, which gets worse each time without changing; (2) the other voice's unfinished compliment; (3) the sung line *"and I'd like that. I would"* — a woman agreeing with herself.
**Narrative & thematic.** **Emotional tone signals:** fondness, ease, small pleasure. **Player protection:** ⛔ nobody is a fool, nobody is pitied, nobody is scolded *(D6)*.
**Devil's advocate, logged.** THE LATE-CAPITALISM AUDITOR (after Jameson): *"It offers absolution at zero cost."* Answered only by **D4** — nobody finds out, ever, including after the song ends. Where the second voice comes close, she must **foreclose** the insight (*"you're very good about it"*), never approach it.

## GUIDE 5 — **WHAT THE SONG REFUSES TO EXPLAIN** *(serves F8, F9)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (i) *The occasion held offstage* — the event everyone is organised around is never described, only prepared for; (ii) *the terminal gesture*, a story ended by a hand finishing a task rather than by a conclusion.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Anticipation with no content. The listener knows something is tomorrow and is told nothing about it. ⭐ **Two Distances In One Frame**: a rented room, near and ordinary; and a thing four days and several hundred miles away that never gets a noun.
**The refusal, absolute.** ⛔ The words *eclipse, sun, moon, sky, star, shadow, totality, corona, solar, filter* appear **nowhere**. A piece of black glass is a piece of black glass. A field is a field. ⭐ **A stranger with no astronomy loses nothing** — the Small Room test *(after Bob Boilen's seat)*: two women at a desk, unamplified, and it still works.
**Instrumentation & texture.** ⛔ No cosmic signalling: no shimmer pads, no bell trees, no wide synthetic wash. Wooden and skin instruments; the only sustained tone permitted is a **tuned** harmonium note, and only in the last variation. ⛔ No untuned drones *(L22 — they get smoothed and the intent is lost)*.
**Production & effects.** The room is small and real. Distance is created by **arrangement placement**, not by reverb size.
**Structure & dynamics.** Each song ends on a completed physical act, mid-life, with the room still going: a coat pocket zipped; a kettle clicking off and tea poured; ⭐ **the page put back under the wet kettle**; the alarm set and hands returned to the chest. ⛔ **Nothing is kept, saved, treasured or looked at twice** *(D8, the Tinguely seat's price)*.
**Rhyming schemes.** The final lines resolve the vowel chain but **not** the sentence — the last words still break off.
**Poetic devices.** *Metonymy* (the trip stands in a folding chair), *synecdoche* (a whole night in a green standby light).
**Hook techniques.** (1) The closing physical act as a rhythmic event — a zip, a click, a page going down; (2) the falling line reaching its lowest note on the last word; (3) a repeated four-word line that has quietly changed meaning by the time it lands last.
**Narrative & thematic.** **Central aspect:** the occasion is the reason and never the topic. **Special innovation:** the page returns to the wet ring it came from — the most consequential object in the song is put back where it was getting ruined.
**Devil's advocate, logged.** THE DESTROYER OF DOCUMENTS (after Tinguely) did **not** fully withdraw: *"no song may end with the object being kept."* His price is paid four times over; his agreement is not claimed.

## GUIDE 6 — **WILDCARD: TWO PEOPLE READING DIFFERENT BOOKS UNDER ONE LAMP**

**Cross-disciplinary seed.** From painting: the *pentimento* — an earlier line left visible under the finished one, both true, neither corrected. From theatre: Caryl Churchill's overlap notation, where a slash in the script means *do not wait*. From bookbinding: two signatures sewn through one spine that never become one gathering. ⭐ From the picture that started this run: **two objects in one frame that appear to touch and are impossibly far apart, and the frame cannot show it.**
**Storytelling techniques.** (i) *Simultaneity without synthesis* — two complete texts occupying one duration, the listener choosing a focus and losing the other; (ii) *the unreliable middle distance*, where a listener's certainty about who is speaking is deliberately taken away and never given back.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Companionable. ⛔ This is not tension. Two people who like each other, doing separate things, comfortably, in the same lamplight.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Let the guitar and the bass occasionally sound like each other for a bar — a second confusable pair, at instrument scale, **without a second crossing**. ⭐ **Modern Antiquity**: a hammered dulcimer and a bass clarinet in the same phrase; nothing signals a period.
**Production & effects.** ⭐ **The gap must be audible** *(D11)*. It is made audible by **space between the two voices in the arrangement**, never by volume, and never by putting one of them further away.
**Structure & dynamics.** Let the simultaneous section outlast the listener's patience by a little. The discomfort of not being able to follow both is the argument.
**Rhyming schemes.** Two chains running past each other; ⭐ at exactly one moment they land on the same syllable, which belongs to both chains and to neither.
**Poetic devices.** *Chiasmus* across the two strophes (one falls while the other rises, and the shape reverses at the seam), *homoioteleuton* inside each stream.
**Hook techniques.** (1) The shared syllable; (2) the byte-identical four-word line; (3) ⭐ in the last variation, **the second voice stops mid-word and does not come back**, and the first carries on alone — the most memorable event in the song is an absence.
**Narrative & thematic.** **Key sequence:** the last thirty seconds of the fourth variation, one voice, low, still going. **Emotional tone signals:** ease, then quiet, then a single hand moving in the dark.
**Devil's advocate, logged.** THE SMALL ROOM (after Bob Boilen): *"could this be sung at a desk, unamplified, and still work?"* ⭐ **Yes — arguably better.** Two women at a table, one singing short falling lines, one talking across her, is the entire piece with nothing removed.
