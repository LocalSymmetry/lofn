# GUIDE 1 — **THE CORRIDOR AND THE FOREARM** *(facets F1 · F2)*

**Storytelling techniques (two, named).**
**(a) Hypophora on a form's terms** — the song asks the questions a form asks and answers them in the form's own voice: *where, when, what did it feel like, is there anything else.* The technique is borrowed from procedural prose (an intake script, a duty log), where a question is only ever asked in order to be closed. **(b) Ekphrasis of an object with no beauty in it** — the classical move of describing an artwork at length, aimed instead at a data row. The row is described with the attention a poem would spend on a face, and it does not repay any of it. Neither technique may be commented on inside the lyric.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Open in **Composure** with a thread of **Ennui** — the flat, skilled, faintly impatient state of a person doing the last competent thing before they can leave. Do **not** descend into Sorrow and do **not** ascend into Resolve. The permitted movement is a narrowing: Composure → Composure under slight **Impatience** → **Detachment** at the hinge → Composure again, unchanged. ⛔ **Reverence is the failure mode.** The listener's emotion — **Dismay**, then **Recognition** — is never available to the singer and must never be written into her line.

**Instrumentation & texture.** Bass clarinet (the low reed carries the 7/8 and is the only instrument allowed to sound tired), viola played with almost no bow pressure, a felted upright piano struck in the middle of the keyboard, fretless electric bass fingered not plucked, and a kit reduced to rim, closed hat and a single brushed snare — **no cymbal wash anywhere.** Above these, and belonging to neither, the **two struck bells** of Guide 3. Texture is thin and hard; the ensemble is five people who can all hear each other and none of whom is being asked for feeling.

**Production & effects.** ⭐ **This is the run's only dry, close-miked pair, and here is why it earns it.** A bare corridor with two doors and no furniture is an extremely *live* space — hard walls, nothing to absorb, a bright slap on every consonant. **The capsules sit a hand's width from each source, so the corridor's own ring never reaches the record.** The room has a sound and the recording does not include it. That is not a mixing preference; it is **the same act as the threshold**, performed by the engineer, and it is why a procedural song needs a room with no opinion. ⛔ No plate, no tail, no section-boundary verb change, **no tape hiss, no crackle, no "found recording" framing** — the retro trap is banned by D10 and the negatives belong in the Exclude field, not the style prompt.

**Structure & dynamics.** Through-composed in 7/8 at a walking 96 BPM. Six unrepeating strophes and one 4-bar refrain in 4/4 returning four times. Run-time **3:50–4:20**. ⭐ **The single dynamic is held by addition, never by gain** — instruments arrive and leave, nobody plays louder, and **the highest note in the vocal happens in the first third**, structurally forbidding a build into the end. The final section is the shortest and thinnest thing in the song.

**Rhyming scheme, per section.** Strophes: paired /oʊ/ couplets, **AABB CCDD**, where each capital is a *different three-character ending on the same vowel* (`-old`, `-ote`, `-low`, `-row`, `-one`, `-ose`). Refrain: **AABB**, byte-identical on every return. ⛔ No section is two couplets pretending to be an eight-line stanza — every strophe changes its ending-cluster at least twice and its clause shape at least once.

**Poetic devices (at least two, and these are the two).** **Epizeuxis** — a word repeated immediately with no conjunction (`A note. A no. A note.`), which is the crossing itself. **Homoioteleuton** — like-sounding endings across unlike grammar, which is what a single-vowel chain actually is when it works: *told · below · code · slow* are four different parts of speech landing on one sound.

**Hook techniques (three, and the song must choose exactly one — this pair has no adoptable hook by design).**
1. **The refrain as a stamp.** Four bars of 4/4 arriving inside 7/8 like a rubber stamp coming down: same words, same pitches, same four bars, every time. The hook is *recognition of a procedure*, not singability.
2. **The two bells as a signature.** A listener learns to hear the second bell arrive slightly wrong, bar after bar, and by the third refrain is waiting for it — an anticipation hook made entirely of arithmetic.
3. **The vowel itself.** Ninety line-ends on one vowel is a hook in the mouth rather than in the memory; the song is remembered as a *sound* the singer makes, not as a phrase.
⭐ **Selected: 1, with 2 running underneath it.** 3 is a property of the writing, not a device to aim at.

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.** First person, present tense, **listener as defendant** (D5). One person, one corridor, one row at a time. ⛔ Never plural, never "we," never an institution speaking. Her competence is the thing the song admires.

**Central aspect of the concept.** A body with nowhere to put anything down.
**Emotional tone signals.** Cheap fluorescent green *(Flair #9)*; the hour is irrelevant because the light is the same at all of them.
**Vocal techniques.** Solo female soprano, high and plain, **no vibrato**, almost choirboy; consonants placed early in the beat; **no breath audible at phrase ends** — she is not moved and she is not out of breath.

# GUIDE 2 — **THE ONE VOWEL** *(facet F3 — the RETURN VEHICLE)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **(a) The constrained lipogram-adjacent oath** — the writer swears, before the first line, that every line-end is /oʊ/ and never breaks it; the reader who notices is being told something about a rule nobody can be argued out of. **(b) Anaphoric field-prompts** — sentences that begin the way a form's fields begin (*Time of… · Reported by… · Nothing further…*) so that the grammar itself is institutional.

**Mood & emotional arc.** The vowel does the arc the singer refuses to do. Early strophes let the /oʊ/ land open and unhurried; at the hinge the same vowel is set on shorter words (`no`, `low`, `so`) so it arrives clipped without anyone singing harder; in the last strophe it is given the longest syllables in the song (`whole`, `told`, `alone`) and simply held. **Nothing about her delivery changes. Only the word lengths do.**

**Instrumentation & texture.** The viola shadows the vowel: wherever a line-end lands, the viola is already on that pitch, arriving a sixteenth early and leaving before the syllable does — so the ear hears the rhyme *supported and then abandoned*, ninety times. Bass clarinet never doubles the voice; it moves in contrary motion so the two lines never agree about direction.

**Production & effects.** Vocal close and level, **no de-essing sheen and no air-band lift** — she should sound like a person a metre away in a hard hallway, recorded honestly. ⛔ No doubling, no stacked harmony anywhere in this pair: the vocal configuration is **solo**, and a stacked chorus would delete the pair's own axis.

**Structure & dynamics.** The couplet-cluster pattern is the map: a strophe that opens `-old / -old` must not close `-old`; it migrates through at least two other endings before the section stops. That migration is what stops ninety long-O line-ends from becoming a drone.

**Rhyming scheme.** ⭐ **The whole song is one rhyme.** Locally it reads as couplets; globally it is a single unbroken assonance chain. **Removal-of-rhyme debt: none owed.** Nothing has been stripped; the vehicle is pure addition, which is what L21 asks for and what the `gates.yaml` floors measure.

**Poetic devices.** **Polyptoton** — the same root returning in different grammatical clothes (*told · tells · untold*) to keep the chain from feeling like a word list. **Consonance on hard stops** — `t`, `d`, `k` closing the long vowels, so a soft vowel never produces a soft line.

**Hook techniques (three).** (1) A single line-end word repeated across two strophes at the same metrical position — a *positional* return rather than a lexical one. (2) The refrain's second line ending on the same word as its first (`told / told`), which is the only place the chain touches itself twice in a row. (3) The very last line of each song ending on the longest available /oʊ/ so the song stops on the vowel it has been on the whole time.
⭐ **Selected: 3 in every variation; 2 in V1 only.**

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.** The vowel is the sound of *below*, *no*, *row*, *told* and *threshold* — the material's own phonetics. Nothing needs to be said about it, ever.

**Key sequences.** Hinge of V1: `four point five` sits **mid-line**, so the numeral never takes the rhyme position and the chain is not interrupted by the one thing everybody will remember.
**Lyric structure.** 5–7 words per line, 6–8 syllables, seven-syllable lines dominant so the 7/8 is carried by the words as well as the bar.
**Special effects and innovations.** ⛔ None. This pair's innovation is arithmetic and vocabulary; a sound-design idea here would be an apology for the writing.

# GUIDE 3 — **THE TWO BELLS** *(facet F4 — THE FALSE INTERSECTION)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **(a) Dramatic irony held at the level of the instrument** — the audience can hear that the second bell is out of phase and the singer never refers to it. **(b) Chiasmus at the crossing** (*note · no · note*), the one rhetorical figure whose shape is literally a crossing.

**Mood & emotional arc.** The bells carry the entire emotional argument and the voice carries none of it. Before the crossing: mild irritation, a thing arriving slightly wrong over and over. At the crossing: one hard beating dissonance, gone in an eighth. After: exactly the same as before, which is worse.

**Instrumentation & texture.** ⭐ **One bell sample, used twice.** Struck metal with a short decay — a small tubular bell or a struck tine, dry, no bloom. **Line A: B♮4, on the downbeat of every 7/8 bar (once every seven eighths). Line B: B♭4, once every eight eighths from bar one, never resetting.** Identical timbre, envelope, length and level. **Neither ever changes pitch, rate or level anywhere in the song.**

**Production & effects.** ⛔ **The crossing may not be produced.** No duck, no pan, no send, no filter sweep, no level move at the coincidence. **A Somatic objection answered in the production spec is not answered** (L22 THE GRAIN LAW). The bells sit in the same place in the stereo field as each other, mono-adjacent, at the same level, for the whole record. If a listener notices the crossing it must be because seven and eight do not agree, and for no other reason.

**Structure & dynamics.** B♭ walks one eighth-note later through the bar every bar and takes seven bars to come around. The bar-count before the **third** refrain is set so its phase is **zero there and only there**. At that refrain the two strikes coincide, a semitone apart, and the un-fired tone comes out the other side still walking. ⭐ **One crossing per song — N = 1, ONE SEAM.** The fourth refrain is identical and nothing happens at it; that is D11 and it is the wince.

**Why these two pitches.** In D Dorian the **B♮ is the note that makes the mode**; the **B♭ is the note that would make it ordinary.** For the four bars in which the un-flagged event is finally on the beat, the song sounds like plain minor — **the one moment it is in time is the one moment it is unremarkable.** Then the 7/8 returns.

**Rhyming scheme.** The crossing has a lyric twin: `A note. A no. A note.` — placed once, in the strophe immediately before the third refrain, and nowhere else in the pair. Both words sit on the vowel spine, so the crossing happens *inside* the rhyme rather than beside it.

**Poetic devices.** **Antanaclasis** — *clear* used as "exceeds the bar" and as "nothing obstructing," once each, far apart. **Diacope** — a word split by one intruder and then resumed, which is what the crossing is.

**Hook techniques (three).** (1) Let the un-fired bell be the very first sound, alone, for one bar, so the listener meets it before it has anything to be late for. (2) Silence both bells for the final strophe so the song's last minute has no clock in it at all. (3) Put the fired bell exactly under the word *note* every time it coincides with one.
⭐ **Selected: 1 and 2. Not 3** — that would be the mix explaining the joke.

**Musical style and effects.** 7/8 at 96 BPM; **the eighth-note pulse never changes, only the count.** The 4/4 refrain is the same tempo, differently barred.
**Emotional tone signals.** *Two Distances In One Frame* (Flair #10): near and impossibly far, with **no depth cue between them.**

# GUIDE 4 — **ONE ROW AT A TIME** *(facets F5 · F7 — the anti-list architecture and the no-scold rule)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **(a) Procedural chaining** — each verse's action is only possible because of the previous verse's action (you cannot check a figure twice before you have checked it once; you cannot code a call before it ends; you cannot turn off a light you have not walked past). **(b) Metonymy of labour** — the row stands for the event, the code stands for the call, the lanyard stands for the shift, and the substitution is never explained.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Competence, sustained, without a single moment of doubt. The arc is not *she learns*; the arc is *she finishes*.

**Instrumentation & texture.** Each strophe adds or removes **one** instrument at its start and holds that population to the end of the strophe — so the ensemble itself is handled one at a time. Nothing crescendos into anything.

**Production & effects.** One continuous take implied throughout: bleed is permitted **between the players**, never from the room's ring. Entries are by cue, not by grid — somebody comes in slightly early because they heard the bass clarinet turn, not because a bar started.

**Structure & dynamics.** ⭐ **The enforceable test, applied per strophe before it is allowed to stand: could the contents be reordered without loss? If yes, it is a list and it is a repair.** Through-composition helps and does not finish the job — the *actions* must chain, not only the music. **Recorded per variation in step 10.**

**Rhyming scheme.** Where a strophe risks becoming an inventory, the rhyme cluster is deliberately changed **mid-action** so the ear hears a turn rather than another item.

**Poetic devices.** **Asyndeton** — clauses set side by side with no conjunctions, which is how a form reads and, crucially, **not** how a list-song reads: a list-song joins with *and*, and this one never does. **Zeugma** — one verb governing an object and an abstraction (*I close the call and the afternoon*), used **once** in the whole pair.

**Hook techniques (three).** (1) The same physical verb (`I put`) opening three widely separated strophes. (2) A completion phrase that recurs at three different points in the procedure and means something slightly different each time by position. (3) The final line of each song being a completed physical act with no comment after it.
⭐ **Selected: 1 and 3.** (2 belongs to P05's axis — *a one-word refrain that changes meaning by position* — and **a device belonging to another pair is a repair regardless of how well it works.**)

**⛔ THE NO-SCOLD RULE, CHECKED PER VARIATION, NOT ONCE FOR THE PAIR.** The threshold exists for good reasons: attention is finite, and a bar is how a small team stays able to respond to the thing that actually needs a response. **She applies it correctly. The song never scolds her, and the song never pities the person on the other end of it.** In V3 she **believes** the caller — the failure is structural, not personal, and writing it as personal would be the run's smug version and a straightforward D6 breach.

**Narrative elements.** Nothing collapses. Nobody is hurt. The largest physical event anywhere in this pair is **water going over the side of a bowl.**

# GUIDE 5 — **THE WITHHOLDING AND THE FIGURE** *(facets F6 · F8)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **(a) Dramatic irony, structural** — the listener is given every piece of the picture and the singer is given all of them except one, and the missing one is the whole thing. **(b) The fact-arrival limb grafted from THE MEASUREMENT** — ordinary scene, the figure arrives, same scene recontextualised, **and the person in it does not notice the recontextualisation.**

**Mood & emotional arc.** The hinge is not a swell. The figure arrives inside an ongoing action and the action does not pause for it. ⭐ **The response to the number is a speed, not a feeling:** she does not slow down, because slowing down would be an opinion and she has not got one.

**Instrumentation & texture.** At the hinge, **subtract**: piano and viola drop out for two bars, leaving bass clarinet, the two bells and the voice. The hinge is the **thinnest** moment in the song. ⛔ **It is not the loudest, and if it becomes the loudest the pair has lost to the generator.**

**Production & effects.** No treatment change at the hinge whatsoever — same mic, same level, same distance. The only thing that changes is who is playing.

**Structure & dynamics.** Hinge at strophe 4 of six, roughly 55–60 % through. ⛔ **D4 THE VINDICATION BAN:** no line may promise, imply or console with future recognition. **Nobody finds out.** No "someday," no "one day they'll," no final image of the record being read. ⛔ **D8:** the song ends on a **completed physical act** — the row is filed, the call is coded, the door is gone through — and never on the object being kept.

**Rhyming scheme.** ⭐ The one sung numeral **sits mid-line and never takes the rhyme position.** The chain must not be interrupted by the one thing everybody will remember.

**Poetic devices.** **Litotes** — understatement by negation (*not slow*, *nothing further*, *no field*), which is the native rhetoric of a form. **Epistrophe** — successive clauses ending on the same word, used at exactly one place per song, always on a /oʊ/ word.

**Hook techniques (three).** (1) The figure spoken flat on a single repeated pitch while the bass clarinet moves underneath — the voice refuses to shape it. (2) The line **after** the figure being the shortest line in the song. (3) A half-bar of nothing before the figure, so it arrives into a hole.
⭐ **Selected: 1 and 2. Not 3** — a hole before the figure is the arrangement telling you it matters, and this singer would never.

**⭐ THE ONE SUNG NUMBER, RESTATED SO IT CANNOT BE SPENT TWICE.** **`four point five`**, spelled in words, in **V1 only**, at the hinge. **V2, V3 and V4 sing no numeral of any kind — not fifteen, not fourteen, not "two doors."** ⛔ **This pair holds the run's entire numeric allowance; no other pair sings a number.** Digits are permitted only in `[Theme:]`, `[SONG FORM:]`, EMO headers, BPM and key — none of which is voiced.

**Central aspect of the concept.** You do not get to know which of your work was the work — **and she never will, and that is the point, and the song must not console anyone about it.**

# GUIDE 6 — ⭐ **WILDCARD: THE FORM AS A LITERARY GENRE** *(cross-disciplinary — administrative prose, and the acoustics of a room that is not recorded)*

**The wild-card premise.** Treat the intake form as a **genre with its own poetics**, the way a sonnet or a ship's log is a genre. Forms have a metre (field, field, field), a volta (the free-text box), a closing couplet (the disposition code and the timestamp), and a strict prohibition on the first person's interiority. ⭐ **A form is the only literary genre designed so that nothing in it can be moved without being wrong** — which is precisely the anti-enumeration property D7 demands, arriving from architecture instead of from restraint. Borrow the genre wholesale: write the song *in* it rather than *about* it.

**The cross-disciplinary import — architectural acoustics.** A corridor with two doors and no furniture is a **flutter-echo chamber**: parallel hard walls, a slapback between them, and nothing to break it up. Everyone who has ever stood in one has heard their own consonants come back. ⭐ **This song is recorded so that it does not.** Close capsules, a hand's width out; the ring exists and is not captured. **The engineer performs the same act as the analyst, and neither of them is doing anything wrong.** That equivalence is the wild-card's whole contribution, and — critically — **it appears once in the lyric** (V4: the corridor rings and nothing here writes that down) so that it is not a production idea pretending to be a musical one.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Bureaucratic present tense as a lyric mode: the strange calm of a document that has no opinion about what it contains. Under it, one thread of **Impatience**, which is the only heat this pair is allowed.

**Instrumentation & texture.** Let the bass clarinet play the form's own rhythm — a question, a gap the length of an answer, a question — and let the viola fill exactly none of those gaps. **The gaps are the field boxes.**

**Production & effects.** ⛔ No keyboard clicks, no printer, no office foley, no phone ringtone as a sample. **The form is a grammar, not a sound effect**, and dressing it in Foley would be exactly the medium promoted from substance to subject (L37) that this run was built to avoid. The one permitted SFX is the pair's own two bells.

**Structure & dynamics.** The four songs are four **positions on one corridor**, not four episodes: the arithmetic (V1), the one that cleared (V2), the call (V3), the end of the shift (V4). Nothing is resolved between them and nobody in any of them finds out.

**Rhyming scheme.** The free-text box is the only place in a form where the sentences are allowed to be long — so in V3 the strophe describing it is written in the song's **longest lines**, still on the vowel, and then the disposition code snaps them short again.

**Poetic devices.** **Anaphora** on field-prompts. **Aposiopesis** — a sentence that stops because the box ran out.

**Hook techniques (three).** (1) A refrain that reads like a rule and scans like a stamp. (2) A recurring completion phrase whose meaning is fixed and whose position moves. (3) The vowel as the hook of last resort.
⭐ **Selected: 1.** (2 is P05's device and is not touched.)

**Narrative & thematic suggestions.** Present tense; second-person never used; the listener is the defendant and is never addressed as one.
**Special effects and innovations.** ⛔ Deliberately none. **The innovation is that the song is made OF a form rather than ABOUT one** (L38: a piece is made OF a material, never ABOUT materials).
