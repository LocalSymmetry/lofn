## GUIDE 1 — **THE FIFTEEN SECONDS** *(F1 · F12 — the visible act)*

**Storytelling techniques.** *In medias res*, borrowed from the ballad opening: the song starts with the hand already moving and never explains how it got there. And **synecdoche** — the thumb stands for the whole judgement, so the song can show a decision without ever staging one.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Begin at *Composure* — a man at the flat end of a shift, kettle cold, till counted, the last job in his lap. Move to *Confidence* as he states the reason, then to *Mirth* as the taunt warms, then back down to *Composure* for the send. ⛔ **No ascent to feeling.** He is exactly as moved at the end as at the start; the listener is not.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Open thin: one bare kick and a shout coming through an open door. Organ and fuzz guitar enter together on the first sung line and never leave. **Cheap Fluorescent Green** is the sound-colour to aim at — a reedy combo organ with no warmth in it, sitting where a string pad would be in a lesser song.
**Production & effects.** One room, everybody in it. Bleed is the point: kit in the vocal mic, the shop floor audible through the propped door — a mop, a shutter, chairs. **Analogue warmth is a property of the room; it is never a texture applied on top.**
**Structure & dynamics.** 2:45–3:10. Verse, verse, shouted pre-chorus, chant, verse, verse, break, shouted pre-chorus, chant-with-crossing, two-bar tag. The tag is the only place the band is not playing.
**Rhyme & line.** Verses in loose paired rhyme, four to six words a line, strong first stress. Chant lines are a two-to-four-word call plus a three-word answer. Keep every section's syllable count inside ±2.
**Poetic devices.** **Anaphora** (`Look at you. Look at you —`) and **epizeuxis** (`Still going. Still going.`).
**Hook techniques.** (a) The call is a *question a room already knows how to answer*; (b) the answer is byte-identical every time and never harmonised; (c) the top note of the answer is the word the answer is about.
**Narrative & thematic seed.** Second person throughout. One person, one room, one thing being set aside, one crossing. The absent person is present only as an observed act — a side door, a pen going past a hand — never as a mind.
**Central aspect.** The list is something that happens **to** him; it is never a shape the song adopts.

## GUIDE 2 — **THE CROSSING THAT CHANGES NOTHING** *(F2 · F3 — the counterpoint)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **Antanaclasis** — one phrase repeated in a changed sense. And **chiasmus** at the structural level: above-then-below becomes below-then-above across the second chant.
**Mood & emotional arc.** *Detachment* is the target here specifically, not excitement. The crossing is the least eventful bar in the song and must be written that way.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Both lines are **the same tenor**. No second singer, no octave double, no harmony. The sung chant rises F♯4–A4–B4; the shout falls B4–A4–E4; they pass at A4 on the middle syllable, on the same beat, in contrary motion.
**Production & effects.** ⛔ **Nothing is answered in the mix.** No level change, no widening, no reverb move, no filter, no arrival. If the crossing can only be described as a production event, this guide has failed. The band plays the same figure through it that it played before it.
**Structure & dynamics.** The crossing lands in the **first half** of the second chant, not at its end — so that whatever lift a generator wants to apply to a final chorus arrives *after* the crossing, on material identical to the first chant, by which time the shout is already underneath. The chant is at **full size on its first appearance**; there is nothing left to inflate into.
**Rhyme & line.** The two lines are the same three words with one word changed. That is the whole device and it needs no support.
**Poetic devices.** **Polyptoton** on *down* (down as direction, down as writing, down as insult) and **contrary motion** as a figure of speech rather than a mix note.
**Hook techniques.** (a) Print the shout above the chant before the crossing and below it after — order is the one thing a generator always honours; (b) let the shout shorten to a single word after it passes under; (c) never let the two lines land on the same note in the same direction.
**Emotional tone signals.** Flat, quick, unimpressed. The most important bar in the song is the one nobody in the room looks up for.
**Key sequences.** Pre-chorus (shout alone, above) → chant (sung alone) → pre-chorus (shout alone) → chant with the shout interleaved, above for three lines, then below for the rest.

## GUIDE 3 — **THE ANSWER THAT MEANS TWO THINGS** *(F4 · F5 — the wince)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **Dramatic irony**, held all the way to the end and never released to the speaker. And the **unreliable narrator by competence** — he is right about everything he says, which is exactly why he cannot see the one thing he is doing.
**Mood & emotional arc.** *Playfulness* on the surface for the whole chant. The wince belongs to the listener and must never be handed to the singer. ⛔ No line may show him understanding, hesitating, or softening.
**Instrumentation & texture.** The answer is sung by **the whole room** — the lead plus whoever else is around, ragged, unrehearsed, nobody quite together. That raggedness is the point: it is a room agreeing without listening.
**Production & effects.** The answer is never doubled, tuned, stacked or sweetened. If it sounds like a produced gang vocal, the room has become a choir and the joke is gone.
**Structure & dynamics.** The target of the taunt must stay genuinely ambiguous for two verses — the listener, the absent person and the singer must all be plausible — and resolve onto the singer in the last chant, mid-task, while he is sending the list.
**Rhyme & line.** Calls arrive in **rhymed pairs** so the room can predict the second one. That predictability is what makes the last, unpaired call land.
**Poetic devices.** **Antimetabole** in the final call-and-answer, and **epistrophe** — every line in the chant ends on the same word.
**Hook techniques.** (a) The last call names the singer's own role without naming him; (b) the tag strips the band away so the answer arrives with nothing behind it; (c) the very last sound is the physical act, not a word.
**Narrative & thematic seed.** The three words mean *add my name* in the room and *diminish me* in the ear. Only the listener hears the second. ⛔ **The song never points at it.** A single explanatory line destroys the pair.
**Special effects.** The final answer is spoken more than sung, with the band already stopped.

## GUIDE 4 — **THE HOUSEKEEPING REGISTER, INDIGNATION SIDE** *(F6 · F9 — the voice)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **Playground taunt** — second person, insulting and affectionate, the register of children who like each other. And **occupatio**: the song lists what he is *not* doing (not deciding, not weighing, not sitting) to show the speed of what he is.
**Mood & emotional arc.** *Impatience → Confidence → Mirth → Detachment → Composure.* ⛔ Never *Sorrow*, never *Nostalgia*, never *Pity*. He is not sad; he is finishing up, and he is good at it.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Male tenor, early thirties, plain regional English, bright and adenoidal, half-laughing between lines, **cracking when he goes for the top note**. ⭐ This is the run's one non-female lead and the crack is load-bearing — it is what keeps bratty from becoming cruel.
**Production & effects.** Vocal close but not clean; the room is on it. ⛔ No amplitude vocabulary anywhere in any field — **name the person, not the volume.**
**Structure & dynamics.** Loud from the first bar to the last, with no ramp. The dynamic interest is entirely in *what is being said*, which is where the accessible arm has to earn its keep.
**Rhyme & line.** Short lines. Four to six words. Paired rhymes a child could predict. Mean words per line well under the ceiling **by construction**, never by trimming afterwards.
**Poetic devices.** **Alliteration** carried by ordinary function words (*that / they / there*, *who / when / would*) rather than by ornament, plus **diacope** on the imperative.
**Hook techniques.** (a) Half-laugh into the top note so the crack reads as amusement; (b) let the shout be thrown sideways through a door, not projected at the listener; (c) keep the last consonant of every call hard so the room's entry is unmistakable.
**Vocal techniques.** One voice, two behaviours. ⛔ No second vocalist, no gang overdub that sounds arranged.
**Emotional tone signals.** Somebody doing the ninth iteration of an annual job at ten past closing and enjoying being good at it.

## GUIDE 5 — **THE ROOM AND THE RETURN** *(F8 · F10 — the floor under everything)*

**Storytelling techniques.** **Refrain-as-frame**, kept from the archetype's parent seed: the chant frames the verses rather than resolving them. And **the call-and-answer**, the oldest work-song device there is, doing the job it was invented for — a room agreeing to something while its hands are busy.
**Mood & emotional arc.** The room's mood is *closing*: fluorescent, low-ceilinged, everybody already half gone.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Combo organ, fuzz guitar on five notes, four-on-the-floor kick with a **pummeling** snare across beat and offbeat, tambourine, handclaps where the variation wants them. Everything blown out. Bass sits under the organ's left hand.
**Production & effects.** ⛔ **No tape hiss, no vinyl crackle, no cassette warble, no found-recording framing** — D10, the retro trap, is named and banned. The record is *unspent*, not sepia: present tense, this evening, lights still on.
**Structure & dynamics.** Songs get bigger by **addition**, never by gain — and in this pair the only addition is the second voice arriving at the crossing, which is why the crossing is written as an accretive event.
**Rhyme & line.** Every floor in `gates.yaml` must clear **twice**: once as written and once with the three-word answer stripped from every line. A song that only returns because one phrase returns has not returned. Verses therefore carry their own paired end-rhymes and their own alliteration.
**Poetic devices.** **Homoioteleuton** (paired line-endings inside the chant) and **asyndeton** in the verse openings — nouns dropped flat with no connectives: *Back office. Carpet tiles. Closing.*
**Hook techniques.** (a) Byte-identical chant blocks — a byte-identical chorus is correct craft and needs no defence; (b) the shouted pre-chorus returns unchanged, so its *movement* at the crossing is the only new information; (c) the last two bars remove everything so the return is felt by its absence.
**Structure note.** 2:30–3:10. Four songs, four lengths, V4 the shortest by lines, sections and characters.

## GUIDE 6 — **WILDCARD: THE ROTA AS A DRAWING** *(cross-disciplinary)*

**The borrowed discipline is life drawing — specifically, negative space.** A beginner draws the chair; a competent draughtsman draws the shape of the air around the chair and the chair appears for free. **This pair draws the air.** The absent person is never described, never voiced, never given a motive of their own — and the more precisely the song renders the sheet, the pen, the thumb, the propped door and the table, the more sharply the missing shape resolves. That is why D7's ban on enumeration is not a restriction here but the method: a list would draw the chair.
**Second borrowing: the fugue's stretto**, where a subject enters against itself before it has finished. The crossing is a two-bar stretto with a single voice, and it resolves nothing, which is exactly what a stretto in a fugue refuses to do until much later — except here, later never comes.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Fluorescent, flat, unbeautiful, funny. **Cheap Fluorescent Green** as a colour instruction: a light nobody chose, that makes everyone look tired, and that nobody in the room notices because they are leaving.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Let the organ take the melodic interest a string section would take elsewhere. The instrument should sound *bought secondhand*, not restored.
**Production & effects.** The most radical production choice available here is **refusing the last-chorus lift** — a garage stomp that ends the same size it began. That is the thing this pair is actually risking, and the render is where it will be won or lost.
**Structure & dynamics.** Two bars alone at the end. A completed physical act as the final sound: **the list is sent**, and he leaves.
**Poetic devices.** **Zeugma** across the tag — one verb governing the pen and the person.
**Hook techniques.** (a) End on an act, not a word; (b) let the room's last answer be the singer's, alone; (c) do not resolve the melody — leave the chant on its top note and cut.
**Narrative seed.** *A person who is good at this, doing it correctly, for the ninth time, and being right.*
**Special innovation.** The device and the return vehicle are **the same object seen twice** — a put-down, turned inward. Neither has to be explained because each is the other's explanation.
