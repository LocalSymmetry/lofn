## 1 — THE REFINEMENT PASS (a real argument, with a dissent that does not fully resolve)

**BIG-BAND TRANSLATOR (after Gershon):** *Working from the Addis charts —* I asked for two lines named by interval and I have been handed something better than I asked for, so let me say what it is before anyone dresses it up. **The crossing here is not a gesture. It is arithmetic.** One bell every seven eighths, one bell every eight, neither resetting. That is a phase relationship, it is deterministic, and I can write the bar count that puts the coincidence at the third refrain and nowhere else. **Nobody has to feel anything for it to work.**

One correction, and it is a chart correction, not a taste one. **Do not let the players count the bells.** If the drummer is counting to seven and the bell player is counting to eight, somebody will "fix" the drift by ear within four bars, because musicians are physically unable to leave a wrong-sounding thing alone. **The B flat must be played by a click or by one person whose only instruction is: ignore the band.**

**HARD SECTION (after Brenneck):** Agreed, and I'll go further — **that is the one player who should not be able to see the others.** Same room, back turned. Everything else stays as written: five people, one take, no overdubs, entries by cue.

But I want to argue with the mic plan, because on paper it reads like a mixing preference and I do not accept mixing preferences. *(pauses)* **Actually — no. I've read the reason and I withdraw the objection before I've made it.** The corridor is hard and parallel and it rings; the capsules sit a hand out so it does not reach the record. **The room is real and the record leaves it out. That is the song.** I have spent twenty years arguing that the room *is* the record, and this is the one case where excluding it is the take. I would not have found that.

**ORCHESTRATOR OF CHARACTER (after Ellington):** Then let me have the singer, because she is being written as a setting and she is a person. *High, plain, no vibrato, almost choirboy* — that is a **timbre**, and I asked for a **behaviour.** Here is the behaviour: **she never lands late.** Every consonant is early, every phrase ends where it says it will, and she does not take breath in a place that could be read as feeling. **A singer who is never late is a singer who is never moved**, and that is the whole character, done in the delivery instruction rather than in an adjective.

And one thing the writing has already got right that I want protected: **the highest note is in the first third.** Do not move it. A soprano's top note late in a song is a climax whether anyone intends one or not.

**SMALL ROOM (after Boilen):** My test, then. **Could this be sung at a desk, unamplified?** *(runs it)* — V1 and V3 yes, unequivocally: a person standing up, one voice, and you know inside twenty seconds that she is filing something and that one of the things she is filing is her own. V4 yes. **V2 I am less sure of**, because its subject is the thing that *did* clear, and a listener has to hold "this one got a map and the others didn't" before the wince lands. **That is one inference more than the others ask for.** I am not calling it a failure — I am saying V2 is the pair's third-strongest card and should not be the one anybody leads with.

**⚠️ MAXIMALIST (after Kamasi):** Now me. I withdrew the greyness objection on one condition and **this is the pair that tests it, so I am going to be difficult in the specific way I said I would be.**

Look at what is on the page. Odd meter. One dynamic. Dry. Close. No cymbals. No harmony. No key change. No build. **Every single element is a subtraction, and the room told this run — in writing, in the medium step — that subtractive specs are exactly what the generator smooths away.** You have written a song whose entire identity is a list of things it refuses to do. **A generator will simply do them anyway, and what comes back will be a pleasant 4/4 chamber piece with a nice build, and every one of these refusals will have been invisible.**

**BIG-BAND TRANSLATOR:** That is a fair hit and I will answer it with the chart rather than with a promise. **The crossing is accretive, not subtractive.** It is *add a second bell.* The odd meter is *add a beat that is missing* — nobody has to remove anything to hear seven. And the vowel spine is pure addition: ninety line-ends that rhyme where a normal lyric would have thirty.

**MAXIMALIST:** Those three are additions, yes, and I accept them. **The dynamic instruction is still a refusal**, and it is the one I care about, because it is the one that carries my objection.

**HARD SECTION:** Then rewrite it as an addition and stop refusing. Not *"no build"* — **"instruments arrive and leave; the last section is the shortest and has the fewest players in it."** That is a positive instruction. A generator can do it. **You cannot ask a machine to not-do something; you can ask it to do something else.**

**MAXIMALIST:** *That* I will take, and I want it in all four prompts in exactly that form. ⛔ **And I am not withdrawing.** My condition was that **the gap be audible**, and everything decided here is about whether the *arrangement* survives. **Whether a listener winces at the third refrain and the singer does not is not decidable in this room and is not decidable from a text gate.** **Only a render audit settles it, and I want it flagged as such.** If the fourth refrain comes back and it is indistinguishable from the third — if the coincidence did not land — then this pair is six small grey songs and I was right the first time.

**ORCHESTRATOR OF CHARACTER:** That is the correct place to leave it.

**⚠️ TRADITION CUSTODIAN (after Marsalis, invited for the one thing he asked for):** I was promised two lines named by interval before any words. **I have them: B natural against B flat, one every seven eighths, one every eight, neither altered anywhere.** That is a tune with a job and it answers my first defect properly.

**My suspicion is not withdrawn and I said it would not be.** A semitone held for four minutes is one bad decision away from being a mistake nobody can hear as intentional. **But the decision here is not mine to overrule**, and I would rather this than another song about a situation. **Signed, with the suspicion recorded.**
