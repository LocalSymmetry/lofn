## 2 — ⭐ D3 DISCHARGED: THE TWO LINES, NAMED BY INTERVAL, BEFORE A WORD OF LYRIC

*The Marsalis seat's unwithdrawn condition: "show me the two lines, named by interval, before you write any words." `step04_medium.md` named the interval (**semitone**) at the coordinator layer and forbade inventing a new one. This is the refinement, not a reinvention.*

**KEY: D Dorian. METER: 7/8 throughout at a walking 96 BPM. The eighth-note pulse never changes — only the count does.**

> **LINE A — THE TONE THAT FIRES.**
> A single struck bell, **B♮4**, on the downbeat of every 7/8 bar. It therefore sounds **once every seven eighth-notes**.
>
> **LINE B — THE TONE THAT DOES NOT FIRE.**
> The **identical bell**, **B♭4** — **one semitone below** — striking **once every eight eighth-notes** from bar one, and **never resetting to the bar line.**
>
> Same sample, same envelope, same length, same level. **Neither ever changes pitch, rate, or level. Not once. Not anywhere.**

**THE CROSSING — and why it is in the notes and could not be in the mix.**
Seven and eight do not agree. B♭ therefore walks **one eighth-note later through the bar, every bar**, and takes seven bars to come all the way around. The song's **single 4/4 refrain** is where the two clocks agree: for four bars the downbeats are eight eighths apart, which is B♭'s own rate.

**The number of 7/8 bars before the THIRD statement of the refrain is chosen so that B♭'s phase is zero there — and only there.** At that one refrain the un-fired tone arrives on the same eighth as the fired one, **passes through it a semitone apart**, and comes out the other side still walking. Nothing was retuned, nothing was ducked, nothing was panned. **The crossing is a consequence of the meter and of nothing else.**

**Why B♮ and B♭ and not any other semitone.** In D Dorian, **B♮ is the note that makes the mode** — the raised sixth is the whole difference between Dorian and ordinary minor. **B♭ is the note that would make it ordinary.** So: the alert that fires *is* the mode; the alert that does not fire is the note that would flatten the song into something nobody would look at twice. For the four bars in which the un-flagged event is finally on the beat, **the song sounds ordinary.** Then the 7/8 returns and the drift starts again.

**And the same crossing, in the lyric** (D3: *"executed in the lyric and in the notes — never in the mix"*; L22 THE GRAIN LAW). The two words are **"note"** and **"no"** — one syllable apart in the mouth, both on this pair's vowel spine, the two things a row can receive. **They cross exactly once**, in the strophe immediately before the third refrain, written as `A note. A no. A note.` — the *no* passes between the two *notes* and **neither becomes the other**. ⛔ Once per song. **N = 1. ONE SEAM.**

**And the fourth refrain is byte-identical and nothing happens at it.** That is D11: *the listener winces and the singer does not.* She sings the same four bars the same way. **The wince is that the refrain does not change.**

## 4 — ⭐ THE RETURN VEHICLE, SPECIFIED: THE VOWEL IS **/oʊ/**

**Every sung line in all four variations ends on the long-O vowel.** No exceptions, no drift, no relief.

The vowel was not chosen for prettiness. It is the vowel the material is already made of: **thresh·old · be·low · row · no · note · told · hold · whole · code · close · slow · zero · goes · own · shown · alone · phone · tone · home.** The word *threshold* and the word *below* are the same sound; so are *note* and *no*.

- **Sung by a plain soprano with no vibrato, a long O is the least ornamentable vowel in English.** It cannot be scooped or shaken. It arrives, it holds, it stops. That is the register.
- **Removal-of-rhyme debt: none is owed.** Nothing is being stripped. This is *addition* — a return vehicle that binds every line-end in the song, which is the strongest possible reading of L21 THE RETURN.
- **Line-ends are additionally clustered in couplets on the same three-character ending** (`-old / -old`, `-ote / -ote`, `-low / -low`, `-row / -row`, `-one / -one`, `-ose / -ose`) so the vowel spine registers on `scripts/measure_soundcraft.py → strict_end_rhyme()` as well as in the ear. **Measured, not asserted** — see step 10.
- ⛔ **`door` and `floor` never take a line-end.** They are the two most important nouns in the room and they are a *different* vowel; letting them end a line would blur the spine. They live mid-line, every time.
