## §1 — THE FOUR INFLUENCES, AND THE ONE THING EACH ONE CHANGED

*Deliberately not the famous name. The step file asks for lesser-known practitioners, and in this scene the lesser-known ones are the ones the credit is most owed to.*

| | Influence *(prose only)* | Aspect of style | Two signature techniques | The one function imported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **Emahoy Tsege Mariam Gebru** (1923–2023) — composer, pianist, nun; a solo keyboard practice recorded largely alone | rests composed as carefully as notes; a low left hand that refuses to fill its own space | (1) a bass register that stops and is not covered; (2) phrases that end early and are allowed to | ⭐ **The bass sits out for one entire section and the gap is not filled.** In a song about somebody at the end of a day, the silence is the tiredness — and it costs nothing to render. |
| **V2** | **Hailu Mergia** (b. 1946) — keyboardist and arranger; still touring; reissued from a cassette bin | the combo organ as a conversational partner rather than a bed | (1) right hand answering the vocal in the gaps; (2) left hand holding a drone that never comments | ⭐ **The organ is split in two.** The left hand holds the unmoving low note — which *is* the first of the two crossing lines — while the right hand answers her between lines, so the instrument becomes the second person in the van while the visitor is talking. |
| **V3** | **Getatchew Mekurya** (1935–2016) — saxophonist who adapted an unaccompanied solo vocal form to the horn | a single line that stands complete before anything joins it | (1) an unaccompanied opening of real length; (2) a solo voice that does not wait for a downbeat | ⭐ **The first eight bars are the hum with nothing at all underneath.** The listener meets the figure naked, which is the only way the crossing can later register as a crossing. |
| **V4** | **Mahmoud Ahmed** (b. 1941) — singer of the golden-age scene | a low, unhurried vocal placement that sits behind the band without dragging | (1) phrases landing fractionally late and never corrected; (2) consonants softened at line-ends | ⭐ **Every vocal phrase lands just behind the beat and is never pulled forward.** It is what a tired person does, it is the last thing a quantised generator will do unprompted, and it is the difference between speech and recitation. |

## §5 — SELF-CRITIQUE

1. **The refinements are small on purpose and one of them is worth more than the other three.** The V2 split-hand organ is the only change that alters what a listener will actually hear — it turns the pair's unmoving low note into one hand of a real instrument, which makes it far likelier to survive a generator than "a hum." The other three are honest but modest.
2. **The length pass is where refinement fought the gate, and the gate won.** Adding four functional clauses pushed V2 to **1,019 chars — nineteen over the hard cap.** Every prompt was then trimmed back into the 870–960 band. ⚠️ **Some of what was cut was real** ("band joining by ear" left V2; "kit in the vocal mic" left V1) — the character budget is genuinely binding and the trims were not free. Recorded so nobody reads the tidy final numbers as evidence that nothing was lost.
3. **The influence choice is the most contestable thing in this artifact.** I picked four practitioners from the same scene, which concentrates the debt rather than spreading it, and every one of them is credited with a link. **A reasonable reading is that four borrowings from one living scene is exactly the "costume department" the Simon seat warned about, even at one pair of six.** I do not think it is, because what was taken is four unrelated *techniques* rather than a sound; but I am not the right party to settle that and I am not claiming to have.
