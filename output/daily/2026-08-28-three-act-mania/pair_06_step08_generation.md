## 1. THE COLD OPENS, WRITTEN FIRST AND ALONE (R1)

R1 says the first sung line is the song's biggest line, that there are **zero lines of preamble**, and that
it opens **mid-argument** — *"there is no line before this line."* So all four were written before anything
else, and each was tested against one question: **what unheard claim is this line answering?**

| song | first sung line | the unheard claim it answers |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | **Nobody asked the lake.** | *"the process was followed / everyone signed off"* |
| V2 | **The ink is real.** | *"it's only a word — it doesn't cost anything"* |
| V3 | **Every old map is now wrong.** | *"nothing has actually changed"* |
| V4 | **Watch me name the whole room.** | *"you can't just do that"* |

⭐ **All four are answers, and none of them is loud.** R1.4 is explicit: the instruction is to put **the
voice** in front of whatever ramp the generator builds, never to tell the arrangement to start loud.
Every style field says the lead is *"in front of everything from the first syllable"* and **not one of them
contains the word loud.** ⭐ **V3 is the purest test of that rule** — its Act I is a held tone and a woman
already mid-sentence, and it should still hit harder in the first three seconds than a fanfare, because
T5's timbre snap-judgment happens before any word arrives and *a voice with no bed under it is unusual.*

## 2. THE DECISION THAT SHAPED EVERY LYRIC: THE NEW NAME IS NEVER SPOKEN

Taken at step 06 and held through all four drafts. **The song's central noun is the one word it refuses to
say.** Everything in the song says it — the maps, the placemats, the ferry board, the hall — and the singer
says *"the new name," "that word," "the word underneath."*

**Three things it buys, none of which cost anything:**
1. **It is the stance, made audible** (A-D3). Refusal is a position a listener can hear without being told.
2. **It keeps both chairs occupiable** (R5). An actual new name empties one of them instantly.
3. **It survives the news cycle.** The mechanism travels; the news item does not.

## 4. THE ACT-TWO PROBLEM, AND HOW IT WAS SOLVED IN EACH SONG

Act II is the hardest act in every three-act song: it has to be a genuinely different record **and** it has
to be caused. **Four different answers, so the four songs do not converge:**

- **V1 — a break.** The reprinting *is* mechanical repetition at speed, and a breakbeat is literally a
  cut-up piece of somebody else's record re-used under other names. The material argues the subject.
  ⭐ **And it obliges a debt that is normally invisible** — see the dedication.
- **V2 — the brass.** The party is Act II because that is where the money went. The hands keep working
  underneath it, and Act II ends with them still working.
- **V3 — a work stomp.** Erasure is physical: a scraper, a ladder, flakes in a bag. The rhythm is the labour.
- **V4 — patter over a break.** T4 exactly: *flight of ideas is a thought-process finding* — topic-jumping
  on sound and association, *"the listener borne along by the flow."* ⛔ **B-D3: mania is not speed.** The
  BPM is stated once and never modulates; what accelerates is the **association rate**, in the words.
