# PAIR 05 — STEP 07 · SONG GUIDES
### four songs, one clock, twelve acts, eight seams · **SUNNA R5**

**Global, all four:** 156 BPM, unchanged, no tempo automation. Female lead, full chest, close and
bright, no whisper. Drums **+8 to +14 ms late** against the grid; bass **locked on** the grid.
Master −14 LUFS integrated, ≥10 dB crest. ⛔ No riser, no build, no drop, no airhorn, no supersaw
wall, no generic trap hats, no ritardando, no fade-out, **no gap at either seam.**

⭐ **ATTACCA is free and I am not spending budget defending it (C-D2).** The generator fills voids.
What I spend budget on is the thing that is *not* free: **contrast AT the cut, not after it.**

# V1 — **"LEAVE IT ON"**
### *THE ASK* — she asks for it out loud and it is granted

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | one **held tone that never re-strikes** for the length of the record, and the three words **"Leave it on."** landing on it |
| **arc** | **Trance → half-time gospel-soul round → chopped breakbeat** |
| **bars** | **64 / 32 / 48** = 144 bars = **44% / 22% / 33%** = **3:42** |
| **body** | a call **answered from a lower register in stacked thirds** |
| **chassis** | clipped electronic drums · muscular synthetic sub · hard transient edits · spectral restart at both seams · bright parallel vocal stack |
| **setting** | flat tar roof, four storeys, two in the morning, still and warm, bare feet, phone face down |
| **wolf** | June: somebody took the light out of the stairwell; she stopped coming up after dark; she lost a summer of the roof. **Resolves — she puts one back.** |
| **fact** | **"a hundred per cent"**, answered: *"Nobody gets a hundred per cent."* |
| **the hole** | bar 60: the pluck's third sixteenth is muted for one bar and never explained |

### THE FORM
**ACT I — Trance, bars 1–64.** Dry filtered sixteenth pluck, one note per beat, offbeat open hat,
the held tone underneath from bar 1. **The voice is in bar 1, over whatever the arrangement is
doing.** Sub enters flat at bar 17 and never moves again. ⛔ No pad wash, no riser into anything.

**SEAM 1 (bar 64→65) — an associative word-leap: OUT.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** The last word of Act I is *"out"* — as in *extinguished*. The first word of Act II
  is *"Out"* — as in **outdoors, come out here.** ⭐ The genre changes on a word, not a reason.
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** Before: she is on her own, cataloguing what she can see. After: she has
  **asked**, out loud, for company. The predicament moves from *I can see everything* to
  *I have said what I want and now somebody has to answer.*
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** On the downbeat: the sixteenth pluck stops re-striking,
  **the sub is removed entirely**, the felt pulse halves to 78 while the clock stays at 156, and
  what is left is two voices in thirds, one dry clap on 3, and the held tone — which is now
  audible for the first time because everything masking it has gone. **Density falls off a cliff on
  the beat.** ⭐ **Changed layer: percussion grid + low end. Invariant: the held tone and the clock.**

**ACT II — half-time gospel-soul round, bars 65–96.** Voices in stacked thirds entering a bar
apart, one dry clipped clap on 3, no sub, no kit. ⭐ **This is the quiet act and it is LOUDER in the
mids than Act I** — close, wide, full chest. That is what stops it being a breakdown. **The hope
line lives here**, plainly, no hush.

**SEAM 2 (bar 96→97) — an event: a window answers.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** She sings *"Is anybody up?"* and on the next beat a window goes up and a kitchen
  light comes on one floor away. **Something happens.**
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** She is no longer the only one awake. The question turns over from *will I
  get it* to *how much can we spend before it changes over.* **Finitude as fuel, never grief.**
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** On the beat: chopped breakbeat with 32nd edits, the sub
  returns an octave down **and never moves again**, the vocal jumps from two voices to a
  crowd-width unison. ⭐ **Changed layer: vocal width + percussion. Invariant: the sub.** ⛔ There is
  no riser into this and no silence before it — Act III starts over Act II's last held tone.

**ACT III — chopped breakbeat, bars 97–144.** 32nd edits, vocal chops answering the lead, held tone
finally sitting on top of the spectrum because the fast material sits under it. **Ends one bar
early, mid-word, last chord unplayed.**

# V2 — **"EVERYTHING AT ONCE"**
### *THE INVENTORY* — a list that keeps arriving, and its last item is the thing she wanted

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | a **four-note bass figure** whose note-order never changes, and the five syllables **"All of it at once."** |
| **arc** | **Nu-disco strut → half-time synth hymn → Trance** |
| **bars** | **60 / 40 / 52** = 152 bars = **39% / 26% / 34%** = **3:54** |
| **body** | a **melodic forward bass motion** that arrives a hair early every bar |
| **chassis** | FM/wavetable sub · micro-sampled punctuation · formant-shaped doubles · current lift geometry |
| **setting** | top deck of an empty multi-storey car park, painted arrows, wind doing nothing much, just after three, a coat she is not wearing |
| **wolf** | ⭐ **none.** Pleasure doing no job, unredeemed |
| **fact** | **"fifteen days old"**, answered: *"That's nothing. That's a fortnight."* |
| **the hole** | the bass figure drops its fourth note for the whole of Act II and comes back with it |

### THE FORM
**ACT I — nu-disco strut, bars 1–60.** The bass figure IS the tune and it is present before the
first line and after the last (**the hum test**). Dry claps, no pads, no guitar, no horn section —
**the strut is the bass's timing, not an ensemble.**

**SEAM 1 (bar 60→61) — an event: she turns the headlights off.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** She has been pointing two beams at a wall as if the wall were the thing. She says
  *"Watch this"* and kills them.
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** She stops **illuminating** and starts being **illuminated**. From
  performing the view to receiving it — and the moment the beams go, the moon is doing the work and
  everything has an edge on it.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** Drums cut dead on the beat **over a root that is already
  sounding** (⛔ not a silence — the bass figure's first note is held under the cut, so attacca is
  intact), the spectrum restarts wide and formant-stacked, felt pulse halves to 78.
  ⭐ **Changed layer: drums + spectrum. Invariant: the bass figure, now held as one root.**

**ACT II — half-time synth hymn, bars 61–100.** Choir-width formant stack, no drums, the bass
figure sustaining its root. ⛔ **No whisper. Full chest, close, loud in the mids.**

**SEAM 2 (bar 100→101) — an associative word-leap: CHANGE.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** Act II ends *"...and I want to be here for the change—"* (alteration). Act III
  opens **"Change. Change in my coat."** (coins). ⭐ A connection nobody authorised.
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** The want stops being a wish and becomes an **inventory item** — the thing
  she wants goes onto the list with the aerials and the crane, which is what makes the list's last
  entry possible.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** Sixteenth pluck enters on the beat at full density, the
  sub returns and never moves, the phrase turns over into a different cadence.
  ⭐ **Changed layer: cadence + density. Invariant: the sub.**

**ACT III — Trance, bars 101–152.** The anchor territory arrives last and is the arrival. The list
recites itself at speed and its final item is **"still here."** Ends one bar early, mid-word.

# V3 — **"DON'T TELL ME HOW FULL"**
### *THE REFUSAL OF THE NUMBER* — awe against measurement

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | a **two-note falling call**, whistle-shaped, answered late across space, and the words **"Don't tell me how full."** |
| **arc** | **Half-time dub-space → Trance → gospel-soul handclap round** |
| **bars** | **40 / 56 / 40** = 136 bars = **29% / 41% / 29%** = **3:29** |
| **body** | **the hole where the third note should be**, and an answer that arrives late across space |
| **chassis** | granular and transient edits · spectral motion · pitch-shaped doubles · present-day transition grammar |
| **setting** | a kitchen window she has not opened in a year, then the fire escape; half four; cold enough to see her breath; a mug going cold on the sill |
| **wolf** | ⭐ **none.** |
| **fact** | ⭐ **zero numeric facts, by design.** The refusal is the song |
| **the hole** | the third stroke of every call is missing for the whole of Act I and arrives once, in Act III, played by a hand |

⭐ **This variation's shape is deliberately inverted: the MIDDLE act is the biggest.** V1, V2 and V4
grow toward the end; V3 puts its mass in the centre and gets **smaller and warmer** to finish, so
the four songs do not share one dramatic curve. *A pair that shares one curve has one song.*

### THE FORM
**ACT I — half-time dub-space, bars 1–40.** Felt 78. Enormous negative space; the call goes out and
the answer comes back late, from somewhere else in the field. ⛔ No organ, no spring echo, no
skank, no walking bass — **the inheritance here is the SPACE, not the band.**

**SEAM 1 (bar 40→41) — an associative word-leap: FULL.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** Act I ends on *"full"* (the moon, the number she refuses). Act II opens **"Full"**
  — a full mug, a full room, *fed up.* ⭐ Same word, three meanings, one beat.
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** She stops refusing and starts **spending.** Before the seam she is
  defending her right not to measure; after it she is out of the window and using the night.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** ⭐ **The negative space closes on the beat** — the hole
  fills with a continuous sixteenth grid, the felt pulse doubles to 156, granular edits everywhere.
  **Silence is the thing that disappears.** ⭐ **Changed layer: space/density. Invariant: the call.**

**ACT II — Trance, bars 41–96.** The anchor territory, in the middle, and it is the biggest act.

**SEAM 2 (bar 96→97) — an associative word-leap: STILL.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** Act II ends *"...and it is still—"* (motionless). Act III opens **"Still"** —
  *nevertheless, anyway, even so.*
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** The song stops being about the sky and becomes about **the people awake
  under it.** She goes from looking at one thing to being one of several.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** All programmed percussion is replaced **on the beat** by
  hands — claps and stamps only, dry, close, in the room. ⭐ **Changed layer: the entire percussion
  source. Invariant: the two-note call, now sung by everyone.** ⛔ Not a drop; the low end
  *leaves*, it does not arrive.

**ACT III — gospel-soul handclap round, bars 97–136.** No kit, no sub, hands and voices, and the
missing third stroke of the call finally arrives — played by a hand. Ends one bar early, mid-word.

# V4 — **"I CAN SEE YOU FROM HERE"**
### *THE DUET WITH THE ROOM* — call-and-answer from bar one

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | a **five-stroke clap cell** and the words **"I can see you from here."** |
| **arc** | **Trance (dry, minor, arp-only) → footwork skitter → half-time soul-gospel wide finish** |
| **bars** | **52 / 56 / 36** = 144 bars = **36% / 39% / 25%** = **3:42** |
| **body** | the **late pickup** — a punctuation that arrives behind the beat on purpose |
| **chassis** | footwork-grade 32nd-note percussion · clipped sub · vocal chops as percussion · crowd-width stack |
| **setting** | the middle of a street, wet from a hose, other windows above, ten to five |
| **wolf** | last winter she asked for something out loud on this street and it stayed dark, and she did not ask again for a year. **Resolves — the windows answer.** |
| **fact** | none |
| **the hole** | the clap cell's fifth stroke is absent every second bar of Act I and returns permanently at seam 2 |

### THE FORM
**ACT I — Trance, dry and minor, arp only, bars 1–52.** No pads at all. The clap cell and the arp,
and a late synth stab that is always behind the beat and is never quantized. ⭐ **The lateness is
the swagger.**

**SEAM 1 (bar 52→53) — an event: the streetlights cut out on a timer.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** The street lighting goes off on its own schedule. ⭐⭐ **And everything gets
  BRIGHTER** — because the orange goes and the moon is doing the work and now she can see the far
  end of the road, the windows, the wet, and her own hard shadow. **This is the pair's best seam
  and it is a physical fact, not a metaphor.**
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** Her sight range triples on one beat. Before: she can see her own doorway.
  After: she can see everybody's, and she is visible from all of them.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** ⭐ **Everything jumps an octave up** on the downbeat — the
  arp inverts, the percussion becomes a 32nd triplet skitter, the sub clips and shortens, the
  vocal doubles at the top of her chest voice. **A record does not usually get brighter when the
  lights go out. Mine does, and that is the whole idea.**
  ⭐ **Changed layer: register/spectrum. Invariant: the clap cell.**

**ACT II — footwork skitter, bars 53–108.** 156 is footwork's native clock, so this is the same
grid heard differently — nothing is sped up. Vocal chops used as percussion; the late stab keeps
being late. **The wolf is named here, in one breath, and the band does not slow down.**

**SEAM 2 (bar 108→109) — an event: somebody sings it back.**
- **(i) CAUSE.** From a window above, another voice lands on her five-stroke cell. **The thing she
  stopped doing a year ago works.**
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** The song stops being a broadcast and becomes a duet. Her want has been
  answered by a stranger she cannot see and will not name.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** On the beat, the 32nd grid stops and the felt pulse halves
  to 78; the clap cell is picked up **by hands** and doubles in width; the vocal opens into
  crowd-width thirds. ⭐ **Changed layer: the room joins — one voice becomes many. Invariant: the
  clap cell.**

**ACT III — half-time soul-gospel wide finish, bars 109–144.** ⭐ The fastest song in the run ends
at a felt 78, wide open, with hands. Ends one bar early, mid-word.

## THE R5 PRE-DISPATCH AUDITS — run on all four before any style field was frozen

- ⭐ **CENTURY AUDIT.** Delete every lineage/era word from each style field. What remains — clipped
  electronic drums, synthetic sub, transient and granular edits, formant/pitch-shaped doubles,
  spectral restart, crowd-width stacks, digital negative space — still specifies a record made now,
  four of four. **PASS.**
- ⭐ **MUSEUM-GLASS AUDIT.** Mute the inherited marker (the stacked-third answer, the forward bass,
  the hole, the late pickup) and a complete current song remains in each. Mute the chassis and each
  one loses its defining world. **PASS, and never the reverse.**
- ⭐ **EXCLUDE AUDIT.** No exclude field prohibits electronic drums, synthetic sub, pitch/formant
  processing, digital edits or modern transitions. Each bans the specific cliché threatening that
  song: the festival edit, the moon lexicon, the period reconstruction. **PASS.**
- ⭐ **ENSEMBLE AUDIT.** Historical markers per prompt: V1 one (stacked-third answer) · V2 one
  (forward bass) · V3 one (the hole/late answer) · V4 one (the late pickup). **Never more than
  one. No complete old band anywhere. PASS.**
- ⭐ **CONSEQUENCE LEDGER.** Eight seams, six distinct consequence classes, **zero drops, zero
  risers, zero sidechain answers.** The set's one club/drop consequence is not spent here.
- ⭐ **ANGER AUDIT.** Zero distortion pedals in the pair. Nothing here is cross. **PASS.**
- ⭐ **CLUB AUDIT.** Zero complete club-derived beds. Trance is present as an act in all four, but
  never as a full four-on-the-floor club song with a build and a drop — ⛔ that is the collapse
  template I named, not the plan. **PASS.**
- ⭐ **SETTING AUDIT.** Roof · car park deck · fire escape · street. Four rooms, four closing
  places, one domestic opening that is abandoned inside thirty seconds. **PASS.**
- ⭐ **R5 FIRST-LISTEN TEST.** *Does this sound like a woman making a record now, with enough
  inherited bodily memory to move differently from everybody else?* The first answer is not a
  decade — it is a clock and a chant. **PASS.**

## THE R3.2 SWAP TESTS — written in the same breath as the clause they falsify

- ⭐ **SEAM SWAP.** My most-specified object is the seam, so: swap any seam block between any two
  variations. **All twelve swaps break**, because the four songs use four different seam
  architectures — V1 word/event, V2 event/word, V3 word/word, V4 event/event — and no two seams
  share a consequence class *and* a pole type.
- ⭐ **RETURN SWAP (the ending, where the formula hides).** Swap the closing sections. **They break:
  four different last words, four different rooms, four different arrivals** — a light changing
  hands over chimneys · a list finding its last item · a missing stroke arriving in a hand · a
  street singing back. ⛔ And the endings are not four nouns in one sentence shape: they are four
  different *sentences*.
- ⭐ **WOLF SWAP.** Two wolves, non-interchangeable (a place given up vs. a thing stopped), and two
  songs with no wolf at all.
- ⭐ **POLE SWAP.** Impossible by construction — four different **kinds** of object.
