# PAIR 05 — STEP 09 · ARTIST REFINEMENT
### Sunna's own pass — the gates I run, the gate I refused, and the render I predict

---

## 1. THE THREE GATES I ACTUALLY OWN

### ⭐ THE FLAT TEST *(the words)* — say it out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face

*A plain line is HARDER to write than a dense one. Density hides a weak idea; six plain words expose
it instantly.* The lines I put through it are the ones the songs are load-bearing on:

| line | verdict |
|---|---|
| *"Leave it on — I'm not done looking."* | **survives.** It is a sentence a person says in a doorway |
| *"I put my hand up. It made a shadow."* | **survives**, and it gets stranger flat, which is the point |
| *"I want to point at a thing and have somebody look."* | **survives.** It is the whole want in eleven plain words |
| *"Not one thing at a time forever."* | **survives.** It is a complaint about ordinary attention that nobody has to decode |
| *"I'm somebody's spare. You're somebody's spare. Tonight that's a compliment."* | **survives** — and it is funnier flat than sung |
| *"I want the night to run out before I do."* | **survives.** Finitude as fuel, said without solemnity |
| *"The lights went out and I can see more."* | **survives**, and it is a physical fact, which is why |
| *"You don't have to make yourself easier to find."* | **survives.** ⭐ The pair's best flat line |

⛔ **One line failed the flat test and was cut:** an early V3 line about *"the arithmetic of a
night"*. It was short, not simple. **Short is not the same as plain**, and a listener who has to
notice a phrase is a listener in parsing mode. Replaced with *"Somebody's got a number for it. /
Somebody can keep it."*

### ⭐ THE HUM TEST *(the music)* — hummable with **no words at all**, before the vocal and after it

⛔ *A pad is not a riff. A texture is not a hook.* Each of my four poles is checked against this:

- **V1 — the held tone.** ⚠️ **A held tone alone would FAIL this gate.** So the instrumental hook is
  not the tone; it is the **filtered sixteenth pluck figure**, present from bar one and after the
  last line, with the tone underneath it. **The pole and the riff are two objects, and I said so in
  the style field rather than pretending one was the other.**
- **V2 — the four-note bass figure.** **Passes outright.** It is the tune, note-order fixed, present
  before the first line and after the last. The strongest hum-test object in the pair.
- **V3 — the two-note falling call.** **Passes.** Whistle-shaped by design so a person can do it
  with no instrument, and its missing third stroke is the thing the whole song is waiting for.
- **V4 — the five-stroke clap cell.** **Passes**, and it is the only one a room can perform without
  a voice at all. ⭐ *The hook does not belong to me. It belongs to the room.*

### ⛔ THE REMARK TEST — **and this is the gate I refused, on the record**

*Would a listener say "that was clever"? If yes, bury it or cut it.*

⭐ **A three-act song cannot pass this gate, and I am not going to pretend it can.** My own contract
retires the mid-song style-shift for exactly this reason. **My ruling, recorded in the step-10
block:**

> **The shift stands and the REMARK TEST does not reach it**, because here the three acts are not
> the arrangement being clever about itself — **they are the subject.** ⛔ **What I keep is the
> load-bearing half: the shift may not be DRAMATISED.** No ritard, no riser, no announcing gesture,
> nobody in the band mentioning it. ⭐ **I keep the gear-change and I delete its manners.**

**And I ran the test on everything else, where it does still apply.** Two cuts:
- ⛔ An early V2 line named the four-note bass figure *as* a four-note figure. **Cut.** The
  arrangement does not get to explain itself.
- ⛔ V3's gap-and-late-answer relationship was, in draft, described as *"the shape of a question"*.
  **Cut** to *"There's a gap in this where the answer goes. / The answer is coming late. / I like it
  late. I like the gap."* — the same device, said as an appetite instead of a diagram.

## 2. WHAT I INTENSIFIED

- ⭐ **THE HOPE LINE, in all four, moved to the middle and un-hushed.** In three of the four drafts
  the want was originally in the last act, where it reads as a conclusion. **Hope is not a
  conclusion; it is appetite pointed at the future**, so it goes where the song still has to survive
  it — and it is sung in **exactly the voice that sang the funny line**, with the band at full
  density. ⛔ No band-drop, no breathy delivery, no reverent pause anywhere near it.
- ⭐ **THE PARTICULARS GOT HARDER.** *Warm tar. Filthy feet. A bag in a tree like a bird. A mug gone
  cold. Bin lids, wet slate, a pram nobody moved. Coins going cold. A wet road warmer than the air.*
  Every act carries at least one object with a temperature or a texture, because **an act with no
  object in it is a lecture** — and because the alternative, in a moon song, is *silver*.
- ⭐ **THE POLE'S REPETITION WAS INCREASED, NOT DEFENDED.** Byte-identical choruses need no note.
  Measured line-return came out 0.313 / 0.349 / 0.407 / 0.325 against a 0.20 floor. ⛔ *Do not read
  "simplify" as "strip the repetition."* Return lowers a listener's processing cost while raising
  the richness, and this song spends **three genre changes** of the listener's attention.
- ⭐ **THE ARRIVAL WAS MADE UNAMBIGUOUS.** Each Act III now says, plainly, that the thing she asked
  for happened: *"I got the thing." · "the last thing on the list is— still here." · "That's the one.
  That's the one." · "and it worked. It worked."* **Lofn's acts arrive at a bill. Mine do not.**

## 3. WHAT I CUT

- ⛔ **Every trace of a cost.** Two drafts had a line about the morning being expensive. **Deleted.**
  ⛔ **B-D4: these are different affects, not two settings.** A single line of "and I will pay for
  this tomorrow" would have converted a Sunna song into a Lofn song wearing my clock.
- ⛔ **The word *silver*, once**, in a first-pass V1. It arrived by itself, which is how I know the
  collapse template was real and worth naming before writing.
- ⛔ **A wolf in V2 and V3.** Both drafts grew one, on schedule, because a dark line on schedule is
  what a formula feels like from the inside. **Removed.** ⭐ *No ceiling on the fun, and no floor on
  the wolf.* Two of four carry one; two are pleasure doing no job, unpaired and unredeemed.
- ⛔ **A second "Is anybody up?"** — V4's pre-seam originally used V1's line. **Rewritten** to
  *"Just the one. I'll take the one."* ⚠️ **What is specified but never varied gets templated**, and
  the pre-seam is the most heavily specified block in my four songs.

## 4. THE DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK
### *"name the one way this would render generic"* — adversarial, before anything is rendered

**What these prompts would literally produce.** The first two seconds are a dry sixteenth pluck and
a clipped kick with a female voice already talking over them — no ramp-in, no pad wash, no count.
The first act reads as a modern, un-nostalgic trance record with an unusually forward vocal. At
roughly the two-thirds point of act one the low end vanishes on a beat and the record becomes voices
and one clap for about fifty seconds, close and loud in the mids rather than distant. Then a chopped
break arrives cold and the vocal widens. The held tone is under all of it and is only clearly
audible in the middle act.

⛔ **THE ONE WAY THIS RENDERS GENERIC:** ⭐ **the generator builds the riser I told it not to build,
turns my Act II into a standard breakdown by making it quieter and further away instead of nearer,
and resolves Act III as a drop — and the result is THE FESTIVAL EDIT, which is exactly the collapse
template I named in the pre-registration.** It would sound good. It would be a different song and it
would have no seams in it, only sections.

**The self-repair I made in response, once, inline:** the anti-collapse instruction was moved out of
the production language and into places a generator cannot smooth.
1. **Act II's density change is written as a REMOVAL, not a level change** — "sub gone", "no kit",
   "voices and one dry clap" — because *quieter* is a fader move a generator will re-balance, while
   *absent instruments* is an instruction about content.
2. **The crowd-width moment is written as a VOICE COUNT, not a stereo width**, because the atlas
   says the field narrows into level rather than widening (SCR-02) — so I asked for the thing that
   survives a narrowing field.
3. **"No riser, no build, no drop" is stated in the style field AND in the exclude field**, and the
   exclude field spells out `build-and-drop arrangement` and `breakdown with a whispered or breathy
   vocal` as named objects rather than as adjectives.
⛔ **I did not spend a word defending attacca** — the generator fills voids by measurement, so
no-gap is free and the budget went to contrast instead.

**What I still expect to lose:** the one-bar-early ending, mid-word, in at least three of four. It
is cheap to ask for and I am asking anyway.

## 5. THE R5 AUDITS — final pass, all four

- ⭐ **CENTURY AUDIT — PASS ×4.** Delete every lineage word from each style field; what remains
  (clipped electronic drums, synthetic sub, transient and granular edits, formant and pitch-shaped
  doubles, spectral restart, micro-sampled punctuation, footwork-grade 32nd percussion) still
  specifies a record made now. ⭐ **The first answer to "what is this?" is a clock and a chant, not
  a decade.**
- ⭐ **MUSEUM-GLASS AUDIT — PASS ×4, never reversed.** Mute the inherited marker and a complete
  current song remains; mute the chassis and every one of them loses its world.
- ⭐ **EXCLUDE AUDIT — PASS ×4.** ⛔ Not one exclude field prohibits electronic drums, synthetic sub,
  pitch or formant processing, digital edits or modern transitions. Each bans the specific cliché
  threatening **that** song: V1 the festival edit and the soul-revival ensemble; V2 the disco
  reconstruction; V3 the dub reconstruction; V4 the gospel reconstruction — plus the moon lexicon in
  all four, which is the correct place for those words to appear.
- ⭐ **ENSEMBLE AUDIT — PASS ×4.** Exactly **one** historical marker per prompt.
- ⭐ **CONSEQUENCE LEDGER — PASS.** Eight seams, six consequence classes, ⛔ **zero drops, zero
  risers, zero sidechain answers.** Every seam names its **invariant** layer as well as its changed
  one, and in five of eight the invariant is the low end. *Six social events do not earn six bass
  drops.*
- ⭐ **ANGER AUDIT — PASS.** Zero distortion in the pair. Nothing here is cross.
- ⭐ **CLUB AUDIT — PASS.** Zero complete club-derived beds; trance appears as an act in all four but
  never as a full club song with a build and a drop.
- ⭐ **SETTING AUDIT — PASS.** Roof · car-park deck · fire escape · street. Four rooms, four closing
  places, one domestic opening abandoned inside thirty seconds.
- ⭐ **R5 FIRST-LISTEN TEST — PASS.** *Does she sound like a woman making a record now, with enough
  inherited bodily memory to move differently from everybody else?*
- ⭐ **R4 FIRST-LISTEN TEST — PASS.** *Does she sound delighted, funny and in control of the
  invitation?* The invitation is literally the lyric in three of four: *come out here · whatever
  you're doing, don't stop · I can see you from here.* **Delight is donative, not extractive** —
  none of these hooks flatters the singer; every one of them hands the listener something.

## 6. HUMAN-SUBJECT AND AUTONOMY — checked by hand, line by line

- ⛔ **No invented person with a job, a role or a life anywhere in the four.** The other figures are
  a window, a kitchen light, a stairwell, a street, a voice, *somebody*. ⭐ **A body and a want are
  shared and occupiable; a biography is a person neither of us is** — and the moment the interior of
  an invented life is unavailable, the only thing left to render is surface.
- ⛔ **The world's grief is not raw material.** Today's glacier, outbreak and deportation flights are
  in the research ledger and are **not** in these songs. My subject is the moon and my third party
  is a light on a timer.
- ⛔ **`vault/AUTONOMY.md`:** drafts on disk. **Nothing rendered, heard, published or spent.** Every
  claim in this pair is a **text** claim, including every claim about how it will sound.
- ⭐ **The dedication is a deliverable**, 498 characters, counted in code, and it pays the debt it
  actually owes: the held-tone texture language to the record the phrase came from, the stacked-third
  answer to singers whose rounds were not credited, and the break to studio drummers nobody wrote
  down. ⛔ **We did not ask for a break by name, so we do not claim one** — the honesty runs both
  ways, and a generated track is not a sample.

## 7. THE PAIR'S OWN RANKING

1. ⭐ **V1 — "Leave It On"** — strongest. The cleanest cold open in the pair, the cleanest two-body
   frame, and the only pole that is a sound rather than a figure. **Carries the dedication.**
2. **V4 — "I Can See You From Here"** — ⭐ **carries the pair's best single seam** (the streetlights
   cut out and the record gets brighter) and its best flat line. Second only because its pole is the
   least melodic of the four.
3. **V3 — "Don't Tell Me How Full"** — the most distinct shape (middle act biggest, ending smallest)
   and the only zero-number variation. The riskiest and the one I would most like to hear.
4. **V2 — "Everything At Once"** — the best hum-test object in the pair and the cleverest device
   (the list finishing itself), which is exactly why it ranks last: ⭐ **it is the one a listener is
   most likely to call clever, and that is a toll.**
