## §0 — THE PAIR-LEVEL CONTRACT (binding on all four guides)

### THE POLE (R4), one sentence

> ⭐⭐ **One reed-organ tone held unbroken at the same pitch through all three acts, and above it a short flat
> phrase sung on the same falling third every time it appears — re-clothed by each genre, never
> re-harmonised, never sung faster or slower — and each variation keeps the tone and swaps only which words
> sit on it.**

⭐ **Why the pole is a HELD TONE and not a riff, and this is the pair's one real idea:** a drone both *supplies
the harmony* and *forbids the modulation.* It is the thing that feeds the song and the thing that fences it.
**The pole is the tether.** So the form and the subject are one object, which is `ONE SEAM` at the difficulty
rather than a technique laid on top of a theme.

⭐ **And it is the radial figure made audible:** three genres are three arcs; the tone is the fixed pole they
turn around; *the motion is imperceptible* because the centre never moves.

**Per-variation words on the tone:** V1 *"Say how far."* · V2 *"Green at the edge."* ·
V3 *"Nothing ever pulled."* · V4 *"The word for it is feeding."*

### THE STANCE (A-D3), identical in all three acts of all four songs
**She wants the length said out loud.** Not shortened, not removed — **named.**

### THE COLD OPEN (R1), pair-level ruling
Every song's **first sung line is its own pole**, unaccompanied or nearly so, over a drone that is already
sounding when the record starts. ⭐ **Zero preamble by construction:** the hook is line one, so there is no
line before it to write. **T5:** the listener's snap judgement lands in seconds 1–3 and gets the hummable
thing inside it.
⛔ **We do not ask the generator to start loud.** The drone is already up when the voice arrives — the voice
is in front of whatever ramp is built underneath. (`L22 THE GRAIN LAW`: run with the grain.)

### ATTACCA (R2.2 / C-D2)
Both seams land **on the beat**, no gap, no ritard. ⭐ Cheap: the generator fills voids (`interrupting-
silence`: SMOOTHED, 2/2). **We do not spend spec budget defending it** — one clause in the style field, one
exclude token, done.

### ⛔ ONE NUMBER PER SONG
One number-word, at the emotional hinge, **answered rather than recited**, written as a word. The word *one*
is banned outright in sung lines across the pair so the count is unambiguous when measured.

# V1 — **SAY HOW FAR** ⭐ (flagship)

**Facet F-b — THE MEASURE.** *A length was chosen by something that will never live inside it.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | *"Say how far."* — three words, falling third, over the held reed tone |
| **key / tempo** | D minor-ish modal, drone on D · **72 BPM**, one pulse for the whole record |
| **acts** | **I. chamber folk** (≈42%, 40 lines) → **II. handclap gospel-stomp** (≈24%, 23) → **III. vocal round over the reed drone** (≈34%, 33) |
| **hinge number** | **nine** — the answer arrives with **no unit** |
| **first person** | yes |

### The three acts

**ACT I — CHAMBER FOLK.** Contralto close, chest, no vibrato. Twelve-string, upright bass, brushed kit, reed
drone underneath from bar one. She describes the ring: bare in the middle, a grey road worn round the post,
green standing up like a wall exactly where the reach ends. She asks for the length. **Object:** a hand's
width of bare ground.

**ACT II — HANDCLAP GOSPEL-STOMP.** The pulse does not change; the **subdivision doubles.** Boots and hands
on packed ground, a room of voices answering, no drum kit and no pitched instrument except the drone. The
vocal opens out — mid-room, open-throat, still not belted. ⭐ **It is a travelling song sung by something
that cannot travel**, and the listener is *"borne along by the flow"* (T4). **Object:** the green wall at the
far side that never gets closer.

**ACT III — VOCAL ROUND OVER THE DRONE.** Hands stop dead; the drone is already there. One voice, then the
same phrase entering a bar behind itself, then a bar behind that — a canon that chases itself and never
lands. Half-time. The record ends on the tone alone. **Object:** the post, cold, and the cord coming out of
the body.

### The two seams (R3 — all three conditions each)

**SEAM 1 · Act I → Act II · CAUSE: the word "bound."**
Act I's last sung word is *bound* (tied). Act II's first sung word is *Bound* (travelling toward).
⭐ Immediately preceded by the **event**: the answer *nine* arrives with no unit. Both kinds of cause fire at
once — the word is the declared one.
- **state change:** she stops asking and starts *travelling*. The predicament is measurably worse: she is now
  spending the last of the reachable ground on a journey that cannot leave the circle.
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on that beat — twelve-string, bass and brushes stop; boots, hands and a
  room of voices enter at double subdivision; the voice moves from close to mid-room. Density up, register
  up, instrumentation swapped, tempo constant. **The drone does not move.**

**SEAM 2 · Act II → Act III · CAUSE: the word "round."**
Act II's last sung word is *round* (coming round again). Act III's first sung word is *Round* — and it opens
an actual **round**, the canon form.
- **state change:** she stops travelling and starts *counting*. The want changes from *tell me the length* to
  *say that it was chosen.* And the cost lands: the green is finished, and **nothing ever pulled.**
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on the beat — every hand and boot stops, the voices drop to one, the
  subdivision halves back, the phrase begins entering against itself. Maximum bodies to a single body, in one
  beat. **The drone does not move.**

### Render prediction
See step 10 §A. Short form: the stomp will arrive as a ramp rather than a cut in at least one take, and the
round will be partly harmonised into a pad. **The one thing that must survive is the drone's constancy.**

# V2 — **GREEN AT THE EDGE**

**Facet F-c — THE EVIDENCE.** *Read the ground. The geometry accuses; nobody is in the frame until late.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | *"Green at the edge."* — with its answering half *"Bare in the middle."* |
| **key / tempo** | modal, drone on G · **70 BPM** throughout, including the stomp |
| **acts** | **I. shape-note a cappella hymn** (≈26%, 24 lines) → **II. folk & singer-songwriter** (≈42%, 39) → **III. capstan work-song stomp** (≈32%, 29) |
| **hinge number** | **seven** — the count of rings, *all the same size* |
| **first person** | no in Act I, yes from Act II |

### The three acts

**ACT I — SHAPE-NOTE A CAPPELLA HYMN.** Voices only, open fifths and bare fourths, no instrument but the
drone. Impersonal, third person, doctrinal in tone — a statement of a general fact about ground. ⭐ **The
coldest possible opening and the most legible:** unaccompanied voices at second zero.

**ACT II — FOLK & SINGER-SONGWRITER** *(the anchor, and it arrives second)*. One voice, close. Twelve-string,
upright bass, brushed kit. The general fact becomes **a place she walked to**: the peg, the flattened grass,
the smell of eaten ground, and the count of the other rings.

**ACT III — CAPSTAN WORK-SONG STOMP.** ⭐ **A capstan is the radial figure with people in it**: a crew walks a
circle around a fixed post to raise an anchor. Same tempo, half-time stomp on one and three, boots, a
hand-drum, call-and-answer. **And the anchor does not come up.** The labour is the circle. The song ends
mid-pull.

### The two seams

**SEAM 1 · hymn → folk · CAUSE: the word "still."**
Act I ends *"...and it is standing still."* Act II opens *"Still, I went out to look at it."*
(motionless → nevertheless).
- **state change:** a general fact becomes a **particular place with her in it.** Before: it is true of ground.
  After: it is true of this ground, and she is standing on it.
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on the beat — the voices vanish, a twelve-string strikes, one close voice
  with breath left in, upright bass and brushes underneath. Choir to soloist, cathedral to small room, in one
  beat. Tempo constant. **The drone does not move.**

**SEAM 2 · folk → shanty · CAUSE: the word "give."**
Act II ends *"...and I felt the ground give."* Act III opens on *"Give! — Give!"*, the pull-call
(yield → heave).
- **state change:** she stops looking and starts **working** — trying to raise the thing that anchors her,
  which is the most futile act available, and she knows it while doing it.
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on the beat — the twelve-string stops dead, boots and a hand-drum land on
  one and three at half-time, a room of voices answers every call, the vocal moves from close and breathy to
  shouted mid-room. **The drone does not move.**

# V3 — **NOTHING EVER PULLED**

**Facet F-d — THE NON-EVENT.** *The cord never went tight. There was slack the whole way. That is called
freedom, and it is the outrage.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | *"Nothing ever pulled."* |
| **key / tempo** | modal, drone on A · **68 BPM** |
| **acts** | **I. folk & singer-songwriter** (≈44%, 40 lines) → **II. wide slow desert-country with steel** (≈34%, 30) → **III. bare unaccompanied hymn** (≈22%, 20) |
| **hinge number** | **three** — three times round today, and the third one told her |
| **first person** | yes, and it is the hardest of the four |

### ⭐ The structural inversion — this is why V3 exists

Every other song about a tether gets **denser** as it goes: the band arrives, the walls close in, the strings
swell. **V3 gets emptier and more spacious.** Act II is the widest, most open, most beautiful-sounding music
in the pair — pedal steel, a slow backbeat, a large room — **because the freedom is real.** Nothing is
holding her. She can go anywhere inside the reach and she does. ⛔ **And that is exactly how the ground gets
used up.** Then Act III takes the instruments away, because there is nothing left to play them over.

### The two seams

**SEAM 1 · folk → country · CAUSE: the word "slack."**
Act I ends *"...and my hand came back with slack."* Act II opens *"Slack. / Slack is a country."*
(loose cord → an amount of open room).
- **state change:** she stops testing the cord and **goes out into the space.** She accepts the freedom as
  genuine — which it is — and starts spending it.
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on the beat — the close guitar is replaced by a slow wide backbeat, pedal
  steel and tremolo; the room opens from small to very large; the voice steps back from close-mic to
  mid-distance. Tempo constant, space transformed. **The drone does not move.**

**SEAM 2 · country → bare hymn · CAUSE: the word "gone."**
Act II ends *"...and the green is gone."* Act III opens *"Gone is not taken."*
(absent → and then re-read: nothing was taken from her; she ate it).
- **state change:** the arithmetic is done and the object of the complaint changes. She is no longer
  describing her own situation — **she is asking, for the next ring, that the number be said.** The song
  becomes third-party at the exact moment it becomes quietest. ⭐ This is R6 discharged in the form.
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on the beat — steel, kit and bass all stop; one unaccompanied voice and the
  drone; half-time. Widest to barest in one beat. **The drone does not move.**

# V4 — **THE WORD FOR IT**

**Facet F-a — THE NAMING** (+ F-f, the survivor). *Two words for the same length, and the soft word is the
lie.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **pole** | *"The word for it is feeding."* |
| **key / tempo** | modal, drone on C · **74 BPM**, Act III rocking half-time |
| **acts** | **I. folk & singer-songwriter** (≈38%, 36 lines) → **II. chamber-baroque processional** (≈26%, 24) → **III. lullaby** (≈36%, 34) |
| **hinge number** | **two** — *"Two words for the same length."* |
| **first person** | yes in Act I, addressed in Act II, sung-to in Act III |

### ⭐ Why the third act is a LULLABY, and it is the coldest thing in this pair

Act II is the sound of officialdom being **kind to you**: harmonium, pizzicato strings, a slow formal
processional, a voice further back and choir-adjacent. ⭐ **The genre is the euphemism.**
Then Act III: the arrangement **sings her to sleep in the language of care.** *Rest. You are fed.* A real
lullaby — soft, high, close, rocking — over the same unmoved drone.

⛔ **And the cost is the correct Lofn cost:** mania is attention that cannot be switched off, so the worst
available ending is **the attention switching off.** She stops asking *say how far.* The last time the pole
appears, it is sung as a comfort, by the fence, and she does not answer it.

### The two seams

**SEAM 1 · folk → processional · CAUSE: the word "keep."**
Act I ends *"...they call it my keep."* Act II opens *"Keep. / Keep to it."*
(upkeep/food → stay, hold to — and a keep is also a fortress; three meanings, one word).
- **state change:** the euphemism stops being something she is describing and becomes something **addressed to
  her.** She is inside the language now instead of outside it, and she starts answering in it.
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on the beat — the acoustic guitar stops; harmonium and plucked strings
  enter in a slow processional; the voice becomes formal, further back, no breath, no scoop. Intimate to
  ceremonial, in one beat. **The drone does not move.**

**SEAM 2 · processional → lullaby · CAUSE: the word "rest."**
Act II ends *"...and the rest is provided."* Act III opens *"Rest. / Rest is a word for it too."*
(the remainder → sleep).
- **state change:** instruction becomes comfort, and **the want is finally answered — with a lullaby instead
  of a number.** She stops asking. That is the cost.
- **audible contrast AT THE CUT:** on the beat — strings and harmonium drop to a single sustained reed; the
  pulse halves to a rocking two; the voice comes very close, soft, high and sweet for the first time in the
  record. **The drone does not move.**
