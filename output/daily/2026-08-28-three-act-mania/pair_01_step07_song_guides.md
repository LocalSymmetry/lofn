# PAIR 01 · STEP 07 — SONG GUIDES (four blueprints, locked before any line was written)
### `2026-08-28-three-act-mania` · **LOFN-PRIME** · ACCESSIBLE · NEWS · INDIGNATION

**Frozen at this step and binding on steps 08–10:** the pole, the stance, the two-body sentence, the eight
seam words, the act orders, the tempi, the line budgets, and the proportion targets.

---

## 0. THE SHARED SPINE (identical in all four — this is what makes them variations and not four songs)

- **POLE:** the four words **"It works better there."** on the same three-step falling figure over **one
  unbroken held reed-organ tone**, in all three acts of all four songs. ⛔ **Written with a full stop every
  single time, never an exclamation mark** — the line is flat and damning; the fervour lives in the section
  header, not in the punctuation. *(This also keeps every occurrence an exact repeat for `line_return`.)*
- **STANCE (A-D3):** she is standing where the thing still works, refusing to accept that a decision is
  weather, furious **on behalf of the working itself.** Identical in every act.
- **REGISTER (R6):** INDIGNATION, third-party. **The singer is not the one harmed.** The object is the
  decision — a motion, a minute, a hand going up.
- **R1 COLD OPEN:** ⭐ **the first sung line is the pole itself, quoted with contempt, and the second line
  answers it.** There is no line before it because the listener never heard the thing being quoted. **No
  `[Intro]` with sung words. No instruction to "start loud"** — the voice is simply in the first bar.
- **R2 ATTACCA:** both seams land **on the beat**, no gap, no fade, no ritard, no drum fill announcing the
  change. *(C-D2: this is free — the generator fills voids. No spec budget is spent defending it.)*
- **ENDING:** all four **stop mid-phrase**, on the antagonist's sentence, cut. No resolution, no outro.

## 1. ⛔ THE LINE BUDGET AND THE PROPORTION TARGETS

House gates: **70–120 sung lines**, target **78–110**; **mean words/line ≤ 7.5**; rhyme return **≥0.30**;
line return **≥0.20**.

⚠️ **Honest note on how the proportions are estimated.** A-D2 forbids a three-way even split and puts a
floor of ~20% of *runtime* on every act. **Runtime cannot be measured — nothing is rendered** (AUTONOMY).
The proxy used here is **lines ÷ BPM**, normalised, which is crude in one known direction: it *under*-states
slow acts, because a slow act holds more instrumental bar for the same word count. Proportions below are
therefore given as **intended**, with the proxy shown beside them, and they are a **text claim**.

| V | Act I lines | Act II lines | Act III lines | total | proxy split (lines÷BPM, normalised) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | 47 | 20 | 31 | **98** | 42 / 29 / 29 | dominant Act I, no act < 20% ✅ |
| **V2** | 30 | 30 | 30 | **90** | 30 / 42 / 28 | dominant **Act II** (the room) ✅ |
| **V3** | 44 | 16 | 30 | **90** | 43 / 34 / 23 | dominant Act I, shortest Act III ✅ |
| **V4** | 30 | 26 | 34 | **90** | 30 / 24 / 46 | dominant **Act III** (the win) ✅ |

⭐ **Note the design in that column:** each variation puts a *different* act in charge, so the four are not
one shape rendered four times. **V2's Act II is the longest act in the pair** — that is the whole reason
V2 exists.

**Per-act instrumentation, carried in the local headers (C-D1):**

- **III:** massed handclaps, floor tom, organ to **full drawbars**, gang answer, **no strings**.

## 4. V3 — **THE LONG WAY ROUND** · *the one who stayed*

**Acts:** **I** motorik post-punk, **one chord**, **144** · **II** drone-folk canon, **66** ·
**III** Punk & Hardcore, **176**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **why this variation exists** | a nag is a **loop**, so the first act is built as one — motorik repetition makes the form and the symptom the same object, and the anchor territory lands **last**, as the cost |
| **seam I→II** | **ROUND** — *"I'm taking the long way round."* → *"Round."* |
| **seam II→III** | **HOLD** — *"And we hold."* (a held note) → *"Hold it open."* |
| **one numeric fact** | **none.** Hinge is F04, non-numeric: *the ground didn't move all day, not once, anywhere — and everybody moved.* |

⭐ **The brick.** V3's Act III is the only place in the pair where the mechanism gets its hardest image:
**nobody locked the fire door; somebody took the brick away.** It is saved for the fastest, shortest act.

**Per-act:** **I** eighth-note bass, one chord, dry kit locked, no fills, the held tone under everything ·
**II** everything drops to **one sustained reed-organ pitch**, no kit at all, voices entering in canon ·
**III** blast tempo, downstroke guitars, gang shout, the held tone screaming through.

## 5. V4 — **NOTHING WAS TAKEN** · *the alibi*

**Acts:** **I** bright jangle power-pop, **142** · **II** Punk & Hardcore, **152** · **III** doom-gospel
chorale, **66**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **why this variation exists** | the nag has three states — **ask, insist, win** — and only this one lets the first act be *pleasant*, which is what makes the alibi audible |
| **seam I→II** | **FREE** — *"and it's free."* → *"Free."* |
| **seam II→III** | **SAY** — *"Say it."* → *"Say it like you're grateful."* |
| **one numeric fact (hinge)** | **"a hundred per cent"** (F22 — the moon at full), *responded to*: *"It lit the near way and the far way the same… and everybody moved."* |

⚠️ **DECLARED RISK, and the mitigation is part of the design.** V4's shape — bright, then hard, then slow and
huge — is one wrong move from the run's collapse template (*soft verse, heavy chorus, orchestral outro*).
**It is not that**, and here is what makes it not that, in writing, before the lyric:
1. ⛔ **No strings and no orchestra anywhere.** Act III is massed unison voices, distorted organ, floor tom.
2. **It is loud and slow, not soft and slow** — the chorale is the heaviest thing in the song.
3. **It is the antagonist WINNING.** An orchestral outro is a resolution; this is a defeat sung in major by
   everybody who moved.
4. ⛔ **No ritard, and it ends mid-word.**
If a render returns Act III as a soft string outro, **V4 has failed and should be cut, not repaired.**

## 6. ⛔ THE STYLE-FIELD CONTRACT (C-D1) — how all four are written

**Suno's style field is global. Three genres listed as a sequence returns an average.** So every style field
in this pair is written to this shape, in this order:

1. **The pole first and at length** — the held tone, the voice, the room, the falling figure, what never
   moves. *(This is the field's dominant mass.)*
2. **One sentence** naming the three genres **in order as an arc**, each with its tempo and its cut.
3. **Back to the pole** — mono low end, no ritard, no fade, no fill, the new world on the old world's last
   beat.
4. **A closing sentence about what to keep.** The field must end as a complete sentence.

⛔ **Not a list of three genres.** ⛔ **No artist, company or marque token, in any field, ever** — the S2
vocabulary enters as **texture language only** (sustained reed/pipe/organ, long decay, room). The debt for
that texture is paid in the **dedication**, where a name is a debt and not an instruction to imitate.

## 7. WHAT STEP 08 IS ALLOWED TO DO, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

**Allowed:** write the four drafts from these blueprints.
**Not allowed:** change the pole, the stance, the two-body sentence, a seam word, an act order, or a tempo.
Those are frozen here. If a draft needs one of them changed, the draft is wrong.
**Measurement comes after step 08, not during it** — the drafts are written blind to the numbers and then
scored, so the repair record in step 09 is real rather than decorative.
