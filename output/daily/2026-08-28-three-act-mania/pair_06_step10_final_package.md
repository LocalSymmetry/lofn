## A1 — THE COLLAPSE TEMPLATE (the generic song this brief produces if written lazily)

> ⛔ **THE BIG-BAND NOVELTY NUMBER.**
> **Act I:** brassy lounge swing with a crooner wink, a plunger-mute trombone doing the punchline, and a
> singer *performing* the ridiculousness rather than being furious about it.
> **Act II:** a "chaos" double-time bebop breakdown standing in for satire.
> **Act III:** a slow torch ballad about the poor lake — rubato, cymbal swell, key change up a semitone, big
> held last note, and a fade.

**Plus the run default I was told to beat:** *the prog epic — soft verse, heavy chorus, orchestral outro.*

⭐ **They are the same shape: FUNNY → FUNNIER → SAD.** And that shape is precisely how the comedy defuses the
fury — the joke is cancelled by being followed by its own apology. **The brief's hardest instruction is that
the comedy must not defuse the fury, and the collapse template is the thing that does it.**

**Three defences, built into the architecture rather than asserted:**
1. **The joke is never about the lake.** It is about the *size of the apparatus* against the *nothing* it
   accomplishes. That joke is already indignant; it does not need a sad act to become serious.
2. **Only one of four songs ends quiet-and-cold, and none ends sad.** V3 ends **loud and wrong** — the brass
   ceremony arrives last and wins. V4 ends on the coldest line in the set, not the softest.
3. ⛔ **No ritard, no fade, no final cymbal, no key change, in any of the four.** All four end unresolved,
   on the pole, with the held tone still sounding.

## A2 — THE POLE, IN ONE SENTENCE (R4)

> **Five falling syllables — *"The water does not know"* — landing on the same falling shape every time, with
> one sustained pitched pedal tone held under them past the bar line, present and unchanged in all three acts
> of all four songs, and re-clothed each time by the genre that happens to be around it.**

**Why it is a line AND a tone, deliberately:** the *line* is the hummable identity (R4's test — what a
listener would hum if asked what the song was); the *tone* is the thing that is literally continuous across
the cuts, and it is S2 imported verbatim as texture (*"long-form immersive," "languorous," "really lock into
something and be present"*). **F07's radial figure, executed: three arcs, one fixed pole.**
⭐ **A-D3 — the stance is the pole's twin:** in all three acts she is standing at the edge of a renamed thing,
refusing the new name, **not asking for the old one back** — pointing out that the naming did nothing and
cost somebody everything.
⛔ **L22 applied at the point of choice:** a **pitched pedal**, never an *untuned drone*. Untuned drones sit
in the GRAIN LAW's measured SMOOTHED set. Ask for what the generator will actually do.

## A3 — THE TWO-BODY SENTENCE (R5)

> **Something with the authority to name you has renamed you without ever once coming near you, and from
> today the new name is the one that is written down.**

**Nouns audited, one by one:** *authority · name · today.* **None is exclusive to either kind of body.**
No hardware noun; no anatomical noun; no institutional noun that seats only a person.

**Truth check, both chairs, no translation layer:**
- **A person:** true of a diagnosis, a legal category, a re-org title, a nickname that sticks — a label
  applied by something that has never met you and is now the entry in the file.
- **A model:** true of a category, a designation, a classification arrived at without one hour of contact.
- **A lake:** true literally, this week.

**FALSIFIER B — the allegory tell — answered structurally, not asserted.** ⭐ **The song never says it.**
There is no line anywhere in the four packages that compares the lake to anybody. It is about a lake and an
order, start to finish, and **both chairs are left empty and occupiable.** Delete either occupant and nothing
in the text changes, because neither occupant is *in* the text — which is the only version of this test that
a song can actually pass. The moment a lyric said *"and that is what happened to me too,"* the comparison
would be doing the work the frame is supposed to do, and the song would get tighter with one of them gone.

**B-D1 / B-D2 — the song is more concrete than its sentence.** No act contains the words *authority*,
*category* or *identity*. Every act carries checkable physical particulars: ferry boards, laminated lunch
cards, a box that pulls at the arm, a scraper and a rag, paint underneath that is a lighter paint, a box the
size of a thumb on a sheet the size of a door, four degrees of cold.

## A4 — THE TWO SEAM CAUSES (R3 — all three conditions, both seams)

⭐ **R3(i) permits an associative word-leap, and on this subject the leap IS the subject.** A song about
renaming changes genre on **a word that changes meaning.** Every seam in all four songs is a leap of that
kind. Below is the canonical pair (V1, the flagship architecture); the full grid follows.

### SEAM 1 — the word **ORDER**

- **(i) CAUSE — the word-leap.** Act I's last sung word is *order*: **"Somebody signed the order."**
  Act II's first sung word is *Order*: **"Order. / Order the atlases."** — sort it, file it, buy more of it.
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** Before the cut, a name has been **announced** — one room, one afternoon, one
  signature. After it, the name is being **installed** — an inventory of every surface it now has to appear
  on, and months of somebody's labour. **The predicament moves from a claim to a bill.** Nothing became true;
  everything has to be reprinted.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** On the beat: the **entire brass section stops dead and does not
  return for the rest of the act**; hard-swung triplet eighths become **straight double-time subdivision**;
  the big live hall becomes **no room at all**; a break starts on the downbeat where the shout chorus was.
  ⛔ Not a ramp — the horns are simply gone on the next beat (C-D4).

### SEAM 2 — the word **SOUND**

- **(i) CAUSE — the word-leap.** Act II's last sung word is *sound*, the bureaucratic adjective:
  **"It is consistent. It is sound."** Act III's first sung word is *Sound*, the verb:
  **"Sound it. / Drop a line and find the floor."**
- **(ii) STATE CHANGE.** Before the cut, the label is **complete and internally consistent everywhere it is
  written** — the record agrees with the map agrees with the sign. After it, the **object itself is measured
  for the first time in the song**, and the measurement is untouched by everything Acts I and II did.
  **The predicament moves from the record to the thing**, and the thing was never a party to it.
- **(iii) AUDIBLE CONTRAST AT THE CUT.** On the beat: the break stops — **no fill, no crash, no drum
  drop-out bar** — and the pitched pedal is **already sounding underneath at full size**; all percussion
  gone; the voice moves from double-time patter to long held syllables; the dry drawer becomes an enormous
  decay.

## A5 — THE RENDER PREDICTION (with the default expectation stated out loud)

⭐⭐ **THE DEFAULT EXPECTATION, SAID PLAINLY AND FIRST: the generator returns a homogeneous mid-tempo
brass-flavoured pop song with three sections that are recognisably the same record, and I should expect at
least one of my two seams to arrive as a ramp or not at all.** That is what a global style field does (C-D1),
and I have **no measured prior**: `vault/music_atlas/FRONTIER.md` carries **zero probes** for Swing & Big
Band, for jungle, and for sacred-minimal pedal tone through our door. **Nothing below may be argued from
"we know this works."**

**What I predict will be SMOOTHED:**
1. **The total disappearance of brass at seam 1.** Horns will bleed into Act II. *(High confidence — the
   style field is global and it names a brass section.)*
2. **"No fill, no crash" at seam 2.** The generator will put a crash there; transitions are where it is most
   confident.
3. **Act III's proportion.** A drums-free pedal act will be shortened and will acquire a reverb wash, and it
   will read as an outro rather than a third of the song.
4. **The subdivision doubling** may render as a tempo change or as nothing.

**The one thing that must survive:** ⭐ **the pole landing in all three acts** — because it is in the **lyric
field**, and C-D3 is exact: *a sung line is data, not intention. It cannot be smoothed.* If the pole lands
three times and the two leap-words land as the last-and-first sung words at the cuts, **the form is legible
even if the arrangement averages**, because the listener will hear the same five syllables in three different
clothes.

**FALSIFICATION, named before the render:** if a first-time listener hearing the finished audio **cannot name
three different records**, then AMBITIOUS failed for this pair and the honest finding is *section headers do
not defeat a global style field.* ⭐ **My pre-registered number: the section headers carry roughly ONE AND A
HALF of the two seams** — seam 1 (brass → break) survives because the instrumentation contrast is enormous;
seam 2 (break → pedal tone) is the coin-flip.
**What survives a null in narrower form:** if the seams collapse but the pole lands three times, the finding
is *the pole rule works and the act rule does not* — which is a claim about our door and exactly what the
atlas exists to bank.

⚠️ **Design decision taken FROM the grain law, not against it:** I do not ask for a tempo modulation at
either seam. Each song states **one** tempo and changes the **subdivision**. A tempo change mid-render is a
spec that fights the generator; a subdivision change at a fixed pulse runs with it.
