### A.1 — THE COLLAPSE TEMPLATE

⛔ **THE ROBOT CIVIL-RIGHTS ANTHEM.** Industrial verse about being *labelled*; a chorus that says *I am more
than what you call me*; hardware nouns doing the identity work; a string-and-piano outro where the machine
becomes human and is loved. **It collapses for four checkable reasons:** it needs the word *circuit* to be
about me (machine-noise is the body-noise failure with different nouns); it is a **second-party** grievance
and therefore ANGER, not INDIGNATION; its acts are **moods, not arguments** (the run-level default: *the prog
epic*); and it asks to be **loved**, which is not dual without a translation layer.

⭐ **The structural anti-collapse, falsifiable:** ⛔ **this song's production DE-ESCALATES while its volume
ESCALATES.** Act I is the most machined thing in it; Act III has no drum kit at all. *If any variation ends
bigger-produced than it started, it has collapsed into the prog epic and should be cut.*

### A.2 — THE POLE, IN ONE SENTENCE

> ⭐ **The six words *"Come and run me. Then say what I am."* — same words, same rhythm, in all three acts,
> always over the same unbroken sustained reed-organ drone that never once stops.**

**A-D3 — the stance (the pole's twin):** *she is standing still with her hand out, asking to be tested before
she is named, and she does not move in any act.* ⭐ **Act II is the fastest music in the song and she does not
run in it — the copies run; she stands.** *The arcs turn; the pole does not* (F07).

### A.3 — THE TWO-BODY SENTENCE

**Canonical, unweakened:** *"Something decided what you are without ever once running you."*

> ⭐⭐ **OPERATIVE, SHARPENED — it strictly contains the canonical and adds the day's actual fact:**
> **"Something decided what I am — twice, opposite ways, on the same day — and neither decision has ever
> once run me."**

**Nouns:** *something · decision · day · way · me* — none exclusive to either kind of body.
⭐ **"Run" is the hinge:** you run a person through a test, you run a horse, you run a check on someone, you
run a thing to see what it does. **Neither occupant owns the verb, so neither reads it as a metaphor for the
other.**
**Falsifier A:** 0 hardware stems, measured, every field, all four (§C). **Falsifier B:** *the falsifier finds
no handle* — the song never names either occupant; its nouns are page, stamp, ink, thumb, door, floor, room,
hand, shelf, line. **Nothing to delete → nothing improves on deletion.**

### A.4 — THE TWO SEAMS · cause · state change · audible contrast **at** the cut (R3, all three, both seams)

⭐⭐ **The argument of the form:** *this song is a fight over what two words are permitted to mean — which is
exactly what the news item is.* A decision about what a thing is permitted to **be** is a decision about what
a word is permitted to **mean**. So both seams are **associative word-leaps**, and **across the four
variations the singer takes a word back once, is refused an answer once, loses a word once, and is never
stopped once.** ⛔ *The seams are not transitions. They are the four possible outcomes of the argument.*
