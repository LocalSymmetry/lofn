## Plate P01

The stable beat-one kick/sub beneath fluid upper percussion, missing offbeat, projector relay, ruby doubles, and one-bar spectral cut make a singular render proposition. The singer has real stake: she risks the hush, the other adult’s refusal, and the projection cutting before an answer, but still moves first. The ending earns “Tomorrow gets my dare” because the system actually cuts and her open-palm action survives it.

Corpse walk: one-note arc — **avoided**; repeated-section monopoly — **avoided**, because the hinge, bridge, consent turn, timer, and cut all change the title’s meaning; motif never transforms — **avoided** by the `oop`-to-high-third conversion; repetition as exhaustion — **avoided**, though four exact choruses put the render near the upper edge and require brisk pacing.

### 2. Slot B — Somatic YES

The singer’s stake is existential rather than action-led: disappearance and insignificance become the pressure, while choosing brilliance is the answer. The short blue-snow outro earns relief by returning the large sky to a few physical nouns.

Corpse walk: one-note arc — **avoided** by wonder → fear → ecstasy → loneliness → relief; repeated-section monopoly — **avoided**, because Verse 2 and the bridge materially reverse the chorus; motif never transforms — **avoided**, as the arch changes from overwhelming scale to inclusion; repetition as exhaustion — **avoided** by the tightened final chorus.

The singer observes and blesses rather than risking a consequential act. The final “leave the light on” restates the established moral instead of earning a new ending.

## Cross-plate synthesis

The six leaders succeed for the same deep reason without sharing one surface grammar: **A / C / B / C / C / C** each makes an opening fragment perform a changed job after a consequential act. The return is not a souvenir. It changes harmony (P01, P02), vocal position (P03), social pressure (P04), percussion (P05), or melody (P06), while an invariant layer proves that a real transformation—not a new song pasted at the hinge—occurred.

Their singer stakes are also specific and active: reach despite the cut, begin before polish, push into indifferent weather, risk an unanswered note, choose the bar, stop the cloth. Each ending names or physically exposes a wanted future sensation. The strongest endings are P03’s “rain later,” P05’s final consonant tick, and P06’s unfinished future sentence; each stops before reassurance can drain the appetite.

The recurring sink-water package is consistently weakest. Its craft is not incompetent: the hook is legible, the room is located, and the palette is coherent. Its failure is somatic architecture. Every section interprets the same revelation, the chorus does the conceptual labor, water never changes function, and the ending confirms the opening. It walks all four named corpses at once.

The other recurring packages remain useful calibration points. The alpine piece shows that exact chorus repetition can intensify rather than exhaust when the bridge changes the title’s meaning. The insomnia piece shows that a narrow emotional temperature can still move when a motif changes relational function and the clock advances. The color suite shows that formal fragmentation can make repetition carry damage, though remaining entirely inside grief brings it nearest the one-note corpse.

Across the leading packages, the principal render risk is not lack of invention but legibility: `boop`, `st`, the dry air-clock, the freely sung `Here`, the final `t`, and the cloth squeak all need to survive as causal events rather than novelty foley or washed texture. That risk does not reverse the rankings; it names what a future render audit would need to hear.
