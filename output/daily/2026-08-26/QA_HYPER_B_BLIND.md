## Cross-plate calibration

The decisive distinction is not polish or schema age. A less current package can still earn a Somatic YES when scene, exposure, and return genuinely move the body; conversely, a fully specified modern chassis cannot rescue a lyric that explains its ethics after the event has already shown them.

Across the strongest slots, four things recur without becoming one formula: a compact hook the room can seize, a risk that costs the singer an action, an invariant physical carrier, and a small fragment returning with a demonstrably new job. The weakest recurring tendency in the newer packages is over-legislation: multiple lines about choice, permission, ownership, proof, or guarantees convert live flirtation into policy. The weakest recurring tendency in the older-style packages is temperature lock: many images or movements are mistaken for an arc while the singer’s position never changes.

Calibration against the named corpses:

- **One-note emotional arc:** emotion labels and color changes do not count; a changed decision or exposure does.
- **One repeated section doing all the work:** exact choruses are healthy when verses and hinge change their stakes; they are dead when the rest only glosses the chorus thesis.
- **Motif never transforms:** timbral damage alone is insufficient; the returned fragment must conduct harmony, rhythm, social answer, or action differently.
- **Repetition as exhaustion:** repetition earns its duration when each pass changes tense, addressee, consequence, or ownership; reiterating the moral after the event is depletion.
