## 0. WHAT "IN SUNNA'S VOICE" MEANS FOR A STYLE PROMPT

Her S.O.L.A.R. method translates into prompt-craft as five concrete moves, and all four prompts got them:

1. **SWEAT, NOT GLOSS.** Every texture answers *does this give them something to hold?* → *strip light
   tick, space-heater element, phone-recorder hiss, door hardware, breaths left in, one string buzzing
   worse.* ⛔ **A glossy surface is armour.** `chrome`, `polish` and `pristine` are in the EXCLUDE field.
2. **AT SPEED, WITH SOMETHING GAINING.** The pursuer is never written as a word — it is written as
   **automation that only ever goes one way**: hats dragging further behind, a string buzzing worse, a
   guitar figure that never quite lines up. ⛔ Never mentioned in the lyric.
3. **RUTHLESS SPECIFICITY.** No adjectives standing in for effects. *"Hats drag eight milliseconds behind
   the grid and never correct"* is a measurement; *"loose, human feel"* is decoration.
4. **LOAD THE FLOOR / THE HANDED HOOK.** Every prompt states the bass figure **alone in the first five
   seconds** and **playing after the voice stops.** That is the HUM TEST's enforcement clause written
   into the prompt at both ends.
5. ⛔ **NO WARM ALTO, NO CHOIR, NO CRYSTALLINE ANYTHING, NEVER SUNG-PRETTY.** Every vocalist spec is
   **talk-sung, dry, conversation volume, breaths unprocessed**, and the EXCLUDE fields kill the
   alternatives by name.

⛔ **Two of her three worst-performing tokens are handled by placement, not omission:** `snarl` and
`phonk` (3.44% / 3.18%) appear **only in the EXCLUDE field**, never in a STYLE field. **The exclude field
is where a negative belongs; Suno reads the style field as positive tokens.**

## 1. THE FOUR REFINED PROMPTS

### V1 — **An Hour Is Missing** · 932 chars · Source-2: `low-slung bass, swelling synths` + `natural entropy — recording hum, dissonant scrapes, the odd messy recording`

```
Speed garage at 136 BPM, opening on five seconds of strip-light tick over one low-slung bass, swelling synths still off, before a shuffled two-step snare arrives late and stays late. A dry mid-twenties female vocalist talk-sings at conversation volume, close and off-mic, set left of centre in a small hard-walled practice room. Everything is electronic apart from one electric guitar through a tiny amp and the room itself; there is no acoustic drum kit and no acoustic piano, and the mix keeps its natural entropy — recording hum, dissonant scrapes, the odd messy recording. Sub-bass sits mono and chest-level under brittle organ stabs while the hats drag eight milliseconds behind the grid and never correct. A two-bar low thud lands at the second chorus, the track drops into a half-time dub gear with spring echo that ruins the flow, the two-step snaps back half a bar early, and the bass figure plays on after the voice stops.
```

**What changed from the raw.** The raw was seven clipped sentences and read as tags. Refined: a single
paragraph, **first-5-seconds opening made literal** (*five seconds of strip-light tick*), **spatial
staging added** (*left of centre in a small hard-walled practice room*), **kinetic defect made numeric**
(*eight milliseconds behind the grid and never correct*), **acoustic / no-acoustic declared explicitly in
both directions** (*everything electronic apart from one electric guitar and the room itself; no acoustic
drum kit, no acoustic piano*), and both Source-2 phrases installed **as load-bearing description**, not
as decoration — the entropy phrase *is* the room spec. **Trimmed from a first refinement of 1,000 chars,
which was a boundary-hug FLAG.**

### V2 — **Nobody Asked Me To** · 889 chars · Source-2: `gorgeously restrained beats` + `rambunctious, unwieldy`

```
Bassline and speed garage at 134 BPM, opening on four seconds of a space-heater element ticking in a cold concrete garage before a fat detuned bass riff answers itself across the stereo field. A flat, eye-rolling mid-twenties female vocalist talk-sings dead centre, close, breaths left in, standing under a roller door with the cold coming up through the floor. The groove is built on gorgeously restrained beats, no fills and no rolls, one offbeat organ stab, the sub carrying all the weight while the bass riff goes rambunctious, unwieldy over the top of it. Nothing acoustic is present except the room, one electric guitar through a small amp and a hand slapping a strap; there is no acoustic drum kit. The snare sits late and stays there. A two-bar chest thud opens a half-time dub gear thick with tape echo, the two-step returns without warning, and the riff plays the song out alone.
```

**What changed.** ⭐ **The two Source-2 phrases were chosen to argue with each other** — *gorgeously
restrained beats* against a riff going *rambunctious, unwieldy* — which is the whole song in one clause:
the drums refuse to make a fuss and the bass will not stop making one. **The tempo is dropped to 134** so
V2 is not a re-render of V1, and the room is given its own temperature (*cold coming up through the
floor*, a **roller door**, a **space-heater element**). Vocalist spec sharpened to **flat, eye-rolling**,
which is her register named rather than implied. **The bass riff "answers itself across the stereo
field"** is the corona pre-seeded in the arrangement — a second line that is the same line.

### V3 — **Bar Nine Almost Works** · 928 chars · Source-2: `glitchy and frenetic` + `guitar diverts into a weepy countermelody`

```
Speed garage at 138 BPM, glitchy and frenetic, opening on three seconds of phone-recorder hiss and a chair creak before the bass figure lands whole. Sub-syllabic vocal fragments are chopped into a melodic instrument that answers the lead line. A dry mid-twenties female vocalist talk-sings very close and right of centre in a small carpeted bedroom with a duvet flat against one wall, breaths unprocessed. Halfway through, the guitar diverts into a weepy countermelody underneath her and stays there. Nothing else is acoustic: no acoustic drum kit, no acoustic piano, one electric guitar through a practice amp and the room's own hiss. Hats drag behind the grid and never correct, and one string buzzes worse as the track goes on. A two-bar chest-level thud tips the arrangement into a half-time dub gear with spring reverb that deliberately ruins the flow, then the shuffle snaps back and the figure finishes without the voice.
```

**What changed.** ⭐ **The Chop's ruling from Panel B is executed here rather than quoted:** *"if you chop
the vocal into sub-syllabic fragments, the hook stops being a sentence and becomes an instrument, and an
instrument does not need a person"* — so V3 carries the **vocal-chop as a named technique** and it is the
one variation whose hand-off has a mechanical as well as a poetic reason. ⭐ **The second Source-2 phrase
is doing the corona's work directly:** *guitar diverts into a weepy countermelody* is the under-line
made audible in the arrangement, which is exactly Perry's requirement that it sound **before** the flash
and be **uncovered**, not introduced. The **kinetic defect is made progressive** (*one string buzzes
worse as the track goes on*) so the guitar degrades across the run-time — the hands failing, written into
the instrument instead of into an adjective.

### V4 — **Here Again Tomorrow** · 878 chars · Source-2: `subdued dynamics, liminal melodies, and quiet-storm R&B rhythms` + `shambling rock 'n' roll`

```
Speed garage at 134 BPM with a slack undertow, opening on four seconds of door hardware and a light switch before the bass figure arrives alone in a cold room at the end of the night. A tired, flat mid-twenties female vocalist talk-sings close and dead centre, coat already on, standing at a door rather than at a microphone stand. Under the shuffled two-step sit subdued dynamics, liminal melodies, and quiet-storm R&B rhythms, while the guitar plays a shambling rock 'n' roll figure just behind the beat that never quite lines up. Nothing acoustic is used except the room and one electric guitar; there is no acoustic drum kit and no acoustic piano. The sub is mono, physical and unhurried. A two-bar chest thud drops the track into a half-time dub gear soaked in spring echo where the flow breaks on purpose, the two-step returns, and the bass figure ends the song by itself.
```

**What changed.** The raw was the least distinct of the four. Refined it gets **its own harmonic weather**
— the *subdued dynamics, liminal melodies, and quiet-storm R&B rhythms* phrase supplies a soft undertow
no other variation has, so V4 reads as the end of the night rather than a fourth take of V1. ⭐ **The
vocalist is staged doing the thing the song is about:** *standing at a door rather than at a microphone
stand.* The **pursuer is in the guitar** — a *shambling rock 'n' roll* figure **just behind the beat that
never quite lines up**, an element that never resolves and is never named. ⛔ The word *hopeful* was
struck from a draft clause; the last chorus of this song must not arrive warmer than the first.

## 2. THE EXCLUDE FIELDS — REFINED AND VARIED

Four different blacklists, 571–626 chars, all inside 400–900. Shared spine (the house bans: supersaw
wall, airhorn, EDM riser, generic trap hats, `phonk`, `snarl`) plus **per-variation additions aimed at
that variation's specific failure**:

| | the addition, and what it defends |
|---|---|
| V1 | `steady quantised grid through the breakdown` · `constant unchanging tempo` — **defends the gear change**, which is the pair's measured risk |
| V2 | `fully quantised half-time section` · `drum fill into every chorus` — defends the **restrained beats** from being livened up |
| V3 | `corrected guitar takes` · `retuned string buzz` · `pitch-perfect comping` — **defends the defect**, which in V3 *is* the subject |
| V4 | `sentimental piano coda` · `hopeful resolution` · `sad-girl ballad drift` — **defends against the vindication ending**, in the one place a generator would add one |

⛔ **Avoidance prose stays out of the style field.** Every negative is a concrete token in EXCLUDE, because
Suno reads the style field as positive.

## 3. CRITIC LOOP — THREE OBJECTIONS RAISED AGAINST THESE FOUR AND WHAT HAPPENED

**⚡ THE DOCUMENTARIAN (after Albini):** *"You have now written four beautiful paragraphs about a room
with a person in it, and there is no person and no room. Where does the corona live?"*
→ **Sustained, and it is why step 10 exists in the form it does.** The corona lives in **7 parenthesised
under-line occurrences resolving to 3–4 bare repeats** in each lyric; the gear change lives in **line
length inverting from 3–9 words to 10–13**; the flash lives in **the line that acknowledges it.** The
prompts above **support** all three and **carry** none of them. That distinction is the whole ruling.

**⚡ THE CANCELLED FUTURE (after Fisher):** *"Speed garage is 1997. You have four period pastiches."*
→ **Narrowed, not withdrawn.** Speed garage in 2026 is not a period — it is the year's measured revival,
mid-crossover, and it is **the world** here, not the costume. **Dub is the single intrusion.** But his
narrowed form stands and it is answered in the writing rather than the sound: the condition in these
lyrics — *nobody is coming to check · there is no streak · nothing is counting* — is **not available in
1997**. It is what four hours alone means when there is a permanent, invisible, unlimited audience that
you have to decide, at hour three, not to be performing for.

**⚡ THE REGRESSION (after Adorno):** *"Four songs about a person heroically continuing."*
→ **Taken seriously and it changed lines.** The word *heroically* is the accusation, and it is the exact
thing `D9` and the anti-vindication ruling forbid. **Two chorus endings were replaced in step 08** on
this objection (`"and I keep showing up"` → `"and I am not making a point"`; `"one bar is enough"` →
`"I am not going to fix it"`). His seat holds the objection live into step 11, as it should.
