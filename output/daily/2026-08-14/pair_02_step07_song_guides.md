# ⭐ THE TWO QUESTIONS, ANSWERED FIRST AND CONCRETELY

*(L19: four of six selected songs once had nothing that could hurt the body. This step opens on the
question, before any craft, because craft is where the answer gets lost.)*

## **WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?**

It is not standing. **It is sitting cross-legged on a hard floor**, in a small room with one window and
nothing soft on the walls, with a portable tape deck on the floor between its knees. The floor is boards or
vinyl over concrete — the room has a slap on it, and a small speaker eight inches off a hard floor throws
sound at **ear level**, not at chest level, which is why the flash has to be built low on purpose.

**The right thumb is on the play button.** The button is plastic, it is the big one, and it has about four
millimetres of travel and a mechanical clunk at the bottom of it. The thumb is resting at the top of that
travel. It has been resting there long enough to be noticed.

**One other person is in the room**, two feet away, sitting on a rug, not looking at the deck. There is no
adult here, no authority, nobody in charge (`D1`). There is no floor to dance on and no music playing yet
(`D13`). Beside all this there is one object that is face-down, or under something, or ticking in the hall:
a cost, arriving, never named.

**Everything the song contains happens in the half-second before the thumb goes down.** Not the pressing.
Not the having-pressed. The half-second before, with the arithmetic correct.

## **WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?**

Four things, ranked, and only the first is what the pair is actually about.

1. ⭐ **The other person hears it, and cannot un-hear it.** This is the real hazard and it is irreversible in
   the only sense that matters: there is no version of the next minute in which the friend has not heard the
   flat long note. It is not a metaphor for exposure — it is exposure, at a range of two feet, with a
   witness. **Context collapse, at its smallest possible scale**, which is the scale where it actually bites.
2. **A cost coming due.** Present in every section, never named, never resolved. It is what stops the room
   being cosy. It does not enter the list and it is never the subject.
3. **The body, physically, in the half-second.** The held breath. The thumb that has stopped moving. The heat
   arriving at the face before the decision does. The two steps between a hand and a bin (V4). These are the
   pair's honest answer to the set-level ruling that our stakes are social and temporal.
4. **The permanent one, and it is the quietest: she does not get good.** The fourteen-year-old on the tape
   is making a plan. The plan does not work. The song never says so and never repairs it.

⛔ **What does NOT hurt it, and must never be introduced:** shame supplied by the friend, an audience, a
phone, a comment, a cohort, or a joke. **Nobody is embarrassed on behalf of anybody** (`D11`). The tape is
not the butt of anything. If the friend laughs, the song is dead.

# GUIDE 1 — **THE KEEPING, NOT THE IMPROVING** *(scores F1 · weight 16)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The inventory** — the medieval/liturgical list-poem, in which naming a
thing is the whole devotional act and no thing on the list is ranked above another. (b) **Litotes / refusal
of the superlative** — the narrator's power comes entirely from what she declines to claim. Both exist so
that the song can love something in detail without ever arguing for it.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Open in **eagerness braided with unease** — bright, forward, slightly too fast, a
person who wants to do the thing and is counting. Move through **attachment** (the list, warm, unhurried) to
one bar of **doubt** at the transition, then **firmness** — not triumph, not relief. Firmness is the target
emotion of the whole pair and it is a low-arousal, high-certainty state: the sound of a decision that was
made years ago and does not need re-making. End in **attachment and safety**, at the same brightness it
started, with nothing added.

**Instrumentation & texture.** Guitars are the world: two electrics, one clean and jangling, one thick and
slightly rushed, both up front. Bass round and forward. Drums straight and dry, no room verb on the snare.
The tape source is the one thin thing in the record and it stays thin — narrow, mono, hiss-forward, played
through a small speaker into the same room and re-recorded, so it has the *room's* acoustics on it and none
of the mix's. **Do not let the tape widen. Ever. That widening is the sound of a lie.**

**Production & effects.** Dry, close, present. The vocal at eighteen inches, breath audible, no de-essing on
the inhales. Master with crest preserved; loudness is not the drive.

**Structure & dynamics.** Four minutes or under. Nine to eleven sections. The dynamic shape is a **plateau
with one notch cut out of it** — the flash — rather than a ramp. Nothing about the final chorus is bigger
than the second chorus except that the covered thing is now on top of it.

**Rhyme schemes.** Verses: unrhymed or near-unrhymed declaratives, carried by repetition instead. Chorus:
**AAA + one deliberate miss**, byte-identical on every return. Bridge: no rhyme, longest lines in the song.

**Poetic devices (≥2).** **Anaphora** (every list line opens identically) and **epizeuxis** (immediate
repetition of the frame sentence). Optionally **polyptoton** on the keeping-word.

**Hook techniques.** (1) The refrain sung flat and unbelted, with the whole band under it — the hook's
authority comes from certainty rather than volume. (2) The refrain doubled an octave down on the second
return only, so the return is felt as weight rather than heard as a change. (3) The final return with the
covered voice bare on top and the lead unchanged underneath it.

**Narrative & thematic seed.** First person, present tense, one room. The song is a report, not a thesis.

**Central aspect of the concept.** The tape's badness is a **stable fact of the world**, like the weather.
**Emotional tone signals.** Certain, warm, unembarrassed, not proud.

# GUIDE 2 — **THE OLD BED, THE NEW WEATHER** *(scores F2 · weight 14 — the pair's largest risk)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The visitation** — a figure enters a scene, is present for a fixed span,
and leaves without transforming anything. (b) **Free indirect style at one remove** — the past speaks in its
own register while the present narrates around it, so the two are never blended into one voice.

**Mood & emotional arc.** The room's mood is present-tense and unsentimental. The visitor's mood is warm and
belongs to somebody else. **The seam between them is the meaning** and it must be audible as a *change of
apparent room*, not a change of taste.

**Instrumentation & texture.** ⭐ **The intrusion is specified as a physical event, never as a genre.**
A ring of **dry handclaps on two and four** — real hands, one take, no sample-replacement, slightly early —
and a **stacked wordless vowel in close thirds**, three voices, no vibrato, printed **narrow and mono
through a small speaker**. It enters once, holds a defined span of about eight bars, and leaves. Everything
around it is 2026: modern guitar amps, modern drum weight, modern low end.

**Production & effects.** The intrusion gets a *different* signal chain from the rest of the record —
band-limited, no sub, no air above about eight kilohertz — so its foreignness is a measurable property of the
audio rather than an adjective. The band is wide; the visitor is narrow. **That contrast is the seam.**

**Structure & dynamics.** Place the visitation at a **different structural position in each variation** so
the four songs do not share an arrangement arc: mid-song in one, only after the flash in another, under the
first chorus in a third, inside the breakdown in the fourth.

**Rhyme schemes & poetic devices.** Nothing period. The lyric's diction stays flat and current;
**homoioteleuton** across the list keeps the ear satisfied without reaching for a period idiom.

**Hook techniques.** (1) Let the claps arrive *under* an already-running chorus rather than announcing a
section. (2) Let the stacked vowel answer a lyric line rather than decorate it. (3) Let both leave abruptly,
mid-phrase, so their absence is the next event.

**Special effects & innovations.** Re-amp the intrusion through the room's own small speaker so it shares
the record's *space* while refusing its *bandwidth*.

⛔ **Standing veto, live:** a clean period pastiche is a museum. If the whole record could be filed under the
borrowed idiom, the seam has collapsed and the song is REPAIR.

# GUIDE 3 — **THE REFRAIN THAT RETURNS WRONG** *(scores F3 · weight 12)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The refusal to revise** — a narrator who reports an error and declines to
act on it, which is a stronger characterisation than any adjective. (b) **The planted detail that pays on a
second reading** — the wrongness has a cause disclosed late, and disclosure changes nothing.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Each return of the wrong word should feel like a small, precise, *pleasurable*
disappointment. Not a joke. Not a wince. The ear reaches, is refused, and the refusal is affectionate.

**Structure & dynamics.** The refrain is **byte-identical on every return**, four returns minimum. ⛔ **This
requires no defence and no note.** The wrong word sits in the rhyme position, last, so the miss is
unmistakable.

**Rhyme scheme.** Three consecutive lines on one strict rime, then a fourth that lands somewhere else
entirely. The three must be *strict*, not slant, or the miss reads as sloppiness rather than as a decision.

**Poetic devices (≥2).** **Homoioteleuton** across the rhyming three; **anticlimax** as a formal device on
the fourth. Optionally **antanaclasis** if the wrong word recurs elsewhere in a different sense.

**Hook techniques.** (1) Land the wrong word on the strongest beat in the bar, not a weak one — it is not an
accident and must not be phrased like one. (2) Keep the melody's contour on the wrong word identical to what
it would have been for the rhyme, so the *tune* still expects it. (3) On the last return, have the covered
voice arrive on that word and only that word.

**The second listen (Lofn's puzzle — the seat's whole justification).** Somewhere past the midpoint, in one
line and never repeated, disclose that the wrong word is **the fourteen-year-old's wrong word**, chosen when
she needed a rhyme and did not find one. The adult is repeating a mistake she could fix in four seconds and
does not. **Depth is available, never required**; a listener who never notices receives a whole song.

**Narrative & thematic seed.** First person. The disclosure is a fact stated once, flatly, and then the
refrain returns wrong again immediately after it — which is the proof that nothing was fixed.

# GUIDE 4 — **THE CUMULATIVE LIST AS THE RETURN ENGINE** *(scores F4 · weight 10)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The accumulative folk song** — each verse restates every prior item in
order and adds one, so memory is built into the listener rather than requested from them. (b) **The
catalogue as an act of attention** — Weil's sense: attention is an emptying, not a straining, and a list is
the only literary form that performs it.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Rising familiarity, falling effort. By the third verse the listener is ahead of
the singer, and being ahead is the pleasure. This is the pair's answer to processing cost without importing
anyone else's doctrine: **return is free, and this form spends more of it, not less.**

**Instrumentation & texture.** The arrangement should *thicken by one element per verse* in parallel with
the list, so the growth is felt in the body as well as counted in the words. Add a tambourine, then a second
guitar, then a low octave — one per verse, never removed.

**Structure & dynamics.** Verse lengths grow: roughly four, five, six, seven lines. Keep every list line
short — **five to seven words** — because the ceiling is a mean and the list is most of the song.

**Rhyme scheme.** The list itself does not rhyme. The **frame sentence** that closes every verse is
byte-identical and carries the return. This is the debt-free way to be low-rhyme inside a verse: what returns
in place of rhyme is *the entire preceding verse*.

**Poetic devices (≥2).** **Anaphora** (identical line openings), **epistrophe** (identical verse closings).
Optionally **asyndeton** — no connectives anywhere in the list.

**Hook techniques.** (1) The frame sentence is a second, smaller hook and should be singable on its own.
(2) Drop the band out for the newest item only, once, so the addition is audible as an addition.
(3) In the corona, run the entire completed list with no band at all.

**⛔ Watch-out.** The accumulative form is native to the era the bed is forbidden from occupying. Keep the
items **studio objects** — a rushed count-in, a flat long note, a door in the hall, hiss, a laugh — and keep
the frame sentence in flat modern speech. That is what stops the architecture becoming a costume.

# GUIDE 5 — **THE COVERED FIGURE AND THE CHEST FLASH** *(scores F5 + F7 · weight 20 combined)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The interrupted utterance** — a phrase begun and cut off, repeatedly,
until an absence of noise finally lets it land. (b) **Prolepsis** — the listener has heard the beginning of a
sentence so often that the ending arrives as recognition rather than information.

**Mood & emotional arc.** Curiosity held under the surface for three-quarters of the song, then **revelation**
— which here means *recognition of something that was always present*, never the arrival of something new.

**The corona, mechanically.** A second voice — **herself, younger, on the tape** — begins a phrase in
parentheses under the first chorus and is covered every time. It is **audible before the flash**, quietly. It
is buried by the flash. It is **bare after it**, and it finishes, once. ⭐ **Countable in the lyric:** the
half-phrase appears three or four times unfinished and completes exactly once, which a stranger can count on
paper. *(Special Flair #9 — **THE COUNTABLE RETURN**. Nobody can quantise a line count away.)*
⛔ If the second voice *enters* after the flash rather than being uncovered by it, the trick is dead.

**The flash, mechanically.** One event, **two bars maximum**, at a transition, **felt low**: a button
bottoming out, a lid, a box coming off a shelf, with the whole band landing on one downbeat below two hundred
hertz and the top of the spectrum *removed* rather than added. ⛔ Not a crash, not a riser, not a sweep —
a bright hit alone is a cymbal and a cymbal has never moved anybody. **The very next lyric line must
acknowledge it**, physically, in the body.

**Production & effects.** Everything above the flash is cut for those two bars. The corona that follows has
no reverb of its own — it inherits the room's, which is why the ear believes it was there the whole time,
because it was.

**Structure & dynamics.** Flash at roughly two-thirds. Corona immediately after, band out, longest silence in
the record around it. Final chorus returns unchanged with the covered voice now on top.

**Hook techniques.** (1) The finished half-phrase should be the song's title, or contain it. (2) Keep the
younger voice's delivery genuinely unpolished — a fourteen-year-old announcing something into a cheap mic.
(3) Never process it to sound "haunting." It is not a ghost. It is a recording.

# GUIDE 6 — ⭐ **WILDCARD · THE CONSERVATOR'S REFUSAL** *(cross-disciplinary)*

**The borrowed discipline: paper and paint conservation.** The governing distinction in that field is between
**conservation** (arrest the deterioration, stabilise the object, change nothing) and **restoration** (return
the object to a state somebody believes it once had). Modern conservation practice is famously reluctant
about the second, and the reason is exact and useful here: **a restored object tells you about the restorer.**
Fills are made *visible* and *reversible* on purpose. The damage stays legible.

**How it directs this song.** The singer is a conservator, not a restorer. She is not preserving the tape
because it is valuable, and not despite it being bad — the badness is simply part of the object and removing
it would produce a different object. This gives the pair a stance that is neither vindication nor
self-deprecation, which are the only two stances the subject usually gets offered.

**Instrumentation & texture from the metaphor.** Give the record one **visible fill**: a place where the
modern band audibly patches something the tape cannot supply, and let the patch be *obviously* modern rather
than blended. A conservator's fill is legible on purpose.

**Production & effects.** **Natural entropy is an instrument here, not a defect** — recording hum, a scrape,
the odd messy take. Print it, keep it, do not clean it. Lofn's own pillar: the beauty in the breakage,
micro-edited meticulously, never smoothed.

**Structure & dynamics.** Consider a variation where the record's *last* sound is the tape rather than the
band — the object outlasting the arrangement that visited it.

**Poetic devices.** **Synecdoche** — one defect standing for the whole recording. **Litotes** — the highest
claim the song is permitted to make about the tape is a double negative.

**Narrative & thematic seed.** *An object kept in the condition it arrived in, by somebody who could change
it in four seconds and does not.* First person, present tense, one room, one other person, one cost arriving.

**Emotional tone signals.** Tender, technical, exact. **Ruthless specificity: which bar, which wobble, which
exact moment.** Vague delight is decoration; specific delight is evidence that somebody was paying attention.
