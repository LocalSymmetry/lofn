# pair_03 · step 09 — ARTIST-REFINED PROMPTS · `A GOOD NIGHT AT THE PERIMETER`
### the raw prompts rewritten in SUNNA's register, plus the DESCRIBE-RENDER self-check and its ONE repair

---

## 1. WHAT THE REFINEMENT PASS IS ACTUALLY DOING

Sunna's register is **dry, eye-rolling, funny as a requirement, never earnest, never reverent, never
explaining.** Applied to a *style prompt* rather than a lyric, that means four concrete edits:

1. **Sweat, not gloss.** Every texture word is replaced with one that gives the ear a handhold — *paint,
   brick, tape, spring, shoe, glass* — because a glossy surface deflects and this record has to be
   grabbable in the first two seconds. ⭐ *It hits before the words.*
2. **The riff is the argument.** The bassline is promoted from "instrumentation" to **the subject of the
   opening sentence** and is required to exist before the first line and after the last. A pad is not a
   riff; a texture is not a hook. If the vocal is the only tune the pillar has failed.
3. **Ruthless specificity.** No adjective survives that could describe two different records. *"Dragging
   hats"* becomes *"eleven milliseconds behind the grid and never correcting."*
4. **The pursuer, spelled to the engine and never to the listener.** One automation lane that only ever
   opens, named in the prompt, absent from every lyric.

⛔ Removed at this pass, everywhere: *warm*, *lush*, *ethereal*, *crystalline*, *soaring*, *anthemic*,
*euphoric*, *uplifting*. Those belong to a register the Pantheon has closed, and three of them are LOFN-PRIME's.

## 3. ⭐ THE DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK (one capped pass)

### What these prompts would actually PRODUCE

Five seconds of a low, slow bass figure with a gap in it and a hum behind it; a dry, close, talky female
voice arriving *in front of* a wet room rather than inside it, saying short flat sentences over a
half-time drum pattern whose hats sit noticeably late; the arrangement getting **thinner** rather than
fuller as it goes; a single loud low event about two thirds through followed by a hole; and a bassline
carrying the last ten seconds with nobody singing. Roughly three minutes. Physically it should feel like
standing near a speaker with your back on a wall, which is the scene.

### ⛔ "Name the one way this would render generic."

Two ways, and both were predicted in the brief:

**RISK A — IT RENDERS AS CHILL-OUT WALLPAPER.** *This is the real one.* Every token in "dub-adjacent,
bass-forward, restrained, subdued, spacious, slow" is also in the centre of the generator's *lo-fi
chill / late-night ambient* prior. The most likely failure is not a bad song — it is a **pleasant** one:
smooth, agreeable, unmemorable, exactly the "perfect fit content" the Platform seat named, and the exact
outcome the Pelly friction test exists to catch. A record about a person having a *specific* night would
arrive as background music for someone else's studying.

**RISK B — THE SILENCE GETS FILLED.** Measured, not feared: the render audit recorded a specified
four-second full stop returning as **0.40 s**, and internal voids are one of the two documented smoothed
classes. A friction bar that a playlist would hate is precisely the friction a generator smooths.

### ⭐ THE SELF-REPAIR (one pass, applied to all four before step 10)

**Against RISK A — four edits, all positive tokens, none of them a disclaimer:**
1. **A hard consonant event inside the first bar of every prompt** — a chair knock, a kicked spring tank,
   a push-bar clack, a taped door. Wallpaper has no impacts in it; a room with a landlord does.
2. **The bassline is stated as the tune, not the bed** — "a four-bar bassline that is its own melody,
   with a hole punched through bar three," so the generator has a figure to render rather than a texture.
3. **The vocal is re-specified as talky and close and dry against a wet room** — sing-speak, unimpressed,
   breaths left in, no de-esser. A chill-out prior wants a soft reverbed vocal *inside* the space; a voice
   standing in front of the reverb cannot sit in the background.
4. **The kinetic defect is given a number** — eleven milliseconds late and never corrected. Wallpaper is
   quantised.

**Against RISK B — the void is moved out of the production paragraph, which is where it was going to die:**
5. **The specified stop is shortened to ONE bar** (about two and a half to three seconds at these tempos)
   rather than a long full stop, because the audit says the long ones come back at a tenth of their length
   and the short ones survive. **Specs that run WITH the generator survive.**
6. ⭐ **And the void is carried by the LYRIC as counted bare lines** — four counts in V1, V2 and V4, and
   **eight counts in V3** — so that even in the worst case, where the mix refills the gap, a listener can
   still point at the moment. **Nobody can quantise a line count away.** The corona is countable whether
   or not the silence survives, which is the whole ruling from the Room seat and the Documentarian's
   grudging acceptance of it.

⛔ **One pass only.** No further loop, no render-verifier subagent, no new tier.

## 4. THE TWO SUNNA GATES, RUN ON THE REFINED MATERIAL

**THE FLAT TEST** — every candidate line said out loud, flatly, to a face. Survivors kept; casualties
listed so the cut is on the record: *"the edge is its own country"* (died — aphorism), *"I am the quiet
part of the loud"* (died — it explains), *"there's a whole record over here"* (died — self-congratulation).
Kept, because they survive being said flat: *"My coat is holding down a chair."* · *"Physics."* ·
*"Nobody has asked me for a trade."* · *"It hasn't once looked in to see who came."*

**THE HUM TEST** — each variation's figure is four bars, playable on one hand, with a hole in it, and it
is specified as present in the first five seconds and alone after the last vocal. If the figure only
exists while the singer is singing, the music is carrying nothing and the words are still paying.

**ONE DEVICE, FOUR WORDS** — *the dropout is the reveal.* Counted: four words. Nothing else in these four
songs is allowed to be a second device.

## 5. STANDING SWEEPS RUN AT THIS STEP (results carried to step 10's table)

- **Pity/coaxing sweep** — no *you'll get there*, no *come on*, no *one day*, no *it's okay*, no
  *don't be shy*, no *nobody's watching*, no *just let go*, no wistfulness aimed at the floor.
- **Second-person sweep** — zero *you / your / yours* in any sung line, all four.
- **Congratulation sweep** — the better mix is attributed to boundaries and pressure and paid for with a
  stated trade; nobody is congratulated, including the singer.
- **Pursuer sweep** — no *clock*, *time running*, *closing time*, *last call*, *hours left* in any lyric.
- **Numeral sweep** — every sung numeral is a word; exactly one research fact is sung per song.
- **Break sweep (STYLE field, word boundaries)** — *one drop* and *steppers* are named drum figures and
  trigger a credit. Carried into the Lineage & Credit block in step 10 under the not-a-sample clause.
