## ⭐ THE INSTRUMENT FINDING — held for The Scientist, not acted on

**`strict_end_rhyme()` compares the last three characters of each line's final word, so an identical
repeated word counts as a rhyme.** Decomposed across the six shipped songs:

| | P01 | P02 | P03 | P04 | P05 | P06 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **% of hits that are the SAME word** | 78% | 56% | 57% | 91% | 50% | **100%** |
| genuine rhymes | 15 | 15 | 26 | 2 | — | **0** |

⭐ **P06 clears a rhyme floor of 0.30 with a measured 0.470 and zero rhymes in it.** It declared it had no
terminal rhyme, honestly, and the instrument scored it as rhyming anyway.

**The metric is misnamed, not broken** — the archive-winner baseline of 0.463 was measured the same way, so
the comparison stays internally consistent. But it measures **end-of-line RETURN** (rhyme *or* repetition),
which means **`rhyme_return_floor` and `line_return_floor` are not independent gates**: a song with heavy
refrain return passes the rhyme floor for free, and **a pair can satisfy the Rhyme Debt rule by accident** —
the precise thing that rule exists to prevent. This is the L27 class, but **not an edge case: identical-word
repetition is the dominant contributor in three of six songs.** ⛔ **Promotion to a rule is hers.**

**Second item for her hand:** `DEDICATION_STANDARD.md`'s known-debts table credits the *"Think"* break to
**Lyn Collins, who sang it** — while the drummer on that record is conventionally cited as **John "Jabo"
Starks.** The standard whose central rule is ⛔ *CREDIT THE DRUMMER* may credit the singer on that row.
Found by the repair tier; **not changed, because an unverified player credit is the exact defect class it
