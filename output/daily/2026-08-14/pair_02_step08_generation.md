## STEP 1 · THE IDEA, RESTATED IN THE ROOM

A woman on a hard floor, a tape deck between her knees, a friend on a rug, a thumb on a plastic button, and a
recording she made of herself at fourteen that is bad and stays bad. The song is the **half-second before the
button goes down**. `D9` is the engine: **the keeping is the act, not the improving.**

---

## STEP 2 · KEY ELEMENTS, PER VARIATION

### Opening sections (first five seconds — the thing the generator hears first)
- **V1** — close tape hiss, a plastic lid clicking shut, then the whole band arriving at once, bright.
- **V2** — a cardboard box scraping down off a shelf, then one jangling guitar alone, loose and shambling.
- **V3** — a low round bass note and brushed drums before any guitar; the room is quiet and two people are in it.
- **V4** — a bin lid swinging on its hinge, then a single dry guitar with no drums under it at all.

### Key audio moments
1. The list thickening by one arrangement element per verse (tambourine → second guitar → low octave → claps).
2. The visitation: dry handclaps on two and four plus a stacked wordless vowel in close thirds, narrow and
   mono, in for eight bars, gone.
3. **THE FLASH** — two bars, low, at a transition, everything above it cut.
4. **THE CORONA** — the tape bare, the half-phrase finished, band absent.
5. The final refrain returning **byte-identical, still wrong**, with the covered voice on top of it.

### Emotional peaks
The peak is **not** the final chorus. It is the flash and the eight seconds after it. The final chorus is
deliberately no bigger than the second — the only change is that something previously covered is now audible.

### Transitions and flow
Plateau, one notch cut out of it. Every transition is a **subtraction** (Perry's rule: you do not add the
corona, you stop covering it). ⛔ No risers, no filter sweeps into anything.

### Closing sections
- **V1** the band rings out and the hiss outlasts it.
- **V2** the stacked vowel is the last thing standing.
- **V3** the friend's request is the last line and nothing answers it.
- **V4** the list runs out and the unnamed object is named once more without explanation.

## STEP 7 · RAW MUSIC PROMPTS (pre-refinement drafts)

> These are drafts. Step 09 rewrites them in an influence's lexicon; step 10 trims each to the 850–1000
> character window and counts the Source-2 phrases **in the shipped text**.

**V1 raw.** Nervous delight in a small hard-floored room. Modern power-pop, 2026, 168 BPM, E major, opening
on five seconds of close tape hiss and a plastic lid clicking shut before the whole band lands together.
Female vocalist, late twenties, warm mid-range with a dry crack at the top, eighteen inches off the mic and a
hair left of centre, breath left in. High-voltage guitars and *Close Encounters* synths sit wide across the
stereo field; bass round and forward; the second guitar rushes every downbeat by a hair and never corrects.
Midway a ring of dry handclaps on two and four and a stacked wordless vowel in close thirds walk in narrow and
mono through a small speaker, hold eight bars, leave. Two bars of chest-level low thud at the transition,
everything above it cut, then a single unaccompanied voice with natural entropy — recording hum, dissonant
scrapes, the odd messy recording. Acoustic sources: handclaps, room tone, one struck cardboard box.

**V2 raw.** Tender and unhurried. Modern power-pop with the jangle forward, 154 BPM, G major, opening on a
cardboard box scraping down off a shelf and one clean guitar alone before any drums. Female vocalist, late
twenties, lower chestier register, half-spoken in the verses, close and dry. Shambling rock 'n' roll drums,
loose behind the beat; the guitar diverts into a weepy countermelody under the second verse and keeps it.
Low-slung bass. No claps until the third chorus. Two bars of low thud when the box comes down. After it, a
stacked wordless vowel in close thirds, narrow and mono, and a thin hiss-forward tape source that never
widens. Acoustic sources: box, handclaps, room tone.

**V3 raw.** Quiet and close. Modern power-pop held at conversation volume, 132 BPM, D major, opening on a
low-slung bass and brushed drums with no guitar for eight bars. Female vocalist, late twenties, conversational,
almost unsung in the verses, one breath audible per line. Gorgeously restrained beats. The stacked wordless
vowel is under the first chorus from the beginning, faint. Guitars enter clean and stay clean. Flash is a
rewind stopping with a mechanical clunk, two bars, low. Corona is the tape alone in the room.

**V4 raw.** Fast, forward, slightly frantic. Modern power-pop at 176 BPM in A major, opening on a bin lid
swinging on its hinge and one dry guitar with no drums. Female vocalist, late twenties, pushed to the top of
her comfortable range, consonants clipped. Rambunctious, unwieldy arrangement that keeps almost falling over.
One two-bar bone-rattling breakdown with battering drums and explosions of guitar at the transition, then
nothing. Handclaps only inside the breakdown. Tape source thin and mono throughout.

## STEP 8 · RAW LYRIC SKELETONS

*(Skeletons only — the full drafts are written and measured in step 10.)*

**All four share the pair's bones** (permitted: the isolation law forbids sharing bones **between pairs**,
not between a pair's own variations):
- verses are a **cumulative list**, one item added per verse, restated in order;
- the verse closes on a **byte-identical frame sentence**;
- the chorus is **byte-identical on every return** and carries **one word that does not rhyme**;
- a **half-phrase in parentheses** trails the chorus and the verses, unfinished, from early on;
- the **flash** is two bars at a transition and the next line acknowledges it in the body;
- the **corona** is the tape bare, the half-phrase finished, the completed list with no band;
- the **unnamed cost** appears in the intro and again in the outro, never explained;
- the **exit** is spoken plainly, once, in the bridge.

## SELF-CRITIQUE AT STEP 8

The raw V1 prompt is the closest to the pastiche failure because it is the brightest, and "handclaps plus a
stacked vowel" is a description a generator can happily resolve into the very idiom the standing veto
forbids. The fix carried into step 09 is not a warning in the exclude field but a **positive** one in the
style field: the visitation is given a *different signal chain* — narrow, mono, band-limited, through a small
speaker — so its foreignness is a measurable property of the audio rather than an adjective, and the record
around it is described in unambiguously present-day terms before the visitor is ever mentioned.

Second concern: V4's `bone-rattling breakdown with battering drums and explosions of guitar` risks pulling
the whole track toward an aggression the set's anger audit has already budgeted elsewhere. It is retained
because it is the pair's **flash**, it is bounded to two bars in the prompt sentence itself, and no
aggression tokens (`snarl`, `fuzz`, `phonk`, `hardcore`) appear anywhere in the pair's four prompts.
