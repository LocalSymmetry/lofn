## ⭐ THE TWO PRE-DRAFT QUESTIONS, ANSWERED FIRST AND CONCRETELY

### 1. Where is the body standing?

**In a function room above a pub, on a Wednesday, at about half past ten.** Strip light in one corner
that nobody has turned off. A P.A. with one blown mid-range, so the vocal in the room sounds like it is
coming through a coat. A folding table with a cash box and a stack of laminated menus that never got
moved. Vinyl-tile floor, tacky under the shoe, and the tack is *audible* — the shoes make a sound. There
are maybe **twenty-two people** and a bar that has already started collecting glasses.

The body is standing **four steps back from the middle, with a coat still over one arm**, weight already
shifted onto the left foot. A man she does not know has been dancing for four seconds. She is doing
arithmetic. **The song is the half-second before the arithmetic comes out the same way it was always going
to come out.** Not the joining. Not the joy. The half-second before, in the body, with the calculation
correct and the shoulder already moving.

### 2. What could hurt it here?

⛔ Not a bolted-on hazard. **The song's own engine is the wound, and the engine is the room emptying.**
`step05` §1 named P01 as one of two pairs required to install its stake this way, and here is the install:

- **She is about to be the only one still moving when the song ends.** The first mover is unobserved —
  that is the finding — which means **the second mover is the one who gets seen.** Joining does not share
  the risk. It *transfers* it. She takes the observation off him and puts it on herself, and the song must
  arrive at that plainly, past the midpoint, as a turn.
- **The room is going home while she decides.** Coats. The towel over the taps. The bar staff stacking.
  Every second she spends counting is a second of the room she is joining. ⛔ It is **never named as a
  threat and it never resolves.** Nobody comes back. The floor does not fill.
- **And the permanent version:** somebody's phone is up in the corner, and this Wednesday will meet every
  audience she has ever had, forever, at a time she cannot choose. **The calculation is correct.** That is
  the new weather over the old bed, and it must be *audible in the writing* — in what she will not say —
  never annotated.

⛔ **The trap, named:** "stop caring what people think" is a lecture, makes the singer the adult in the
room, and is unwritable here. ⛔ **NO VINDICATION.** The half-second is the song. The outcome is not.

## GUIDE 1 — **THE ROOM WITH AN ADDRESS** *(serves F1, F7, F10)*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **Reported detail over statement** — the room is established by three
physical facts a person could sweep up, never by an adjective about atmosphere; (b) **the withheld
subject** — the thing she is deciding is not stated until the count states it for her, so the listener
arrives at the decision at the same instant the body does.
**Mood & arc:** begin in *Watchfulness* — flat, unhurried, faintly amused, the register of somebody
narrating their own hesitation while it is happening. Pass through *Eagerness* held down by arithmetic,
into *Exhilaration* that is never triumphant, and land in *Camaraderie* that is entirely practical.
**Instrumentation & texture:** live-room kit with a dry, tacky snare and audible stick-on-rim; upstroke
guitar on the offbeat, thin and bright, played hard; walking electric bass with finger noise left in;
trombone and baritone sax as a two-horn front line, close-mic'd, breath audible. Organ only as a stab.
**No orchestral strings, no synth pads.**
**Production & effects:** the room mic is the arrangement. High-passed crowd bed sitting eight to twelve
dB lower than instinct wants, bussed to a short plate, never a hall. Vocal dry and close, in-breaths kept,
no de-esser on them, no transparent pitch correction — **audible effort survives to the master.**
**Structure & dynamics:** two-and-a-half to three minutes. Get in, land the hook, leave. Horn figure alone
for four bars before any voice.
**Rhyme scheme:** four-line stanzas, **AABB**, the two A lines hers and the two B lines the answer. The
answer couplet is fixed and returns unchanged after every stanza — **the answer IS the return.**
**Poetic devices:** anaphora on the opening two words of each stanza; consonance on hard /t/ and /k/ so
the words click like the upstroke.
**Hook techniques:** (i) the answer couplet chanted by a loose room unison, no stack, no choir;
(ii) the horns doubling the answer's rhythm rather than its pitch; (iii) the answer arriving *once* with
no drums under it, so the room hears itself.
**Narrative:** first person, present tense, addressed **to the room from inside it** — never to a listener.
**Central aspect:** the coat over the arm. It is the only prop and it does all the work: she is dressed
to leave for the entire song.
**Vocal technique:** dry talk-sing. Playground-flat one bar, matter-of-fact the next. ⛔ Never sung-pretty.
**Emotional tone signal:** delight that has looked at the wolf and kept walking.

## GUIDE 2 — **THE COUNT** *(serves F2, F3, F9 — this is the device)*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **Synecdoche** — a number stands for the whole decision, so the song never
has to explain the decision; (b) **antanaclasis** — the same numeral returns meaning a beat, then a
person, then a position in a queue, with no line spent flagging the shift.
**Mood & arc:** the count begins as a private, slightly ridiculous coping device and ends as the thing the
room owns. Nothing in between is commented on.
**Hook grammar (hard):** four to eight syllables. **Monosyllabic numerals, full stop after each**, landing
on beats the body already has — the count sits on the beat, not across it. **No interval a normal throat
cannot reach while out of breath.** If a line fights the meter, the line loses; rewrite the line, never
the groove.
**Inaudible wrongness (F9), mechanically:** one passage confined to **three adjacent scale degrees, moving
by step only, in loose unison** — Byrd's congregational solution, unmodified. A person who is off inside
that passage cannot be heard being off. Place it immediately before the flash, so the room is already
inside the safest thing in the song when the arrangement changes state.
**Instrumentation:** the count is carried by voices and hand-percussion first. The horns **answer** it and
never state it — an instrument that states the hook takes it back off the room.
**Production:** the count is the one element that is never quantised. Let it drag. The kick sits eight to
fourteen milliseconds late against the grid; the bass stays on the grid so the pair breathes.
**Structure:** the count appears in the pre-chorus, is the whole chorus's answering half, and is the last
thing in the song with music under it.
**Poetic devices:** epizeuxis (the numeral immediately repeated), and **caesura** — the full stop after each
numeral is a rest and must be written as one.
**Hook techniques:** (i) count → silence → count, the silence exactly one beat; (ii) the room's count
continuing one repetition past where the singer stops *(the handed hook)*; (iii) the count doubled at the
octave by the baritone sax, one beat late, and left uncorrected.
**⛔ The failure state to score against:** a sentence *about* counting. If the hook can be paraphrased
without losing anything, the device has already been paraphrased away.

## GUIDE 3 — **THE FIGURE THAT HUMS** *(serves F4, F8-friction, F10)*

**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **the flawed refrain** — a recurring musical object with a defect that is
never repaired, which is how the listener learns the room is real; (b) **object permanence** — the figure
exists before the singer and after her, so the song is something she walked into.
**Mood & arc:** the figure is cheerful and slightly too fast for the P.A. Nothing about it develops. That
is the point: it is furniture, and furniture is what a room has.
**The figure itself:** a two-horn line, trombone under baritone sax, five or six notes, mostly stepwise,
with one leap the trombone does not quite land. **It plays alone in the first four bars, before any voice,
and it plays alone at the end after the last word.** ⛔ If the hook only exists while somebody is singing,
the music is carrying nothing and the words are still paying.
**FRICTION (mandatory, and this is where it lives):** **the horns come in a bar early the first time and
are allowed to stay wrong.** Nobody fixes it. Not in the arrangement, not in a later section, not in the
lyric — the lyric may *notice* it once and decline to do anything about it. A playlist would rather this
were not there. That is the requirement, not a side effect.
**Instrumentation & texture:** guitar upstrokes on the offbeat with the pick audible; bass walking in
crotchets, round and dry; kit playing a one-drop only when the visitor arrives, straight otherwise.
**Production:** horns tracked in the same room as the drums with bleed intact — **natural entropy** is the
texture, not a fault. Tape-style saturation on the horn bus. No sample-replaced snare.
**Structure & dynamics:** the figure is the intro, the turnaround between every section, and the outro.
It never modulates.
**Poetic devices (in the arrangement):** epistrophe — every section ends on the same two horn notes;
polyptoton in the bass, which plays the same three-note cell in four rhythmic forms.
**Hook techniques:** (i) horns alone, no drums, four bars, at the top; (ii) horns answering the vocal's
last syllable through the verses; (iii) the last thing in the song is the figure with nobody on it.
**Emotional tone signal:** unembarrassed. Played hard and fast. **The skank is physically incompatible
with sounding angry** — do not let the mix argue with that.

## GUIDE 4 — **THE FLASH AND THE CORONA** *(serves F5 — mandatory form rule)*

**The flash:** **≤2 bars, at a transition, felt in the chest.** It is the instant *before* the arrangement
changes state — the whole band hitting one low unison and the floor going out from under the tempo, and
then the **rocksteady half-time arriving as the single visitor, once, at the exact moment she joins.**
⛔ Not a cymbal. Not a riser. Not brightness. A bright hit alone is a cymbal and a cymbal has never moved
anybody. Bass and kick carry it; the horns are *absent* from the flash so that their absence afterwards is
not a new event.
**The corona:** **the room's own noise** — the chatter, the glasses, the shoes on tacky vinyl — which has
been **quietly present from bar one**, is covered by the flash, and is **bare immediately after it** when
the horns cut. It is **revealed, never introduced.** ⭐ If it is not already audible before the flash, it
is an entrance and the trick is dead.
**⛔ COUNTABLE IN THE LYRIC — the load-bearing instruction:** the count **continues with no music under
it**, and the listener can count along and land where the song lands. A line count is not quantisable away.
The mix supports it. **The lyric carries it.**
**Placement:** flash at roughly the two-thirds mark, immediately after the inaudible-wrongness unison.
Corona directly after, six to eight bars, then the final chorus with the full room on the answer.
**Production:** the crowd bed rises **by uncovering, never by a fader move** — the arrangement stops, the
room does not. Do not fill the gap. ⚠️ The render audit says interrupting silence gets filled; this is why
the count is in the words.
**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **anagnorisis without a speech** — the recognition is that the noise was
always there, and nobody says so; (b) **delayed completion** — a half-phrase started early is finished
only once the bright thing has passed.
**Mood:** *Revelation*, played completely flat. No awe voice. No widening. ⭐ Narrowing into the climax is
allowed and is better.
**Hook techniques:** (i) the count alone with the room under it; (ii) one hand-clap on the off, from
somewhere not the singer; (iii) the horns returning wrong again, identically wrong, on the last chorus.

## GUIDE 5 — **THE WOLF AND THE DOOR** *(serves F6, F8-exit)*

**The pursuer (never named, never resolves):** **the room emptying.** It is scored, not stated — coats,
glasses being collected, the towel going over the taps, the strip light, the bar staff. One automation lane
runs the whole track and only ever moves one direction: a filter that only opens, or the crowd bed thinning
by a hair every eight bars. ⛔ It must never resolve and **it must never be mentioned as a threat.**
**The turn (mandatory, past the midpoint):** the song's opening stance is that being second is the easy
job. The turn is the finding, said plainly: **the first mover was not brave, he was unobserved — so the
one who joins is the one who gets seen.** Joining does not share the risk, it **transfers** it. The song
must complicate itself here or it is a corpse asserting its final emotion from line one.
**The sung fact (this pair's, non-numeric, once, at the hinge):** the shadow's route — **Greenland,
Iceland, Portugal, northern Spain** — a thing that visited a list of places and left. ⛔ **Responded to,
never recited.** The response is the point and it is a shrug with a blade in it: *it did not check the
room.* Vary the response across the four variations; never vary the route.
**The exit (mandatory):** **the door is named, it is close, and nobody minds.** Leaving costs nothing and
is not a concession — it is the only reason the room is trustworthy. ⛔ Nobody is locked in, obliged, or
shamed for stopping. **A song that will not let its subject leave is a different genre.**
**Mood & arc:** *Unease* braided under *Elation* the entire way, never separated out into a sad section.
**Structure:** turn at the bridge; fact at the hinge inside the turn; exit in the outro, stated in the
same flat register as everything else, and not as a farewell.
**Poetic devices:** litotes (the stake stated as an understatement), and asyndeton in the bridge — clauses
stacked with no conjunctions so the arithmetic sounds like arithmetic.
**⛔ Score to zero:** any ending in which the floor fills, anybody is thanked, or the song congratulates
the listener, the room, or itself.

## GUIDE 6 — **WILD CARD: THE ARITHMETIC** *(cross-disciplinary)*

Borrow from **congregational psalm-setting** and from **crowd egress modelling**, which are the two
disciplines that have actually solved this and have never heard of us. Byrd's setting makes being wrong
inaudible; the egress model says a room is only trustworthy because leaving is easy. Neither says *stop
caring*. **Both change the structure of the task.** So does a physiotherapist, who never says "walk" and
always says "one step, to that line."
**The transfer:** shrink the ask until refusal has nothing to grab. The song must never ask anybody to
dance. It asks for **a count** — an external task, which is Spolin's cure for self-consciousness, and
encouragement is explicitly *not* the cure because encouragement confirms you are being watched.
**Technique for the lyric:** every imperative in the song is a **number**, not an instruction.
**Technique for the arrangement:** presence without contact — the horns sit near the vocal and never
double it; nobody in the song touches anybody.
**Storytelling techniques:** (a) **the list that stops early** — an enumeration that breaks off at the
point where the body moves, so the form performs the interruption; (b) **the observed observer** — one
sentence, late, in which the singer notices she is now the visible one, and does not editorialise.
**Aesthetic anchors from the palette:** *Tape Hiss Kiss* (the room bleed is the record), *DIY Culture*
(the P.A. is bad and stays bad), *Lovepunk* (delight played hard).
**Frames:** *Brass — Grooving Arpeggios: Rising Like A Tide summoning nostalgia* for the horn figure;
*Reggae: Tribal Rolls — Warm Tremors dancing around silence* for the single rocksteady visitor;
*Drums: Rumbling Sparks — Fracturing The Groove dancing around silence* for the flash.
**Special effects:** the shoes. Tacky vinyl under a shoe is a percussion instrument and it is free.
**⛔ Wild card's own limit:** one device. If more than one of these becomes audible as a *technique*, the
piece is about its own craft, and N=1 has been broken.

## Self-critique

**Guide 2 is doing too much of the work.** If the count fails, the pair fails, and I have written four
variations that all depend on one four-word device. That is the doctrine (**ONE DEVICE**), and it is also
a single point of failure — which is why G3 exists and is weighted almost as heavily: **if the count dies,
the horn figure still has to carry the song home with nobody singing.**

**And the honest risk in Guide 4.** The corona is the room's own chatter, which is the single most
smoothable element in the catalogue — a quiet thing under a loud thing, restored by a dropout. The lyric
count is the whole insurance policy. If a render comes back with the gap filled, the count is still in the
words and the failure is legible instead of silent. That is the best available answer and it is not a
guarantee.
