# ⧉ THE DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK (one pass, one self-repair)

## What these prompts would actually PRODUCE

**First five seconds:** a thin, dry tick (strip light / heater element / recorder hiss / a light switch)
with **one bass figure and nothing else** — no pad, no riser. **Vocal placement:** a close, dry,
conversational female voice slightly off-axis, breaths audible, sitting *in* the room rather than in
front of it; the room is small and hard and untreated, so there is early reflection and no tail.
**Groove:** a shuffled two-step at 134–138 with the snare visibly late, sub mono and low, an organ stab
on the offbeat, hats behind the grid. **Bold device:** at roughly 60% a two-bar low thud, then the whole
thing halves into spring-echoed dub for a stretch that is much too long to be a "breakdown", then the
shuffle returns early and the bass figure outlives the vocal.

## ⚠️ **NAME THE ONE WAY THIS WOULD RENDER GENERIC.**

**The gear change smooths.** This is not a hunch — **the render audit measured programmed tempo
transformations flattening to a steady grid 2 of 2.** The likely failure is that Suno keeps 136 BPM
straight through the "half-time dub gear", drops the drums for eight bars, adds reverb, and calls that
the section. **If that happens, the pair's single INTRUSION disappears, ONE SEAM collapses to zero
seams, and Pelly's friction requirement is unmet — and the whole thing becomes six pleasant playlistable
minutes, i.e. supply.**

**Second-order risk, related:** the verses' monosyllabic clip gets sung as ordinary legato pop phrasing,
which would erase **the ONE DEVICE** (*monosyllables break into long words*) in the same stroke.

## ⚙️ SELF-REPAIR — ONE PASS, APPLIED

⛔ **The repair is NOT a better production sentence.** *(Albini's standing veto: an objection answered
only in the production spec is not answered.)* The gear change has been moved **into the lyric and the
form**, where a quantiser cannot reach it:

1. **LINE LENGTH INVERTS ACROSS THE SEAM.** Verses run **3–9 words per line**; the Half-Time Dub Gear
   runs **10–13 words per line**, nine lines of it. Even if the tempo does not halve, **the singer must
   slow down to fit the words in**, and the perceived gear change survives on syllable density alone.
2. **THE INTERNAL-RHYME CHAIN STOPS DEAD.** Every verse chains (*snap → snaps → snap*, *take → take →

3. **THE DEVICE IS COUNTABLE ON THE PAGE.** Verse vocabulary is ≥90% one-syllable; the chorus contains
