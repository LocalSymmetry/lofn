## THE ESSENCE, IN ONE SENTENCE

A woman sits on a hard floor with a tape deck between her knees, her thumb on a plastic button, one friend
two feet away on a rug — and the song lives entirely in the half-second before the button goes down, with a
recording that is bad, that has always been bad, and that she is not going to improve.

⛔ **D9 IS THE SPINE.** There is no version where the tape turns out to be secretly good. **The keeping is
the act, not the improving.** Any line that redeems the tape is `F-x VINDICATION` and is killed.

## THE TEN FACETS (weighted, sum = 100)

*(The contract asks for five; the handoff and this pair's risk profile ask for more. Ten, weighted, because
a flat list of five cannot say which failure loses the song. Facets 1, 2 and 3 are the load-bearing three —
52 points between them — and any variation that fails one of those three is REPAIR regardless of the rest.)*

---

### **F1 · THE KEEPING, NOT THE IMPROVING** — weight **16**
Does the song hold the tape's badness as a *stable fact* the whole way through — flat wobble on the long
note, wrong chord in bar nine, a count-in that rushes — with the singer never once arguing it is good, never
once implying time has improved it, and never once cashing the keeping in for a compliment?
**Sensory test:** the listener should be able to hear, in the mix, that the tape source is genuinely thin —
narrow, mono, small-speaker, hiss-forward — and should never hear it widen or sweeten.
⛔ **FAIL if:** "it's better than I remember" · "turns out I could sing" · a swelling final chorus that
*retroactively* makes the tape good · any line where somebody else praises the recording.

### **F2 · THE INVERSION HOLDS — 2026 IS THE ROOM, 1963 WALKS THROUGH IT** — weight **14**
Is the world of the record unmistakably **modern power-pop, 2026, guitars up**, with the sixties element
entering as **a sound, not a style** — a ring of dry handclaps on two and four and a stacked wordless "ooh"
in close thirds, narrow and mono, in for eight bars and gone?
**Sensory test:** if a listener could plausibly file the whole track under "sixties girl group," the seam has
collapsed and the record is a museum (Fisher's standing veto).
⛔ **FAIL if:** the style prompt names "girl group," "Motown," "doo-wop," or "Wall of Sound" as a *bed* ·
if the handclaps and the "ooh" persist across the whole arrangement instead of visiting · if the drums,
the guitar tone or the master read period rather than present.
⭐ **This is the pair's entire risk.** The generator's strongest prior for "sixties girl group" *is a
pastiche of a sixties girl group.* The past must be named as a **physical event in the room**, never as a
genre label.

### **F3 · THE REFRAIN RETURNS WRONG AND IS NEVER CORRECTED** — weight **12**
Does the refrain come back **byte-identical** every single time, carrying **one word that does not rhyme**,
with no line anywhere in the song acknowledging, excusing, correcting, or winking at it?
**Sensory test:** the ear reaches for the rhyme, is refused, and is refused again on the next return — a
small physical disappointment, four times, deliberately unrepaired.
⛔ **FAIL if:** the wrong word is fixed in a later chorus · if a lyric line explains the wrongness *as a
device* · if the chorus is varied "for interest." **A byte-identical chorus is correct and requires no note.**

### **F4 · THE CUMULATIVE LIST DOES THE RETURNING** — weight **10**
Does each verse repeat every item of the previous verse in order and add exactly one, so that RETURN is
carried by the **form** rather than bolted on — and is every added item a *specific loved defect* rather
than a generality?
**Sensory test:** by verse four the listener knows the list well enough to say the next item before she does;
that anticipation is the song's pleasure and it costs the listener nothing.
⛔ **FAIL if:** an item is abstract ("the feeling," "the memory") · if the list resets · if the list grows so
long the lines exceed the words-per-line ceiling.

### **F5 · THE COVERED FIGURE, COUNTABLE IN THE LYRIC** — weight **12**
Is there a **second voice under the first — herself, younger, on the tape** — audible faintly from the first
chorus as a half-phrase in parentheses, buried by the flash, and then **bare and finished** after it? And is
it countable by a stranger, on paper, without the mix?
**Sensory test:** the half-phrase appears unfinished three or four times, then completes exactly once. That
count is the corona. Nobody can quantise a line count away.
⛔ **FAIL if:** the second voice *enters* after the flash instead of being *uncovered* (then it is an
entrance and the trick is dead) · if it lives only in the production spec · if it never finishes.

### **F6 · SPECIFIC DELIGHT WITH NO BUTT TO THE JOKE (D11)** — weight **10**
Is the love stated as **which bar, which wobble, which exact moment** — and is there nowhere in the lyric or
the paratext a "so bad it's good," a guilty-pleasure frame, an ironic distance, or a second party who finds
the tape funny?
**Sensory test:** read any single line aloud imagining the fourteen-year-old in the room. If a line would
make her want to leave, it is cut.
⛔ **FAIL if:** anyone laughs *at* the recording · if the friend's response is praise (that is vindication)
**or** amusement (that is the butt of the joke). The friend's only permitted response is **a request to hear
one specific part again, with no evaluation attached.**

### **F7 · THE CHEST FLASH WITH A CONSEQUENCE** — weight **8**
Is there one event of **two bars or less at a transition**, landing **low and physical** — a button, a lid, a
box coming down — with the **very next line acknowledging it**, and is the arrangement in a different state
afterwards?
**Sensory test:** felt in the sternum, not heard as brightness. A bright hit alone is a cymbal, and a cymbal
has never moved anybody.
⛔ **FAIL if:** the flash is a riser, a crash, or a filter sweep · if it is longer than two bars · if the
lyric carries on as though nothing happened.

### **F8 · THE UNNAMED COST** — weight **6**
Is there exactly one element that is present in every section, never named, never explained, and never
resolved — an envelope face-down, a card on a counter, a meter counting in the hall, a letter under the bowl?
**Sensory test:** a listener finishes the song able to say *"there was something she has to pay"* and unable
to say what it was.
⛔ **FAIL if:** the cost is named · if it is resolved · if it is metaphorically explained · if it is absent
from the last section.

### **F9 · THE VISIBLE EXIT** — weight **6**
Does the singer say, plainly and in her own words, that she can switch it off at any point — and does
nothing in the song punish, shame, or moralise about that possibility?
**Sensory test:** the exit line should sound like a fact about the room, not a concession or a boast.
⛔ **FAIL if:** the exit is implied but never spoken · if the exit is framed as temptation resisted · if
stopping would cost the singer anything the song makes her pay.

### **F10 · OVERHEARING, NOT ADDRESSING (D8′)** — weight **6**
Are the words **"you," "your," "we," "us," and "our" absent** from every sung line, so the listener overhears
one side of something and is never implicated, instructed, recruited or congratulated?
**Sensory test:** a stranger should feel like a person in a doorway, not a person being spoken to.
⛔ **FAIL if:** any second-person address · any outward-aimed "we" · any imperative aimed past the singer.

## SELF-CRITIQUE

The honest weakness of this facet set is **F2 against F4**: the cumulative list is a form the ear associates
with children's songs and with the exact period the intrusion comes from, which means the *architecture*
leans toward the era the *bed* is forbidden from occupying. The mitigation is that the list's items are
recording defects — hiss, a rushed count-in, a door in the hall — which are studio objects, not period
objects, and the frame sentence in each variation ("that is the list" / "this is the shelf above the door" /
"that is what she knows" / "that is the whole list") is flat modern speech.

Second weakness, named because the set-level check already named it: **this pair's stake is social, not
physical.** `step05` records that only P04's stake can hurt a body. I do not fix that by bolting on a hazard.
I install it where it actually lives: the thumb on the button, the held breath at the transition, the two
steps between a hand and a bin, and the flash arriving at chest height off a hard floor. That is a body, and
it is the pair's own engine rather than a hazard imported from outside it.
