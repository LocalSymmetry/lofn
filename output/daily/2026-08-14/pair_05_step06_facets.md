## 0. THE PAIR, RESTATED FROM THE BRIEF (not re-derived)

A person alone in a room at an odd hour. **Four hours. Nothing made.** The half-second the song lives in
is at **hour three**, deciding whether to keep going. **Would you give the hours back?**

⛔ **THE ONE THING THIS PAIR MUST NOT DO.** *"It wasn't wasted after all."* *"And then it clicked."*
Adorno's seat holds a standing veto on self-congratulation and **this is the pair it is aimed at.**
Sunna's actual position, from her own DNA: she read the archive and noticed **people kept making things
anyway** — under occupation, in plague years, with bad tools and no audience — and she finds that
**unbearably funny and completely sufficient, and does not think it needs to be redeemed by anything.**
**The four hours do not need a payoff. That is the song.**

**The engine, one line:** *the doing is the act, not the improving* (`D9`), sung flatly by someone whose
hands stop working at hour three.

### **F1 — THE UNREDEEMED HOUR** · 0.18
Does the song let four hours produce nothing and **decline to charge them for it**? The failing render is
the one where the last chorus arrives brighter than the first, or a lyric arrives at *worth it*. The
passing render is the one where the emotional temperature at the end is **the same as the middle, and the
singer is still there.** Sensory test: at the final bar the listener should feel *the strip light still on
and the hour still gone* — not warmth, not lift. **Elation is legal; earned elation is not.**
⭐ *Sunna's own inflection:* somebody somewhere is doing this identically, under a worse light, on a worse
instrument, and that is **funny and sufficient**, never noble.

### **F2 — MONOSYLLABLES BREAK INTO LONG WORDS** · 0.16
The pair's **ONE DEVICE, four words.** Verses run on one-syllable words — *strum, stop, snap, knot, lock,
tick* — and the chorus detonates into the longest plain words in the language: *unaccounted,
unrepeatable, accidentally, absolutely, commissioned*. Grade a draft on whether the device is
**countable by eye in the lyric text** (syllable census per section), not merely asserted in a
production note. A chorus that keeps the verse's syllable length has **failed this facet outright**, and
no amount of arrangement rescues it. ⭐ **This is also the seam** — the polysyllable is where the mouth
has to slow down and the two-step will not slow down with it.

### **F3 — THE GEAR-CHANGE THAT RUINS THE FLOW, COUNTABLE IN THE LYRIC** · 0.15
Sunna's own pillar is **THE GEAR-CHANGE** — one song that is three songs, with rolling drum transitions.
Here the single **INTRUSION** is a **half-time dub gear**, and Pelly's friction demand puts it exactly
where a playlist would rather it were not. **The render-audit risk is measured and it is this facet's:
programmed tempo transformations flattened to steady grids 2 of 2.** So the grading question is not *is
the gear change described* but **can you see it in the lyric text with the audio off** — line length
inverts (three-word lines become twelve-word lines), the internal-rhyme chain stops dead, and the words
themselves get long and slack. ⛔ **A gear change that exists only in the production paragraph scores zero
on this facet.** *(Albini's standing veto, applied.)*

### **F4 — HANDS THAT STOP WORKING AT HOUR THREE** · 0.13
The set-level check found **five of six stakes were social or temporal rather than physical.** This pair
pays that debt. The **PURSUER is her own body's limit** — never named, never resolved — and it is
installed **through the song's own engine, not bolted on as a hazard**: the left hand stops closing, the
palm knots, the thumb goes numb, the grip fails for a bar and a half and comes back worse. Grade on
**anatomical specificity** (a named part of a hand doing a named thing) and on **refusal to resolve** —
the hand must not be better by the last chorus. ⛔ **No metaphor may stand in for the hand.** A tired hand
is a fact; *"a weight in my chest"* is a decoration and gets the draft rewritten.

### **F5 — HER OWN SECOND LINE** · 0.12
**THE CORONA**, in this pair's specific form: **a countermelody she has been singing the whole time
without noticing.** Not another person. Not a machine. **Her own under-line.** It sounds quietly before
the flash (Perry's ruling: audible earlier or it is an entrance and the trick is dead), it is covered by
the chorus's top line, and once the top line stops it is **sung plainly and alone.** Grade on
**countability** (Flair #9, THE COUNTABLE RETURN): the under-line appears as a parenthesised line under
every chorus, and its bare repetition at the end is a **number a listener could count**. ⭐ **The reveal is
self-multiplicity, not company** — the room does not join her; she discovers she has been two things.

### **F6 — THE FLAT TEST · THE HUM TEST** · 0.11
Sunna's two gates, both hard, one per channel.
**THE FLAT TEST (the words):** say the line out loud, flat, to a face. *"Nobody asked me to."* survives.
*"The hours arrange themselves into a kind of grace"* dies — it was never simple, it was only short.
**A plain line is HARDER to write than a dense one.**
**THE HUM TEST (the music):** the bassline figure must be recognisable and repeatable **with the vocal
muted, present before the first line and after the last.** ⛔ **A pad is not a riff. A texture is not a
hook.** Speed garage gives this for free if the figure is written as a *tune* rather than a *sound* —
and the free channel is where the complexity is spent, because the words are staying plain.

### **F7 — THE HAND-OFF: SHE STOPS, THE ROOM CONTINUES** · 0.08
Axis 5 in its purest form and it is Sunna's live signature: **she hands the microphone into the crowd and
does not take it back; her sets end one song early.** Here the singer **stops mid-phrase** and the bass
figure finishes the song without her. Grade on whether the stopping is **visible in the lyric** — an
unfinished half-line, a top line that simply does not arrive for its last repetition — rather than a mix
instruction. ⛔ The room here is a **literal room**, not a crowd: she is alone, and a festival chant would
make the whole song a lie.

### **F8 — NOTHING IS OWED** · 0.07
The Crowd Engineer's demand, adopted verbatim: **every song has a visible exit.** In this pair the exit is
not a concession, it is a **line in the chorus** — *nothing made, nothing owed* — and it means she can
stop at any hour and knows it. Grade on whether a listener could leave the song at the second chorus and
be **taking nothing with them that the song asked for.** ⛔ No obligation, no streak, no *don't stop now*,
no discipline framing. **Finitude is the fuel, never the grief** — and never the debt.
