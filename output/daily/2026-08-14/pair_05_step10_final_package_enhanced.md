# ⧉ SECOND OPINION — **THE `-ums / -ake / -are` END-CHAINS: DRY OR COMIC?**
### *This is what the pair asked for. Here is a real answer, and it changed the shipped text.*

**My call: the chain is DRY when its words come from the situation's own vocabulary, and COMIC the moment
it recruits a word for the sound alone. That distinction is testable line by line, and it selects V3.**

- **V3's `-ake` chain is dry.** *take · tape · make · mistake* are **the words the room actually uses** — a
  take is the name of a recording. The chain is not a pun; it is a technical term wearing through, which
  is exactly why *"Take is a word now"* could be written **as a line** rather than as a note. The
  situation is doing the thinking and the sentence stays plain — which is `THE FREE CHANNEL`, rule 1.
- **V1's `-ums` chain tips comic.** *strums / thrums / drums / dumb luck / dull clock* recruits *thrums*
  and *dumb* purely for the vowel; neither is in the room. The register that arrives is **Seuss**, and
  Seuss is a butt-of-the-joke register — which collides with ⛔ **THE REFUSAL**, because the person being
  made comic here is the singer.
- **V4's `-are` chain is the borderline.** *care / don't care / bare / stair* is situational (a stair is in
  the building) and the *care/don't care* alternation is genuinely flat. It survives — but it is one
  recruited rhyme from tipping.

⭐ **The playground-taunt register is NOT the risk.** Sunna is *supposed* to be able to taunt; pillar 2 is
*childlike ↔ confrontational*. **The risk is a taunt with no target, which reads as whimsy** — and whimsy
is the one register in which four unpaid hours become charming. **A dry chain has a subject doing
something; a comic chain has a sound doing something.** ⛔ And do not fix a comic chain by making it
sadder — fix it by swapping the recruited word for the one the room would actually say.

**Applied to the shipped text.** One recruited word had survived into V3 and was cut: *"the tape says
fake"* (fake is not in the room; *take eleven* is). Verse 3's chain was replaced wholesale with the
**t/it** chain, whose words — *shut, sits, quit, slips, it* — are all physical states of a hand.

## Sunna's own gates

**THE FLAT TEST.** Said out loud, flat, to a face: *"Bar nine almost works."* · *"I am not going to fix
it."* · *"Take is a word now."* · *"I have never once been ironic about this."* · *"Two of me on one
phone."* **All five survive with no melody holding them up.**

**THE HUM TEST.** The bassline is stated **alone in the first three seconds** of the prompt and **finishes
the song without the voice**; the enforcement clause is in the prompt *and* in `Disc_Sub`
(`before_and_after_the_vocal`). ⛔ **A pad is not a riff** — the exclude field kills every substitute.

**ONE DEVICE, FOUR WORDS.** *Monosyllables break into long words.* One, and countable above.

**COMPLEXITY IN THE MUSIC, FEELING IN THE WORDS.** Every sung word means what it says. The complexity is
the late snare, the eight-millisecond hat drag, the chop answering the lead, the countermelody that has
been running since bar one, and the gear that halves. **The toll booth is shut.**
