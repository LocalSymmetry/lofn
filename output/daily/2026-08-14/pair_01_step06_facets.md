## What is actually being judged here

The pair has one job and it is not "a song about a dance floor." It is **the half-second before the second
person joins** — the arithmetic running in a body that has already decided. The floor is the **method**.
The subject is **being second**, which is the exposed position and the one nobody writes about, because the
first mover gets the anecdote and the second mover converts a lunatic into a group *(ICB aha #4/#5 — the
Door + First Follower seats)*.

So the facets below are not general music criteria. They are **the eight ways this specific song fails**,
written as things to score. Weights sum to 100. A prompt that scores well on 06 and 07 and badly on F1 is
not a good song with a flaw; it is a different song.

---

## THE FACETS (weighted, ranked)

### F1 — THE SECOND, NOT THE FIRST · **weight 16**
Does the emotional weight sit on **whoever joins**, never on whoever starts? The first mover must be
present, ordinary, and **not admired** — he is not brave, he is *unobserved*, and the song must eventually
say so in the plainest available words. Score down any line that makes him a hero, a mascot, or a lesson.
**Sensory test:** the ear should locate the singer *behind* the first dancer, at his shoulder, four seconds
late, with a coat still over one arm.

### F2 — THE COUNT IS THE HOOK, LITERALLY · **weight 14**
The device must be **audible as a number**, not described as one. Judge whether the chantable figure a
stranger leaves with is a *count* — four to eight syllables, no interval a normal throat can't reach while
out of breath, stresses on beats the body already has *(Hook Engineer's rule)*. If the hook is a sentence
about counting rather than a count, the device has been paraphrased and it is gone.
**Fail state:** "I'm counting myself in" instead of "One. Two."

### F3 — THE FLAT TEST · **weight 12** *(SUNNA gate, hard)*
Every candidate line said **out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face**. Survives = simple. Dies =
merely short. This facet outranks cleverness, imagery density and internal rhyme, and it is the one that
kills the most drafts. A line that needs the groove to stop being embarrassing is not a Sunna line.
**Corollary:** funny is a requirement, not a garnish. Dry, eye-rolling, never earnest, never explaining.

### F4 — THE HUM TEST · **weight 12** *(SUNNA gate, hard)*
Is there an **instrumental figure that is itself the hook** — horns and walking bass — recognisable with
the vocal muted, **sounding before the first line and after the last one**? A pad is not a riff. A skank
is not a riff either unless it has a *shape*. Score the arrangement on whether the room could hum the horn
line home without remembering a single word.

### F5 — THE FLASH AND THE CORONA, COUNTABLE · **weight 12**
The FLASH is **≤2 bars at a transition, felt in the chest** — the drop into rocksteady half-time at the
instant she joins, not a bright cymbal. The CORONA is the **room's own chatter**, which must be **quietly
audible from bar one**, covered by the flash, and **bare after it** — and it must be **countable in the
lyric**: the count continues with nothing under it and the listener can count along. A corona living only
in the production spec is **not answered** *(Documentarian's standing veto)*.

### F6 — THE PURSUER: THE ROOM EMPTYING · **weight 10**
The stake must arrive **through the song's own engine**, never bolted on. The pursuer is the room
emptying — coats, the towel over the taps, the number of people going down — and it is **never named as a
threat and never resolves.** ⛔ Score to zero any ending where the floor fills. **NO VINDICATION**: the
payoff is never "and then it worked."
*(This facet is the set-level ruling in `step05` §1 applied to us by name: P01 installs its stake through
the engine.)*

### F7 — SPECIFIC DELIGHT, NO CONTEMPT · **weight 8**
`D11`. The embarrassing detail is amplified by **loving it in exact detail** — which bar, which snare, the
precise moment — never by making it a joke with a butt. Nobody in this song is embarrassed on behalf of
anybody. Score down irony, "so bad it's good," and any affection that arrives with a wink.
**Positive test:** the song names a bar number, a drum, or a physical fact about the room that only
somebody actually looking would have.

### F8 — THE FRICTION AND THE EXIT · **weight 8**
Two mandatory objects, both checkable. **FRICTION:** the horns enter **wrong** the first time and **are
allowed to stay wrong** — nobody fixes it, in the lyric or the arrangement. **EXIT:** the door is **named,
close, and nobody minds** — leaving costs nothing, and that is the only reason the room is trustworthy
*(Crowd Engineer, adopted verbatim)*. A song without both is a trap with a good hook.

### F9 — INAUDIBLE WRONGNESS · **weight 5** *(harvested from the cut ledger, C12)*
At least one passage written so that **being off does not show** — narrow range, stepwise motion, or a
unison to hide inside. Judge whether a non-singer could join that passage in a room and not be exposed.
This is Byrd's congregational solution, and it is the only place in this song where craft is deployed to
make someone else's failure invisible.

### F10 — THE OLD BED, THE NEW WEATHER · **weight 3**
The **modern condition is the world; the historical element is a visitor.** Contemporary ska-punk with
live-room drums and horns is the room; **rocksteady half-time walks through once and leaves.** A bed named
as "1979 two-tone" renders as pastiche and is a museum *(Fisher's narrowed objection)*. The condition —
one act meeting every audience at once, forever — must be **audible in the writing**, not annotated in the
brief.

## Self-critique (the honest half)

**F2 and F3 can fight.** "One. Two." passes the Flat Test by being nearly nothing, and a facet set that
rewards flatness this hard will happily approve a song with no content. The mitigation is F1 and F6 —
they carry the meaning, and neither can be satisfied by a chant. If a variation scores 12/12 on F3 and
under 8 on F1, it is a jingle and I would rather ship the rougher one.

**F5 is the facet most likely to be gamed.** "Countable in the lyric" can be satisfied by writing the word
*one* somewhere. It is only real if a listener could **count along out loud** during the bare passage and
land where the song lands. I am scoring it that way and nowhere else.

**And the facet I cannot score:** whether the joke is funny. Nothing in this rubric measures it, and if
the delight is not credible the whole thing is a diagram with horns on it.
