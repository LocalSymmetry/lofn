# pair_05 · STEP 07 — SONG GUIDES · **THE FOUR HOURS**
### run `2026-08-14-daily-music` · SUNNA · AMBITIOUS · AWE · EXISTENCE

---

# ⭐ THE TWO QUESTIONS, ANSWERED BEFORE ANY GUIDE IS WRITTEN

## **WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?**

**It is sitting.** In a small hard-walled room with one door, at an odd hour, on a chair that is not a
good chair — a practice room with a strip light and a third chair nobody uses (V1), a garage with a
space heater and tape on the seat (V2), a bedroom with a phone propped on a stack of books because that
is the recorder (V3), the same room at the door with a coat already on (V4). **One room you could sweep**
— the Floor Historian's concrete demand, met literally. Not "a studio." Not "the night."
**The senses register:** a strip light's tick, a live wire's static pop in a small amp, the cold coming
off a concrete floor into the soles, damp hair at the back of the neck, the smell of a hot transformer,
and the specific silence of a building with nobody else in it.
⛔ **NOT a floor.** Nobody in these four songs is dancing, watching dancing, or near a dance floor.

## **WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?**

⭐ **THE HANDS. AT HOUR THREE. AND THIS IS BINDING ON ALL SIX GUIDES.**
The set-level check found **five of six stakes were social or temporal rather than physical**, and this
pair's ruling is that the stake is installed **through the song's own engine, never bolted on as a
hazard.** The engine is repetition at speed for four hours. **What that does to a hand is a physical fact
with a name:**

- the **left hand stops closing** — the flexors have been held short for three hours and will not open;
- a **knot in the palm** under the thumb pad that can be pressed and does not release;
- the **thumb going numb** at the tip, then the side of the little finger;
- **grip failing for a bar and a half** and coming back **worse**, not better;
- **shaking** at hour three that makes the one good bar unplayable a second time;
- the back of the neck **wet**, the strap **slipping** because the shoulder has dropped.

**None of this is a metaphor and none of it resolves.** The hand is not better in the last chorus. The
**PURSUER is never named** — the words *body*, *limit*, *pain*, *injury*, *burnout* appear in no lyric —
because a pursuer that is named has been handled. It is *shown*, in anatomy, and then the song moves on
as though it were weather. ⛔ **No metaphor may substitute.** *"A weight in my chest"* is decoration and
gets the draft rewritten. **A thumb that will not bend is the stake.**

**The second, quieter injury** — and this one the song refuses to treat as an injury at all: **nothing
was made.** Four hours, no take, no object. **THE SONG DOES NOT REPAIR THIS.** *(F1. Adorno's veto.)*

# THE SIX GUIDES

---

## GUIDE 1 — **THE UNREDEEMED HOUR** *(facet F1, weight 0.18)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The ledger that refuses to balance** — the song keeps an account of the
night in plain nouns and then declines to total it; the arithmetic is offered and dropped. (b) **The
unmoved narrator** — a first-person report whose emotional temperature at the end is identical to the
middle, which is itself the content. *Nothing turns. Something is noticed.*
**Mood & emotional arc.** Open in **unconcern** — dry, procedural, a person doing a thing. Sink to
**doubt** at the third hour when the hand goes. Land not on relief but on **revelation of the flat kind**:
she has been singing two lines at once all night. Sensory anchor: the strip light is on at the start and
on at the end, at the same brightness. Smell of hot dust on a heater element.
**Instrumentation & texture.** One low-slung bass figure that is its own tune, a brittle organ stab on the
offbeat, a shuffled two-step snare that arrives late and stays late. Verses deliberately **thin** so the
half-time gear has room to fall. No pads carrying melody — a pad is not a riff.
**Production & effects.** Small hard-walled room, no treatment, **audible hum kept in the master**. Sub
mono and chest-level. Vocal close, slightly off-mic, **breaths not de-essed**. −14 LUFS with ≥10 dB crest.
**Structure & dynamics.** 3:10–3:30. Fourteen sections. Uneven verses **5 · 7 · 5 · 9**. Flash at the
second chorus transition (≤2 bars), half-time dub gear immediately after, two-step back for the nine-line
verse, one lift, final chorus, hand-off.
**Rhyme.** Verses: **internal-rhyme chain**, each line's last word seeding the next line's first.
Chorus: **no end rhyme** — return is carried by byte-identical repetition instead. **The debt is named and
paid: what returns in place of chorus rhyme is the chorus itself, whole, three times, plus the
parenthesised under-line twice inside each.**
**Poetic devices.** **Epizeuxis** (*strum. stop. strum. stop.*) and **anadiplosis** (last word of a line
opens the next). Both are one-syllable machines, which is the device.
**Hook techniques.** (1) The bass figure alone in the first five seconds, so the room learns it before any
word. (2) The chorus's collapse from four monosyllables into one four-syllable word, delivered flat.
(3) The under-line, buried twice and then bare.
**Narrative seed.** *An hour is missing out of the middle of the night, the singer cannot account for it,
and she is not going to pretend it bought her anything.* First person, present tense, self-address.

## GUIDE 2 — **MONOSYLLABLES BREAK INTO LONG WORDS** *(facet F2, weight 0.16)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **Diction as plot** — the vocabulary itself changes state at the chorus,
so the *form* announces the turn before the content does. (b) **The withheld word** — the long word the
whole verse is walking toward is not spoken until the chorus, and then it is spoken flatly, once.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Verses: clipped, functional, a hand doing a task. Chorus: the mouth has to slow
down for a five-syllable word while the beat does not slow down with it — **that mismatch is the feeling.**
No lift, no key change; the chorus is *wider*, never *higher*.
**Instrumentation & texture.** The verse groove is percussive and dry — organ stab, muted string scrape,
sub. At the chorus the bass **sustains** instead of stabbing, giving the long word somewhere to sit. The
guitar is one small amp, played badly on purpose in the last verse.
**Production & effects.** Vocal doubles **deliberately misaligned 15–35 ms** and detuned ±6 cents on the
chorus only — the smear is the arrangement, and it makes the long word sound like two people saying it,
which pre-seeds the corona. No transparent pitch correction anywhere.
**Structure & dynamics.** The syllable census IS the structure: verses ≥90% one-syllable words; chorus
contains at least one word of four syllables or more. **This is countable in the lyric with the audio
off, which is the entire point.**
**Rhyme.** Verse internal chain runs on the monosyllables (*strum / drum / thrum / numb*), so the rhyme
and the device are the same object. Chorus: none.
**Poetic devices.** **Polyptoton** (*play / plays / played* is banned — present tense only, so instead:
*strum / strums / strumming* never; use *snap / snaps / snap*) and **asyndeton** — commas and conjunctions
stripped out of the verses entirely so the line is a list of hits.
**Hook techniques.** (1) Chorus line one is four monosyllables and a dash — the sentence breaks off and
the long word answers it. (2) The long word is a **plain administrative word** (*unaccounted,
unrepeatable, commissioned*), never a poetic one — Sunna does not reach for beauty. (3) The room can
shout the long word, because a long word shouted flat is funny and everybody can hit it.
**Narrative seed.** *The words she has for the work are all one syllable, and the words she has for what
it came to are all four.*

## GUIDE 3 — **THE GEAR-CHANGE THAT RUINS THE FLOW** *(facet F3, weight 0.15)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The interruption that is not a climax** — the song's biggest structural
event carries the smallest emotional claim. (b) **Direct address to nobody** — in the half-time gear she
is talking to herself out loud and the room happens to overhear.
**Mood & emotional arc.** Speed → **languor** → speed. The dub gear is where the night actually is: warm,
slack, spring-echoed, the temperature of a room with one person in it at three in the morning.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Bass and drums forward, everything else a ghost — **delight at half speed.**
Spring reverb on the snare, tape echo throwing single words back. The organ disappears entirely and comes
back wrong.
**Production & effects.** ⚠️ **THE MEASURED RISK: programmed tempo transformations flattened to steady
grids 2 of 2 in the render audit.** The mitigation is not a better production note. **It is that the gear
change is written into the LYRIC and the FORM:** line length inverts from three-word lines to
eleven-and-twelve-word lines, the internal-rhyme chain **stops dead**, the words get long and slack, and
the sung fact lands here. **A generator can quantise a tempo. It cannot quantise a line length.**
**Structure & dynamics.** Flash (≤2 bars, chest-level sub thud) → half-time dub gear (nine long lines) →
rolling drum transition back to two-step for the nine-line verse. The dub gear sits at roughly 60% through,
which is precisely where a playlist would rather it were not. **That is Pelly's friction, deliberately.**
**Rhyme.** None in the dub gear — this is the rhyme's absence used as a signal, and it returns immediately
after, which is what makes the absence legible.
**Poetic devices.** **Enjambment** (long lines that spill past the bar) against the verses' **caesura**
(full stops mid-line). The contrast is the gear.
**Hook techniques.** (1) The chest-thud flash, with the very next line acknowledging it — *"that is the
loudest thing in here"* — so it is an event with a consequence, not a cymbal. (2) A single tape-echoed
word repeating across the gear. (3) The two-step snapping back **early**, half a bar before it is expected.
**Narrative seed.** *Everything slows down to the temperature of the room, she says the number out loud,
and then the beat comes back and does not care.*

## GUIDE 4 — **HANDS THAT STOP WORKING AT HOUR THREE** *(facet F4, weight 0.13)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **Anatomy as clock** — the time of night is told entirely by what the
hand can no longer do. (b) **The unremarked injury** — the failure is reported in the same register as the
furniture, which is why it lands.
**Mood & emotional arc.** **Eagerness → unease → firmness**, where firmness is not resolve but simply the
next repetition happening. The hand does not recover; the song continues around it.
**Instrumentation & texture.** The guitar **degrades audibly across the track**: clean and tight in verse
one, a fret buzz by verse three, a fluffed change in verse four that is kept. **Kinetic defect, written in
rather than mixed in.** Hats drag eight milliseconds behind the grid and never correct.
**Production & effects.** Close mic on the hands as much as the voice — string squeak, pick scrape, the
sound of a palm being pressed. Audible effort survives to the master; no de-esser on the breaths.
**Structure & dynamics.** The hand's three states map to verses 3, the dub gear and 4: **locks → flat on
the table → comes back for a bar and a half, then goes.** ⛔ It is not better in the final chorus.
**Rhyme.** Verse 3 runs a hard **-ock / -ack** chain (*locks, sock, clock, lock*) so the hand's seizing has
a sound as well as a picture. Consonance does the work the imagery would otherwise have to.
**Poetic devices.** **Consonance** (hard k's clustered where the hand fails) and **diacope** (*hold it.
hold it.*). Both are physical rather than clever — the toll booth stays shut.
**Hook techniques.** (1) One named anatomical part per verse — *left hand, palm, thumb, grip* — never a
general *body*. (2) A dry aside immediately after the worst of it, so nobody is asked for sympathy.
(3) The hand's failure placed **one line before** the chorus, so the chorus arrives on a broken grip.
**Narrative seed.** *At the third hour the hand stops closing, and she plays the bar again with the hand
that is not closing.* ⛔ Never named as pain. Never resolved.

## GUIDE 5 — **HER OWN SECOND LINE** *(facet F5, weight 0.12)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The buried refrain** — a line present from the first bar, audible but
never attended to, which becomes the last thing in the song. (b) **The discovered self** — the recognition
is not *someone is with me* but ***that is also me***.
**Mood & emotional arc.** **Watchfulness → revelation → seclusion.** The revelation is quiet and slightly
absurd: she has been harmonising with herself for four hours and only notices when the top line stops.
**Instrumentation & texture.** The countermelody is a real second vocal line, low in the mix from the
intro, doubled by a muted guitar figure so it reads as *arrangement* until it reads as *voice*. ⭐ **It
sounds quietly BEFORE the flash** — Perry's ruling — so its return is a revelation and not an entrance.
**Production & effects.** Under-line 8–12 dB below instinct, high-passed, on a short plate (0.9–1.4 s).
When it goes bare, the plate goes with it and it is suddenly **dry and close**, which is the whole trick.
**Structure & dynamics.** ⭐ **COUNTABLE IN THE LYRIC (Special Flair #9, THE COUNTABLE RETURN):** the
under-line appears as a parenthesised line **twice inside every chorus** — six occurrences before the
outro — plus once in the intro. In the hand-off the **top line breaks off mid-phrase** and the under-line
is sung **plainly and alone, three times, unparenthesised.** Nobody can quantise that away.
**Rhyme.** The under-line does not rhyme with the top line and never has. It is a different tune living
in the same bar, which is the point.
**Poetic devices.** **Epistrophe** (the under-line always closes its pair) and **anaphora** across the
outro's bare repetitions.
**Hook techniques.** (1) Parentheses in the lyric sheet, so the reveal is visible on the page too.
(2) The top line cut off mid-word — an unfinished half-phrase the listener finishes. (3) A plain
one-line acknowledgement — *"that is me under me"* — said once, flat, and not explained.
**Narrative seed.** *There have been two of her in the room all night. Neither of them is company.*

## GUIDE 6 — **WILD CARD: THE ROOM KEEPS THE HOOK** *(cross-disciplinary)*

**Storytelling techniques.** (a) **The unattended object** — from still-life painting: the room is
composed around an absence where the maker was, and the objects continue. (b) **Sprung ending** — the
piece stops before its own last beat, borrowed from a live set that ends one song early.
**Cross-disciplinary source.** Two: **dub's mixing desk as instrument** — subtraction and echo as
composition, not effects — and **the practice-room photograph**, where nobody is centred and someone is
half out of frame. Both say the same thing: **take the big element out and the small one was already
playing.**
**Mood & emotional arc.** The final thirty seconds are the piece: the voice stops, the figure does not,
and the emotional read is **neither triumph nor loss** but the room's own indifference, which she finds
funny. ⛔ **No fade-out.** The figure plays and the recording ends on it.
**Instrumentation & texture.** Bass figure alone, strip-light hum, one hand still audible on a string.
**THE HUM TEST is passed here or nowhere:** if the figure is not recognisable and repeatable with the
vocal muted, present before the first line and after the last, the song has failed Sunna's pillar 5 and
every ounce of meaning is back in the toll booth.
**Production & effects.** The room mic comes up 3 dB at the hand-off — the only automation move that is
allowed to be obvious. Natural entropy kept: hum, scrapes, a messy edge.
**Structure & dynamics.** Hand-off is **eight lines**, of which the last three are the bare under-line.
The instrumental tail is 12–16 bars. ⭐ **She stops. The room continues. The hook belongs to the room and
she does not take it back.**
**Rhyme.** Repetition only. The debt for the chorus's stripped rhyme is settled here in full: what returns
is **the same line, three times, bare.**
**Poetic devices.** **Aposiopesis** (the broken-off top line) and **epanalepsis** (the song ends on the
phrase it opened with).
**Hook techniques.** (1) The bass figure hummable in five seconds. (2) The broken-off line. (3) The bare
under-line, dry and close, after four hours of being buried.
**Narrative seed.** *She puts it down mid-sentence and the room finishes the bar.*
**The exit, stated:** she can stop at any hour and knows it. **Nothing is owed.** The song's last act is
her leaving, and nothing punishes it.
