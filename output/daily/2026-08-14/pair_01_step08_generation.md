## THE SHARED BONES OF THE PAIR *(all four variations; the four ANGLES are what differ)*

- **Room:** a function room above a pub, Wednesday, half past ten. Strip light nobody turned off. A P.A.
  with a blown mid. Vinyl tile that is tacky under a shoe, audibly.
- **Bed (the world):** contemporary ska-punk / two-tone revival — live-room kit, hard offbeat upstroke,
  walking bass, two-horn front line (trombone + baritone sax). **Intrusion (once):** rocksteady half-time,
  at the exact moment she joins. **Aggression: none.**
- **Form:** four-line stanzas, **AABB**, the two A lines hers, **the B pair supplied by the answering
  half — and the answer is the return** (identical every time).
- **Device:** *the count becomes the hook.* Every imperative in every variation is a **number**, never an
  instruction.
- **Flash:** ≤2 bars, one low unison, chest not ears, at the transition into half-time.
  **Corona:** the room's own chatter — under the track from bar one, covered by the flash, **bare after
  it, and counted in the lyric with nothing under it.**
- **Pursuer (never named, never resolves):** the room emptying. **Exit:** the door, four steps, nobody minds.
- **Friction:** the horns come in wrong the first time and stay wrong.
- **Sung fact (this pair's, once, at the hinge, responded to):** the shadow's route — Greenland, Iceland,
  Portugal, northern Spain. **Response varies by variation. The route never varies.**

## Draft-stage self-critique (what step 09 has to fix)

1. **All four music prompts are between 230 and 470 characters.** The gate is 850–1000, written into
   870–960. Every one of them is missing the vocalist depth, the spatial staging, the vivid
   first-five-seconds, the acoustic / no-acoustic declaration and the second Source-2 phrase. This is the
   most mechanical failure in the file and the easiest to repair honestly.
2. **V3 contains the trap.** *"You could just move. It is not that hard."* — that is the lecture the whole
   run was built to make unwritable, it makes the singer the adult in the room, and it breaks D8′ the
   moment the room tries to shout it back. **Kill, do not soften.**
3. **V4 ends in vindication, twice** — in the style prompt and in the outro — and the outro slips into past
   tense, breaking D5. The payoff is never *"and then it worked."*
4. **V1's bridge recites** ("a shadow crossed four countries") instead of responding, and imports a numeral
   that is not this pair's fact. The route is the fact; the response is the point.
5. **V2's chorus reports the device instead of being it** — *"I came in on the three. I stayed"* is a
   sentence about counting. F2's named fail state, verbatim.
6. **Three of the four outros are converging** on the same leaving/evening/thinning rhyme. They are four
   songs, not one song with four coats on.
