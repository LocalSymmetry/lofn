**Upheld, and the pair understated its own finding.** The four do not merely share a room; they share a

## VOCAL FINGERPRINT

Female, mid-twenties. **Dry talk-sing** — playground-flat one bar, matter-of-fact the next, inside eight.
No vibrato. **In-breaths kept and not de-essed**; she **cracks on the count and the crack stays in**. Close
dynamic mic **on the floor, not a stage**, sitting *in* the mix a step behind the horns rather than on top
of it — it hits before the words. Doubles, where they exist, misaligned by 15–35 ms and left smeared.
⛔ **Never sung-pretty. No warm alto, no choir stack, no crystalline anything, no transparent pitch
correction.** The room's unison passages are a **loose room**, never a stack — a stack exposes whoever is
off, and the pre-chorus exists precisely so that nobody can be heard being off.

## PRODUCTION DRAMATURGY — every unusual sound has a job

| element | job |
|---|---|
| **two-horn figure, alone, four bars, before any voice** | THE HUM TEST. The hook is instrumental, it exists before the singer and after her, and the room can carry it home with no words in it. |
| **horns enter a bar early and are never corrected — including the last time** | THE FRICTION. A playlist wants this repaired on the repeat. It is not going to be. ## LINEAGE & CREDIT

**Ska, rocksteady and two-tone are not textures. They are three named scenes with addresses, and two of
them are still working.**

- **SKA and ROCKSTEADY — Kingston, Jamaica, late 1950s into the 1960s.** The offbeat upstroke, the walking
  bass and the two-horn front line in this prompt come from there and nowhere else. Point listeners
  **upstream** to the Kingston studio system that built them — Studio One and Treasure Isle — and to the
  house bands who played the figures, **The Skatalites** foremost among them. ⭐ **And specifically to the
  session horn players and bassists of those rooms, most of whom are uncredited on the records they made.**
  The horn line is the entire hook of this song. Somebody's hands made that shape first, they were usually
  paid a flat session fee, and their name is usually not on the label. **That is exactly the credit a system
  like this forgets, which is why it is written first.**
- **TWO-TONE — Coventry, England, 1979, and the 2 Tone label.** Borrowed as a **visitor**, never as the
  room. It earns its place in *this* song and not another because its founding gesture was explicitly about
  **who is allowed on the floor**, which is this pair's whole subject.
- **THE LIVING SCENE — contemporary ska-punk and the ongoing two-tone revival**, worldwide and mostly
  unsigned, playing rooms exactly like the one in this lyric. **Fusion with lineage, never capture.** We
  claim no lane, no genre name and no first. Crossover telemetry is a reason to **credit harder**, never a
  market opening.
- ⛔ **HONESTY CLAUSE, both ways.** Nothing here is a sample. **We asked for these figures by name, so we
  name the people who made them.** Never *"contains a sample of…"*. A false credit makes the true ones
  worthless.
- ⛔ **DRUMMER SWEEP — RE-RUN ON THE ENHANCED STYLE FIELD, WITH WORD BOUNDARIES.** Swept for
  `amen` · `break` · `breaks` · `breakbeat` · `breakdown` · `funky drummer` · `think` · `apache` · `bongo` ·
  `slap` · `pop` · `chopped` · `winstons` · `one-drop` / `one drop`, case-insensitive, `(?<![a-z])…(?![a-z])`
  so that *ornament* cannot match *amen*. **Result: ZERO HITS.**
  **No break, and no named break figure, is raised in the shipped style text — therefore NO break credit is
  owed and none is given.** Crediting a drummer for generic break language would be a false credit, and this
  package's real credits (the Kingston session horn players) are the ones it would devalue. The sweep is
  recorded so a later reader knows it was run rather than skipped. *(The sweep covers the STYLE field, not
  EXCLUDE — raised ≠ forbidden.)*
- ⛔ **D4 / paratext:** no real person appears in the lyric, and this song is not *about* anybody living. The
  singer, the first dancer and the room are invented (`HUMAN_SUBJECT_STANDARD`).

## MAJOR DEVIATIONS

**1 — REFUSED: the step contract's *"elevate poetic language to literary density."***
- **Reason:** SUNNA's **FREE CHANNEL / TOLL BOOTH** doctrine is a *hard gate on this pair* (THE FLAT TEST,
  THE HUM TEST, ONE DEVICE), and it says the opposite in as many words: *put the complexity in the MUSIC and
  the feeling in the WORDS; a plain line is HARDER to write than a dense one.* Densifying these sentences
  would move the meaning back into the toll booth and nobody has ever been moved while parsing. The handoff
  wins over the step file (L30).
- **How the step's "at least one technique or structural transformation" was paid instead:** in the **FORM**
  — the **shrinking ask** (the count collapses from *One. Two. Three. Go.* to a bare *One.* at the flash to a
  single handed *Two.* at the end, which is Flair #3, THE SMALLER UNIT, made structural) and the **handed
  count** (she stops; the room finishes it one repetition past her) — and in the **SOUND**, measured:
  alliteration **23.31** per 100 words at **5.82** mean words per line.
- **Effect:** the seat stays distinguishable. Writing literary density into Sunna produces a second Lofn and
  spends the seat that justifies a Pantheon.

**2 — CHANGED: *"Nobody fixes it. The song stays long."* → *"Nobody fixes it. They play along."***
- **Reason:** step 09 recorded this line as a rhyme repair, and it shows — it names *"the song"* inside the
  song, which is the one self-reference in a lyric that otherwise only reports the room. The replacement is a
  physical fact about the band. **One variable moved:** the rime key is untouched (`ong`), the syllable count
  and the AABB position are untouched; only the referent changed.

**3 — REMOVED: *"Count it with me."* → *"Nothing under it. Count it or don't."***
- **Reason:** *"Count it with me"* is **encouragement**, and the ICB's Improviser seat (after Spolin) is
  explicit and unanimous with four other domains: **the documented cure for self-consciousness is never
  encouragement — encouragement makes it worse, because it confirms you are being watched.** The replacement
  is the **external task** (count it) plus **the visible exit** (or don't) in six flat words, and it moves
  the exit out of the outro — where it was free — into the climax, where it costs something.
- **It still passes D8′:** a room can shout *"Count it or don't"* back at the singer and it stays true.

**4 — REMOVED: *"That was under the whole song."* → *"That was the room. It was there first."***
- **Reason:** the corona must be **revealed, never annotated**. The old line was the song narrating its own
  device — anagnorisis *with* a speech. The new one states the fact and stops.

**5 — ADDED, and this is the one that matters: *"There is a phone up at the back. It will keep."***
- **Reason:** the pair's own step-07 guide requires the **new weather** — one act meeting every audience at
  once, with an invisible future audience of unlimited size and permanent memory — to be **audible in the
  writing, never annotated**, and V1 shipped **without a single line carrying it**. The Fisher veto (*a clean
  period pastiche is a museum*) was therefore being answered **only in the design document**, which is
  exactly the standing veto: an objection answered in the spec is not answered.
- **Guarded against D2:** the phone is **not the villain**. She does not complain about it, does not ask
  anyone to put it down, and goes anyway — *the calculation is correct and she does it regardless*, which is
  the LOCKED MOOD in one line. It is also the line that removes the shipped song from the pair's shared
  bridge template.

**6 — CHANGED: the outro's last sung syllable is now a bare *"Two."***
- **Reason:** the step-07 guide's **handed hook** — *the room's count continuing one repetition past where
  the singer stops* — was specified and never built. Now she stops on *"One."*, the fixed answer returns, and
  the room lands *"Two."* alone before the horns play out. The title word is the last thing sung, and the ask
  has shrunk to one syllable.

**7 — CHANGED: BPM and key written as digits, and a key added — *"162 BPM, B-flat major."***
- **Reason:** `sung_numerals_spelled_out` governs **sung lines**; the style field is not voiced, and the
  handoff says so explicitly. Suno reads *162 BPM* as a tempo and *"one hundred and sixty-two"* as prose.

## DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK — one capped pass, on the ENHANCED prompt

Four bars of trombone-and-baritone-sax over a room already murmuring; then a fast, dry ska-punk band with
the vocal sitting *in* the mix, talk-sung and unsweetened, the count landing on the beat and the room
chanting it back. At roughly two-thirds the whole band hits one low note, the tempo halves for a single
rocksteady passage, the horns vanish, and there is a bare stretch of chatter and counting before the last
choruses.

**Name the one way this would render generic:** *ska-punk* is a strong enough prior that the generator may
return a bright, tightly quantised festival horn-band with a sunny mixed-forward vocal — correct and
weightless — with the crowd bed polished off and the "wrong" horn entry silently repaired into a normal
pickup. **What is new in this pass as insurance:** the two smoothings are now *named as blacklist tokens*
(`polished crowd bed`, `tidied horn entry`) instead of only being hoped for in prose, the wrongness is sung
twice, the corona is four separate bare lines, and the count now **ends the song after the singer stops** —
a handed hook is much harder to smooth than a mixed one, because there is nothing under it to smooth.
**Self-repair pass: none needed; the loop is not re-entered.**

## Self-critique

**The honest cost of deviation #5.** *"There is a phone up at the back. It will keep."* is the one line in
this song that a listener has to hold for a second. It is still plain, it still passes the flat test, and it
is the only place the modern condition is audible — but it is the densest object in an otherwise
completely flat lyric, and if any line in here is going to read as *written* rather than *said*, it is that
one. I am keeping it because the alternative is a museum, and I would rather owe one second of attention
than ship a period piece with a good hook.

**And the thing none of these numbers touch.** The measurement says the return floors are cleared two to
three times over. It does not say *"Not the brave one. Just the two"* is funny, and if it is not, this is a
very well-measured diagram with horns on it. Step 10 said this about itself and it was right to; the
enhancement did not fix it, because no pass in this pipeline can.
