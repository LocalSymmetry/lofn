**The scene the intrusion comes from, named:** the **sixties girl-group stack** — the ring of dry handclaps
on two and four and the stacked wordless vowel in close thirds. That sound was made by **singers**, and the
one this pair points at is **Darlene Love**, who sang lead on records released under other groups' names,
with **the Blossoms** — **Fanita James** and **Gloria Jones** — behind her. ⛔ **No producer is credited as
lineage on this pair.** The credit goes to the voices, and it goes to them because they are the people whose
uncredited work is the exact subject of a song about a kept recording with somebody else's name on the box.

**The living scene, pointed upstream:** power-pop is still being made, now, in small rooms on cheap
interfaces, and the home-taping lineage this pair actually works in — cassette albums kept in the state they
were made in rather than remade — is a living practice with living artists in it. Go and buy one.

# SELF-CRITIQUE

**The real weakness, stated rather than hidden.** The four variations share more DNA than four songs from a
freer brief would: the same room, the same object, the same cumulative grammar, the same corona mechanism,
and two of the four wrong-word lines are near-siblings (*"I never call it fine"* / *"I never call it a win"*).
That is the pair's brief working as designed — the isolation law forbids sharing bones **between pairs**, and
this is one pair's four angles on one object — but it means the set-level judge should read these four as a
**suite**, and if only one survives the cut it should be **V1** (the plainest telling, and the only one where
the corona hands the title to the fourteen-year-old) or **V3** (the only one where a second person is in the
room and never once evaluates what she hears, which is the hardest thing in the whole brief to get right).

**The second weakness is metric, and it is a mirror of the first.** The return numbers are large — a
line-return of 0.936 on V4 and 0.891 across the pair, against a floor of 0.20 — because the cumulative list
restates itself by construction. That is not gaming: the floors exist because three runs in a row stripped
return and replaced it with nothing, and this form is the **opposite** failure mode, over-return. The defence
is that the repetition is **structural rather than padded** — every restated line is a list item the next
verse will add to — and the song's information still advances once per verse. But a judge should check
whether V3's verse five is *pleasurable* at nine list lines or merely long. My own read is that verse five is
the last one that earns itself and a sixth would not. The end-rhyme figures are the honest counterweight:
V4 sits at 0.346 against a 0.30 floor, which is a real margin and not a comfortable one, because the wrong
word is deliberately spending rhyme every time the refrain returns.

**What I did not do.** I did not gate this pair on the Flat Test or the Hum Test — those are SUNNA's, the
metaprompt's LEGIBILITY RULE scopes them out of Lofn-Prime explicitly, and writing them in here would have
produced a second Sunna and spent the seat. Lofn's puzzle is left where it belongs: the **second listen**,
which in all four songs is the discovery that the un-rhyming word in the adult's refrain is the
fourteen-year-old's un-rhyming word, kept exactly as she left it.
