## STEP 1 · THE FOUR INFLUENCES, AND WHY THESE FOUR

The brief asks for **lesser-known** musicians connected to the medium. The medium here is **a person, a
room, a tape machine, and a record kept in the state it arrived in** — which is a real and specific lineage
with real and specific and largely uncelebrated practitioners.

| variation | influence | why this one, for this variation |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **Emitt Rhodes** (1950–2020) — home-recorded power-pop, one man, one garage, borrowed machines | The whole point of his records is that a full, bright, multi-tracked pop band is one person in a small room. V1's world *is* a bright band and its truth *is* one person on a floor. |
| **V2** | **Linda Smith** — Baltimore home-taped cassette pop, made in the late eighties, kept and reissued decades later in the state it was made in | The exact ethic of the conservator guide, in a real practice: the cassette is not a demo of a better record, it is the record. |
| **V3** | **Barbara Manning** — quiet US indie-pop; plain declarative lines delivered close and low, kit held down | V3 is a whole song at conversation volume with two people on a floor, and its register is *stated*, not performed. |
| **V4** | **Nikki Sudden** (1956–2006) — shambolic, ramshackle rock 'n' roll that keeps almost falling over and never gets tidied | V4's arrangement has to sound like something that could tip, because the plot is a decision that could tip. |

## STEP 2 · WHAT EACH INFLUENCE SUPPLIES

### V1 · the home-recorded power-pop lexicon
- **Aspect of style:** density built out of scarcity — three guitar takes stacked by one pair of hands, so
  the "band" is audibly a person who has done the same thing several times.
- **Two signature techniques:** (i) doubles that are *nearly* aligned and left that way, so the smear is the
  arrangement; (ii) a bass part played like a second melody rather than a root.
- **Emotional focus:** brightness with a small door left open in it.
- **From the influence's viewpoint:** *"the room is on the record whether the record wants it or not, so
  print the room and stop apologising for it."*
- **What it changes in the prompt:** the kinetic defect becomes specific and singular — **the second guitar
  rushes every downbeat by a hair and never corrects it** — rather than a general "loose" adjective. This is
  the pair's answer to the Documentarian's veto: a defect a machine cannot silently smooth into "tight."

### V2 · the home-taped cassette-pop lexicon
- **Aspect of style:** an arrangement that never argues. Instruments arrive, do a job, and are not asked to
  build.
- **Two signature techniques:** (i) a guitar line that answers the vocal and then takes it over entirely;
  (ii) leaving hiss, room and mechanical noise in the master because removing them would remove the record.
- **Emotional focus:** attachment without nostalgia — the object is present tense.
- **From the influence's viewpoint:** *"it is finished when it exists."*
- **What it changes in the prompt:** an **instrumental turn** where the countermelody takes the vocal's job,
  and the instruction that the cassette source **never widens**. Also the decision that the last thing
  standing is the visitor, not the band.

### V3 · the quiet indie-pop lexicon
- **Aspect of style:** the voice sits at speaking level and the band is arranged *underneath* speech rather
  than around singing.
- **Two signature techniques:** (i) one audible breath per line, kept; (ii) a kit that never opens up, so the
  dynamic range lives entirely in the guitar's presence.
- **Emotional focus:** affection stated as a fact and left there.
- **From the influence's viewpoint:** *"say the thing once, at the volume you would actually say it."*
- **What it changes in the prompt:** the eight-bar guitar-free opening, the plate reserved for the refrain
  only, and the ruling that the stacked vowel is **already there** under the first refrain and becomes
  audible only by subtraction — which is the corona's whole mechanism, borrowed from a real habit.

### V4 · the ramshackle rock 'n' roll lexicon
- **Aspect of style:** an arrangement that sounds like it is being held together in real time.
- **Two signature techniques:** (i) a percussion element consistently ahead of the grid and never fixed;
  (ii) guitars stacked past the point of tidiness so the wall has seams in it.
- **Emotional focus:** urgency without aggression.
- **From the influence's viewpoint:** *"do not straighten it. The straightening is the only thing that could
  kill it."*
- **What it changes in the prompt:** the tambourine that is **always a fraction early and never fixed**, and
  the bounding of the breakdown to **one two-bar** event so that a power-pop record does not become a heavy
  record by accident.

## STEP 3 · THE REFINED PROMPTS

> These are the step-09 outputs. Step 10 makes only **trim-level** edits (character-window fitting and
> Source-2 casing) and ships them. Structure, vocalist spec, progression order and blacklist are unchanged
> from here.

**V1 — refined.** *Nervous delight on a hard floor.* Modern power-pop, 2026, 168 BPM in E major, opening on
five seconds of close tape hiss and a plastic lid clicking shut before the whole band arrives at once. Female
vocalist, late twenties, warm mid-range with a dry crack at the top, sung eighteen inches off the mic and a
hair left of centre, breath left in, consonants unpolished. Wide across the field sit high-voltage guitars
and Close Encounters synths, with bass round and forward, and the second guitar rushes every downbeat by a
hair and never corrects it. Midway a ring of dry handclaps on two and four and a stacked wordless vowel in
close thirds walk in narrow and mono through a small speaker, hold eight bars and leave. At the transition,
two bars of chest-level low thud with everything above it cut, then one bare voice over natural entropy —
recording hum, dissonant scrapes, the odd messy recording. Acoustic sources are hands, room tone and a
struck box.

**V2 — refined.** *Tender and unhurried at the top of a stepladder.* Modern power-pop with the jangle
forward, 154 BPM in G major, opening on a cardboard box scraping down off a shelf and one clean guitar alone
for eight bars before any drums. Female vocalist, late twenties, low chesty register, half-spoken in the
verses and only sung in the refrain, dry and very close, audible swallow before each entry. Drums are
shambling rock 'n' roll and sit behind the beat, the guitar diverts into a weepy countermelody under the
second verse and keeps it there, and the bass never leaves the root. Handclaps stay out until the third
refrain. A stacked wordless vowel in close thirds then arrives narrow and mono through a small speaker and is
the last thing standing. Two bars of low thud when the box comes down, everything above it cut. The cassette
source is thin, mono, hiss-forward and never widens. Acoustic sources are the box, hands and room tone.

**V3 — refined.** *Quiet certainty at conversation volume.* Modern power-pop held down to the size of a
room, 132 BPM in D major, opening on low-slung bass, swelling synths and brushed drums with no guitar at all
for the first eight bars. Female vocalist, late twenties, conversational and almost unsung in the verses, one
audible breath per line, close and dry with a hair of plate on the refrain only. Everything sits on
gorgeously restrained beats, with clean guitars that stay clean and one tambourine that arrives late and
stays. A stacked wordless vowel in close thirds is already faintly under the first refrain, narrow and mono
through a small speaker, and becomes audible only when the band leaves. The flash is a rewind mechanism
stopping with a low mechanical clunk, two bars, everything above it removed. After it the cassette source
plays alone in the room, thin and unwidened. Acoustic sources are brushes, tambourine and room tone.

**V4 — refined.** *Fast and slightly frantic in a kitchen.* Modern power-pop at 176 BPM in A major, opening
on a bin lid swinging on its hinge and one dry guitar with no drums under it at all. Female vocalist, late
twenties, pushed to the top of her comfortable range, consonants clipped, a small break on the held notes,
doubled a beat late through the refrain. The arrangement is rambunctious, unwieldy and keeps almost falling
over, with guitars stacked three deep, bass driving straight eighths, and a tambourine that is always a
fraction early and never fixed. At the transition, one two-bar bone-rattling breakdown with battering drums
and explosions of guitar, then nothing above the low end. Dry handclaps on two and four live only inside that
breakdown. A stacked wordless vowel in close thirds arrives narrow and mono for the last refrain. The
cassette source stays thin and mono. Acoustic sources are hands, tambourine and room tone.

| **F7 the chest flash** (8) | ✅ All four now specify **everything above it cut / removed**, i.e. the flash is defined by *subtraction of the top*, which is the only way a bright instant lands in the chest. |

### Where refinement was refused
1. **No influence's lexicon was allowed near the lyric.** The step-09 contract says to rewrite prompts in an
   influence's voice; the risk it carries in this house is L18's mold effect, and the lyric is where a mold
   would actually show. The influences shaped *how the sound is described* and nothing else.
2. **V4's breakdown was not softened.** It is the flash, it is bounded to two bars inside the prompt sentence
   itself, and the blacklist carries `metalcore breakdown`, `palm-muted chug riff`, `blast beats`,
   `double kick` and `screamed vocal`. Softening it would have moved the pair's one physical event into the
   production notes, which is where events go to die.
3. **Nothing was added to make the record bigger at the end.** All four finals are the same size as their
   second choruses. A triumphant last chorus is how a song about a bad tape becomes a song about a good one.

## SELF-CRITIQUE

The honest cost of using four influences is that all four are, broadly, **one lineage** — people who made
records in small rooms on cheap machines and did not tidy them afterwards. That narrows the lexical range
across the four prompts and is part of why the four style fields read as a family. It is defensible for a
*pair* (four angles on one object) and would be a defect in a *set*, so it is flagged here for the set-level
judge rather than argued away.

The second cost is that three of the four are dead and cannot be pointed at as living scenes. The living
credit therefore has to be carried elsewhere, and it is: **the sixties girl-group singers in the Lineage &
Credit block of step 10**, plus the still-running home-taping practice, credited as a scene rather than as a
person. **Crossover telemetry is a reason to credit harder, never a market opening.**
