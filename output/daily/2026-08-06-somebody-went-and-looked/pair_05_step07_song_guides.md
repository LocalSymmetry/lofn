# 07 — SONG GUIDES · PAIR 05
`2026-08-06-somebody-went-and-looked` · **SUNNA** · P05 · *two voices arguing* · G minor · 140→70→140 · 2:50
**ICB:** 69095 B · sha256 `85ed1348b7e22fdfb8e4b06dadd21daa1f4c85ede5ea7e150105f41e49f44a15`

> **Guide law for this pair:** the riff is fixed and shared (G–B♭–C–B♭ guitar hard left, D–C–B♭–G bass hard right, four bars, before the first word and after the last). The guides do not re-invent it. They decide **where the body is standing** and **what could hurt it there.**

---

## GUIDE 1 — **THE TRADE** *(serves F1)*

***Where is the body standing?*** In front of a gas ring with the extractor fan on, close enough that the fan is louder than the room. The first thing the listener hears is that fan clicking on, and then four notes of muted guitar coming from the left side of their head.

***What could hurt it here?*** The guitar figure getting smoothed into a chord bed the moment a voice arrives. Fight it in the form, not the note: **the trade is written as an eight-bar instrumental before the vocal, is the only thing in the last twelve seconds, and every chorus ends on two bars of trade with no words over it.** Give the generator nowhere to put a pad.

**Instrumentation & texture:** one crunchy single-coil-ish electric, palm-muted on the first two notes and open on the last two; one fuzz bass mid-forward with a slow envelope filter. Nothing sustains. Drums are a tight machine kit with the kick 12 ms late and the bass on the grid. One dry woodblock tick at 140 that never changes speed.
**Structure & dynamics:** 0:00 fan + trade alone · 0:11 first voice · **chorus by 0:23** · 1:26 dub · 1:52 slam · out at 2:50 on the trade alone.
**Poetic devices:** epizeuxis (a word said twice in a row where the pan is repeated), and antithesis carried entirely by stereo position rather than by any word.
**Hook techniques:** (1) the trade doubled an octave up on the last chorus only; (2) the guitar half muted out so the bass answers a hole; (3) the room whistling the guitar half back before the band re-enters.

---

## GUIDE 2 — **THE TWO SIDES** *(serves F2)*

***Where is the body standing?*** One at the hob with her back to the room; one on a stool behind her, half-turned, being helpful. Twelve feet apart and on opposite sides of the listener's head.

***What could hurt it here?*** ⭐ **The renderer centring both voices.** The counter-move is to make the two voices *impossible to blend*: different mic distance, different rasp, different rhythmic feel. Left is warm, talky, slightly ahead of the beat, sentences that finish. Right is flat, deadpan, slightly behind, sentences that stop. **They overlap only at the last syllable of a phrase** — never in unison, never a duet, never harmonised.

**Vocal technique:** both female, both sing-speak, no vibrato, no pitch correction, breaths left in, no de-esser. Left sits a touch brighter and closer; right sits drier and a hair further back.
**Mood & arc:** begin in ordinary domestic warmth, tip into flat frustration by the second verse, arrive at a stalemate that is not unpleasant.
**Rhyme discipline:** verses run short couplets with hard end sounds (AABB or ABCB); the chorus is a fixed eight-line call/answer with two-syllable answers.
**Emotional tone signals:** two people who like each other and cannot get across.

---

## GUIDE 3 — **THE ONE WHO FOUND IT EASY** *(serves F3 — the sympathy guide)*

***Where is the body standing?*** At a kitchen table with a biro and the back of a receipt, writing numbered steps for someone else because she wants to help.

***What could hurt it here?*** Making her a butt. **She is right, and she stays right, and the song never takes it away from her.** She learned it at nine. She has done it a thousand times. She offers to come round and show them. The single most important line in the whole pair is hers and it is not an apology: ***"I'd have done it for you."*** She is never corrected, never humiliated, never given a shame arc. The most she ever learns is to say the same true thing more slowly.

**Storytelling techniques:** the *gift that lands wrong* (a kindness delivered accurately and received as a wound, with no fault at either end), and *the numbered list* — her generosity is legible as three imperatives and a count.
**Narrative seed:** somebody trying to hand you something, and you can't take it, and neither of you did anything.
**Poetic devices:** anaphora on her instructions; polyptoton on *easy / easier / easily* — used once, never explained.
**Central aspect:** ⛔ **nobody in this song is being cruel, and it lands anyway.**

## GUIDE 4 — **THE EMPTY BAR** *(serves F4 — the dub)*

***Where is the body standing?*** Same kitchen, later, with the extractor off and the pan cooling in the sink, and the argument still going in a room that has stopped helping either of them.

***What could hurt it here?*** A pad. A string bed. A reverb tail carpeting the silence. Every generator reaches for one. **The specification is dead air between phrases** — you can hear the room, a fridge, a breath, and nothing else. Spring echo is permitted *before* the hole (three slaps on the last word of the dub-in) and must be gone inside two bars, decayed to nothing rather than faded under.

**Production & effects:** short spring, no hall, no plate on the dub vocals. When the band slams back it is a **hard cut on the downbeat** with no riser, no build, no crescendo — the guitar simply exists again at full volume.
**Structure:** 1:26 half-time dub, eight bars · 1:39 **everything out**, eight bars of two voices only · 1:52 slam.
**Special effect:** the woodblock tick stays at 140 through the 70 BPM section, so the half-time reads as double-speed. It is the only element that survives the removal, and it is never mentioned.

---

## GUIDE 5 — **THE PLAIN NOUN** *(serves F5 + F6)*

***Where is the body standing?*** At a butcher's counter, holding two of them, because you always buy two.

***What could hurt it here?*** Reaching for the meaning. The lyric must **continue the story instead of interpreting it** — the second one goes in the bin and the line after that is what she told people, not what it meant. Every hook line is tested by being spoken flatly to a face: *"I bought two." "I always do." "Who showed you?" "My mum. I was nine." "I'd show you. Say when."*

**Imagery discipline:** one concrete object per two lines — pan, receipt, biro, smoke alarm, window, bin bag, sink, fridge. No abstractions. No adjectives doing emotional work.
**Lyric structure:** 3–6 words per line; short declaratives; full stops mid-line as caesura; verse stanzas of 4–6 lines so the shape never settles into a block of couplets.
**Poetic devices:** anaphora (*Nobody taught me / Nobody stood / Nobody said*), asyndeton in the instruction lines (*Pan, pinch, plate*), and heavy plosive alliteration across the p/s/b families so the words hit like the percussion does.

---

## GUIDE 6 — **WILD CARD: THE ANSWER THAT DOESN'T COME** *(serves F7 + F10, and shapes V4)*

***Where is the body standing?*** In a kitchen at the end of the night with one light on and the other person gone up to bed mid-sentence.

***What could hurt it here?*** Sentiment. The temptation is to make the silence sad and let the song mourn itself. **Sunna does not.** The argument does not end because anybody won or because anybody was hurt — it ends because somebody got tired. The last removal is the bass answer: the guitar plays its two bars and there is a two-bar hole where the reply lives, once, and then the record stops.

⭐ **And then she gets another one out of the fridge and starts again with nobody watching.** That is the run's whole subject arriving inverted: this pair is the one where *nobody went and looked*, and it is never said out loud, never winked at, and never explained. A listener who takes only the surface gets two friends, a pan, and a joke about steak. That is a whole song.

**Cross-disciplinary borrow:** the last panel of a comic strip where the speech balloon is drawn and left empty — the shape of a reply, printed, with nothing in it.
**Emotional tone signals:** dry, unbothered, slightly funny, entirely unresolved.
**Hook technique:** the final chorus is byte-identical to the first, and the only thing that has changed is what is playing underneath it. The listener hears "the same thing again" and feels it thin out, and cannot say why.
