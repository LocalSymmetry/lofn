## ⭐ THE IRONY ADJUDICATION — MY OWN, NOT THE PAIR'S NOTE

**The method:** every candidate line read aloud flat, no music, and one question asked of it — ***who is this at the expense of?*** A line survives only if the answer is *nobody*. Four lines failed. One line the pair flagged is adjudicated differently from how they flagged it.

**1. "Some nights it's a daft thing to be doing." (V4, bridge) — REPAIRED, and not for the pair's reason.**
The pair filed it as a *delivery* risk: *"a vocal performance could manufacture a smirk the lyric does not contain,"* with the remedy *"if the render lifts it, the line goes."* **I reject that remedy and keep the finding.** Nothing in this run is rendered — `vault/AUTONOMY.md` stops autonomous work at drafts on disk — so a defect whose only cure is an audit of a render that will not exist is an **unrepaired defect**, and THE GRAIN LAW is explicit that an objection answered outside the lyric or the form is not answered.
And the substance is different from what they wrote. "Daft" is not ironic — it is the man's own word about himself, and Sunna's refusal covers irony *about what someone loves*, not a person's honest self-doubt. V1 and V2 carry the same doubt (*"nobody wants this. Nobody's coming."*) and nobody flagged those. **The asymmetry is the tell: across 4 × 61 lines, "daft" is the only place the loved activity itself is called stupid.** Every other doubt is a fact about the world; this one is a verdict on the thing. A reading that wants comedy needs exactly one handhold, and that was it.
**Repaired to: *"Some nights he can hear how it sounds."*** The doubt is preserved intact and relocated into the room's judgement, off-stage, unspoken. The song never once says the thing is daft. Nobody is the butt, including him.

**2. "Who do you send a bolt to? Everyone's gone." (V2, bridge) — REPAIRED. This was the worst one, and it was not flagged at all.**
Read flat, it is a **rhetorical question whose implied answer is "nobody, obviously — it's a bolt."** That is the shape of a joke and the object of the loved thing is the punchline. The next line rescues it (*"Sent it to one person anyway"*) but **the smirk arrives first and the rescue arrives second**, and a listener hears in order. Sharper than "daft," because "daft" is at least self-directed.
**Repaired to: *"Nobody to send a thing like this to."*** Same loneliness, no joke shape, and *"Sent it to one person anyway"* now lands against a statement instead of a gag.

**3. "He wasn't being cruel. He was tired." (V3, verse 1) — REPAIRED as an explanation, not as irony.**
This is the narrator interpreting a character's motive, in a run whose first rule is *depth lives in the situation, never in the sentence* — **continue the story instead of interpreting it.** It was doing real work (it disarms the one person who could be a butt) and it must not simply be deleted.
**Repaired to: *"Falls asleep against the window."*** A sleeping man cannot be a villain. The audience does the disarming themselves, which is the entire doctrine, and the line is now an image instead of a verdict.

**4. "He reads it back. It isn't funny. / He doesn't want it to be funny." (V3, bridge) — REPAIRED.**
The refusal-as-event is the best thing in V3 and it stays. But the second line interprets the first, and two lines saying one thing is where a song starts narrating its own rules — the ICB's hardest NOT is *no song may be ABOUT the doctrine.*
**Repaired to three verbs: *"Types the joke. Reads the joke. / Takes the joke back out."*** The refusal now **happens** instead of being explained, and the joke is physically present and physically removed.

**5. "That's one. That's enough. That's one." (V3, verse 3) — REPAIRED as a moral.**
The most moving line in the package and the only stated thesis in it: *one reader is enough.* A moral is a hard defect here. **Repaired to action: *"He read it twice. Saved it."*** Same arithmetic, performed instead of announced.

**6. "Two thousand words about a bolt." (V1 chorus, ×16) — ADJUDICATED, SURVIVES, UNCHANGED.**
The strongest case against it: *two thousand* against a trivial monosyllable is the exact meter of a setup and a punchline, and "bolt" is the outsider's flattening noun for a thing the song insists is a *fastener*. I took that seriously and I am overruling it. The incongruity **is the verified fact**, not a comment on it; Sunna's register is flat and she means it; and the chorus forecloses the reading **in its own third line** — *"Two thousand words and not one joke"* — byte-identically, four times, before any listener could settle into a smirk. The man's own word for it is elsewhere in the song and is not "bolt" (*"He sets the part down on a towel"*, *"Spells the maker's name right"*). **At whose expense? Nobody's.** The chorus is untouched.

**7. "He's fine." (V1, verse 3) — REPAIRED as pity.**
Not irony about the thing; irony about the man. In English *"he's fine"* defaults to *he is not fine*, which makes him slightly pitiable, and the brief forbids a joke where **the fastener or the person who loves it** is the butt. **Repaired to: *"He's still writing."*** Sunna's answer to neglect is more work, never resignation.
