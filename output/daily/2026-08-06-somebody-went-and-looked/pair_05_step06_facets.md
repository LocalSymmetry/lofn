## ⭐ 0. THE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, BEFORE A SINGLE WORD

**Key G minor · 140 BPM · the trade is four bars long and it is the whole song.**

| bar | who | notes | shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | **electric guitar, palm-muted then open, hard LEFT** | **G → B♭ → C → B♭** | up a minor third, up a fourth, back — **stops on B♭ and never lands** |
| 3–4 | **fuzz bass, mid-forward, hard RIGHT** | **D → C → B♭ → G** | walks down and **lands flat on the root** |

**Span: G3 to D4 — a perfect fifth. Eight notes. All diatonic to G minor. No accidental, no interval wider than a minor third.**

⭐ **THE ARGUMENT IS IN THE RIFF BEFORE ANYONE SINGS.** The guitar asks and stays up there. The bass answers and gets home. One figure resolves and one does not, and *neither of them is wrong* — the bass is not smug, it is simply certain; the guitar is not whining, it is simply still up on that B♭ waiting for something.

**Assignment, and it is the whole design:**
- **the bass, which lands** = **the "it's easy" voice.** For that person the thing genuinely resolves. That landing is warm and complete and the song never takes it away from her.
- **the guitar, which does not land** = **the person for whom it wasn't.**

**THE WHISTLE TEST — answered.** Up-up-back, then down-down-down-home. Eight notes inside a fifth, stepwise except the opening minor third, no chromatics, playable on one string of a bass and four frets of a guitar. **A drunk person can whistle both halves walking away from the venue after hearing it twice, and can whistle the second half as a reply to the first.** That is the test and it clears it.

**Placement (the enforcement clause):** the trade plays **alone for eight bars before the first vocal** and **alone again after the last word**, and in V4 the answer half is removed at the very end so the guitar asks into a two-bar hole.

⛔ **A pad is not a riff.** Nothing in this song sustains. Everything in the hook is plucked, struck, or muted.

## THE DEVICE — **two voices arguing** *(four words; one device; no second one)*

Hard-panned, one each side, sing-speak, overlapping at the edges of phrases. **The device and the riff are the same gesture** — guitar/bass, left/right, ask/answer. This is why P05 is the run's cleanest proof of THE FREE CHANNEL: **there is nothing to decode, because the argument is audible in the stereo field before the first word.**

## THE PURSUER — **a clock** *(⛔ never named in the lyric)*
A dry woodblock tick locked at 140 that **does not slow down when the song halves to 70**, so in the dub it reads as double-speed against the groove. It gains roughly 0.7 dB per section and never resolves. It is never mentioned, never described, never rhymed with.

## THE SITUATION *(F5, verified)*
**"Almost no skill required to cook a steak" — 143 points, 180 comments.** More comments than points: the shape of an argument. **A hundred and eighty people arguing about whether an easy thing is easy.**
The Marketing skeptic promoted this out of a footnote: *"the most quietly cruel sentence in this document. That's every comment section, every family, every time someone says 'it's not that hard' to somebody it was hard for."*

**The object is a pan and a piece of meat.** Bright surface, sharp edge, never explained. A listener who takes only the surface receives a whole song about two friends bickering in a kitchen. A listener who has ever been told the thing that nearly finished them was easy receives the other one, and is never asked to decode a single sentence to get it.

⛔ **The website is never named. The song is not about the internet.** Two people, one kitchen, one pan.

## THE FACETS — 10, weighted. *(Weights sum to 100. Each carries a failure mode and a why-it-matters.)*

### F1 · **THE TRADE IS THE HOOK, AND IT IS AUDIBLE BEFORE AND AFTER THE VOICES** — weight **16**
The four-bar guitar/bass exchange must be recognisable with every vocal muted, must open the record with no voice on it, and must close it the same way.
- **Failure mode:** the renderer treats the figure as accompaniment and buries it under the vocal the instant the vocal arrives; or returns a competent arpeggio instead of a riff and the self-check ticks the box anyway. *(The Rock Titan's live objection, unresolved by design.)*
- **Why it matters:** if the vocal is the only tune, every ounce of meaning goes straight back into the toll booth and the doctrine this run exists to test has failed on the one song built to prove it.

### F2 · **THE HARD-PAN SURVIVES THE RENDER** — weight **15**
Two vocals, two different timbres, one pinned left and one pinned right, for the entire record, overlapping at phrase edges, never blending into a duet.
- **Failure mode:** ⭐ **the renderer collapses both voices into one centred lead and the argument disappears** — the single highest-probability generic failure for this song. Second form: it hears "two voices" and returns a lead plus a backing choir answering it, which is call-and-response, which is P01's device, which is a second device and a repair.
- **Why it matters:** the device *is* the staging. Centre the voices and there is no argument, only a song about an argument — which is the toll booth wearing a kitchen apron.

### F3 · ⛔ **NEITHER VOICE WINS AND NEITHER IS THE VILLAIN** — weight **14**
**THE REFUSAL BINDS HERE.** The "it's easy" voice must be sincere, likeable, and *right* — a person who genuinely found it easy, learned it from her mum at nine, means it kindly, and offers to help.
- **Failure mode:** the "it's easy" voice becomes a strawman — smug, dismissive, or set up to be corrected. The moment she loses, the song becomes a joke that requires a butt, and Sunna will not write one. Equally fatal in the other direction: the "it wasn't" voice becoming a whiner asking for pity rather than a person stating a fact.
- **Why it matters:** ⭐ **the cruelty here is structural, not personal.** *Nobody in this song is being cruel, and it lands anyway.* That is the entire reason the Marketing skeptic called it the best idea in the packet. Give it a villain and you have thrown away the only thing that makes it true.

### F4 · **THE DUB GOES GENUINELY EMPTY** — weight **12**
The gear-change drops to 70 BPM, then removes **everything** — the two voices keep arguing with no instrument under them at all — then the band slams back at full speed.
- **Failure mode:** ⭐ **the renderer fills the hole with a pad, a string bed, an ambient wash, or a reverb tail carpet**, because generators are frightened of silence. Second form: the "slam back" arrives as an EDM build with a white-noise riser instead of a hard cut.
- **Why it matters:** the removal is the emotional event. Two people arguing with nothing underneath them is the sound of a kitchen at midnight. Put a pad there and it becomes a breakdown in a song, rather than the thing itself.

### F5 · **A PERSON DOING SOMETHING IN VERSE ONE** — weight **10**
A hand on a pan. A hand writing steps on the back of a receipt. A hand buying two at a counter. In the lyric, first verse, not the note.
- **Failure mode:** verse one opens on the argument rather than on a body — "people always say" instead of "pan on, pan too cold." The COMPRESS round already caught this run writing a robot record and calling it delight.
- **Why it matters:** the machine is the occasion; the person is the subject. Nobody has ever recognised a position they have stood in from a generalisation.

### F6 · **THE FLAT TEST ON EVERY HOOK LINE, AND THE LINE SURVIVES ON ITS NOUN** — weight **9**
Say it out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face. *"It's easy." "It wasn't." "Pan, pinch, plate." "I bought two." "I'd have done it for you." "Who showed you?"*
- **Failure mode:** short-but-dead lines that need the melody to survive; or a plain line that leans on syntax instead of a noun and evaporates the second it is spoken.
- **Why it matters:** a plain line is harder to write than a dense one — density hides a weak idea and six plain words expose it instantly.

### F7 · **GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE — EVERY RETURN REMOVES** — weight **8**
Chorus 2 loses the hats. Chorus 3 loses the drums for two bars. The dub loses everything. Chorus 4 loses the guitar side of the trade and the bass answers into the hole where it was. V4 removes the answer itself.
- **Failure mode:** "infinitesimal gradations" read as a licence to add, and the song ends with fourteen automation lanes and a producer's note explaining that the listener feels them subconsciously.
- **Why it matters:** a removal is registered instantly and cannot be named, costs nothing, cannot be faked, and the mix never runs out of headroom.

### F8 · **RETURN IS FREE AND IS SPENT HARD** — weight **7**
Byte-identical chorus, four times, no note filed. The answer is two syllables and never changes. The room takes one side each.
- **Failure mode:** reading "simple" as "strip the repetition," and shipping a song whose chorus is varied for the sake of interest.
- **Why it matters:** repetition lowers processing cost while raising richness. It is the cheapest thing on the menu and this song is built to be shouted back by a room that has split itself in half.

### F9 · **DRY, EYE-ROLLING, FUNNY AS A REQUIREMENT** — weight **5**
Feelings stated so flatly you can hear the eye-roll. Playground taunt one bar, flat statement the next.
- **Failure mode:** the subject matter pulls the song earnest, and it becomes a tender ballad about being misunderstood. That is not this voice and it is not this seat.
- **Why it matters:** ⛔ never earnest, never reverent, never explaining. If it stops being funny it stops being Sunna, and if it stops being Sunna the Pantheon has spent a seat on a note.

### F10 · **THE WOLF IS BEHIND HER AND IS NEVER MENTIONED** — weight **4**
One pursuer, rising, never resolving, never named.
- **Failure mode:** the pursuer gets described in the lyric, or resolves at the end and gives the song a bow.
- **Why it matters:** joy that has looked at the wolf is credible; joy that hasn't is a mascot. Name the wolf and you have explained the joke.
