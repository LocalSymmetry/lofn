# P01 · STEP 06 — THE RIFF, THEN THE FACETS

## ⭐ 0. THE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, BEFORE A SINGLE WORD

**Instrument:** brass-synth horn stab — one voice, short, hard, no tail. A baritone-brass patch played
like a klaxon, not a section.

**Interval:** a **minor third**. Two pitches only: **C5 (523 Hz) → A4 (440 Hz)**, falling.

**Octave range:** A4 to C5. **Three semitones. Inside one octave by a factor of four.**

**Placement:** the first note lands on the **and-of-two**; the second lands flat on **beat three**.
The figure is a *lurch*, not a pulse — it arrives late and then plants itself.

**The two-bar shape (this is the whole riff):**

```
bar 1   .  .  C5 A4  .  .        (falling — the call)
bar 2   .  .  A4 C5  .  .        (rising  — the answer)
```

**The riff is the device.** The horn calls itself and answers itself for four bars with nothing else
alive, so the room learns the shape of a call-and-answer **before anybody sings a word about it.**
When the horn is removed in chorus four, the hole it leaves is a hole the listener already knows how
to fill — because they have been hearing it filled since second one.

**Where it lives:** four bars alone at the top, over silence. Present under every chorus except the
fourth. Alone again over the dead stop at the end. **The record opens with it and closes with it and
the vocal is a guest in between.**

### ⭐ THE WHISTLE TEST — the honest answer

*Could someone whistle it walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, drunk?*

**Yes — and the honest reason is that it is closer to a doorbell than to a melody.** Two pitches a
minor third apart in a fixed rhythm is the cheapest memorable object in music, which is exactly why it
survives four pints and a cold walk to a night bus. A drunk person cannot whistle a topline. A drunk
person can whistle a doorbell, and does, unprompted.

**The honest risk, stated rather than hidden:** two notes is so little that it can read as a *signal*
rather than a *tune*. **The rhythm is what makes it a tune** — the and-of-two entry is syncopated
enough that whistling it requires you to feel the beat you are not whistling, which is the thing that
gets stuck. Mitigation is structural, not decorative: the horn plays **alone, over silence, for four
bars, twice per record.** A figure that has survived four bars of nothing has proved it is content.

## THE EIGHT FACETS — weighted, each with its failure mode

Weights sum to 100. **F1, F2, F3 and F8 are GATES: a FAIL removes a variation from selection.**

### ⭐ F1 · THE RIFF IS THE FIRST AND LAST THING — weight 18 · GATE
The two-note horn stab is present before the vocal enters and after it leaves, and it is the only
element allowed to be alone.
- **Failure mode:** the horn arrives *with* the band, or arrives as a sustained brass chord under the
  chorus. At that moment it stops being a hook and becomes a colour.
- **Why it matters:** THE ROCK TITAN's charge, carried live into QA: *"put the complexity in the music"
  is what people say right before they put it in a plugin.* If the riff cannot hold four bars of
  silence, the words are still paying the toll and the doctrine has failed on its first song.

### ⭐ F2 · ONE DEVICE, AND THE ROOM'S ANSWER NEVER CHANGES — weight 16 · GATE
Call and response. Every chant line is answered by the room with **the same 4 syllables, every time,
in every variation: "Somebody saw."**
- **Failure mode:** the answer grows — gets a second phrase, gets a harmony, gets a variation on the
  last chorus "for a lift". Or a second device sneaks in (a list, a count, a misheard line).
- **Why it matters:** an answer that changes is a thing to follow. A fixed answer is a thing to *join*.
  The moment the room has to listen for what is coming, they stop shouting and start parsing.

### ⭐ F3 · THE HOLE IS REAL SILENCE — weight 14 · GATE
Between the call and the answer there is an actual gap in the mix. The lead vocal stops dead. The
answer is twelve untrained throats in a room, not a stacked backing vocal on the same singer.
- **Failure mode:** a doubled lead sings its own answer with a short delay on it. It sounds fine and it
  is fatal — a solo with a crowd sample bolted to it.
- **Why it matters:** THE NOISE SCHOLAR's seat: *if the recording doesn't leave a hole where the room's
  voice goes, it isn't a chant.* The hook has to belong to somebody other than the singer, and the only
  proof of ownership is that she is not singing in that bar.

### F4 · A PERSON DOING SOMETHING IN VERSE ONE — weight 12
A named-by-action human hand inside the first eight lines: the woman on the night desk, the man at the
sink, the man awake at ten to four, the woman clocking on early.
- **Failure mode:** verse one is about an orbiter, a photograph, or "somebody" as an abstraction.
- **Why it matters:** the COMPRESS round's verdict — *"you have written a robot record and called it
  delight."* The machine is the occasion; the person is the subject. A first verse with no hands in it
  is a repair, not a note.

### F5 · THE OPENING SHOT STAYS A SHOT — weight 10
Exactly **one line** about the Moon, in verse one, and the song never goes back.
- **Failure mode:** a chorus mentions the picture. A bridge goes back up there. Space becomes the spine.
- **Why it matters:** THE CONNECTION CYNIC withdrew his objection on one condition and this is it.
  *Nobody cares about a spacecraft photograph.* They care that somebody went out of their way. The
  moment the song returns to orbit, it is a fact about hardware again.

### F6 · GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE — weight 10
Chorus 2 loses the hi-hat. Chorus 3 loses the bass for two bars. Chorus 4 loses the horn and the room
answers into the gap. **Nothing is ever added on a return.**
- **Failure mode:** the last chorus gets a guitar octave, a harmony stack, a bigger snare — the reflex
  of every arrangement ever written.
- **Why it matters:** THE MINIMALIST ENGINEER's seat. Gradation by addition needs a producer's note
  explaining that the listener feels it subconsciously. Gradation by subtraction is registered
  instantly, cannot be named, cannot be faked, and the mix never runs out of headroom.

### F7 · THE FLAT TEST ON EVERY HOOK LINE — weight 10
Each call line survives being said out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face.
- **Failure mode:** a line that is merely *short* — "we're all still here", "the light gets in" — a
  shape with no noun in it.
- **Why it matters:** THE SCREAM-AND-WHISPER's seat: a plain line survives on its **noun**, not its
  syntax. *"Nobody's typing"* is unforgettable because of the specific thing that is not happening.
  *"Nobody's hoping"* is a greetings card.

### ⭐ F8 · THE PURSUER IS NEVER NAMED — weight 10 · GATE
One count-in, arriving earlier every section, never resolving into the song it keeps promising. It
exists only in the arrangement: **never in a sung line, never in a section cue, never in a title.**
Its one written trace is a production-channel address (`Disc_Rhythm`), which the render needs and the
listener never reads.
- **Failure mode:** somebody sings "one, two, three, four", or a cue reads *sticks count in* and the
  joke gets explained.
- **Why it matters:** S.O.L.A.R. **A — At Speed**. Joy that has looked at the wolf is credible; joy that
  explains the wolf is a mascot with a footnote. The pursuer is felt or it is nothing.

## THE DEVICE, IN FOUR WORDS
**`call and response`** — and, because the horn calls and answers itself for four bars before anyone
sings, **the riff and the device are one gesture.** That is not a second device; it is the first one,
played on brass.
