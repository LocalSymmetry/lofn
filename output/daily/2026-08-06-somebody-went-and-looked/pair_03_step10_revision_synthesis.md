## THE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, IDENTICAL ACROSS ALL FOUR

**Blown-out drop-D electric guitar. Four notes: E → G → A → B♭.** Up a minor third, up a whole tone, up a semitone, then a **tritone drop** home. **Total range: one tritone — inside one octave.**
Two fast eighths dragging into two quarters, **the flat fifth hung on the and-of-three** for a beat and a half. One amp with a torn cone, fret buzz kept, one take.
**It is the first sound on the record, alone, for eight bars before the first line — and the last sound on the record, alone, after the final word, dying mid-note.**

**WHISTLE TEST:** yes. Four notes, a tritone of range, and the thing you remember is a single sour note held too long. An arpeggio has no wrong note in it; this riff is a quarter wrong note.

**THE PURSUER (⛔ never named):** the octave-up double in the last chorus is played by a **second guitar that has been drifting flat all song** — roughly six cents at the top, roughly thirty by the end. The moment the record is biggest is the moment it is most out of tune. One lane, monotonic, never resolving, never mentioned in a lyric.

## THE ONE DEVICE — **THE LIST THAT LOVES**

Anaphora on the stem **“Look at the ___”**. One item per line, each absurdly specific and completely sincere. **No second device:** the room's answer hole is a bar of real silence in the arrangement and appears nowhere in the words.

**GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE — the list shortens, and every block is a strict subset of block one, in original order:**

V3 carries the wound and the refusal in the same three minutes' worth of material; V1 is the cleanest statement of the device; V4 is the funniest and the most likely to be misread as comedy; V2 is the warmest and therefore the one closest to a register Sunna does not keep.
