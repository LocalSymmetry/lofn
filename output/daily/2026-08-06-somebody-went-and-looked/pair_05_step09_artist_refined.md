## ⭐ THE PRIMARY JOB OF THIS STEP — THE SYMPATHY AUDIT

**The question, asked once per variation:** *is the "it's easy" voice a real person, or a strawman?*
**The test used:** play her lines back to somebody who genuinely finds the thing easy. Would they feel fairly heard, or would they feel set up?

## CRITIQUE 1 — **⚡ THE CONNECTION CYNIC** *(after Bob Lefsetz — the seat that promoted this pair out of a footnote)*

> I picked this one out of your packet and I'll be the one to check whether you ruined it. **Here's what everybody does with this idea: they write the smug guy.** They write the person who says "it's not that hard" as a villain, the audience gets to hate him, everybody goes home feeling correct, and **you have written a song that flatters your listener instead of telling them something.**
>
> Read your own V1 dub back. *"I only meant it's quick."* **That's a defence.** People defend themselves when they've been accused. **You've accused her without writing the accusation** — which is worse, because now she's small and she never got the chance to be big. >
> And check V3 while you're in there. *"Who showed you?" "My mum. I was nine." "Right."* — **that's a gotcha.** That's the internet. I know you followed it with an offer, and the offer saves it, but only just. **Make her offer land like an offer and not like an apology.**

**⛔ SUNNA'S RULING — CRITIQUE ACCEPTED IN FULL. This is the Refusal, and it binds.**

**REPAIR 1 (V1, Voices Alone):** *"I only meant it's quick."* → **"Come round. I'll do the first one."**
She stops defending and starts offering. She is the bigger person in the emptiest part of the record, which is exactly where the listener is paying most attention. And the answer that follows — *"I know. I know you did."* — now lands on a kindness rather than on a plea.

**REPAIR 2 (V3, Voices Alone):** the closing beat is re-weighted so **the last word belongs to the door opening, not to the point being scored.** Right's final line becomes **"Say when."** — which means *I might actually take you up on it.* The exchange stops being a gotcha and becomes two people finding a way through. **Nobody wins, and nobody has to.**

## CRITIQUE 2 — **THE SCREAM-AND-WHISPER** *(after Phoebe Bridgers)*

> *"A plain line survives on its NOUN."* Yours mostly do — *pan, receipt, biro, bin bag, sink, butcher's paper* — that's real, that's a body in a room, I have no complaint there.
>
> But V2: ***"I don't know what I hit."*** **That's a metaphor wearing a plain line's clothes.** *Hit* is doing emotional work the noun should be doing, and the second a listener notices the figure of speech, they're parsing. **It's the toll booth in an apron.**

**RULING — ACCEPTED.**
**REPAIR 3 (V2, Verse 3):** *"I don't know what I hit."* → **"I don't know what I did."**

## CRITIQUE 5 — **THE PLAYER-PRODUCER** *(after Nile Rodgers — the COMPRESS seat)*

> Three questions. **What's the riff? What's the real subject underneath? Can the room play it back?**
>
> Riff: yes. Room: yes — *it's easy / it wasn't* is four syllables and **the room splits in half and takes a side each**, which is the best crowd instruction anybody has written in this run, because a crowd that argues with itself is a crowd that's awake.
