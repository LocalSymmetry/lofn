## ⭐ 0. THE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, BEFORE A SINGLE WORD

**Instrument:** one **fuzz bass**, picked hard with a plectrum, mid-forward, mono centre, floor level. Not a sub. Not a pad. A bass playing a tune.

**The figure — seven notes, F♯ natural minor:**

| # | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| note | **F♯3** | **F♯3** | **A3** | **D4** | **C♯4** | **A3** | **F♯3** |
| move | — | thumb | up m3 | **up P4 → the ♭6** | down m2 | down P4 | down m3 |

**The signature is the fourth note.** F♯ up to **D** is a **rising minor sixth** — the widest gap in the figure, landing on the ♭6 of the key, which is the one note that does not belong to the comfortable triad. It sits there for a beat, sours, then falls a semitone to C♯ and walks home. **That leap-and-sour-and-fall is the whole hook.**

**Range: F♯3 → D4 = a minor sixth. Well inside one octave.** No accidental outside the natural minor. No interval a throat can't do out of breath.

**⭐ THE WHISTLE TEST — ANSWERED.** Two notes of thumb, a jump your mouth wants to make, one sour note, and a fall home. A drunk person walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, whistles **the jump and the fall** — and the two thumb-notes arrive by themselves because the mouth needs somewhere to start. **It passes.** The failure mode it dodges: a scalar four-note noodle that is technically a melody and that nobody's mouth ever chooses.

**⭐ THE ENFORCEMENT CLAUSE.** The bass plays the figure **alone for the first five seconds, before any drum and before any word**, and **alone again after the last word**, and the record ends on it. There is no bar of this song in which the figure is not present. **Nothing else is ever given the tune.**

**The guitar's job (Flair 13, Empowerment Drop-D):** drop-tuned, **stabs only**, short and muted, mid-right. Its lowest open string is **the D the bass leaps to** — so when the guitar hits, it hits the sour note the bass just made, and it never plays a line. **Flair 4, Turbo-Fuzz Bass, is the riff itself.**

⛔ **A pad is not a riff.** There is no pad in this song that could be mistaken for the hook, and the exclude prompt says so in Suno's own vocabulary.

### FACET 1 — **THE BASS HOLDS THE TUNE** · weight **22**
The fuzz bass must be **the melodic lead of the record**, mid-forward and legible as a tune with the vocal muted; the guitar must be **stabs only**; nothing else may carry a line.

- **Why it matters:** this is the pair's assignment and the panel called it *"the single most reliable way to pass the whistle test"* (the melodic-bass seat). If it holds, the free channel is genuinely carrying the load and every ounce of meaning is *not* back in the words. If it fails, THE FREE CHANNEL doctrine is unfalsified here and the song is a lyric with a backing track.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE — and it is the pair's single biggest risk:** *the renderer hands the melody to a guitar or a synth lead and the bass reverts to a root-note bed.* Detected by muting the vocal and asking whether the tune survives — **and by whether the tune is coming from the bass register.**
- **Repair:** the figure plays **naked** in the first five seconds and the last eight bars, so at those two points there is no other instrument that could be holding it; the exclude prompt blacklists *lead guitar melody*, *synth lead melody*, *root-note bass*, and *pad as the main hook* by name.

### FACET 2 — **THE MISHEARD LINE LANDS WITHOUT A NOTE** · weight **16**
The line appears twice, unchanged in text, and the second appearance is the machine's reading. **The song never says so.**

- **Why it matters:** this is the ONE device. One thing heard twice beats four things heard once, and the whole subversive-reference rule turns on it — *a listener who takes only the surface must receive a whole song.* Somebody who never notices the doubling still gets a complete record about a woman running.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE:** a gloss. *"Funny how it reads it wrong"*, *"it thinks I'm a thief"*, an emoji of a wink in the arrangement. Also: **adding a second device** — a count, a list, an argument — because the first one felt too small.
- **Repair:** delete the gloss and put the machine's register (*Motion detected. Grip released.*) around the line instead. The register is the frame; the line does not move.

### FACET 3 — **A PERSON DOING SOMETHING IN VERSE ONE** · weight **12**
A named, invented human with hands, in the first eight bars, doing a physical thing. Rae laces the left one twice. Nell writes a sentence. Tam is asleep with the curtains open.

- **Why it matters:** the COMPRESS round's verdict — *"you have written a robot record and called it delight."* **The machine is the occasion; the person is the subject.** A verse one with no human hand in it is an automatic repair.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE:** opening on the phone, the alert, the algorithm, or "they". A concept note masquerading as a verse.
- **Repair:** put a body and an object in the first line. Boots, a latch, a coffee lid, a grey switch.

### FACET 4 — **GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE AND AUDIBLE** · weight **11**
Four byte-identical choruses; each return **removes** exactly one thing, ending at voice + bass.

- **Why it matters:** Albini's seat. A removal is registered instantly and cannot be named, it cannot be faked in a prompt, and the mix never runs out of headroom. It is also the pair's proof that repetition is not flatness.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE:** gradation by **addition** — the last chorus is the biggest, a string pad arrives, the guitar doubles up an octave. Also: a chorus that is not byte-identical because someone got bored and "improved" a word.
- **Repair:** the section headers name the removal explicitly, and the final chorus is specified as **drums out**. Nothing enters after the first chorus that was not in it.

### FACET 5 — **THE CHORUS IS THE ROOM'S, BY 0:25** · weight **11**
Four to eight syllables, no interval a normal throat can't reach at volume while out of breath, and it arrives before twenty-five seconds. A real hole after each line where the room's voice goes — **silence and the bass figure, never a stacked vocal.**

- **Why it matters:** *Load the Floor.* The hook does not belong to the singer. **"Nobody's taking nothing"** is seven syllables of a thing you say to a policeman, and it survives the flat test with the eye-roll intact.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE:** a chorus that is a sentence about the theme; a "hole" filled with a doubled backing vocal, which is a solo with a crowd sample on it; a chorus arriving at 0:48 because verse one had one more good line in it.
- **Repair:** cut the verse, not the chorus. Intro is eight bars of bass and one door latch.

### FACET 6 — **THE GEAR-CHANGE IS A DROP AND A SPRINT, NOT A REMIX** · weight **10**
128 through the verses; the kit **halves** under the alert; then **170 double-time** to the end, with the bass figure unchanged through all three.

- **Why it matters:** Sunna's pillar 3 — one song that is three songs — and here it is doing narrative work for free: the half-time is the moment the screen decides something, the double-time is her deciding to go faster anyway. **Head-Rush Tempo Swings** (Flair 7) earned rather than decorative.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE:** the tempo change is a genre change; the riff mutates to "fit" the new feel; a white-noise riser announces the drop; the double-time section is where the song forgets its own hook.
- **Repair:** the riff is **the constant** across all three feels — that is the point, and it is what makes the change legible instead of confusing.

### FACET 7 — **THE PURSUER IS PRESENT AND UNNAMED** · weight **9**
A filter climbing through the room bed across the whole track that **never opens**, in every one of the four variations.

- **Why it matters:** *Joy that has looked at the wolf is credible. Joy that hasn't is a mascot.* This song is delight at speed with a thing gaining on it, and the thing is never mentioned, so nobody has to parse anything.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE:** the pursuer gets named in the lyric ("something's watching", "the eye", "the system") — an instant fail of the axis. Or it resolves: the filter opens at the last chorus, which converts dread into a drop and makes the song a mascot after all.
- **Repair:** the automation lane is specified in the prompt only, and it is still climbing when the record stops.

### FACET 8 — **THE REFUSAL HOLDS** · weight **9**
⛔ Sunna will not be ironic about anything anyone loves. **Nell, who wrote the sentence the phone asks, meant it kindly and is not the butt of this song.** The engineers are not idiots. The alert is not a punchline about stupid technology.

- **Why it matters:** `vault/ALLURE.md` — allure without refusal is not charm, it is availability. It is also the difference between this song and every lazy song about a dumb app. The machine is not stupid here; **it is doing exactly what it was asked to do, by someone who was trying to help, and it still cannot read joy.** That is the sad, funny, un-sneering fact the record is built on.
- **⛔ FAILURE MODE:** a joke that requires a loser. Contempt for a product manager. "So bad it's good." Any line where the listener is invited to feel superior to the person who wrote the sentence.
- **Repair:** give the sentence's author a verse of her own (V2), let her be careful and good at her job, and let the song hurt her too.
