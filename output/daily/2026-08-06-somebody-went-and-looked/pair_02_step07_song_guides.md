## ***Where is the body standing?***

**On a wet ring road at six in the morning, three-quarters of a mile from the house, with a phone in the hand because the pocket is a dud.** Not a stage. Not a studio. A cracked footpath past a shut pub, a bend, a bin, and a hill she takes every day whether or not anyone knows. The body is **warm from the inside and cold on the outside**, breathing at the top of its range, and it is *happy* — not thoughtfully happy, physically happy, the way a body is happy when it is doing the exact thing it is for.

Her weight is forward. Her left shoe was laced twice. Her thumb is over the screen so the rain does not wake it.

**And the second body is asleep**, three streets back, curtains open, phone on the pillow, in the six minutes before the sound of a key and a cough and boots on the stairs would have told him everything was ordinary.

## ***What could hurt it here?***

Not the road. Not the rain. Not the wolf.

**What can hurt it is being read.** Not watched — *read*, and read by something that has exactly one category available to it for a body moving fast with a handset. The hurt is not fear and it is not surveillance dread; those are Lofn's registers and they are the toll booth. **The hurt is the specific, small, absurd insult of being converted** — of having the best twenty minutes of your day arrive somewhere else as *Handset moving. Owner unclear.*

And the second hurt, the one that gives the song its refusal: **somebody wrote that sentence, and they wrote it to help.** Nine drafts of trying to be kind. It still cannot read joy. **Nobody in this song is stupid and nobody is a villain, and it goes wrong anyway** — which is the only version of this idea that is not a cheap joke about apps.

And the third thing, which is what the record is actually for: **somebody got the alert, and instead of ignoring it, put his shoes on and went out to look.** *You didn't know you were braced until you stopped bracing.*

⛔ **What must NOT be allowed to hurt it:** a lyric that explains any of the above. Every sentence in this pair stays plain and **continues the story instead of interpreting it.**

# THE SIX GUIDES

## GUIDE 1 — **THE FIGURE THAT WON'T GIVE UP THE TUNE**
*(Facet 1 · weight 22 · the bass holds the melody)*

**Storytelling techniques:** *In medias res* — the record is already moving when you arrive, because the bass has been playing the figure for five seconds before anything else exists, and it is still playing it when the vocal has gone. And **the recurring object**: seven notes that are present in literally every bar, so that the listener's memory of the song is a bass part, not a sentence.

**Mood & emotional arc:** begin in flat physical readiness — the pleasure of a body about to be used — hold it steady through irritation and absurdity, and end in **liberation without triumph**: not a win, just a woman still running. No catharsis, no key change, nothing resolves, and the last thing you hear is the same seven notes it opened with.

**Instrumentation & texture:** one **fuzz bass** picked hard with a plectrum, mono, mid-forward, floor level, distorted enough to have upper harmonics you could whistle along with. A **drop-tuned electric guitar reduced to short muted stabs, mid-right**, whose lowest open string is the exact D the bass leaps to, so the guitar only ever agrees with the sour note and never proposes one. A dry punk kit, kick nudged eight to fourteen milliseconds late while the bass sits locked on the grid. Two room mics, low, high-passed, on a short sweaty plate. **No acoustic instruments.**

**Production & effects:** recorded like a room with the wrong ceiling height and a fire exit propped open. Lead vocal on a dynamic mic, close, **no de-esser on the breaths**, doubles deliberately misaligned fifteen to thirty-five milliseconds and pitched a few cents apart. Master at −14 LUFS with the crest left alone — **weight, not loudness.**

**Structure & dynamics:** 2:40. Eight bars of naked bass, verse, chorus on the button at 0:24, verse, chorus, the half-time hinge, the double-time sprint, two more choruses shedding elements, eight bars of naked bass, stop.

**Rhyming schemes:** verses in loose couplets (AABB) with a deliberately unrhymed final line — the misheard line, which lands flat on purpose. Choruses AAXA where X is the noun line.

**Poetic devices:** **alliteration** used as rhythm rather than ornament (*pocket, pavement, puddle, past the pub*), and **epizeuxis** (*off, and off, and off, and off*).

**Hook techniques:** (1) the figure alone in the first five seconds with a door latch over it, so it arrives as an event and not an intro; (2) the figure under the machine's flat read in the half-time hinge, where it is the *only* melodic thing in the mix and the ear has nowhere else to go; (3) the last chorus with the drums gone — **voice and fuzz bass, nothing else** — which is the moment the listener discovers they have known the tune for two minutes.

**Narrative seed:** a woman running, and a bassline that refuses to be an accompaniment.

**Central aspect:** *the free channel, spent hard.* **Emotional tone signals:** flat, warm, dry, physically pleased. **Rhythm & melody:** the figure is the melody; the vocal orbits it.

## GUIDE 2 — **THE SAME SEVEN WORDS, TWICE**
*(Facet 2 · weight 16 · the misheard line)*

**Storytelling techniques:** **dramatic irony inverted** — the listener and the singer know the same thing at the same moment, so nobody is being tested. And **the found document**: the second appearance sits inside a stack of machine fields the way a real alert does, and the surrounding lines are stolen from the register of status text, not from poetry.

**Mood & emotional arc:** from a neighbour's fond, offhand delight → a two-second vacuum where the drums halve → the flat, courteous, catastrophic register of a status message → and out the other side into defiance that never raises its voice.

**Instrumentation & texture:** at the second appearance the arrangement is **stripped to the bass figure and a close, unaffected voice**, because the line must be heard as text and not as a performance. A single guitar stab on the last word, and nothing else.

**Production & effects:** the machine lines get **no processing at all** — no vocoder, no telephone filter, no robot. Suno will reach for a filtered voice here and it must not have one: the horror is that it sounds like a person reading a form. One buzz on a metal rail, recorded close.

**Structure & dynamics:** appearance one closes verse one, in every variation. Appearance two closes the half-time hinge, in every variation. **Never a third.**

**Rhyming schemes:** the machine block is unrhymed and clipped — full stops mid-line, four short declaratives, then the line. The absence of rhyme *there* is the removal that makes it land.

**Poetic devices:** **antanaclasis** (the same words, two readings) and **asyndeton** in the alert block (*Motion detected. Grip released.*).

**Hook techniques:** (1) say it fondly the first time, over a full band, so nobody is listening hard; (2) say it the second time with the drums gone, at the same pitch and the same speed, and let the arrangement do all of the work; (3) in V4, let the second appearance be **factually wrong** — the phone is on the kitchen table — and do not comment.

**Narrative seed:** one sentence, two owners.

**Key sequences:** the four bars where the kit halves and the bass keeps its tempo, so the figure appears to speed up without changing.

## GUIDE 3 — **HANDS IN THE FIRST EIGHT BARS**
*(Facet 3 · weight 12 · a person doing something)*

**Storytelling techniques:** **the telling detail** — a left shoe laced twice for luck, a coffee lid, a list of little grey switches — and **the ordinary witness**, somebody at a second-floor window who says the thing out loud like a fact.

**Mood & emotional arc:** open in domestic warmth so specific it is almost boring, and let the boredom be the proof of attention. *Vague delight is decoration; specific delight is evidence.*

**Instrumentation & texture:** verse one is the thinnest the band ever gets while still being a band — bass, kick, one hat, stabs. The room mics are barely up. Everything is saving itself for the chorus fourteen seconds away.

**Production & effects:** close, dry, unglamorous. Breath audible before the first word. If the singer laughs mid-phrase, keep it.

**Structure & dynamics:** six to seven lines, then the misheard line, then the chorus. No pre-chorus in verse one — **the chorus cannot wait.**

**Rhyming schemes:** AABB couplets, plain words, one and two syllables, so the verse reads at the speed of speech.

**Poetic devices:** **polysyndeton** in the domestic lines (*and off, and off, and off*), **consonance** on the hard stops (*key, cough, boots*).

**Hook techniques:** (1) put a name in the first three words; (2) put an object in the first line and a body part in the second; (3) end the verse on the misheard line so the chorus arrives as a reply to it.

**Narrative seed:** the machine is the occasion. **The person is the subject.**

## GUIDE 4 — **EVERY RETURN TAKES SOMETHING AWAY**
*(Facet 4 · weight 11 · subtractive gradation)*

**Storytelling techniques:** **the refrain that erodes** — identical words in a shrinking room — and **negative space** as narration: what has left the mix is the plot.

**Mood & emotional arc:** the paradox this guide exists to produce — the song feels like it is *growing* while it is measurably *shrinking*. Chorus one is a room. Chorus four is two people. The listener registers the change instantly and cannot name a single thing that happened.

**Instrumentation & texture:** C1 full — bass, kit, stabs, room, second voice. **C2: guitar stabs out.** **C3: hi-hat and room mics out.** **C4: drums out — voice and fuzz bass, alone.**

**Production & effects:** nothing may be added after the first chorus that was not in the first chorus. No new reverb, no octave double, no string pad, no octave-up guitar. The mix must have headroom at chorus one and more of it at chorus four.

**Structure & dynamics:** four choruses, **byte-identical in the words, no note filed.** The performance is never identical: a hair earlier, a hair drier, one handclap that arrives before the beat and stays early.

**Rhyming schemes:** unchanged, forever. **Return is free.**

**Poetic devices:** **epanalepsis** across the whole song (the record begins and ends on the same seven notes), **anaphora** in the split section.

**Hook techniques:** (1) leave a real hole after each chorus line — silence and the bass figure, so the room's voice has somewhere to go, and never fill it with a stacked backing vocal; (2) drop the lead out of one call entirely and let the hole be the whole bar; (3) make the last chorus the quietest thing in the song and put the singer closest to the mic.

**Narrative seed:** the same sentence, in a smaller and smaller room, getting louder.

## GUIDE 5 — **HALVE IT, THEN DOUBLE IT**
*(Facet 6 · weight 10 · the gear-change, with Facet 5's chorus discipline)*

**Storytelling techniques:** **the turn** — one structural hinge, placed where the story hinges, and nowhere else — and **the chase that never catches up**, carried entirely by a filter nobody mentions.

**Mood & emotional arc:** 128 is a woman running comfortably. The half-time is the two seconds after a screen lights up. **170 is her deciding to go faster on purpose**, which is the record's only act of will and it is completely wordless in its cause.

**Instrumentation & texture:** at the hinge the kit halves and the guitar leaves; at the sprint the kit doubles into sixteenths, the hats open a fraction more each bar, and **the bass figure does not change at all in either place.** That constancy is what makes both changes legible instead of confusing.

**Production & effects:** no white-noise riser, no airhorn, no big-room build, no filter sweep announcing the transition — **the drums simply change and the listener is already there.** A rolling drum fill, human and slightly early, is the only handover.

**Structure & dynamics:** hinge at roughly 1:25, sprint from roughly 1:45, and the last forty seconds are two choruses and eight bars of bass.

**Rhyming schemes:** the sprint is the most rhymed section in the song — close couplets, one and two syllables, because a sprint is where the mouth wants to be full.

**Poetic devices:** **diacope** (*picks it up, picks it up*), **internal rhyme** through the sprint.

**Hook techniques:** (1) the chorus lands at 0:24 and never later; (2) seven syllables, no leap wider than the riff's own minor sixth, so a room out of breath can hold it; (3) the pursuer — the climbing filter — is at its highest and still shut when the record stops.

**Narrative seed:** three tempos, one figure, one morning.

## GUIDE 6 — ⭐ **WILD CARD: THE SENTENCE SOMEBODY WROTE TO BE KIND**
*(Facet 8 · weight 9 · THE REFUSAL — the cross-disciplinary guide)*

**Cross-domain seed:** this guide comes from **industrial design and technical writing**, not music — from the discipline of the warning label, the lift-emergency card, the six words on a small grey panel that somebody agonised over. There is a whole profession of people whose job is to write one careful sentence for the worst two seconds of a stranger's day, and **the world has never once thanked one of them.**

**Storytelling techniques:** **the sympathetic antagonist** — the sentence is the antagonist and its author is a hero — and **the artefact with a maker**: every awful automated message you have ever received was typed by a person who was trying.

**Mood & emotional arc:** V2 begins late at night in an office with one lamp on, in a mood of genuine craft and care, and ends on a bus the following morning when the writer's own good sentence goes out and reads her sister as a thief. Not comedy at her expense. **Grief-adjacent and completely dry.**

**Instrumentation & texture:** the same band, one degree gentler in verse one — the stabs are softer, the kit is brushed rather than hit, and the fuzz bass is the only thing at full strength, because it is the only thing that never doubts itself.

**Production & effects:** the room mics come up slightly on her verse and never again. One page-turn or key-click as the SFX, close and unglamorous.

**Structure & dynamics:** her verse is the longest single stretch of continuous singing in the pair, and it is followed by the shortest gap before a chorus.

**Rhyming schemes:** AABB, deliberately neat and slightly old-fashioned — the prosody of somebody who cares about getting words right.

**Poetic devices:** **anadiplosis** (*writes it kind, writes it short, writes it right*), **homoioteleuton** across the drafts.

**Hook techniques:** (1) make the chorus **the sentence itself** — *"Are you being robbed right now"* — so the room sings the thing that hurt, with no irony available anywhere in the delivery; (2) let the second voice enter a third above on the last chorus and split to a fifth on the final word (Flair 15, Anarchy Harmonizers), so the gap opens exactly where two readings of one line separate; (3) end on the author opening the file again.

⛔ **THE REFUSAL, enforced here:** not one line may sneer at her, at the alert, at the product, or at anybody who has ever trusted a phone to look after them. **No joke in this pair requires a butt.** If a listener could hear a smirk, it is a repair.

**Narrative seed:** somebody wrote a careful sentence to protect a stranger, and it went out and misread the person she loves, and she is going to sit down and write a tenth draft.
