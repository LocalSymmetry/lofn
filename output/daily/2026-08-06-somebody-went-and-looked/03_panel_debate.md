# CONFIGURATION 1 — BASELINE

## PANEL A — CONCEPT
**1. THE SPARK-THROWER** — *after Tillie (b. 2000)* · GROUNDING: DIY punk-pop practice, hook-first writing on minimal means. · **"Somebody went and looked" is already a chant. Four stresses, no consonant cluster, a drunk person can shout it. Don't improve it."**
**2. THE BATTLE-AXE** — *after Ashnikko (b. 1996)* · GROUNDING: documented club-rap/punk hybrid, blunt first-person provocations over heavy low end. · **"Sunna's problem isn't simplicity, it's politeness. 'Somebody went and looked' is lovely and it isn't a taunt. Where's the line that's a bit rude?"**
**3. THE RIFF INSURGENT** — *after Amy Love, Nova Twins (b. 1993)* · GROUNDING: bass-led heavy duo practice; riff as the song's primary content. · **"Every one of these six needs its riff written FIRST, before a single word. If the lyric arrives first you'll bend the riff to fit it and you'll get a texture."**
**4. THE ALT-ANTHEM VETERAN** — *after Fefe Dobson (b. 1985)* · GROUNDING: early-2000s alt-pop anthem construction. · **"Chorus by twenty-five seconds or it isn't an anthem. Sunna's own rule says under three minutes; that means the hook can't wait."**
**5. THE SCREAM LEGEND** — *after Alice Glass (b. 1988)* · GROUNDING: electronic-punk vocal performance, extremity as texture. · **"The gradation rule is the best thing in this seed. Same chant four times, and each time something is wrong-er. Nobody will be able to point at it."**
**6. ⚡ HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE ROCK TITAN** — *after Dave Grohl (b. 1969)* · GROUNDING: documented position that songs must survive being played by a band in a room; long-standing public critique of records built from parts rather than performances.
> **THE ROCK TITAN:** Here's my problem and it's the whole doctrine. **"Put the complexity in the music" is what every producer says right before they put the complexity in the PLUGIN.** A filter sweep is not a riff. A side-chain is not a riff. **A riff is a thing a person plays with their hands that another person can play back.**
> And you've written a Hum Test. Good. **Now make it survive the obvious cheat:** you'll write "hummable riff" in a prompt, the renderer will return a competent arpeggio, and the self-check will tick the box. **The test has to be — could someone whistle it walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, drunk?** If yes, it's a riff. Everything else is arrangement.

> **THE RIFF INSURGENT:** *Actually* — that sharpens mine. Riff first, and riff **playable**: name the instrument, name the interval, keep it inside an octave.
> **THE BATTLE-AXE:** And it answers my complaint too. A rude line isn't rudeness for its own sake — **a riff that's a bit ugly does the same job and costs nothing.** Put the rudeness in the fuzz.

> ### ⭐ AHA 1 — **THE WHISTLE TEST.** *(the Hum Test, made un-cheatable)*
> **Could someone whistle it walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, drunk?** If not, it is arrangement, not a riff. Enforcement: **the riff is written FIRST, before any lyric; named by instrument and interval; and it stays inside one octave.**

## PANEL B — MEDIUM
**7. THE LAYER SURGEON** — *after Misha Mansoor (b. 1984)* · GROUNDING: documented djent production practice — rhythmic guitar as percussion, tight low-end editing. · **"If the guitar IS the rhythm section, you get complexity and groove from one gesture. That's the cheapest richness available."**
**8. THE GRIT POLISHER** — *after Butch Vig (b. 1955)* · GROUNDING: documented alt-rock production — pop structure under abrasive surface, *Nevermind*-era practice. · **"Loud-quiet-loud isn't a cliché, it's the reason a simple chorus feels enormous. You don't need a new device. You need the verse to get out of the way."**
**9. THE MELODIC BASS DRIVE** — *after Brooke Dickson (The Regrettes)* · GROUNDING: melodic bass-led writing and co-production. · **"'The bassline IS the tune' should be more than one pair's axis value. It's the single most reliable way to pass the whistle test."**
**10. THE DIY SOUND-CRAFT** — *after K.Flay (b. 1985)* · GROUNDING: documented DIY alt-hip-hop production; sing-speak over sparse beds. · **"Sing-speak is the doctrine's friend: nobody parses a sing-spoken line, they just receive it. But it needs a REAL melody somewhere or the whole song is talking."**
**11. THE MIX CLARITY** — *after Laura Sisk* · GROUNDING: documented engineering practice on dense contemporary pop. · **"Gradation only works if the mix has headroom to grow into. If the first chorus is already at the ceiling, 'a hair louder' is inaudible and your form rule is decorative."**
**12. ⚡ HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE MINIMALIST ENGINEER** *(seat constructed — the library panel had none)* — *after Steve Albini (1962–2024)* · GROUNDING: documented practice that a record documents a performance rather than manufactures one; hostility to production-as-rescue.
> **THE MINIMALIST ENGINEER:** *"Infinitesimal gradations"* is the most dangerous instruction in this packet. It is a licence to add. **Every one of your six songs will end up with fourteen automation lanes and a producer's note explaining that the listener feels them subconsciously.**
> Here is the honest version: **gradation by ADDITION is a lie. Gradation by SUBTRACTION is a fact.** Take the hi-hat out of chorus three. Drop the bass for two bars in chorus four. **A listener registers a removal instantly and cannot name it — which is exactly the effect you claim to want, and it costs nothing and cannot be faked.**

> **THE MIX CLARITY:** He's right and it solves my headroom objection at the same time. If gradation is subtractive you never hit the ceiling.
> **THE GRIT POLISHER:** And it's loud-quiet-loud at the scale of a single element. Same principle, finer grain.

> ### ⭐ AHA 2 — **GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE.** Each return of the chant **removes** something rather than adding. The listener registers it instantly, cannot name it, and the mix never runs out of room. *(Also kills the "fourteen automation lanes" failure mode before it exists.)*

## PANEL C — CONTEXT & MARKETING
**13. THE NOISE SCHOLAR** — *after a scholar of feminist noise scenes* · **"The chant belongs to the room. If the recording doesn't leave a hole where the room's voice goes, it isn't a chant — it's a solo with a crowd sample on it."**
**14. THE VENUE SOCIOLOGIST** — *after a sociologist of DIY venues* · **"'Somebody went and looked' is a phrase about *effort on behalf of someone else.* That is the most transferable idea in the packet and nobody has said it out loud yet."**
**15. It is how anything becomes recognisable at all. Your run finally agrees with forty years of published evidence."**
**16. THE SCREAM-AND-WHISPER** — *after Phoebe Bridgers (b. 1994)* · GROUNDING: documented practice of devastating plain-language detail in quiet registers. · **"A plain line survives on its NOUN. 'A machine that thinks your running is a robbery' is plain and unforgettable because of the specific misreading, not the syntax."**
**17. THE HEAVY-RIFF DUO** — *after Nova Twins* · **"Post the riff before the song. The room learns eight seconds of guitar and arrives already knowing it."**
**18. ⚡ HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE CONNECTION CYNIC** *(re-derived so no figure holds two skeptic chairs)* — *after Bob Lefsetz (b. 1953)* · GROUNDING: long-running published critique of industry theory divorced from audience reality.
> **THE CONNECTION CYNIC:** **Nobody cares about a spacecraft photograph.** You have built a record on an orbiter turning its camera. That is a fact about hardware.
> And I'll tell you what's worse — **you had the good one and you buried it at F5.** A hundred and eighty people arguing about whether cooking a steak is easy. **That is a song. That's every comment section, every family, every time someone says "it's not that hard" to somebody it was hard for.** *"Almost no skill required"* is the most quietly cruel sentence in this document and it's sitting in a footnote.

> **THE SCREAM-AND-WHISPER:** He's right, and it's the same note I gave. The plain line lives on the specific noun, and *"almost no skill required"* is a specific noun.
> **THE VENUE SOCIOLOGIST:** *Wait* — I want to defend the orbiter, but only by demoting it. The photograph isn't the subject. **"Somebody went out of their way to go and look" is the subject**, and it happens at human scale constantly: someone drives out to check on you. **Keep the fact as the run's title and its opening image; put the feeling in a person.**
> **THE CONNECTION CYNIC:** Then say that in the brief, because right now the brief says the orbiter is the spine. Make it the **opening shot** and I withdraw.

> ### ⭐ AHA 3 — **THE ORBITER IS THE OPENING SHOT, NOT THE SPINE.** The subject is **somebody going out of their way to go and look at something for you.** It happens at human scale every day. **P05 is promoted:** *"almost no skill required"* — a hundred and eighty people arguing about whether an easy thing is easy — is the run's cruellest and most universal situation.

# CONFIGURATION 2 — GROUP TRANSFORM: **COMPRESS**
*Proposed by the baseline panel. Rule: find the MINIMUM number of panelists whose combined expertise covers everything; seek polymaths.*
**Chosen because the run's own doctrine is "one idea, not six" — a panel that cannot practise its own rule has no standing to enforce it.** Eighteen seats compressed to three.

**19. THE PLAYER-PRODUCER** — *after Nile Rodgers (b. 1952)* · GROUNDING: Chic; the documented "Deep Hidden Meaning" method — every song has a real subject underneath — plus the practice of making the guitar part itself the hook.
> **The three questions that replace fourteen: What's the riff? What's the real subject underneath? Can the room play it back?** If a song answers all three it is finished, and if it answers two it isn't started. *"Le Freak" is a party record written about being refused at a door. Nobody needs to know that, and it's in the record either way.*

**20. THE SONG-SHAPE** — *after Butch Vig* *(carried up from Medium — he was already doing two jobs)*
> **Loud-quiet-loud is the entire arrangement doctrine and it is free.** Combined with subtractive gradation it gives you dynamics, growth and a chorus that feels enormous without one extra element.

**21. ⚡ COMPRESSED HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE PLAIN AUDITOR** — *after Bob Lefsetz*
> **Compression exposes what the eighteen were hiding: five of your six pair concepts are about MACHINES.** An orbiter, a phone, a fastener, an AI virus, a botany channel. **The one about people arguing is the only one with a person in it, and it's the one I picked out of a footnote.**
> **You have written a robot record and called it delight.**

> **THE PLAYER-PRODUCER:** *Actually — no, and here is the fix rather than the retreat.* Every one of those machine facts **already has a person in it and the brief keeps cutting them out.** Somebody *aimed* the orbiter. Somebody *wrote* the fastener article. Somebody *typed* the 180th comment. **The machine is the occasion. The person is the subject.**
> **THE PLAIN AUDITOR:** That I'll take. But it has to be in the LYRIC, not in the concept note.

> ### ⭐ AHA 4 — **EVERY SONG NAMES A PERSON DOING SOMETHING.** Not a machine, not an event, not a fact. **The machine is the occasion; the person is the subject.** A song whose first verse has no human hand in it is a repair. *(This is the whole "somebody" in the run's title, applied six times.)*

# CONFIGURATION 3 — SKEPTIC TRANSFORM: **AMPLIFY**
*Chosen by the Hyper-Skeptics jointly. Rule: replace each seat with the MOST EXTREME version of its specialty.*
Their reason: *"the doctrine says one idea repeated until inevitable. Bring people who take one idea further than is comfortable, and see whether the doctrine survives its own logic."*

**22. THE ONE-CHORD MAXIMALIST** — *after Fela Kuti (1938–1997)* · GROUNDING: documented Afrobeat practice — twenty-minute pieces built on a single unchanging harmonic centre, all motion in the interlocking parts.
> **You are frightened of repetition and you have written a doctrine that pretends otherwise.** Four choruses is not repetition; four choruses is a pop song. **The real version is: one riff, and everything else moves around it for the whole song.** If your riff can't survive being the only harmonic event, **it is not a good enough riff and no amount of gear-changing will hide that.**

**23. THE SINGLE-SENTENCE EXTREMIST** — *after Alvin Lucier (1931–2021)* · GROUNDING: *I Am Sitting in a Room* — one spoken sentence, recorded and re-recorded until the room's resonance eats the words and only the music remains.
> The most extreme statement of your own thesis, and it was made in 1969: **take one plain sentence and repeat it until it stops being language and becomes sound.** *"You cannot decode and surrender at the same time"* — Lucier's piece is that sentence proved on tape. **Somewhere in this run one chant must repeat past the point of comfort, until the listener stops hearing the words.**

**24. ⚡ AMPLIFIED HYPER-SKEPTIC — THE PERFORMANCE PURIST** — *after Steve Albini*
> Both of them are describing records that take **twenty minutes**, and Sunna's own spec says **under three.** You cannot have Fela's one-chord patience and a two-minute punk song. **Pick.**
> And notice what just happened: **the amplification broke your constraint, which means the constraint is doing real work.** That is the most useful thing I can tell you. **Sunna is short because the delight is finite — the set ends, that's the point.** So: **one song, and only one, is allowed to be the long one** — and it should be the one that repeats past comfort.

> ### ⭐ AHA 5 — **ONE LONG ONE.** Five songs stay under three minutes. **P04 is designated the long one**: one riff as the only harmonic event, and a chant that repeats past the point of comfort until the words stop being words. *The constraint held everywhere it mattered and bent exactly once, on purpose.*
