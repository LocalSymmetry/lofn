## ⭐ 0. THE RIFF — written before any lyric existed

**Production shorthand: THE STACK.** It has no name in the lyric and never will.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Instrument** | two women's voices, close on a dynamic mic, dead dry — plus fuzz bass doubling **only the lower voice**, one octave down, so the bass is the hook's shadow rather than a second idea |
| **Key** | C natural minor |
| **Figure — lower voice** | **E♭4 · F4 · G4 · A♭4 · G4** |
| **Figure — upper voice** | **G4 · A♭4 · B♭4 · C5 · B♭4** |
| **Interval** | a **diatonic third above, note for note** — M3, m3, m3, M3, m3 |
| **Range** | E♭4 → C5 = a **major sixth**. Inside one octave ✅ |
| **Rhythm** | pickup on the *and* of 4, then four staccato eighths and a held half. It always arrives a beat early — that is the "at speed" |
| **Syllable** | **`Da-da-da-da — dah`** — wordless. Four hard consonant attacks and one held note |

### ⭐ WHY THIS SHAPE AND NOT ANOTHER
**The figure climbs four steps and falls back one.** It never reaches the top of its own arc, and it never
gets home. That is the count, drawn: *up, up, up, up — and not quite.* **The riff and the device are the
same gesture**, which is the cheapest richness on the menu (the Player-Producer's seat: *what's the riff /
what's the real subject / can the room play it back*).

⭐ **And the fifth note is where the number goes.** In every chorus the lead sings the count **on the held
note** while the two voices hold the harmony under it. The riff has a hole in it shaped exactly like the
only new information in the song.

### ⭐ THE SPLIT — the one moment
On the fifth note the lower voice stays on **G4** and the upper voice **refuses the third**: instead of
falling to B♭4 it goes **up to D5**. The closing interval is **G4–D5, a perfect fifth.** The stack has been
thirds for the whole record; on the split **the room hears the gap open** — and it opens *upward and
outward*, not down. Range at the split: E♭4→D5, a minor seventh, still inside one octave ✅.

It happens **exactly twice**: at the last bar of the gear-change break, and as the **final sound of the
record, unaccompanied.** Nowhere else. *(Special Flair 15, Anarchy Harmonizers — the harmony The Scientist
asked for by name. Metaprompt craft spec: "one moment where two voices split and the room hears the gap
open.")*

### THE WHISTLE TEST — how a **WORDLESS TWO-VOICE HARMONY** passes it
*Could someone whistle it walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, drunk?*

1. **The lower line alone is already a complete tune.** Five notes, stepwise, inside a major sixth, no leap
   a whistle can miss. Strip the upper voice and you still have a figure, not a texture. **That is the
   honest answer to "a harmony is not a hook."**
2. ⭐ **A third is the interval a second drunk person finds by accident.** Two people walking out of a venue
   whistling the same tune land a third apart without deciding to. **The whistle test with two whistlers is
   the strongest form of the test**, and this riff is designed for exactly that failure mode of human
   behaviour.
3. **It is wordless by construction**, so it cannot be mistaken for the vocal being the only tune. It exists
   **before the first line and after the last** — that is the enforcement clause, and here it is literal:
   the record opens on the stack alone and ends on the stack alone.
4. **The split is the memory hook.** A figure that does the same thing five times is forgettable; a figure
   that does the same thing every time *except once* is the thing people reproduce badly at the bus stop,
   which is how a hook is actually transmitted.

**Honest weakness, filed rather than argued away:** a solo whistler renders one line and loses the harmony,
which is the point of the pair. **Mitigation is structural, not rhetorical** — the lower line was written to
survive alone first, and the third is the part the room supplies. If the room never supplies it, the song
still has a tune. That is the receipt.

## ⛔ 0C. THE THREE ABSOLUTE CONSTRAINTS — restated as build rules, not warnings

**1 · THE SECOND NUMBER IS NEVER IN THE SONG.** The report also carries a count of the dead.
⛔ It is **never counted, named, numbered, implied by arithmetic, or alluded to in any sung line.**
⭐ **It is the unnamed pursuer** — realised as **a sub creeping a semitone**, running the whole track, never
resolving, never mentioned. *(Sunna's technical note: "The Wolf: one automation lane running the whole
track… It must never resolve, and it must never be mentioned in the lyric.")*
**The thing the song refuses to say is the thing the song is about.** Depth in the situation, at zero toll.
**Build rules this generates, applied line by line:**
- The count only ever **goes up**. No subtraction anywhere, in any line, in any form.
- ⛔ No *some / not all / the rest / who didn't / the ones who / lost / missing / never came*.
- ⛔ No line that reports an **absence of an expected arrival**. Two drafted lines were cut for exactly this
  (see step 09) — *"Didn't say the rest of it"* and *"Nobody sits down till they've seen who."* Both were
  near-misses under §3.0 and near-misses **HOLD**, they do not get argued.
- ⛔ **One numeric fact only.** The count is the licensed exception because the count **is** the device. No
  second number exists anywhere in any sung line — no times, no ages, no quantities. *(This cost real lines:
  "four in the morning" → "since dark"; "boiled the water twice" → "boiled the water again"; "six phones off
  one" → "everybody's phones".)*

**2 · HUMAN SUBJECT STANDARD** — §0B above, in full.

**3 · NO MORAL, NO WARNING, NO CHEER, NO POLITICS NAMED.** Sunna never explains and never editorialises.
**Not a lament** — it has a pulse and a floor. **Not a celebration** — nothing here is fine. The song says
*seventy-eight thousand people decided to go*, and lets the listener carry the rest.
⭐ **The refusal is written into the lyric once, as a joke, which is the only way Sunna is allowed to state
a rule:** V1's ***"Nobody made a speech. Everybody went."***

## THE TEN WEIGHTED FACETS
**F1, F2, F3, F4, F6 are GATES.** A FAIL on any removes the variation from selection outright.

### F1 · THE WHISTLE TEST — the riff exists before the first word and after the last · **weight 14 · GATE**
**What it measures:** instrument named, interval named, inside one octave, present alone at both ends,
and a wordless figure that survives with the vocal muted.
**Why it matters:** this is the whole doctrine's falsifiability. If the music does not carry what the lyric
used to carry, the load-swap is *"capitulation with a rationale attached"* (the binding condition). ⛔ The
Rock Titan's live objection — *"'put the complexity in the music' is what people say right before they put
it in a plugin"* — is carried into QA unresolved. **A filter sweep is not a riff.**
**Failure mode:** the prompt says "hummable stacked harmony," the renderer returns a competent pad, the
self-check ticks the box. ⭐ **This is the pair's #1 render risk and it is fought in prompt AND exclude.**

### F2 · ANTI-CHOIR — the stack must never become sacred · **weight 13 · GATE**
**What it measures:** does the wordless two-voice harmony render as **choir / angelic pad / hymnal texture**?
**Why it matters twice over.** ⛔ First, it is the exact register Sunna forbids by name: *no warm alto, no
choir, no crystalline anything, never sung-pretty.* ⭐ Second, and worse **here specifically** — a wordless
women's harmony over a song about a mass crossing, if it goes sacred, **becomes a hymn for the dead.** That
is precisely the second number arriving through the back door, in the one channel the lyric cannot police.
**Failure mode:** long reverb tail + sustained vowels + no consonant attack = choir. **Defences, all three
structural:** (a) the syllable is `Da-da-da-da` — four hard plosives, a choir cannot happen on a hard D;
(b) dead-dry, close dynamic mic, no reverb tail on the voices; (c) fuzz bass doubling the lower voice an
octave down, which is a punk instrument standing where the organ would be.

### F3 · THE MORAL AUDIT — no editorial, no mourning, no cheer · **weight 13 · GATE**
**What it measures:** can any single line be read as a position, a warning, a lesson, a lament or a
celebration?
**Why it matters:** *"Sunna never explains."* The instant the song has an opinion about the crossing, it
stops being a song about people going and becomes a song about the writer. And the run's charge — the
Pantheon argument — only lands if the song **reports** rather than **argues**.
**Failure mode:** the last chorus acquires a tone. ⭐ Sunna's own guard is the EMO set: **not one EMO tag in
this pair is Triumph and not one is Sorrow.** The choruses are `EMO:Resolve` — a fact with a floor under it.

### F4 · ONE COUNT, AND IT COUNTS DEPARTURES · **weight 12 · GATE**
**What it measures:** exactly one number climbs; it counts *going*; nothing else in the chorus changes; no
second numeric fact anywhere.
**Why it matters:** *one idea, repeated until inevitable.* The number is the **only new information in the
song** — that is what makes a byte-identical chorus feel like it is growing. **Failure mode:** a second
number (a time, an age, a quantity) enters a verse and the device is now two devices and neither lands.

### F5 · THE FLAT TEST — a plain line survives on its NOUN · **weight 11**
**What it measures:** say the hook flatly, to a face, no music. Survives = simple. Dies = merely short.
**Why it matters:** *"a plain line is HARDER to write than a dense one — density hides a weak idea; six
plain words expose it instantly."* (Bridgers' seat.) **Failure mode:** short lines that are only short —
elliptical, atmospheric, nouns swapped for moods.

### F6 · A PERSON DOING SOMETHING IN VERSE ONE · **weight 11 · GATE**
**What it measures:** a human hand, physical, in the first verse, in the lyric and not the concept note.
**Why it matters:** the COMPRESS round's verdict — *"you have written a robot record and called it
delight."* **The machine is the occasion; the person is the subject.** Here the occasion is a number in a
news bulletin and **the person must arrive before the number does.** ⛔ Failure mode: the person is a
symbol, a representative, or a statistic with a face on it. **Ordinary, physical, specific — or repair.**

### F7 · GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE · **weight 8**
Every return removes; never adds; the removal is registered instantly and cannot be named.
**Failure mode:** fourteen automation lanes and a producer's note explaining that the listener feels them
subconsciously. *(The Minimalist Engineer named this exact failure.)*

### F8 · THE PURSUER IS EVERYWHERE AND NOWHERE · **weight 8**
One rising element, never resolving, ⛔ never named. Here: **a sub creeping a semitone** across the whole
track. **Why it matters:** *"joy that has looked at the wolf is credible; joy that hasn't is a mascot."*
And in this pair the pursuer carries the constraint that the lyric is forbidden to carry. **Failure mode:**
the pursuer leaks into a line, or resolves at the end and lets everybody off.

### F9 · THE ROOM'S BAR IS REAL SILENCE · **weight 6**
The chant is designed to be sung *back* — 4–8 syllables, no interval a normal throat can't reach at volume
while out of breath. ⭐ Here the division is exact: **the room owns the wordless riff; the singer owns the
number.** The room's part never changes, so it is learnable in one pass. **Failure mode:** the hole is
filled with a stacked vocal, and the chant becomes a solo with a crowd sample on it.

### F10 · THE EXIT — end one song early · **weight 4**
2:45, no key change, no long fade, no fifth chorus. *Her sets famously end one song early.*
**Failure mode:** a triumphant tag, an added coda, a resolution.

**Total 100.** Ranking is **within the AMBITIOUS arm only**, per the daily mandate.

## FAILURE MODES — the ranked list this pair is actually defending against
1. ⭐ **The stack renders as a choral/angelic pad.** Kills F1 and F2 together, and converts the record into
   a hymn for the dead — the one thing it must never be. *Fought in prompt (hard plosives, dry close mic,
   fuzz-bass shadow) and in exclude (choir, choral pad, angelic vocal, sacred/hymnal texture, cathedral
   reverb, wordless aahs).* 
2. ⭐ **The last chorus acquires a moral or a mourning tone.** *Fought with the EMO set (no Triumph, no
   Sorrow), with a byte-identical final chorus that resolves nothing, and in exclude (lament, elegiac,
   mournful strings, requiem, triumphant ending, moralising tag).* 
3. **The second number leaks by arithmetic or allusion.** *Fought by the build rules in §0C: the count only
   climbs, no absence-of-arrival line survives, and near-misses are cut rather than defended.* 
4. **A pad instead of a riff.** *Fought by naming the exact five notes and both voices in every prompt.* 
5. **The person becomes a symbol.** *Fought by keeping every verse-one action physical and household —
   a bottle cap, a zip, a crate, a radio dial.* 
6. **A second device sneaks in** (a shared chorus across the four, a second count, a formal trick).
7. **Sentimentality** — the packer's goodbye, the mat, the kettle. *Fought by evidence, never inference:
   the song may report what an object is doing; it may never say what that means.*
