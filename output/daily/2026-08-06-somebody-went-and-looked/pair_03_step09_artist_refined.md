## 🔪 THE CRITIQUE — seven seats, genuine dissent, no polite agreement

### 1 · **THE CONNECTION CYNIC** *(after Bob Lefsetz)* — the hardest hit
> **"Nobody cares about a bolt.** I said this about the orbiter and I'll say it louder about this one. You have written a song about a *fastener*. Not about a person — about a part. And worse: I can hear exactly how this gets sold. Somebody is going to call it *charming*. Somebody is going to put it on a playlist called 'wholesome'. **The second a song is charming it is finished**, because charming is what people say about a thing they have already decided not to take seriously."

### 2 · **THE BATTLE-AXE** *(after Ashnikko)*
> "It's polite. Same note I gave the whole run. *'Look at the lip on the lead-in'* is lovely and it isn't a taunt. **Where is the line that's a bit rude?** You've got a man who got two words back from a stranger and you didn't even let him swear about it."

### 3 · **THE ROCK TITAN** *(after Dave Grohl)* — Hyper-Skeptic, Concept
> "Four notes and one of them is a flat five. Fine. **Now prove it isn't a prompt.** You wrote 'blown-out' in a box and a machine is going to hand you back a clean, tight, double-tracked rhythm guitar with the amp sim on 4, and your self-check will tick the box because the word 'blown-out' is in the file. **A riff is a thing a person plays with their hands that another person can play back.** Where in this record does a polite guitar physically fail?"

### 4 · **THE MINIMALIST ENGINEER** *(after Steve Albini)* — Hyper-Skeptic, Medium
> "Your gradation table is the best page in the packet and I don't believe you'll hold it. **Item five is the best line in the list** — *the shine the belt leaves* — and it's the second thing you delete. Everybody says they'll cut their favourite. Nobody does."

### 5 · **THE SCREAM-AND-WHISPER** *(after Phoebe Bridgers)*
> "*'Two thousand words and not one joke'* is the line. It survives being said flatly to a person's face and it survives on its noun. But **you have four songs and one of them is doing all the work.** V2's chorus — *'Somebody loved it. So do I'* — is the only place where somebody says something about themselves, and it's the softest thing in the pair. It might be the whole point or it might be the leak."

### 6 · **THE ONE-CHORD MAXIMALIST** *(after Fela Kuti)*
> "You are frightened of your own riff. You put a gear-change in because two minutes felt long. **If four notes can't hold two minutes, they can't hold four bars.**"

### 7 · **THE PLAYER-PRODUCER** *(after Nile Rodgers)* — the three questions
> "**What's the riff?** Four notes, a hanging flat five, present at bar one and at the last bar. Answered.
> **Can the room play it back?** Four syllables, open vowels, silence left for it. Answered.
> **What's the real subject underneath?** You keep saying it out loud in your own notes and you never put it in the song, and that's correct — *nobody needs to know, and it's in the record either way.*"

## ✅ THE RESOLUTION — what changed, what was refused

**To 1 (THE CONNECTION CYNIC) — CONCEDED IN FULL, AND IT SET THE FORM.**
He is right that *charming* is the death of this song, and he has named the exact mechanism: charming is what you call a thing you have decided not to take seriously. **A charming record ends on the gag with the band hitting.** So this one does the opposite and it does it structurally: after the last chorus the arrangement **subtracts to one voice saying one item**, and then a single out-of-tune guitar plays four notes and the amp is switched off mid-note. There is no button. There is no wink to land on. **You cannot put a song on a wholesome playlist if it refuses to resolve.**
He is wrong that there is no person: **verse one of every variation is a human hand** — a man with a torch and a paper towel, a woman sitting up straighter on the second page, a man on a bus with a wet coat, a man checking a number and not saying it. *The machine is the occasion; the person is the subject,* and it is in the lyric, not the concept note.

**To 2 (THE BATTLE-AXE) — TAKEN, BUT SPENT WHERE SHE HERSELF SAID TO SPEND IT.**
Her own line, in the frozen debate: *"A rude line isn't rudeness for its own sake — a riff that's a bit ugly does the same job and costs nothing. **Put the rudeness in the fuzz.**"* This song's rudeness is **the flat fifth left hanging on the and-of-three, and a guitar that goes a third of a semitone flat and is never tuned back.** It is not a taunt, because ⛔ **the only available target is a person who loves something**, and that is the one thing Sunna will not do. Politeness is not the charge that sticks here. Sneering would be.
**And there is a rude line — it just isn't aimed at anybody:** *"Two thousand words. Two words back."*

**To 3 (THE ROCK TITAN) — CARRIED LIVE, AND ANSWERED IN THE FORM RATHER THAN THE SPEC.**
He is right that "blown-out" in a box is not a defence, and the run's own doctrine agrees: an objection answered in the production spec is not answered. So the answer is where a render cannot dodge it — **the gear-change strips the band to one voice and one handclap, and the guitar's return is the only event in the bar.** A polite guitar arriving alone into silence is audibly a failure; there is nothing for it to hide behind. **And the last statement of the riff is guitar alone and out of tune.** A clean, tight, quantised guitar physically cannot pass that bar without the listener hearing that it is the wrong record.
**His objection is carried unresolved into QA anyway, as the ICB requires: whistle-test the render, do not read the claim.**

**To 4 (THE MINIMALIST ENGINEER) — HELD. THE FAVOURITE WAS CUT.**
*"the shine the belt leaves"* is the best line in the list and it is deleted at the second pass, exactly as the table says. **The gradation ledger is a subset test, not an intention:** every later block is a strict subset of block one in original order, and it is checkable by diff rather than by trust. What survives to the end is not the prettiest item. It is *the notch that can't fit wrong*, which is the least decorative and the most loved.

**To 5 (THE SCREAM-AND-WHISPER) — ACCEPTED AS DESCRIPTION, KEPT AS DESIGN.**
It is the leak and it is deliberate, and it happens exactly once in four songs. *"Somebody loved it. So do I."* is the only first-person claim in the pair, and it belongs to the **reader**, not to the writer and not to Sunna. **The reader is allowed to be moved because she is the one who went and looked.** Everywhere else the affection is carried by what is being counted, never announced.

**To 6 (THE ONE-CHORD MAXIMALIST) — REFUSED, WITH THE REASON ON THE RECORD.**
The gear-change is P03's assigned axis value and it is not fear of the riff. **The riff is present in every bar of the song including the ones with no band under it** — the voice-and-clap section is the riff still being counted, out loud, by a person with no instrument. His position was already answered in the frozen debate: **the long one is P04**, one riff as the only harmonic event, and the constraint bent exactly once, on purpose, somewhere else. *"Sunna is short because the delight is finite."*

**To 7 (THE PLAYER-PRODUCER) — NOTHING TO CHANGE.**
The deep subject stays subversive and unstated: **an exploded technical drawing is an act of love performed as documentation, by an anonymous person, for a stranger who will need it later** — which is the same act as a spacecraft turning its camera on somebody else's crash site. **The song never says this and never will.** A listener who takes only the surface receives a whole song about a man, a lamp, and a small piece of steel.

## 🔎 THE IRONY AUDIT — the test this step exists to run

**The charge:** *find one line in four variations that a listener could hear a smirk in.* Every line was read aloud flat, to a person's face, with the question **"who is this at the expense of?"**

| Suspect line | Why it was suspected | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| *"Two thousand words about a bolt"* | the whole hook rests on a deflating plain noun; the scale mismatch is the joke | **CLEAR.** The very next line forecloses the reading: *"Two thousand words and not one joke."* The song tells you, in its second breath, that nobody was kidding. The plainness is the FLAT TEST doing its job, not a deflation. |
| *"He wasn't asked. He wanted to."* | could carry a raised eyebrow | **CLEAR.** There is no target. It is the plainest possible statement of unprompted devotion and it is admiring in every reading. |
| *"Nobody has asked for part three. / Part three is going up on Friday."* | the funniest couplet in the pair | **CLEAR — and this is the model for the whole thing.** It is funny because of *how far he is going*, and every word of it is true and on his side. Delete the affection and the comedy dies with it. That is the test, and it passes. |
| *"Bought a better lamp for it."* | domestic absurdity, one beat from a punchline | **CLEAR.** It is a purchase made out of care. Nobody is watching him do it and the song does not point at him doing it. |
| *"Some nights it's a daft thing to be doing."* | **the closest call in the pair.** *Daft* is a word that can be said down at someone. | **CLEAR, CONDITIONALLY — and the condition is in the line itself.** It is **his** thought, on a bad night, not the singer's verdict, and the next line answers it in the same breath: *"Some nights he knows exactly what he's doing."* ⚠️ **Flagged for the render: if the delivery lands "daft" with any lift or lilt, the line goes.** It is the one place a vocal performance could manufacture a smirk the lyric does not contain. |
| *"Two thousand words. Two words back."* | the asymmetry could read as contempt for the commenter | **CLEAR — because of what follows it.** *"Somebody typed it and meant it."* He is granted sincerity inside the chorus that names his reply. And the song's answer to him is not a rebuttal: **"He didn't reply to the other. He just added to the list."** |
| *"He wasn't being cruel. He was tired."* | — | **This is the pair's guarantee.** It is the line that makes a butt impossible. |
| *"He could put a joke in the top, / the kind that says I know, I know."* | a song about refusing irony risks being smug about refusing irony | **CLEAR.** It is a situation, not a thesis: a man considering armour and putting it down. He is not congratulated for it. The next line is *"He doesn't want it to be"*, and then the band comes back and nothing is made of it. |

### ⚠️ TWO LINES CUT AT THIS STEP, NOT REPAIRED — DELETED
1. **RAW V3:** *"Two words. Both spelled right, at least."* — makes the commenter the butt. **Gone, no replacement.**
2. **RAW V1 verse 2:** *"the kind of man who reads the manual for fun."* — affection performed *at* the subject. **Gone, no replacement.**

### THE VERDICT
**No line in the four finished variations requires the fastener, the writer, the reader or the commenter to be diminished in order to work.** Every laugh in the pair is a laugh at *distance travelled*, and every one of them dies if you delete the affection — which is the only test that means anything.
**One live risk survives into the render and is declared rather than hidden: the word *daft*, and the vocal that says it.**
