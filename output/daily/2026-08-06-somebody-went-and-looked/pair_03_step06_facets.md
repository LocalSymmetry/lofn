## ⭐ 0. THE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, BEFORE A SINGLE WORD

**Instrument:** one electric guitar, drop-D, through a single amp with a torn speaker cone. No DI, no reamp, no second take.

**The four notes:** **E → G → A → B♭.**
**The intervals:** up a **minor third**, up a **whole tone**, up a **semitone**, then a **tritone drop** home to the root.
**Total range: a tritone.** Comfortably inside one octave. Nothing here needs a throat that has warmed up.

**The rhythm:** two fast eighths into two dragging quarters — *da-da · DAA · daaa* — with the **B♭ left hanging on the and-of-three** for a beat and a half before it falls back to E. The flat fifth is the wrong note and it is the whole point: it is the one you remember, and it is the one a drunk person gets slightly wrong and grins about.

**WHISTLE TEST — could someone whistle it walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, drunk?**
**Yes.** Four notes, a tritone of range, no leap larger than a minor third on the way up, and the memorable event is a single sour note held longer than it should be. It is closer to a football chant than to a guitar part. The failure mode this defeats is the "competent arpeggio" the Rock Titan warned about: an arpeggio has no wrong note in it, and this riff is 25% wrong note.

**Placement (enforcement clause):** the riff is the **first sound on the record**, alone and dry, for eight bars before the first line — and it is the **last sound on the record**, alone again, after the last word, dying mid-note when the amp is switched off. The vocal never has the tune to itself.

**Where it goes in the last chorus:** doubled an octave up, per assignment. ⭐ **The octave-up double is also the pursuer** — a second guitar that has been drifting flat by roughly a third of a semitone across the whole track, so the moment the song gets biggest is the moment it is most out of tune, and the room cannot name why the last chorus feels seasick. One automation lane, never resolving, never mentioned in the lyric.

## ⛔ THE BINDING CONSTRAINT — THE REFUSAL, AT ITS HARDEST

**Not one line may be ironic about the thing being loved, or about the person loving it.** No "so bad it's good." No guilty-pleasure framing. No joke that requires the fastener, the writer, or the reader to be the butt. **If a listener could hear a smirk anywhere, that is a REPAIR.**

The humour comes from **the scale of the sincerity** and from nowhere else. The listener laughs at *how far this goes*, never at *who it happens to*. There is no line in this pair whose comedy survives if you delete the affection.

**The structural guarantee, not the promise:** the song does not end on a punchline. It ends **smaller** — one item, said once, then the riff alone. A novelty song ends on the gag with the band hitting; this ends on a man saying the word *notch* into a room that has stopped playing. **A comedy record cannot be built to that shape.**

### FACET 1 — **THE RIFF SURVIVES THE MUTE BUTTON** · weight **18**
With the vocal muted, is there still a tune? Four blown-out notes with a hanging flat fifth, present at bar one and at the last bar, recognisable from the first two.
**Why it matters:** the entire FREE CHANNEL doctrine is falsifiable here and nowhere else. If the vocal is the only tune, every ounce of meaning is back in the toll booth and the run has failed its own test.
**Failure mode:** the renderer returns a competent chugging arpeggio, or a pad, or a filter sweep, and the self-check ticks the box. *(THE ROCK TITAN's live objection, carried unresolved.)*
**Detection:** hum it. If you have to sing a word to find it, it is arrangement.

### FACET 2 — **ZERO SMIRK** · weight **16**
Every line about the fastener, the writer, the reader and the commenter is sincere. No wink, no air quotes, no ironic distance, no vocal mugging.
**Why it matters:** THE REFUSAL is Sunna's admission ticket to the Pantheon. A single smirk turns unembarrassed delight into contempt-as-a-bit, which is the register the Pantheon has deliberately closed.
**Failure mode:** the "isn't this ADORABLE" voice — affection performed *at* the subject rather than *with* them. Second failure mode: the commenter in V3 written as a philistine to be beaten.
**Detection:** read every line aloud in a flat voice to another person's face. If any line needs a raised eyebrow to work, it is a repair.

### FACET 3 — **THE FLAT TEST ON EVERY HOOK LINE** · weight **12**
Each hook survives being said out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face — and survives on its **noun**, not its syntax.
**Why it matters:** a plain line is harder to write than a dense one; density hides a weak idea, six plain words expose it instantly.
**Failure mode:** short-but-dead. "Somebody loves the little things" is short. It is not simple. It has no noun in it.
**Detection:** *"Two thousand words and not one joke."* Say it flat. It lands. *"Two thousand words. Two words back."* Say it flat. It lands harder.

### FACET 4 — **THE LIST IS ONE DEVICE AND ONLY ONE** · weight **11**
Anaphora: one item per line, same opening stem — **"Look at the ___"** — each item absurdly specific and completely sincere.
**Why it matters:** nameable in four words, or it is more than one device. One thing heard four times beats four things heard once.
**Failure mode:** a second device sneaks in — a call-and-response answer hole written into the lyric (that is P01's device), a misheard line (P02's), a climbing count (P06's). The room's answer hole in this song is an **arrangement fact**, a bar of real silence, and it never appears as `(parenthesis)` in the lyric.
**Detection:** name the device in four words. *"The list that loves."* Anything else present is a repair.

### FACET 5 — **GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE AND THE LIST SHORTENS** · weight **11**
List block 1 = **6 items** · block 2 = **4** · block 3 = **3** · block 4 = **2** · coda = **1**. Every later block is a strict **subset of block 1, in original order.** Nothing is ever added. Arrangement subtracts in parallel: hi-hat gone in chorus two, bass out for two bars in chorus three, whole band gone at the gear-change.
**Why it matters:** *(THE MINIMALIST ENGINEER's seat.)* "Infinitesimal gradations" is a licence to add fourteen automation lanes. Gradation by addition is a lie; gradation by subtraction is a fact — registered instantly, unnameable, un-fakeable, and the mix never runs out of headroom.
**Failure mode:** a new, better item appears late because it is a good line. It is a repair regardless of how good it is.
**Detection:** diff block 4 against block 1. Every word must already be there.

### FACET 6 — **THE CHORUS IS BYTE-IDENTICAL AND BELONGS TO THE ROOM** · weight **10**
Same five lines every time, no note filed. The room's part is **four syllables — "TWO THOU-SAND WORDS"** — all open vowels, no interval a normal throat can't reach at volume while out of breath, with a bar of **real silence** left for it in the last chorus.
**Why it matters:** RETURN IS FREE. Repetition lowers processing cost while raising richness; it is the cheapest thing on the menu and this run is instructed to spend more of it, not less. And the hook does not belong to Sunna — *"if the crowd can't own it by the second chorus, rewrite it."*
**Failure mode:** the hole gets filled with a stacked backing vocal, which converts a chant into a solo with a crowd sample on it.
**Detection:** is the gap silence, or is it a vocal? Silence, or repair.

### FACET 7 — **THE PURSUER IS AUDIBLE AND UNNAMED** · weight **8**
A second guitar drifts flat across the whole track and is worst exactly when the song is biggest. It never resolves and it is never mentioned in a lyric.
**Why it matters:** *"Joy that has looked at the wolf is credible. Joy that hasn't is a mascot."* The detuning is the only thing in this song that is not fine, and it is the reason the delight reads as a decision rather than a temperament.
**Failure mode:** the renderer tunes it back up, or the lyric explains it. Either kills it.
**Detection:** is there a beating, sour double under the last chorus? Does any lyric line refer to tuning? The second answer must be no.

### FACET 8 — **THE GEAR-CHANGE IS A CLIFF, NOT A RAMP** · weight **8**
Full band → **voice and one handclap** (list block 3, naked, three items) → full band slamming back with the riff doubled an octave up.
**Why it matters:** one song that is three songs is Sunna's third pillar; loud-quiet-loud is the entire arrangement doctrine and it is free. The clap section is also the load-bearing anti-polish device: when the guitar returns after silence, its distortion is *the event*, and a polite render has nowhere to hide.
**Failure mode:** a filtered, tastefully automated transition. A ramp costs nothing and does nothing.
**Detection:** does the drop happen inside one bar with no riser? A white-noise riser here is a repair.

### FACET 9 *(carried, unweighted — a gate, not a score)* — **A PERSON DOING SOMETHING IN VERSE ONE**
A human hand in the first four bars of every variation. **The machine is the occasion; the person is the subject.** *(The COMPRESS round: "you have written a robot record and called it delight.")*
A first verse whose only actor is an object is a repair, full stop.
