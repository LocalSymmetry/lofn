### The judgements that matter
**V1 — the only clean sweep, and it holds the verified number.** The count lands on **16**, which is the
fact, in the loudest position in the song, and the line after it is *"Then I got my coat."* That
anticlimax is the pair's whole thesis executed in six words: **the making is real, the pursuer is real,
and nobody draws a moral.** F5 is the strongest in the set — *pot, chair, mug, coat* — and the moral audit
came back with nothing to argue about.

**V3 — one point behind, and it owns F8.** The pursuer is nowhere in the lyric and everywhere in the
record, because **she physically leaves the room and the count keeps climbing without her.** *"Somewhere a
light I never left on"* is the best unnamed-wolf line generated in the pair. It loses to V1 only on the
number: 9→12 is the boring middle of a count, which is funny, but it is not the fact.

**V2 — PARTIAL on F7.** Gradation is subtractive and the removals happen, but V2 starts nearly empty by
design, so *"chorus two loses the hi-hats"* costs less than it costs anywhere else. **The Minimalist
Engineer's point survives here in a small way:** you cannot take much away from a room that arrived empty.
It is still the funniest of the four — four choruses of total seriousness about the numbers one, two,
three and four — and *"Loads of board left."* is the set's second-best flat line.

**V4 — PARTIAL on F10.** It is the most unnerving and it is the only one that does not resolve, and both
of those are deliberate; but the last chorus wants a fifth chorus and the spec says leave. **It leaves.**
The partial is the cost of the choice, filed rather than argued away.

## SYNTHESIS — three transplants into the set

**S1 · V3's evidence grammar into V4.** V4's *"Lights were on. Chair's been moved."* became **"Lights were
on. Chair's been moved. Mug's been moved."** — three pieces of evidence and no inference, which is how V3
handles the light it never left on. **Evidence, never inference**, is now the pair's house rule for the
pursuer: the song may report what an object is doing; it may never say what that means.

**S2 · V1's coat into V4's chorus.** V4 originally closed on tea, which collided with V3. It now closes on
**"And I've not got my coat off."** The coat now runs the whole set — **V1 puts it on, V4 has not got it
off** — and the set reads as one shift and the morning after it, which no single variation could do alone.

**S3 · V2's pressed-down marker into nothing, deliberately.** *"So I pressed it down. I pressed it down."*
was offered to V1 and **rejected**: V1's person is at the end of a long day and would not press. The line
stays V2's, where it is a person being careful with a one on an empty board, which is the whole variation.

### Considered and REJECTED
- **A shared chorus across all four.** Rejected: the device is *the count that climbs*, not *the chorus
  that recurs across songs*. Four songs sharing one chorus would make the pair a suite, and a suite is a
  second structural idea. **One device.**
- **Naming the thing on the board, once, in V4.** Rejected hard. It is the one line that would convert the
  record into a position, and the position would be a moral. ⛔ **Never explaining is the rule.**
- **A fifth chorus in V4 at 21.** Rejected: *her sets famously end one song early.*

## ⭐ DESCRIBE-RENDER SELF-CHECK

**What this PRODUCES.** Two women, close and dry, singing a five-note figure a third apart with a hard
`D` on the front of every syllable, over a 132 four-on-the-floor with a late kick and a fuzz bass playing
the same figure an octave down. A flat, unbothered voice reporting objects. A chorus every forty seconds
in which one word — a numeral — is different and nothing else is. Halfway through, the floor breaks to a
165 amen and the harmony opens once from a third to a fifth. Elements leave and never come back. The last
nine seconds are the two voices alone, and the last thing you hear is the gap opening.

**Name the one way this would render generic.**
**It renders as an anthem.** Not a choir and not a moral — a third failure that eats both: the renderer
hears *"two female voices in harmony"* plus *"the room's part"* plus *a number climbing*, and gives back
an uplifting festival chorus with the vocals stacked wide and reverbed, the count reading as a countdown
to something, and the last chorus arriving **bigger** than the first. That is generic, and it is generic
in exactly the direction that installs a moral without a single word being written — **because a chorus
that grows tells you the thing was good.**

### The two declared risks, and what fights them
**Risk 1 — the wordless stack renders as a choral/angelic pad.** Fought four ways: the plosive `Da-`
onset; the figure that *ends* rather than sustains; the fuzz bass doubling the lower voice so the hook is
also a played part; and `Disc_Pad` used as **negative addressing** — `no_choir_no_choral_swell_no_angelic_texture`
— so the channel that would otherwise summon a choral wall is spent blocking one. Field 1B blacklists
choir, choral pad, angelic vocal, sacred and hymnal texture, gospel stack, layered oohs and reverb tail.

**Risk 2 — the song acquires a moral in the last chorus.** Structurally impossible as written: **the last
chorus is the third chorus with one digit changed.** There is nowhere for a verdict to go. Reinforced by
the banned-vocabulary grep (zero hits), by the seven killed lines logged at step 08, by the refusal of the
Battle-Axe's rude line at step 09, and by field 1B blacklisting *moralising tag, warning tone, triumphant
ending.*

### SELF-REPAIR, ONE PASS — applied
