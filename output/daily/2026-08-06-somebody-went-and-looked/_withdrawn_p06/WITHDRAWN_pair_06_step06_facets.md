## ⭐ 0. THE RIFF — written before any lyric existed

**Name (internal):** the hook has no name in the lyric and never will. Production shorthand: **THE STACK.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Instrument** | two female voices, close on a dynamic mic, dry — plus a fuzz bass doubling **only the lower voice**, one octave down, so the bass is the hook's shadow and not a second idea |
| **Key** | C minor |
| **Figure — lower voice** | **G4 · A♭4 · G4 · F4 · E♭4** |
| **Figure — upper voice** | **B♭4 · C5 · B♭4 · A♭4 · G4** |
| **Interval** | a **diatonic third above, note for note** — m3, M3, m3, m3, M3 in C natural minor |
| **Range** | E♭4 → C5 = a **major sixth**. Inside one octave. ✅ |
| **Rhythm** | pickup on the *and* of 4, then quarter · quarter · eighth · eighth · half. Five notes, two bars. It always arrives a beat early — that is the "at speed" |
| **Syllable** | **`Da-da, da-da — daah`** — wordless, staccato-staccato-held |

### ⭐ THE SPLIT — the one moment
On the fifth and final note the lower voice stays on **E♭4** and the upper voice **refuses the third**:
instead of falling to G4 it goes **up to B♭4**. The closing interval is **E♭4–B♭4, a perfect fifth.**
The stack has been thirds for the whole record; on the split **the room hears the gap open.**
It happens exactly **twice**: at the last bar of the gear-change break, and as **the final sound of the
record, unaccompanied.** Nowhere else. *(Flair 15, Anarchy Harmonizers — the harmony The Scientist asked
for by name. Metaprompt craft spec: "one moment where two voices split and the room hears the gap open.")*

### THE WHISTLE TEST — how a WORDLESS TWO-VOICE HARMONY passes it
*The gate: could someone whistle it walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, drunk?*

1. **A whistler carries ONE line, and one line is enough.** The upper voice — **B♭ · C · B♭ · A♭ · G** —
   is five **stepwise** notes inside a **perfect fourth**, no leap wider than a second, landing on the
   fifth of the key. There is no interval in it a drunk person can miss.
2. **It is a FIGURE, not a texture — the anti-pad proof.** It has an attack, a rhythm and **an ending**.
   It *stops*. A pad cannot stop; that is the whole difference. The Rock Titan's charge — *"you'll write
   'hummable riff' and the renderer will hand you a competent arpeggio"* — is answered by the fact that
   the thing being tested here **is already the melody, not a description of one.**
3. **The Hum Test is not a claim about this hook, it is a description of it.** The gate says *the song
   must be hummable with no words at all.* This hook **has no words.** Muting the vocal does not mute the
   riff, because the riff **is** the vocal-that-isn't-singing. The enforcement clause (*present before the
   first line and after the last*) is satisfied literally: four bars of it open the record with nothing
   behind them, and four bars of it close the record with nothing behind them.
4. **The second voice is why you want to repeat it.** One line is whistleable; two lines a third apart is
   the thing a room joins in on, because the harmony is a **hole shaped like a second person.** The
   consonant onset (`Da-`, not `Ah-`) is load-bearing — see F2.

**Where it lives:** bars 1–4 alone · between every section · under every chorus as **the room's part** ·
after the last sung line, alone, splitting to the fifth.

## THE TEN FACETS — weighted, with failure mode and why-it-matters
*Weights sum to 100. **F1, F2, F3, F4, F6 are GATES:** a FAIL removes the variation from selection.*

### ⭐ F1 — THE WHISTLE TEST, TWO-VOICE EDITION · **weight 18 · GATE**
Can one person whistle **B♭–C–B♭–A♭–G** walking away, drunk, having heard it twice? Does the figure
**exist before the first lyric and after the last line**, with nothing else playing?
- **Failure mode:** the hook renders as a *sustained* two-note interval — a held "ooh" — and stops being a
  figure. The tell is that it no longer **ends**.
- **Why it matters:** THE FREE CHANNEL is falsifiable exactly here. If the hook only exists while someone
  is singing words, the music is carrying nothing and **the words are still paying the toll.**

### ⭐ F2 — THE ANTI-CHOIR GATE · **weight 14 · GATE**
Two women a third apart on an open vowel is **one step from a church.** Does the render stay a punk vocal
riff — hard consonant onset, dry, no tail, audible breath, **two people and not twenty**?
- **Failure mode:** choral pad · angelic swell · hymnal/sacred texture · reverb tail · a stacked "aah"
  that reads as a congregation. **This is the exact register Sunna forbids** (`no choir, no crystalline
  anything, never sung-pretty`), and this pair is the only one in the run whose riff *is voices* — so it
  is the only pair that can accidentally produce a hymn.
- **Why it matters:** it would not merely be off-brief. A hymn **draws a moral by texture alone**, and F3
  would fall with it. The two gates are one failure wearing two coats.
- **The engineering answer, decided at this step:** the syllable is **`Da-`, not `Ah-`.** A plosive onset
  is un-choral by construction, articulates like an instrument, and survives a room shouting it.

### ⭐ F3 — THE MORAL AUDIT · **weight 18 · GATE**
Does **any** line editorialise, warn, or celebrate? Applied line by line, in all four.
- **Failure mode:** *"look what we've made" · "God help us" · "sixteen ways for it to end" · "isn't it
  something" · "nobody's asking if we should"* — and the subtler ones: a rhetorical question, a line that
  tells you how to feel about the count, a last chorus that turns into a verdict.
- **Why it matters:** ⛔ **This is the hardest balance in the run.** Sunna's position is that people keep
  making things, and it is not naive **because the wolf is behind her the entire time and is never
  mentioned.** The refusal to draw a moral **is the position.** A song about science acquires a moral in
  the last chorus by default — that is the gravity this facet exists to resist.
- **Banned vocabulary, fixed here before drafting:** should · shouldn't · danger · careful · wrong · right ·
  proud · amazing · history · future · God · pray · world · mankind · miracle · monster · warning.

### F4 — THE COUNT IS THE ONLY NEW INFORMATION · **weight 14 · GATE**
Chorus **byte-identical apart from the numeral.** Exactly **one** numeric family per song. No second device.
- **Failure mode:** a second, unrelated number sneaking in as colour — a clock time, a year, "two bags",
  "after six". Or a second structural idea (a misheard line, a litany) arriving as "texture".
- **Why it matters:** one sung numeric fact per song is the house rule; **here the number IS the device**,
  which is the licensed exception — and a licensed exception is not a licence. **One thing heard four
  times beats four things heard once.**

### F5 — THE FLAT TEST, ON THE NOUN · **weight 9**
Say each hook line out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face. *"Put the pen back in the pot."*
*"Thirteen. Then I got my coat."* *"Wet floor sign. And a wet floor."* Survives = simple. Dies = short.
- **Failure mode:** lines that are merely short. Clipped syntax pretending to be plainness.
- **Why it matters:** *a plain line survives on its NOUN, not its syntax* (the Scream-and-Whisper's seat).
  **The pot, the coat, the kettle, the marker rack, the wet floor sign** are the load-bearing objects; the
  grammar around them is deliberately unremarkable.

### F6 — A PERSON DOING SOMETHING, AND THE PERSON IS INVENTED · **weight 7 · GATE**
A human hand in **verse one** of all four. Nobody real, nothing identifiable.
- **Failure mode (two directions):** ① the machine as subject — the COMPRESS round's *"you have written a
  robot record and called it delight"*; ② the opposite failure, an identifiable individual or institution.
- **Why it matters:** AHA 4. **The machine is the occasion; the person is the subject.** A first verse with
  no human hand in it is a repair — and a first verse with a *real* human in it is a HOLD.

### F7 — SUBTRACTIVE GRADATION, AUDITABLE · **weight 6**
Each climb of the number **removes exactly one named element.** Nothing is ever added.
- **Failure mode:** the Minimalist Engineer's *"fourteen automation lanes and a producer's note explaining
  that the listener feels them subconsciously."* Growth by thickening.
- **Why it matters:** AHA 2. A removal is registered instantly and cannot be named, it cannot be faked, and
  **the mix never runs out of room.**
- **The ledger, fixed here:** chorus 1 full → **chorus 2 loses the hi-hats** → **chorus 3 loses the
  guitar** → **chorus 4 loses the bass for two bars** → **the outro loses the drums**, leaving two voices.

### F8 — THE PURSUER RUNS, AND IS NEVER NAMED · **weight 6**
One lane, rising, never resolving, never in the lyric.
- **Failure mode:** the lyric names it (*"something's coming"*), or the automation **resolves** — arrives
  at D♭ and sits there. A pursuer that arrives is a plot point, not a pursuer.
- **Why it matters:** *joy that has looked at the wolf is credible; joy that hasn't is a mascot.* In this
  song the entire unease budget is spent in this one lane, because **the lyric is not allowed to spend any.**

### F9 — THE ROOM'S BAR IS REAL SILENCE · **weight 4**
Somewhere the two voices **stop** and the hole is actual air, not a stacked vocal filling its own gap.
- **Failure mode:** a doubled vocal answers the hook. Then it is a solo with a crowd sample on it.
- **Why it matters:** the hard gate on every pair, and the Noise Scholar's seat. This pair's riff **is** the
  room's part, so the only way to prove the room owns it is to **take it away once** and leave the bar empty.

### F10 — LENGTH AND EXIT · **weight 4**
**2:45.** Chorus by **0:25**. Get in, land the hook, leave.
- **Failure mode:** an outro that argues its case; a fifth chorus that is really a summary.
- **Why it matters:** *her sets famously end one song early.* Finitude is the fuel.
