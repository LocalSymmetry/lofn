## 1 · THE CHORUS SKELETON — generated before any verse existed

Three plain statements with the wordless stack between them. **Only the numeral moves.**

```
<NUMBER> on the board.
Da-da, da-da — daah
<flat procedural line, fixed for the whole song>
Da-da, da-da — daah
<NUMBER>. <flat consequence line, fixed for the whole song>
Da-da, da-da — daah
```

**Why this shape and not a cleverer one.** The number lands on beat one of the chorus, twice, with the
room's part between the statements — so the only new information in the song arrives **in the loudest,
most repeated position available**, and the room is already singing when it does. Nothing else in a
chorus is permitted to move; a chorus that also changed a word would make the number a detail rather
than the event.

### The four fills

| | V1 · the sixteenth | V2 · the first one | V3 · going home | V4 · the next morning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **count** | 13 · 14 · 15 · **16** | **1** · 2 · 3 · 4 | 9 · 10 · 11 · 12 | 17 · 18 · 19 · **20** |
| **line 3** | Put the pen back in the pot. | Loads of board left. | Coat. Bag. Badge. Door. | I only just got in. |
| **line 5** | *N.* Then I got my coat. | *N.* And I turned off the light. | *N.* And I'm going home for my tea. | *N.* And I've not got my coat off. |

**FLAT TEST, all four, said out loud with no music:**
*"Thirteen on the board. Put the pen back in the pot. Thirteen. Then I got my coat."* — **survives.**
*"One on the board. Loads of board left. One. And I turned off the light."* — **survives.**
*"Nine on the board. Coat. Bag. Badge. Door. Nine. And I'm going home for my tea."* — **survives.**
*"Seventeen on the board. I only just got in. Seventeen. And I've not got my coat off."* — **survives.**
Each lives on a **noun** — pot, board, badge, coat — and not one of them tells you what to think.

## 4 · RAW MUSIC PROMPT — the shared spine, before per-variation colouring

Order per the master guide: **emotion → genre → instruments/production → vocalist → tempo/key →
progression → (exclusions in field 1B, never in field 1).**

> Two women's voices, close and dry, land a wordless stacked-third figure in the first two seconds with
> nothing behind them — five staccato notes a diatonic third apart, the upper line stepping B-flat, C,
> B-flat, A-flat, G — and that figure is the whole hook: it opens the record **before a single lyric**,
> sits under every chorus as the room's part, and **returns alone after the last line**, where the two
> voices refuse the third and open to a **bare fifth** so the gap widens in the last second. Electropunk
> and fuzz-bass punk in C minor at 132, kick ten milliseconds behind the grid against a bass locked on
> it, blown-out drop-D guitar stabs hard left, hats crisp right, room tone and tape hiss where a pad
> would be, **no acoustic instruments** other than breath on the capsule. Female sing-speak, mid-twenties,
> flat and eye-rolling, no vibrato, no head voice, consonants dropped off the ends of lines, breath left
> in. Halfway the four-on-the-floor collapses into a 165 breakbeat. One sub glides a semitone upward
> across the whole track and never arrives.

**Per-variation colouring assigned here, applied at step 09:** V1 *small, empty, late-shift* · V2 *bare,
noise floor audible, bass arrives like furniture* · V3 *bass-forward, weather after the break* · V4
