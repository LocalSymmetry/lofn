# P06 · STEP 07 — SONG GUIDES

**The slot:** 132→165 BPM · C minor · **2:45** · four-on-the-floor → breakbeat · pursuer = a sub creeping
a semitone · riff = the stacked-third wordless hook (`Da-da, da-da — daah`), locked at step 06.

**The situation, verified (F6):** *"Artificial Intelligence used to design brand new viruses"* — **16
successful.** ⭐ **Sixteen things that had never existed. And then you go home for your tea.**

**⛔ The standing order for all four guides.** This is **not a warning song and not a cheer.** The making
is real and unembarrassed; the pursuer is real and unnamed; **nobody in the song draws a moral.** Every
gram of unease is carried by one automation lane and by the flatness of the reporting. **Never explaining
is the rule** — so these guides are the only place the pressure is allowed to be described at all.

---

## THE TWO QUESTIONS, AND WHY THEY ARE THE RIGHT ONES HERE

***Where is the body standing?*** forces AHA 4 into the arrangement rather than into a concept note — you
cannot mix a room you have not located a body in, and **a person doing something in verse one** is the
consequence of answering it honestly.

***What could hurt it here?*** is where the wolf gets to live **without a word being spent on it.** In a
song forbidden to warn, the danger has to be somewhere. It goes in the answer to this question, and then
it goes into **the sub, the lights, and the objects** — never into a sentence. *(This is also the guide's
own safety catch: if the answer to question two ever ends up in the lyric, F3 has failed.)*

# GUIDE 1 — V1 · **the sixteenth**
### ***Where is the body standing?***
At a whiteboard, at the end of a shift, cap off a dying marker, **arm up**, writing a numeral into a
column of numerals. The coat has been on the back of the chair since morning. Somebody else's mug is on
the chair. The room is empty and the ceiling lights are on a motion sensor.

### ***What could hurt it here?***
**The lights.** They go off on their own, because the room has decided nobody is in it. Nothing else in
the room is a threat and nothing in the room is described as one — but the building is quietly running a
timer on her, and **the sub is doing the same thing under the floor, a semitone at a time.** ⛔ Neither is
mentioned. The hurt is that the room is already treating her as gone while she is still standing in it.

### Mood & emotional arc
`EMO:Equanimity` → `EMO:Amusement` → `EMO:Delight` → `EMO:Contentment`. **Flat all the way through, and
the flatness is the joy** — the eye-roll is audible, nothing is being performed for anyone. The arc is not
emotional escalation; it is **an arithmetic escalation with a constant emotional temperature**, which is
precisely what makes the fourth chorus land.

### Storytelling techniques
① **Litotes by inventory.** The song reports only objects and procedures — marker, pen, pot, chair, mug,
coat — and never once reports a feeling about them. The listener supplies the whole meaning and never
notices being asked. ② **Deferred subject.** The thing on the board is never described, only counted. The
count is total and the referent is absent, which is the situation, not a riddle: *the deep reference stays
subversive, never explained, never winked at.*

### Instrumentation & texture
The two voices are the melodic content and the fuzz bass is their shadow. Blown-out drop-D guitar stabs
sit hard left as punctuation, not as a part. Hats crisp right and **gone by the second chorus.** No
acoustic instrument anywhere except the breath on the capsule and a marker cap clicking.

### Production & effects
Dry, close, small. Kick **+10 ms behind** the grid with the bass locked on it, so the pair breathes.
Doubles misaligned 15–35 ms, ±6 cents. **No de-esser on the breaths.** −14 LUFS, ≥10 dB crest. Room tone
and tape hiss instead of a pad — ⛔ **no reverb tail on the two voices, ever**; a tail is where the choir
gets in.

### Structure & dynamics
Riff alone (4 bars) → V1 → **chorus at 0:22** → V2 → pre-chorus → chorus 2 → **break: 132 collapses to a
165 breakbeat, riff splits to a fifth** → chorus 3 → V3 → pre-chorus (byte-identical) → chorus 4 →
chorus 4 again → riff alone, split, out. **Loud-quiet-loud is the whole arrangement doctrine and it is free.**

### Hook technique
The chorus is three plain statements with the riff between them, and only the numeral moves: **13 → 14 →
15 → 16.** The room owns the wordless part by chorus two; in chorus four **one of its bars is taken away
and left empty**, so the room fills it or nothing does.

# GUIDE 2 — V2 · **the first one**
### ***Where is the body standing?***
In front of a whiteboard with **nothing on it**, drawing a box around a single digit because there is so
much board left it looks silly on its own. Marker brand new. The squeak is the loudest thing in the room.

### ***What could hurt it here?***
**The empty board.** Not as menace — as *scale*. Everything that is going to be written has not been
written, and the person cannot see that and neither can the listener, and **the arrangement can, because
it is the emptiest mix of the four and it fills in later.** ⛔ Never said. The pursuer here is that the
room has room.

### Mood & emotional arc
`EMO:Curiosity` → `EMO:Zeal` → `EMO:Delight` → `EMO:Equanimity`. Brighter and sillier than V1 — this is
the one where the person is **pleased**, and being pleased about a small thing in an empty room is Sunna's
native register. ⛔ Pleased is not the same as celebrating; the pleasure is in the box she drew, not in
what the count will become.

### Storytelling techniques
① **The diminutive as evidence** — the box round the one, the new marker, the loads of board left: the
smallness is the proof somebody cared enough to be neat. ② **Prolepsis withheld.** The listener knows
where the count goes and the song does not. **The song must not blink**, and it doesn't.

### Instrumentation & texture
The sparsest of the four for the first minute: the two voices, one guitar note left ringing where a stab
should be, and a lot of room tone. The fuzz bass enters late and arrives like furniture being moved in.
No acoustic instruments.

### Production & effects
Same dry small room, but the noise floor is **audible on purpose** — the emptiness has to be a sound, not
an absence of sound. Marker squeak used as the SFX. Everything else per the pair's technical block.

### Structure & dynamics
Same skeleton, but chorus 1 is nearly bare and the gradation therefore has to start from less — **the
removals still remove**, which is the point: gradation is subtractive even when there was not much there.

### Hook technique
Count **1 → 2 → 3 → 4.** ⭐ The four smallest numbers in the run, sung with total seriousness, which is
the joke and the whole song. *"Loads of board left."*

# GUIDE 3 — V3 · **going home**
### ***Where is the body standing?***
Coat on, bag up, badging out through a door that never reads the card first time; then a bus stop; then a
kitchen with a kettle and a telly on for the noise. **The whiteboard is behind her and stays behind her.**

### ***What could hurt it here?***
**A light she never left on.** She is at home and something in the flat is on. That's all. The song does
not investigate it and does not return to it. ⛔ Never named as anything. *(This is the pair's sharpest
implementation of "the wolf is behind her the entire time and is never mentioned" — she has physically
left the room and the count is still climbing without her, which the last chorus states as a fact about a
whiteboard and nothing else.)*

### Mood & emotional arc
`EMO:Contentment` → `EMO:Amusement` → `EMO:Relief` → `EMO:Equanimity`. The most physically comfortable of
the four: this is the **relief of the coat going on**, and relief is the only warm emotion the pair is
allowed, because it is somatic rather than moral.

### Storytelling techniques
① **Travelogue of the mundane** — door, card, wet floor sign, bus, kettle, telly. The journey is the form.
② **Refrain-as-address:** *"Board's still there"* is said twice in a row and means nothing more the second
time, which is exactly what makes it land. **Return is free; spend more of it.**

### Instrumentation & texture
Bass-forward — this is the pair's *bass problem* variation, the fuzz bass loudest in the set and the
guitar sparest. The breakbeat lands under the bus. No acoustic instruments; the kettle is an SFX, not a
percussion part.

### Production & effects
Slightly wider than V1 (she is moving through spaces), but the two voices stay **centre and dry**. The
sub's semitone climb is most audible here, under the quietest passage, and it still never arrives.

### Structure & dynamics
The gear-change is the door closing behind her: four-on-the-floor is the building, the breakbeat is
outside. Everything after the break has weather in it.

### Hook technique
Count **9 → 10 → 11 → 12** — the boring middle of a count, which is the funniest place to put a chorus.
*"Nine. And I'm going home for my tea."*

# GUIDE 4 — V4 · **the number the next morning**
### ***Where is the body standing?***
Just inside the door, **coat still on, bag still on her back**, looking at a board that says a bigger
number than the one she left on it. The marker has been put back in the rack by somebody else. The kettle
is warm.

### ***What could hurt it here?***
**That it kept going without her.** Somebody was here first, somebody is always here first, and nobody
says who or when. ⛔ Not named, not resolved, not asked about. The song's answer to it is **to go and put
the kettle on**, and the count climbs three more times while she does.

### Mood & emotional arc
`EMO:Curiosity` → `EMO:Amusement` → `EMO:Delight` → `EMO:Vigilance`. The only variation that ends on an
`Anticipation`-family tag, and it ends there **without a single line of foreboding** — the vigilance is a
performance instruction for the last chorus, not a lyric.

### Storytelling techniques
① **The unreliable interval.** She was gone for one night and the record never says how long; the number
does all the timekeeping. ② **Comic anticlimax as structure** — every chorus is interrupted by the fact
that she has not got her coat off yet, and the song ends before she does.

### Instrumentation & texture
The busiest of the four at the top — the room is already going when she walks in, so the riff enters
*over* a room already moving rather than opening a silence. **Exception, declared:** the riff still opens
the record alone, for two bars instead of four, before the room arrives. Everything else per pair spec.
No acoustic instruments.

### Production & effects
Brightest master of the four within the same −14 LUFS / ≥10 dB crest envelope; more hi-hat information at
the top **precisely so its removal at chorus two costs more.** Gradation is subtractive; a bigger start is
not an addition, it is a bigger thing to take away from.

### Structure & dynamics
Same skeleton, faster onset. The break arrives earlier by four bars and the last chorus does not resolve —
the record ends on the split fifth with the count still standing at its highest value.

### Hook technique
Count **17 → 18 → 19 → 20**, established against a "sixteen" in verse one that is the same count one value
back, **not a second numeric fact.** *"Seventeen. And I've not got my coat off."*

## WHAT ALL FOUR SHARE (and must, for the device to read as one idea)
- The wordless stack opens the record before any lyric and closes it after the last line, splitting to a
  fifth. **The harmony IS the riff.**
- The chorus is byte-identical apart from the numeral. **No note is filed about that.**
- One removal per climb: **hats → guitar → bass (two bars) → drums.**
- A person doing something in verse one; the person is invented; the objects are real.
- ⛔ Nobody draws a moral. Not once, not at the end, not in the outro, not in the title.
