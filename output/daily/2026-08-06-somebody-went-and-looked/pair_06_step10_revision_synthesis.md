### The judgements that matter
**V1 — the clean sweep, and it holds the shape of the whole pair.** Verse one is four physical actions and
a woman **giving the useful thing away before she goes**, with nothing made of it. F5 is the strongest in
the set — *bottle, cap, boot, torch, bag, door, dark* — and the hook line *"Seventy-five thousand went"*
survives the flat test on a four-letter verb. ⭐ It also carries the pair's refusal, once, as a joke:
*"Nobody made a speech. Everybody went."* The moral audit came back with nothing to argue about.

**V2 — one behind, and it holds the best single line in the pair.** *"Wrote nothing on the tag."* is a true
packing detail, the most ordinary line here, and the identifiability rule performed instead of stated. It
loses to V1 only on F7: its gradation is identical to V1's rather than its own. **The book verse**
(*took the book out / put the book back / took the book out / left the book on the side*) is four movements
of one object and the entire emotional content of the song, with not one word about feeling in it.

**V4 — third, and it owns F7 outright.** ⭐ **The last removal is the number itself.** The outro drops the
count, leaves the two voices, and the figure steps up one degree with nothing under it: **the count keeps
climbing after the song stops saying it.** That is the doctrine executed in the last eight bars, and it is
subtractive — V4's ending contains less than any other point in the record. It sits third only because V1
and V2 are stronger at F5.

**V3 — fourth, and it was the hardest to write.** *"Watching the road"* sits one word from *"waiting for
someone who does not come,"* which is the second number coming in the front door. The variation survives
because **nobody in it waits for anybody**: they move a crate, roll a mat, boil the water again, charge
everybody's phones, cut somebody's hair. The gate is a thing that *goes*. ⭐ *"Kid's got a ball and the
ball's got a hole"* is the strongest anti-lament image in the pair.

## SYNTHESIS — three transplants into the set

**S1 · V2's evidence grammar becomes the pair's house rule.** *"Bag's gone and the hook's still there."*
⭐ **EVIDENCE, NEVER INFERENCE** — the song may report what an object is doing; it may never report what
that means. Applied backwards into V1 (*"Behind her somebody puts the kettle on"*) and V4 (*"Chalk's down
to nothing. Chalk gets found."*). It is what keeps three sentimental traps — a goodbye, a mat, a kettle —
from ever becoming sentimental. **No face is described anywhere in this pair.**

**S2 · V1's joke into the pair's spine.** *"Nobody made a speech"* replaced an earlier *"Nobody waved"*
(step 09, critique 2) and the swap set the register for all four: **where a mood was about to arrive, put a
flat report instead.** V4's *"Everybody heard it the first time"* is the same move, and V3's *"Radio on.
Nobody listening"* is the third.

**S3 · V4's subtractive ending was offered to the others and REJECTED.** Dropping the number from V1's outro
was tried and it reads as a trick when the song has not spent three verses on a morning. It stays V4's,
where the frame earns it. ⛔ **One idea, in the one place it belongs.**

### Considered and REJECTED
- **A shared chorus line across all four.** ⛔ Rejected: the device is *the count that climbs*, not *the
  chorus that recurs across songs.* Four songs sharing one chorus would make the pair a suite, and a suite
  is a second structural idea. **One device.** *(The Venue Sociologist's note, declined with reasons —
  step 09, critique 6.)*
- **A fifth chorus, or a count past the verified figure.** ⛔ Rejected twice over: the count stops where the
  report stops — nothing is invented upward — and **her sets famously end one song early.**
- **A rude line, per the Battle-Axe.** ⛔ Rejected in the lyric, accepted in the fuzz. A rude line here is a
  line with an attitude about a real crossing, and an attitude is a position, and a position is a moral.
