# P01 · STEP 10 — RANKING, SYNTHESIS, AND THE FOUR PACKAGES

## RANK — V4 · V3 · V1 · V2

**V4 leads, and that is the surprise of this pair.** The step-09 cut that deleted night two's
escalation left a song in which **nothing happens twice and she sends it anyway** — which is the run's
whole subject with the news taken off it. It scores highest on F4 (a person doing something with no
occasion at all), F6 (it is the only variation whose *story* is subtractive as well as its mix), and
F7 (`Two dots, then nothing, then two dots` is the best noun in the pair).

**V3 second** — the tightest lens, the only present-tense take, and the only one where the drop in the
chest happens on the page: `He puts his arm over his eyes`. It loses to V4 only on F5-adjacent risk:
four minutes of one man on his back is the variation most likely to be heard as a ballad in a punk suit,
and the 3-3-2 bounce bridge is doing more work here than anywhere else.

**V1 third** — the cleanest F4 in the pair (she has hands, a pen, a mug and a shift) and the only one
carrying the full Moon fact in its natural place. It loses on F2 pressure: it was the variation that
grew a second device in draft, and the repair left verse two a line shorter than it wants to be.

**V2 fourth, and fourth is not a discard.** It has the single best physical act in the pair —
`He puts the phone back down / Face up this time` — and the pair's driest joke, `He'll send a thumbs
up at nine`. It ranks last only because its first verse spends six lines establishing a habit before
anything happens, and Sunna's spec is *get in, land the hook, leave.*

---

## SYNTHESIS — three transplants into V4

**S1 · TAKE `Who did not have to` FROM V2.** V2's strongest line was an aside in its second verse. In
V4 it is moved into the bounce bridge, where the argument of the pair actually sits, as
**`Nobody's put that on a rota / Nobody's going to`** — the same idea rendered as a *document that does
not exist* rather than as a statement about obligation. V2 keeps its own line; this is a transplant of
the idea, not the words, so both songs survive with their own noun.

**S2 · TAKE THE PHYSICAL LANDING FROM V2 AND V3.** Both losing variations end on an object being put
down and left (`Face up this time`; `He leaves it there`). V4 originally ended on a statement. It now
ends on **`Same again tomorrow`** — a rota entry, which is an object, not a conclusion.

**S3 · TAKE V3's TIMESTAMP ARITHMETIC AND SPEND IT AS SILENCE.** V3 proves that a listener will do the
maths themselves if you give them the number. V4 gives them **`Two dots, then nothing, then two dots`**
and never says what it means. Same mechanism, no sentence.

### Considered and REJECTED
- **Giving V4 a fifth chorus.** Rejected: four returns and a subtractive ladder that ends on the room
  alone is the form. A fifth return would have nothing left to remove and would therefore have to add.
- **Making V1's chorus call verbs match V4's.** Rejected: the *answer* is what must never change, and
  it does not. The calls are the point of view. Locking them would flatten four songs into one.
- **A harmony line on the last answer.** Rejected twice — it is an addition (F6) and it is Flair 15,
  which belongs to P06. A room out of breath sings in unison.
