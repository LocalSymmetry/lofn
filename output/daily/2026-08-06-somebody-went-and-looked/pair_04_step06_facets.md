## ⭐ 0. THE RIFF — WRITTEN FIRST, BEFORE A SINGLE WORD

**Instrument:** one electric guitar, drop-D tuning (lowest string down to D), **that string and no other string**, fuzz already saturated at the first note, no clean channel anywhere on the record.

**The figure — four notes:**

| | note | fret (low D string) | move |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **F2** | 3 | — |
| 2 | **G2** | 5 | up a **major 2nd** |
| 3 | **C3** | 10 | up a **perfect 4th** |
| 4 | **A2** | 7 | down a **minor 3rd** |

**Span:** F2 → C3, a **perfect fifth**. Comfortably inside one octave.
**The rule that makes it the axis value:** the open string *is* D, the tonic. **The hand never leaves the neck and the open string is never allowed to ring.** The one note this song is named after is the one note the riff will not play. It ends hanging on A, the fifth, which is the most open, least terminal note available — every repetition re-opens instead of closing.

**One bar, 4/4, at 108 BPM. Four notes, one per beat, palm half-muted on 1 and 4.** Present from bar 1 to bar 136. Never doubled, never harmonised, never transposed, never octave-stacked.

### THE WHISTLE TEST — could someone whistle it walking away from the venue, having heard it twice, drunk?
**Yes.** You whistle it an octave and a half up and it is unchanged, because a contour survives transposition and a texture does not. The contour is: **step up, jump up, drop back, stop short.** Four events, one leap, one landing that isn't a landing. That is a shape a drunk person can reproduce because it has exactly one surprise in it (the fourth) and one refusal (the ending). It is a thing a person plays with their hands at fret 3–5–10–7 and another person can play back — the Rock Titan's condition, met with a fingering, not a claim.

## ⭐ THE ARGUMENT — why this riff survives being the ONLY harmonic event for five minutes

*The panel's amplified seat (after Fela Kuti): "if your riff cannot survive being the only harmonic event, it is not a good enough riff, and no amount of gear-changing will hide that." This is the pair that has to prove it. Six reasons, in order of load-bearing weight.*

**1. It never completes a sentence, so the ear never files it.** A chord progression is a grammatical unit — I–VI–IV–V is a sentence, and after four readings the ear has parsed it and demands the next one. A four-note single-string figure that stops on the fifth is not a sentence; it is a **question shape**. It cannot be marked *done*, so it does not generate the appetite it would then have to satisfy. **Boredom is the ear finishing something. This never finishes.**

**2. A chord change here would not relieve the listener — it would repair them.** This is the real argument and it is structural, not aesthetic. The device is *the name repeated until it stops being a word*. That effect is a **semantic collapse**, and it only happens if nothing re-engages the listener's parser. A chord change is the single most reliable way to make a listener wake up and start tracking form again. **The modulation is not omitted for taste; it is forbidden because it would undo the song.** Anything that says *here comes a new section* hands the words back their meaning, and the last ninety seconds die.

**3. All the motion the piece needs is available below harmony, and there is more of it.** Rhythm, register, density, room, hand-noise, breath, and — above all — **removal**. Eight subtractive events at roughly 35-second intervals, each of them a loss the ear registers instantly and cannot name. An unchanging harmony makes each removal *audible*; a moving harmony would mask every one of them. **The constraint is what makes the gradation legible.**

**4. The chant is the second axis, and it is in 3 against the riff's 4.** The riff is four notes per bar in 4/4. The chant "Ocean Going" occupies **three beats**, said twice, then a **two-beat hole** — a 3-3-2 across two bars, which is the run's anchor cell arriving as rhythm instead of melody. The chant's accent therefore walks against the riff and re-meets it on a cycle the listener feels as *arrival* without a single chord moving. **That is the chord change, relocated into meter. Receipt kept.**

**5. The two-beat hole is where the room goes.** The lead vocal is muted for the answer; two room mics carry it. That is a real hole, not a stacked vocal. A riff that has a hole in it every two bars is not a wall — it is a call, and a room fills calls.

**6. The pursuer supplies the only thing a static harmony genuinely lacks: direction.** A low irregular knock under the floor of the mix, one hair closer to the surface every minute, never arriving, never named. It gives the five minutes a vector without giving them a chord.

**⚡ The honest cost, on the record:** this is harder to write than a gear-change and easier to get wrong. If the render smooths the removals into a single texture, the song is four minutes of loop and the skeptic was right. **That risk is answered in the prompt and again in the exclude field, twice, in both directions** — and it is the pair's #1 declared risk.

## THE PERSON *(Law 5 — in the lyric, verse one, not the concept note)*

**A woman flat on her back on a garage floor, sanding rust off a hull with her thumb, in November, for the ninth year.** She built a boat in a garage the boat cannot fit through. She painted a name on the stern with a small brush. **She says the name out loud every time she touches it.**

**THE NAME: `OCEAN GOING`.** Four syllables. Open vowels — OH-uh-OH-ih. A room can shout it out of breath. It is what she intends the boat to be and it is said with a completely straight face, and the song never once suggests she is wrong.

**Why this name and no other:** repeated past comfort it comes apart in a specific direction — *Ocean Going · O-shun-go-ing · shun-go-ing · go-ing · going* — and the boat that will never leave the garage decays into **the word for leaving**. This is never explained, never winked at, never mentioned in the lyric. It happens in the ear, not in the mind. **A listener who takes only the surface gets a whole song; a listener who hears the decay gets the second listen.** Free channel. No toll.

**THE SITUATION:** *doing the thing that does not pay, and refusing to be embarrassed about it.* Everybody has stood next to somebody's unreasonable project and heard them say its name.

## ⭐ THE WEIGHTED FACETS — 9, ranked

*Weights sum to 1.00. Each carries its failure mode and why it matters. These are the scoring rubric for steps 08–10.*

### F1 · THE ONE HARMONIC EVENT HOLDS — **weight 0.18**
The four-note figure is the only harmonic content in 136 bars, and at 4:30 it is still the reason you are listening rather than the thing you are enduring.
**Failure mode:** the renderer hears five minutes of one riff as an *invitation to escalate* and inserts a chord, a lift, a relative-major turnaround, or a "bridge." One borrowed chord and the pair has failed its only mandate.
**Why it matters:** this is the run's single sanctioned exception and the only place the doctrine's most extreme claim gets tested. If it holds here, "the riff is the argument" is proven rather than asserted.

### F2 · THE NAME STOPS BEING A WORD — **weight 0.16**
Somewhere past 3:30 the listener stops hearing language and starts hearing sound, and cannot say when it happened.
**Failure mode:** the repetition reads as an **error** — a stuck loop, a generation fault — rather than a mechanism. The listener thinks *something has gone wrong* instead of *something is happening*.
**Why it matters:** it is the device. Without the collapse, this is a three-minute song with two extra minutes on it, and the exception should be revoked.

### F3 · GRADATION IS SUBTRACTIVE AND AUDIBLE — **weight 0.13**
Everything the record will ever own is present by 0:20. From there it only loses parts: hi-hat, second guitar, snare, bass, kick, claps, room. By 4:30 there is the figure, four vowels and a room.
**Failure mode:** the render *adds* into the last chorus — the universal generator reflex — and the whole arc inverts. Or the removals are so gentle they read as a mix fade rather than eight decisions.
**Why it matters:** subtraction is the only growth mechanism that cannot run out of headroom, and it is the only one a static harmony makes legible.

### F4 · THE FLAT TEST ON THE HOOK LINES — **weight 0.11**
*"It doesn't fit through the door. She named it anyway."* Said flatly, no music, to a person's face, it survives — on the nouns, not the syntax.
**Failure mode:** the plain line goes *poetic* under pressure and becomes merely short. Any line that needs the music to work has failed.
**Why it matters:** the free-channel law. The words carry feeling; the music carries complexity. A line doing structural work is a toll booth.

### F5 · THE WHISTLE TEST, RENDER-SIDE — **weight 0.10**
Hum the record with the vocal muted and the four notes are there, before the first line and after the last.
**Failure mode:** the renderer returns a **pad, a drone, or a competent arpeggio** in the riff's place and the self-check ticks the box anyway. The Rock Titan's charge, carried live.
**Why it matters:** if the vocal is the only tune, every ounce of meaning is back in the toll booth and the doctrine is a rationale.

### F6 · SUNNA'S REGISTER — DRY, FUNNY, NEVER EARNEST — **weight 0.10**
Sing-speak, flat, eye-rolling, audibly amused. Feelings stated so plainly you can hear the shrug.
**Failure mode:** ⛔ **earnestness at length.** Five minutes is where a singer reaches for sincerity and a renderer reaches for a warm alto and a swell. A reverent take on this material would be unbearable and would break the persona outright.
**Why it matters:** it is the seat. Sunna is admitted on *unembarrassed delight*; a solemn version of this song is a different artist.

### F7 · THE REFUSAL HOLDS — **weight 0.08**
The boat is loved and the song never sneers at it, never says it will not float, never invites the room to laugh at her. The neighbours who laugh are not punished either.
**Failure mode:** one smirking line, one "bless her," one *so bad it's good*. A joke that requires a butt.
**Why it matters:** ⛔ she will not be ironic about anything anyone loves. This binds hardest on a song whose subject is somebody else's unreasonable project.

### F8 · THE PURSUER, UNNAMED AND UNRESOLVED — **weight 0.08**
Something under the floor, rising one hair a minute for five minutes, never arriving, never once mentioned in the lyric.
**Failure mode:** it resolves (arrives, drops, becomes a bass part) — or worse, a lyric names it and the whole thing turns into a horror song.
**Why it matters:** joy that has looked at the wolf is credible. Joy that hasn't is a mascot. At this length the wolf is the only reason the delight doesn't curdle into contentment.

### F9 · THE ROOM OWNS THE CHANT — **weight 0.06**
Four syllables, no interval a normal throat can't reach at volume while out of breath, and a real two-beat hole every two bars where the lead is muted and the room mics carry it.
**Failure mode:** the hole is filled with a stacked backing vocal, i.e. the singer answering herself. Then it is a solo with a crowd sample on it.
**Why it matters:** *Load the floor.* Her voice is not the product. The room is.
