# P01 · STEP 07 — SONG GUIDES

## ⭐ WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?

**Behind a desk that is bolted to the floor, at 03:50, in a room lit by two screens and one overhead
tube that hums.** Feet flat, both of them, because the chair has no gas left in it and sinks. A cold
mug at the right hand with a skin on the tea. A coat still on, because the heating goes off at one.
The body has been in this position for six hours and will be in it for two more.

**And in the second half of the record, the same body is lying down**, on its back, phone on the chest,
screen going dark on its own, a boiler ticking somewhere below the floor.

That is the whole geography. Two positions: **upright at a desk doing something nobody asked for**, and
**flat on a bed receiving it.** The song crosses between them and never leaves the building.

## ⭐ WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?

Nothing in the room. **That is the point, and it is the danger.** The threat is not physical:

1. **The count that will not arrive.** Something keeps counting four and the song it is counting in
   never starts. It gets closer every section. By the last chorus it is under everything, still
   counting, still not resolving. *It is never mentioned and it is never explained.*
2. **Six o'clock.** The shift ends whether or not the thing she noticed matters. Finitude, not doom —
   she has four spare minutes and she is spending them.
3. **The thing that would actually hurt: being right about being alone.** Three weeks of a phone face
   down is not grief, it is a *habit*, and a habit is harder to interrupt than a wound. The body's real
   risk in this song is that it braces so well it stops noticing it is braced.

The song's whole job is the fourth chorus, where the horn is gone and **twelve strangers answer into
the gap anyway.** Nobody in the room asked them to either.

## GUIDE A — THE ARCHITECTURE (binding on all four variations)

**Tempo/key:** 150 BPM, A minor, 2:20. **Chorus lands by 0:20.**

**Form:** Intro (horn alone, 4 bars) → Verse 1 (8) → Chorus 1 (8) → Verse 2 (8) → Chorus 2 (8) →
Bounce Bridge (12) → Chorus 3 (8) → Verse 3 (4) → Chorus 4 (8) → Outro (horn alone).

**Gear-change:** punk → bounce → punk. The bounce is the bridge only: half-time feel, 3-3-2 kick,
fuzz bass to the front, guitar out, **no room voices at all** — so their return in chorus three is a
door opening.

**The pursuer:** a count-in that keeps arriving. Sticks, four beats, entering one bar earlier each
section until it is continuous underneath the last chorus. It never resolves into anything. **Direct
it in the production channels and the style prompt; forbid it from every sung line.**

**Gradation, subtractive, no exceptions:**
| return | what is removed |
|---|---|
| Chorus 2 | the hi-hat |
| Chorus 3 | the bass, for two bars |
| Chorus 4 | **the horn** — and the room answers into the gap |

**The mix, non-negotiable:** kick 12 ms late, bass on the grid. Doubles misaligned 15–35 ms, ±6 cents.
No de-esser on the breaths. Room mics 8–12 dB below instinct, high-passed at 180 Hz, short plate
0.9–1.4 s. Master −14 LUFS, ≥10 dB crest.

**Acoustic policy, stated because the prompt must state it:** nothing acoustic on this record except
**twelve human throats and two wooden sticks.** No acoustic guitar, no piano, no strings, no acoustic kit.

## GUIDE B — THE CHANT (binding on all four variations)

**The call is three negations and one fact. The answer is four syllables and it never changes.**

- **Call shape:** `Nobody's <verb>ing` × 3, then `Somebody went and looked`.
- **Answer, fixed, all four songs, all sixteen returns:** **`Somebody saw`**.

**Why that answer and not another.** It is 4 syllables; it opens on an unvoiced *s* a room can start
together without a conductor; it ends on an open *aw* that can be held at volume with no breath left;
it contains no consonant cluster; and **it alliterates with itself**, which is why it survives being
shouted badly. It is also the plain rebuttal of the only fact in the song — *nobody had even seen it*.

**Direct, do not draft:** each variation supplies its own three `Nobody's …` verbs from its own point
of view, and **the fourth call and the answer are byte-identical across all four songs.** File no note
about the repetition. Return is free.

**The hole:** write the answer on its own line. The lead vocal is silent in that bar. In chorus four
the horn is silent too, so the answer is the only sound in the building.

## GUIDE C — THE FOUR VARIATIONS (angle, body, and what each is forbidden)

### V1 — THE ONE WHO LOOKED
**Angle:** her shift, start to finish, in the third person and the past tense.
**Body:** upright, at the desk, then walking out into a cold car park at six.
**Required:** the single Moon line, in verse one. Her *not* signing it. The detail that she is asleep
before he ever opens it — **she never finds out what it did.**
**Forbidden:** any line in which she knows it landed. Any line that calls her kind, brave or unsung.
**Verbs for the call:** checking · coming · looking.

### V2 — THE ONE WHO'D STOPPED EXPECTING
**Angle:** his evening and his four a.m., third person, past tense.
**Body:** at a sink with his sleeves up; then sitting on the edge of a bed with both hands on a phone.
**Required:** the habit stated flatly and without self-pity — *three weeks of a phone face down.* The
gap between the buzz and him moving. The last physical act of the song is **putting the phone back
down the other way up.**
**Forbidden:** tears, thanks, a reply that says anything. He is allowed one thumbs-up, nine hours late.
**Verbs for the call:** writing · counting · waiting.

### V3 — THE PICTURE ARRIVING AT 4AM
**Angle:** four minutes, present tense, close-up. The shortest span and the tightest lens.
**Body:** flat on his back, awake before it arrives, arm going over his eyes after.
**Required:** the room going white for a second. The arithmetic he does on the timestamp — *ten to
four means she was still at work, which means she stopped* — done in plain lines, never explained.
**Forbidden:** past tense. Any line about the Moon after verse one. Any line that tells us what he feels.
**Verbs for the call:** calling · typing · coming.

### V4 — THE NEXT NIGHT, LOOKING AGAIN
**Angle:** the second night. She does it again, and this time there is nothing to find.
**Body:** upright at the same desk, early, on purpose.
**Required:** **nothing happens.** She sends it anyway. Two typing dots that appear and stop and appear
again, and neither of them mentions it. The song ends on a rota, not a revelation.
**Forbidden:** a bigger event than night one. Escalation of any kind. This is the quietest of the four
and it is the one that proves the record.
**Verbs for the call:** watching · asking · paying.

## GUIDE D — REGISTER (binding, and the easiest thing to lose)

- **Sing-speak.** Talky and rhythmic, on the beat, close to the mic. Childlike for a bar, flat threat
  the next. **Never sung-pretty. No warm alto, no choir, no crystalline anything.**
- **Dry and eye-rolling.** Feelings stated so flatly you can hear the eye-roll. *"Not sad about it,
  just stopped."* Funny is a requirement, not a garnish.
- **⛔ THE REFUSAL.** Nobody in this song is a butt. The man who stopped checking his phone is not
  pathetic. The woman on the desk is not a saint. **Neither of them is available as a joke.**
- **⛔ Never explaining.** The lines continue the story; they never interpret it. If a line tells the
  listener what the situation means, cut it and write the next thing that happened instead.
- **⛔ Not about the doctrine.** No line in any variation is about riffs, simplicity, attention, looking
  as a virtue, or being seen. The song is about a woman with four spare minutes.

## GUIDE E — WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS GENERIC (write against these)
1. A chorus that resolves. The chant states a fact; it does not conclude.
2. Any sung "I". This record is third person throughout and gets its intimacy from the camera position,
   not from confession.
3. A key change, a final-chorus stack, an octave-up guitar, a big last snare. **All four are additions.**
4. Weather. No dawn, no light, no sky doing anything meaningful. The sky is used once, as a fact, and
   then the song is indoors for good.
