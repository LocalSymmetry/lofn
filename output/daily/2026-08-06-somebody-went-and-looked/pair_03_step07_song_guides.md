# 07 — SONG GUIDES · PAIR 03
`2026-08-06-somebody-went-and-looked` · **SUNNA** · P03 · *the list that loves* · 165 BPM · E, drop-D · 2:10

---

## ***WHERE IS THE BODY STANDING?***

**At a kitchen table, late, with the good lamp pulled over.** The kettle has boiled and gone cold. There is a paper towel flat on the wood and a small steel fastener sitting in the middle of it, and a phone propped against a mug, and a torch held in the other hand at an angle that keeps slipping. The body is leaning in far enough that its own shadow keeps getting in the shot. It smells like cold tea and metal. The chair is the wrong height and has been for years.

That is the body for all four variations. The other bodies in the pair are **one bus seat with a wet coat on it**, and **one sofa in the small hours with a phone held up over a face**, and they are all the same body from a different angle: **somebody paying close attention to a thing that has not asked them to.**

⭐ The body is *not* standing in space, and it is not standing near an orbiter. The orbiter was the opening shot of the run and this pair does not return to it. The subject is somebody going out of their way to look at something for you, and here it happens at a table, in a house, for free.

## ***WHAT COULD HURT IT HERE?***

**Three things, in order of likelihood.**

1. **A smirk.** Not from the world — from *us*. The single most available move in this material is affectionate condescension: the *isn't this adorable* voice, the raised eyebrow, the "so bad it's good" reflex. It would kill the song instantly and it would kill Sunna's seat with it. **The pair is written so that no line's comedy survives the deletion of its affection.**
2. **Two words on a screen.** V3's actual wound. Two thousand words go up; two words come back. It genuinely hurts, and the song is not permitted to make the person who typed them a villain, because *he was tired, and he meant it, and he is not the point.*
3. **The lamp being the only light in the house.** The quiet threat under all four: nobody is coming. The list is not going to be rewarded. The pursuer — one guitar drifting flat, never named, never fixed — is that fact turned into a sound.

**What does not hurt it:** being ignored. That is the premise, not the injury. A song that treats obscurity as tragedy would be Lofn's song, and this is not Lofn's song.

# GUIDE 1 — **THE RIFF AS THE ARGUMENT** *(Facet 1, weight 18)*

**Storytelling techniques:** *in medias res* (the record is already playing when it starts — no count-in, no fade, a guitar mid-thought) and *epizeuxis* (a phrase said twice with no development, so the second one means "I meant it").
**Mood & arc:** begins as physical amusement — the riff is a bit ugly and knows it — and ends as flat devotion with nothing underneath it but a dying amp.
**Instrumentation & texture:** one electric guitar in drop-D through one amp with a torn cone. E, G, A, B♭: minor third up, whole tone up, semitone up, tritone down home. Two fast eighths into two dragging quarters, the flat fifth hung on the and-of-three. Fuzz bass locked dead on the grid so the late kick can lean on it. Kit is small, close, and slightly too loud in the room mic.
**Production & effects:** no reamp, no double-tracking of the main riff, fret buzz kept, one take. The room mic is the reverb. Master at −14 LUFS with ≥10 dB crest — weight, not loudness.
**Structure & dynamics:** riff alone for eight bars before the first line; riff alone after the last word, dying mid-note. It never disappears for more than two bars anywhere in between.
**Hook techniques:** (a) hang the B♭ a full beat longer than comfortable; (b) let the guitarist come in a hair early on the repeat and leave it; (c) in the last chorus double it an octave up with a guitar that has been going flat all song.
**Poetic devices carried into the words:** anaphora, epizeuxis.
**Central aspect:** the whole doctrine is falsifiable at this one point. Mute the vocal. Is there a tune?

# GUIDE 2 — **THE LIST THAT LOVES** *(Facets 4 + 5, weight 22)*

**Storytelling techniques:** *catalogue verse* (the oldest device there is — Homer's ships, a seed packet, an auction lot) and *ruthless specificity* (the detail is the proof somebody was paying attention).
**Mood & arc:** absorption that never breaks into announcement. It does not build to a thesis; it thins to a single item.
**Instrumentation:** the list rides the riff without ornament. Under list block 3 there is no band at all — one voice, one handclap on two and four, one boot.
**Structure & dynamics:** **6 items → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1.** Every block is a strict subset of block one, in original order. Nothing is added, ever, no matter how good the new line is.
**Rhyme scheme:** the list does not rhyme at the ends and does not need to — the return lives in the identical opening stem and in the byte-identical chorus that follows it. Choruses carry the rhyme.
**Hook techniques:** (a) the stem — **"Look at the ___"** — is the run's own spine said in a kitchen; (b) items land on hard nouns: *lip, spanner, notch, jobs, shine, weight*; (c) the last surviving item is the one about the notch that cannot go in wrong, because a part designed so it cannot be fitted backwards is a small act of care by somebody the writer will never meet.
**Poetic devices:** anaphora (the device), alliteration, epizeuxis, asyndeton.
**Narrative seat:** third person for the writer, first person only in V2 where the reader speaks.
**Failure to police:** a second device dressed as craft. No `(response)` parentheses, no misheard line, no climbing count.

# GUIDE 3 — **ZERO SMIRK** *(Facet 2, weight 16)*

**Storytelling techniques:** *granting the antagonist a body* (the commenter gets a wet coat and a stop to get off at) and *refusal shown, never stated* (the writer cuts a joke inside verse two — it happens, it is not discussed).
**Mood & arc:** dry and unbothered on the surface, with real affection carried underneath and never announced. Funny is a requirement here; contempt is a disqualification.
**Vocal technique:** sing-speak, talky, flat, close dynamic mic, breaths left in, no de-esser. **The delivery must never be a comic voice.** Nothing is performed *at* the subject. The humour is structural — the sheer distance travelled for a small metal object — and it evaporates the moment the singer indicates that it is funny.
**Production:** no cartoon punctuation. No slide whistle, no boing, no laugh-track ambience, no ironic key change. The song has no punchline architecture at all: the last thing anybody hears is one item said once into a room that has stopped playing.
**Structure & dynamics:** the ending goes **smaller**, deliberately. This is the anti-novelty guarantee that lives in the form rather than in a production note, and it is the only kind that survives a render.
**Hook technique:** *"Two thousand words and not one joke."* It is a report on somebody else's discipline and it is also the whole brief, and it never once says so.
**Emotional tone signals:** Delight · Admiration · Devotion. Never mirth-at.
**Test:** read each line flat to a person's face. If it needs an eyebrow, it is a repair.

# GUIDE 4 — **THE GEAR-CHANGE AND THE ROOM** *(Facets 6 + 8, weight 18)*

**Storytelling techniques:** *the withheld chorus* (the room already knows the four syllables by the second pass, so the hole in the fourth is unbearable in the right way) and *loud-quiet-loud as narrative* (the quiet is not a bridge, it is the same person with the band taken away).
**Mood & arc:** communal detonation → one person and a clap → communal detonation, worse in tune and better in every other way.
**Instrumentation:** at the gear-change everything leaves inside one bar with no riser. What is left: voice, one handclap on two and four, one boot on a board. When the band returns it returns on the downbeat, whole, with the riff doubled an octave up.
**Production:** the room bed is two mics 8–12 dB lower than instinct wants, high-passed at 180 Hz, into a short plate of about one second — never a hall. Vocal doubles deliberately misaligned 15–35 ms and ±6 cents; the smear is the arrangement.
**Structure & dynamics:** chorus lands by 0:25. Chorus two loses the hi-hat. Chorus three loses the bass for two bars. The last chorus opens **a full bar of real silence** where four syllables go, and that silence is not filled with a stacked vocal — filling it converts a chant into a solo with a crowd sample on it.
**Hook techniques:** (a) four syllables, all open vowels, shoutable while out of breath; (b) three of the chorus's five lines are byte-identical to each other; (c) the odd line out is the one about the joke, which is the one nobody expects to be the different one.
**Poetic devices:** anaphora, epistrophe, diacope.

# GUIDE 5 — **THE THING THAT IS GAINING** *(Facet 7, weight 8)*

**Storytelling techniques:** *the unspoken antagonist* and *dramatic irony without a wink* — the listener feels the wrongness and is never told about it.
**Mood & arc:** the song's mood does not darken. The tuning does. That gap is the entire effect.
**Instrumentation:** a second guitar doubling the riff, starting perhaps six cents flat and arriving at roughly thirty by the last chorus. One automation lane, monotonic, never resolving, never corrected in a mix note.
**Production:** the beating between the two guitars should be audible as *texture* before it is audible as *pitch* — chorusy at first, seasick by the end. Nobody in the room will say "that's flat." They will say the last chorus felt bigger, and they will be right, and they will be wrong about why.
**Structure & dynamics:** it is worst exactly where the song is largest. The thing that makes the finale huge is the thing that has been going wrong all along.
**Narrative rule:** ⛔ **never named in the lyric.** Not as tuning, not as a peg, not as a metaphor for anything. If a line refers to it, the line goes.
**Why this is credible joy:** the wolf is behind the chariot. The set ends because it ends. This is the one element in the song that is not fine, and it is what makes the delight read as a practice rather than a temperament.

# GUIDE 6 — **WILDCARD: THE EXPLODED VIEW**

**Cross-discipline seed:** the technical illustration — the *exploded axonometric*, where a mechanism is drawn hanging apart in mid-air on dotted lines so every part can be seen at once, each one numbered, each number keyed to a plain sentence at the bottom of the page. Nobody signs those drawings. Somebody drew every one of them.
**How it becomes music:** the song is an exploded view. The riff is the dotted line every part hangs on. The list is the key at the bottom of the page. Each verse is one part lifted out and turned over — and the gradation is the drawing being reassembled, part by part, until only the one that matters is still floating.
**Texture translation:** hard edges, no blur, no atmosphere. A technical drawing has no reverb in it. Keep the plate short, keep the room small, keep every element separable — you should be able to point at each of the four sources at any moment in the track.
**Palette:** graph-paper blue-grey, one warm lamp, steel with a fingerprint on it.
**Narrative gift:** an exploded view is an act of love performed as documentation, by an anonymous person, for a stranger who will need it later. That is the same act as the article, the same act as the orbiter turning its camera — and the song can carry that without ever saying it, because a listener who takes only the surface still receives a whole song about a man and a lamp and a small piece of steel.
**Poetic devices:** synecdoche (the notch stands for the whole care), asyndeton.
**Run-time:** 2:10. Get in, land the hook, leave. Sunna's sets end one song early on purpose.
