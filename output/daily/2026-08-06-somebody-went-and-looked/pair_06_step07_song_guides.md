# P06 · STEP 07 — SONG GUIDES

## ⭐ THE TWO QUESTIONS, ASKED FIRST, FOR THE PAIR AS A WHOLE

### ***Where is the body standing?***
**In a room, in the hour before somebody leaves it, with a bag already by the door.**
Not on a road. Not at a border. Not at a fence. ⭐ **The song never goes to the crossing** — it stays in the
kitchen, the doorway, the yard, the room with the mat down. Every one of the four bodies is standing
somewhere with a **tap, a kettle, a hook, a wall** within reach of a hand.

That is a deliberate structural decision and it does four jobs at once:
1. **It is the situation, not the sentence.** *"The hour before somebody goes"* is a position every listener
   has stood in — a lift to the airport, a hospital bag by the stairs, a kid moving out. **They will
   recognise it instantly and decode nothing.** The scale is what is new; the room is not.
2. **It makes the person ordinary by construction.** You cannot write a statistic doing a bottle cap.
3. **It keeps the geography out.** No place can be named because no character ever arrives anywhere.
4. ⭐ **It is where the second number cannot follow.** The pursuer belongs to the road. **The song stays in
   the room, so the road stays in the sub.**

### ***What could hurt it here?***
Five things, ranked, each with the countermeasure written before the first lyric:

| # | What hurts it | Countermeasure, structural |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐ **The two voices go sacred.** A wordless women's harmony over this subject, given reverb, is a hymn for the dead. It would import the exact thing the song refuses to say, through the one channel the lyric cannot police | **Hard plosives** (`Da-da-da-da`), **dead-dry close mic, no tail**, **fuzz bass** doubling the lower voice an octave down. A punk instrument standing where the organ would be |
| 2 | ⭐ **Somebody gets a moral.** One line of position and the record becomes an argument | **Evidence, never inference** — the song reports what an object is doing, never what it means. The refusal is stated once, as a joke: *"Nobody made a speech."* |
| 3 | **Sentiment.** A goodbye, a mat, a kettle: three sentimental traps in a row | Every emotional beat is **displaced into an object**. Nobody's face is described in any of the four songs. Not once |
| 4 | **The second number arrives by implication** — an unanswered phone, an empty place, somebody waiting | ⛔ **No line reports an absence of an expected arrival.** Two such lines were drafted and cut (step 09). The count only climbs |
| 5 | **The count becomes a cheer.** 78,000 shouted in a club is one bad arrangement decision away from a football chant | `EMO:Resolve`, never Delight, never Triumph, on every chorus. The floor is flat, not rising. **The final chorus resolves nothing and repeats byte-identically** |

## THE PAIR'S SHARED SPINE — what is identical across all four

**THE RIFF (step 06 §0).** Two women's voices, wordless, a diatonic third apart.
Lower **E♭4 · F4 · G4 · A♭4 · G4** · upper **G4 · A♭4 · B♭4 · C5 · B♭4** · `Da-da-da-da — dah`.
Opens the record alone, sits under every chorus as the room's part, closes the record alone.
**The split to a fifth (G4–D5) happens exactly twice** — the last bar of the break, and the final note.

**THE DEVICE.** `the count that climbs` — **75 → 76 → 77 → 78 thousand**, one step per chorus, and
**nothing else in the chorus changes.** The climb lands the verified figure in the loudest position in the
song. ⭐ **It counts departures.** It is the only new information in any of these four songs.

**THE CHORUS SKELETON** (byte-identical inside each song, one constant line per variation):
```
<count> thousand went.
Da-da-da-da — dah
<the variation's constant line>
Da-da-da-da — dah
<count> thousand went.
Da-da-da-da — dah
```
⭐ **The division of labour is the whole arrangement:** the room owns the wordless line, which never
changes and is learnable in one pass; the singer owns the number, which is the only thing that does change.
**Load the floor.** A byte-identical chorus needs no defence and no note.

**THE FORM** (all four): intro riff alone / V1 / **Chorus 1 (75) — by 0:25** / V2 / pre-chorus /
Chorus 2 (76, hats gone) / **break — four-on-floor collapses to a 165 breakbeat, the voices split to a
fifth** / Chorus 3 (77, guitar gone) / V3 / pre-chorus / Chorus 4 (78, bass out two bars, the room's bar is
real silence) / Chorus 4 again / outro riff alone, splits to a fifth. **2:45. No key change. No fade.**

**THE PURSUER.** A sub creeping a semitone across the whole track, never arriving, never resolving,
⛔ never named. It is under every bar of all four songs.

**WHAT VARIES.** The person, the room, the constant line, the pre-chorus, and the first five seconds.

## VARIATION 1 — *the woman and the hour she went*
**Title: BAG BY THE DOOR SINCE DARK**

**Where is the body standing?** At a tap, filling a bottle, in a room where the bag has been by the door
since it got dark. **What could hurt it here?** She could become Everywoman. Countermeasure: she does four
specific physical things and is never described.

**The person (Law 5, verse one):** a woman fills a bottle at a tap, **turns the cap till it sits straight**,
laces the left boot and then the right, and **puts the good torch in somebody's hand** — she gives it away
before she goes. Invented entirely. Unnamed. Unplaced.

**Constant chorus line:** *"Bag by the door since dark."* — flat test: survives on three nouns and one
preposition. It is also the WHEN slot, doing its job without a numeral.

**Grammar:** third person once, then **subject-dropped action lines** — *"Turned the cap till it sat
straight."* Subject-dropping is punk, it is fast, and it dodges the two registers that would sink this
song: a first-person migrant narrator (appropriation) and an observing third-person narrator (documentary).
**A list of actions has no narrator.**

**First five seconds:** the two voices alone, completely dry, nothing behind them. The floor arrives after.

**The line that carries the refusal:** *"Nobody made a speech. Everybody went."* — the only line in the pair
that acknowledges the existence of an opinion, and it acknowledges it by reporting that nobody had one.
Funny, flat, and it is Sunna's own eye-roll aimed at the song she is refusing to write.

**Ends on:** *"Behind her somebody locks the door. / Behind her somebody puts the kettle on."* Life
continues in the room she left. **No comment is made about this.**

## VARIATION 2 — *the one who packed for her*
**Title: I PACKED IT SO IT SHUTS**

**Where is the body standing?** On the floor, sitting on a bag, making a zip go round.
**What could hurt it here?** This is the sentimental one — the goodbye is in it. Countermeasure: the singer
never says what she feels; she says what she did to a bag.

**The person (verse one):** *"I rolled it. I never fold."* Charger down the side where you'll find it. Sat
on the lid till the zip went round. Good socks on the top. ⭐ **"Wrote nothing on the tag."**

⭐ **THAT LINE.** It is a true packing detail, it is the most ordinary line in the pair, and it is the
Human Subject Standard performed instead of stated. **A listener who takes only the surface receives a whole
song** and never notices. *(Rule 3 — the deep reference stays subversive, never explained, never winked at.)*

**Constant chorus line:** *"I packed it so it shuts."* — flat test: survives, and it is funny and it hurts,
which is the register. It is a person claiming the only competence available to them.

**Grammar:** **first person.** The one who packs is the one who stays — voicing the person left in the room
carries no appropriation problem and lets the pair run two registers across four songs.

**First five seconds:** the two voices with **one fuzz-bass note underneath from bar one** — the packer's
weight, the sound of somebody sitting on a lid.

**The verse that does the most work:** *"Took the book out. Put the book back. / Took the book out. / Left
the book on the side."* Four movements of one object. It is the entire emotional content of the song and
**not one word of it is about feeling.**

**Ends on:** *"Bag's gone and the hook's still there."* An object reporting an absence. ⛔ **Evidence, never
inference** — the line does not say what the hook means.

## VARIATION 3 — *the ones already there, watching the road*
**Title: ROOM ON THE MAT**

**Where is the body standing?** On the other end. In a room with a mat, a slow tap and a queue for it.
**What could hurt it here?** ⭐ **This is the highest-risk variation in the pair** — "watching the road"
sits one word away from "waiting for someone who does not come," which is the second number arriving
through the front door. **Countermeasure, absolute: nobody in this song waits for anybody.** They *do
things*. Every line is an action taken by somebody who is already there, and the gate is a thing that
**goes**, never a thing that fails to go.

**The person (verse one):** he moves a crate to make the room, rolls the mat out with his foot, **boils the
water again because**, and holds a phone at the window for the bars.

**Constant chorus line:** *"Room on the mat for whoever."* — unsentimental hospitality. *Whoever* is the
whole ethic and it costs one word. Flat test: survives on the mat.

**First five seconds:** the two voices **doubled by a room mic 8 dB under instinct**, high-passed at 180 Hz
into the sweat plate. The room is the subject of this one, so the room is in the hook.

**The anti-lament image:** *"Kid's got a ball and the ball's got a hole."* Permitted explicitly by the
standard's §5 (a child in the abstract; not identifiable, not a victim) and declared in step 06 §0B. It is
the single strongest guard in the pair against the whole thing going solemn.

**The device inside the situation:** *"Counting them in off the top of the wall. / Lost count. Started
again."* Somebody is out there counting, badly, by hand. **The song's device is a thing a character is
literally doing.**

## VARIATION 4 — *the number the next morning, still climbing*
**Title: BIGGER BY BREAKFAST**

**Where is the body standing?** At a table, the morning after, with a wet hand on a radio dial.
**What could hurt it here?** A morning-after song wants to be a reckoning. Countermeasure: it is a
breakfast. Butter, bread, a kettle, chalk on a wall, and a number that got bigger while everyone slept.

**The person (verse one):** she turns the radio up **with a wet hand** — she was doing the washing-up. The
butter is out and soft. The bread is the end of the loaf again. **Everybody eats standing up.**

**Constant chorus line:** *"Bigger than it was last night."* — flat test: survives, and it is the "still
climbing" stated as plainly as it can be stated.

**Constant line to the pair:** *"Everybody heard it the first time."* — the dry, eye-rolling centre of the
whole pair. The radio says the number, the radio says it again, and nobody in the room needs telling.

**First five seconds:** the two voices arrive **already mid-figure**, as if the stack never stopped
overnight. No count-in, no downbeat — the record joins something in progress. **That is "still climbing,"
rendered in the first two bars rather than announced in a lyric.**

⭐ **THE LAST REMOVAL — and it is the best thing in this pair.** The gradation's final subtraction is
**the number itself.** V4's outro drops the count entirely, leaves the two voices alone, and **the figure
steps up one degree and stays there.** The count keeps climbing **after the song stops saying it.**
The number stops at the verified fact; the riff does not stop. ⛔ Nothing is added to achieve this — the
song ends with less in it than any other point in the record.

**Ends on:** the split to a fifth, unaccompanied, and then nothing. **No fifth chorus.** *Her sets famously
end one song early.*

## PRODUCTION SPEC — shared, and the render-risk defences are in it

- **132 BPM four-on-the-floor**, kick nudged **+12 ms late**, bass locked on the grid. At the break the
  floor collapses into a **165 BPM breakbeat** and the second half of the record runs there.
- **Vocals:** two women, close dynamic mic, **no de-esser on the breaths**, hard rule against transparent
  pitch correction, doubles misaligned 15–35 ms and ±6 cents. **Sing-speak, flat affect, no vibrato, no
  head voice, consonants dropped off line ends.** ⛔ Never sung-pretty.
- **The stack is DRY.** No plate, no hall, no tail on the two voices. The only wet thing on the record is
  the crowd bed (two room mics, 8–12 dB under instinct, high-passed 180 Hz, short 0.9–1.4 s plate).
- **Fuzz bass doubles the lower voice one octave down** — the hook's shadow, not a second idea.
- **Blown-out drop-D guitar stabs hard left, closed hats crisp right, tape hiss where a pad would be.**
  ⛔ There is no pad on this record. There is hiss.
- **No acoustic instruments** beyond breath on the capsule.
- **The Wolf:** one automation lane, a sub gliding up a semitone across the whole track. Never arrives.
- **Master −14 LUFS, ≥10 dB crest.** Loudness is not the drive; weight is.
- ⛔ No supersaw wall, no white-noise riser, no airhorn, no generic trap hats, no key change, no fade.
