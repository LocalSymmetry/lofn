⭐ **You cannot decode and surrender at the same time.**

### The four rules, binding on every song in this run
1. **DEPTH LIVES IN THE SITUATION, NEVER IN THE SENTENCE.** A listener will not decode a sentence; they will always **recognise a situation.** Name a position they have stood in, then let the lines stay plain and **continue the story instead of interpreting it.**
2. **ONE IDEA, REPEATED UNTIL INEVITABLE. ⛔ ONE device per song — not six.** If you cannot name the device in four words, it is more than one device.
3. **THE DEEP REFERENCE STAYS SUBVERSIVE.** Bright surface, sharp edge, never explained, never winked at. **A listener who takes only the surface must receive a whole song.**
4. **RETURN IS FREE — SPEND MORE OF IT.** Rhyme, refrain, chant, byte-identical choruses **lower** processing cost while raising richness. ⛔ "Simplify" never means "strip the repetition."

### The two gates — one per channel, both hard
- **THE FLAT TEST (words):** say the line out loud, flatly, no music, to a person's face. Survives = **simple**. Dies = merely **short**.
- **THE HUM TEST (music):** the song must be hummable **with no words at all.** The instrumental hook alone is recognisable and wants repeating, and it **exists before the first line and after the last.** ⛔ *A pad is not a riff. A texture is not a hook. If the vocal is the only tune, the song has failed.*

### ⚡ The binding condition, carried not resolved
> **Relocate the difficulty. Do not delete it. Keep the receipt.** If the harmony, riff and arrangement do not *actually* carry what the lyric used to, this is capitulation with a rationale attached. **This is MORE work, not less.**

## 🎛️ SOURCE 3 — THE FORM RULE
**F9, "Infinitesimal gradations," is on-doctrine and is the run's form law.** It looks like the opposite of simplicity and is in fact its engine: **the way you make a simple thing feel alive is a thousand tiny changes inside it.** One chord, one riff, one chant — and underneath, *nothing is ever quite the same twice.*

**In every song:** the repeated element (riff, chant, chorus) returns **byte-identical in the words** and **never identical in the performance** — a hair louder, one instrument added, the hi-hat opening a fraction more, the harmony arriving a bar earlier. **The listener hears "the same thing again" and feels it grow, and cannot say why.**

**This is exactly where the relocated difficulty goes** — into gradation the ear registers and the mind never audits. **Free channel, spent hard.**
