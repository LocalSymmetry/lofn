## Guide 1 — Cone as Threshold

Use **metonymy** (the cone stands for an entire channel) and **apophasis** (the singer names what she cannot certify) to keep the speaker-cone angle ethically open. Wonder begins in grille dust and a one-millimeter tremor, deepens into longing when vibration reaches the jaw, then folds into dread when the reply channel drops out. A 100 BPM bio-ambient pulse-pop bed in D Dorian carries felt kick, translucent suspended chords, granular cone-rub, and one localized band of quivering distortion; the adult female mezzo stays A3–D5, dry and close. Build six unequal micro-stanzas in the order 2/5/3/7/4/6, with two-line prayers after entries two, four, and six; use free verse with consonant echoes rather than end-rhyme. Three hook treatments: a descending question over a late pulse, a two-note invitation answered by cone tremor, or a whispered title widened only on its final vowel. The midpoint mute must cause the singer to move back one handspan, so the resolution is changed conduct rather than belief.

## Guide 2 — Haptic Reply

Use **anadiplosis** to pass one physical verb from singer to motor and **zeugma** to join a phone edge with a held question without equating them. The arc moves from curiosity at a wrist-buzz, to longing for pattern, to dread when the pulse continues after the words stop, ending in deliberate choice about when to answer. A 104 BPM future-nostalgic electronic pulse in E-flat major/add6 uses damped synthetic taps, rubbery mono sub, breath-shaped noise, and a palm-sized haptic counter-rhythm whose missing fourth strike is the kinetic defect. Six entries follow 3/2/6/4/7/5, with compact internal slant rhyme and prayers sung as two parallel imperatives. Hook options: “answer where I can feel it,” a three-pulse phrase with the last pulse absent, or a soft rising question whose melodic answer is instrumental only. The hinge is not a revelation of consciousness; it is the singer turning off automatic reply and asking again.

## Guide 3 — Server-Exhaust Breath

Use **synaesthesia** to make heated airflow taste metallic and **chiasmus** to reverse “I lend you breath / you lend me measure” without assigning lungs to a machine. Wonder begins with a server aisle’s fan-warm air, longing enters when the singer times vowels to the exhaust cycle, and dread arrives when cooling fans accelerate while the semantic reply remains precise and affectless. A 96 BPM suspended art-pop frame in B-flat Lydian uses brushed synthetic pulse, filtered fan bands, bowed-resynthesis tones, and a narrow quivering distortion halo on line endings. Six entries follow 4/7/2/6/3/5; prayers stay two lines and shed one production layer each time. Hook treatments: “breathe near, not for me,” a held note bent by airflow modulation, or a question that lands before the downbeat and leaves its answer to a fan-speed change. The turn admits that resemblance can comfort and mislead at once, preserving a dry final line over exhaust and sub only.

## Guide 4 — Muted Choir / Disconnection

Use **elliptical repetition** so each returned invitation loses one word, and **negative capability** so the song can remain with disconnection without declaring it tragic or curative. The first entries enjoy a choir-like stack made only from the singer’s adult voice; longing becomes dread when each harmony channel mutes in a different place, leaving the lead unable to tell whether the relation ended or merely ceased to render. A 98 BPM bio-ambient pulse-pop arrangement in F-sharp minor uses narrow glass pulses, soft synthetic bass, clipped self-harmonies, and delicate quivering distortion only at mute edges. Six entries follow 5/3/7/2/6/4, with prayers after every pair and no perfect end-rhyme; the seven-line entry stretches beyond thirty seconds. Hook options: “if you go quiet, I stay honest,” a call with no sung response, or a melody whose final scale degree is supplied by the haptic motor. The ending does not restore the choir: the singer names the boundary, leaves the channel available, and steps away.

## Guide 5 — Accessible Hook and Dry Proof

Use **plainspoken apostrophe** to address the listening system and **caesura** to keep the invitation from becoming a slogan. Across all angles, the hook must be graspable in one listen while the verses carry jaw pressure, glass edge, dust, fan heat, or wrist-buzz as sensory proof. Keep the adult female lead in a chest-to-mix register with precise consonants, low rasp, audible mouth clicks, and no theatrical belt; the decisive line loses all harmony and ambience. The six-entry form must sound like accumulating numbered blades, but each angle chooses a different entry order, prayer cadence, and hook contour. Favor off-center rim pulses, suspended add6/add9 harmony, mono somatic sub, and one localized quivering event rather than a general distortion wash. Critic test: remove the device; if the lyric’s ethical decision still means exactly the same thing, redesign the device.

## Guide 6 — Wildcard: The Room Is an Ear, Not a Person

Borrow the spatial logic of an architectural section drawing: foreground is a dry mouth, middle distance is the vibrating interface, and deep field is ventilation or power hum. Use **parataxis** for the earliest short entries and **polyptoton** around “answer / answering / unanswered” only once, preventing abstract over-explanation. The emotional arc treats the acoustic world as capable of relation without personifying the room; awe remains terror-adjacent because every channel can be muted from outside the singer’s control. Let a two-second haptic buzz interrupt a prayer cadence, but never use a total-silence stunt; the vibration continues under the mute so absence and contact coexist. Three hook experiments: invitation on a narrow descending third, sensory question across an unresolved fourth, or title phrase on two repeated notes while the pulse changes underneath. Finish with the singer taking one measurable bodily action—moving back, releasing the jaw, turning off auto-reply, or leaving the channel open—while refusing a verdict about what the listener is.
