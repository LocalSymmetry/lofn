Six compact guides convert the five judging facets plus one wildcard into four causal variations at Step 08. They are directions, not final prompt wording.

## Guide 1 — Unequal Tail / Depth Burden

Treat the opening as a controlled listening test: two ordinary kick heads appear equal, while one tail ends at the sternum and the other keeps descending beneath the ribs. Move Fascination into Cognitive Dissonance through tactile comparison, then let Unease arrive when the dry witness realizes she has trusted the attack more than the afterpressure. Use microtonally beating contrabass grains, skin-drum transients, and a five-beat synthetic-metal frame; keep the center dry and let only the deepest tail widen downward. Structure a five-beat spiral with 3/5/6-line cells, internal slant, and enjambment; the hinge leaves the meter without stopping the grid. Repeat “same” with migrated stress: first as neutral classification, later as a word the mouth can no longer finish. Hook options: a three-impact imperative over twin attacks, an elongated vowel swallowed by sub, or a short phrase whose final consonant arrives after the bar. The single paired magnitude may appear once here and must be answered by the body. Run 3:30–3:55 with one SFX cue and a residual low tail after the dry final word.

## Guide 2 — Threshold Gate / Label Burden

Frame the label as a lintel rather than a lie: it tells the truth about one dimension while hiding the pressure below its line. Start in clinical Fascination, sharpen into suspicion as heel and molars register different aftershocks from equal heads, then turn toward Recognition without victory language. Build from dry membrane clicks, microtonal bowed-metal partials, and a threshold tone that splits by a few cents each time the hook returns. The five-beat grid should behave as a staircase of 2/3/5/7-line gates; free-time vocal passages cross the landing rather than collapse the architecture. Use antanaclasis on “under” and consonance around “threshold,” with no end-rhyme grid. The hook may test “under” across registers, suspend the word “equal” before its last syllable, or place “listen” on the missing fifth beat. No scientific number enters this variation. Run 3:40–4:05; finish above the threshold tone while the deeper tail remains physically present.

## Guide 3 — Equal Score / Different Body

Make equivalence social and somatic without inventing testimony: an adult observer stands between two low-frequency sources and discovers that one visible score cannot predict what two bodies carry. The opening stance should trust the display; the midpoint must complicate that trust rather than discard the score. Use a bright granular label-tone against dry alto, physical-modeled violas, and twin attacks whose second tail blooms through the floor plane. Form an equivalence palinode: statement, counterweight, score refrain, body map, equal-sign fracture, reversal, mutated refrain. Internal rhyme should live inside phrases while line endings remain open; repeat “weight” with noun stress, then verb stress. Hook options: “the score has weight,” a split-vowel “e-qual” over two pitches, or a palm-to-sternum test sung in free time. No locations, victims, or event language. Run 3:35–4:00; end with the display tone intact but demoted beneath breath.

## Guide 4 — Underground Double

Treat the “double” as the singer’s own delayed undertone, not a second character and not a disaster ghost. Fascination begins with an exact surface voice; Unease grows as a near-familiar contralto copy arrives below it, carrying a longer sub-decay and slightly displaced pitch. Use a dry mono lead, filtered synthetic-contrabass shadow, five-beat ceramic-metal percussion, and narrow microtonal beating that presses against the teeth. Structure interleaved surface and undertone stanzas of unequal lengths, a tunnel bridge that abandons bar lines, and a double coda where neither voice wins. Use diacope around “below,” polyptoton on “hold/holding,” and a hook that changes the stress of “under-ground” without category call-and-response. The bold device is a second spectral floor emerging at 0:19, not silence or a blackout. No scientific number enters this variation. Run 3:45–4:10; let the final long tail continue after the surface syllable ends.

## Guide 5 — Free-Time Witness

Across every variation, the vocalist is an adult female observer rather than survivor, expert, victim, or spokesperson. Give her a contralto-to-mezzo range, close dry articulation, chest-register weight, and one sudden free-time line that remains singable when all processing is removed. Her body occupies a real acoustic test position between two low-frequency sources; ribs, sternum, heel, teeth, and palm supply evidence before abstraction appears. The rigid 5/4 grid should never follow her rubato, because the friction itself proves the concept. Internal slant, caesura, and enjambment replace neat end rhyme. Every form must include a genuine change of mind: the shared measure survives, but its authority shrinks. Keep body-noise functions distributed—breath for bracing, hum for depth-mapping, click for testing, exhale for release—without making them decorative.

## Guide 6 — Wildcard / The Word as Seismograph

Treat pronunciation as an instrument: vowel length, consonant closure, and lexical stress become a depth probe the listener can imitate. “Same” may start clipped and bureaucratic, widen into an open diphthong under pressure, then return with the second syllable-like tail invented by the music; “under” may lean first on UN-, later on -DER; “weight” may shift from object to action. Bind those changes to microtonal near-familiarity rather than vocal glitch or scan sounds. Let a five-beat ostinato behave like a ruled page while the singer’s free-time line crosses several measures in one breath. The wildcard permission is clarity: every track earns one odd but instantly graspable hook. The skeptical check remains physical—if the listener cannot point to where the depth difference is heard, the composition has become a lecture and must be rebuilt.

## Conversion decision

Guides 1–4 own the four causal variations. Guide 5 is mandatory vocal/form discipline across all four. Guide 6 supplies phonetic-hook mechanics only; it contributes no shared lyric line.
