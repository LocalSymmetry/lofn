## Guide 1 — Lovers' Near-Tuning / “Stay Near, Stay Clear”

**Story and arc.** Begin as a body report: two adult women at opposed microphones, upper teeth buzzing from a narrow interval. Use antiphonal testimony and semantic antanaclasis: “near” is answered by “clear,” changing intimacy into intimacy-with-boundary. Intimacy moves through unease into devotion when both singers stop trying to erase the beat frequency. The late turn admits that perfect blend had been used as proof of love; the final act is to remain audibly distinct.

**Sound and form.** Microtonal green-synth chamber leads; glitch-baroque clipped harpsichord keys provide the kinetic defect. Questions occupy 7/8, responses settle into 4/4, then the response lengths shrink as categories fail. Use close dry adult female contralto and mezzo, plucked synthetic gut-string, fluorescent reed pad, and twin harmonizers moving inward before stopping short. Runtime 4:05; sectional map: bite-tone intro → paired verse → Near/Clear refrain → category ledger I → hinge duet → category ledger II → unanswered coda.

**Rhyme and hook.** Verses use linked slant pairs with alternating long/short lines; refrains use one-phoneme near rhyme rather than couplet closure. Hooks: “Stay near / stay clear” over a bar-boundary displacement; “Keep the interval” on two held pitches; a wordless ee/ah dyad whose beating becomes the percussion. SFX is one brief partial-beat cue. The ending question receives only held breath, not a lyric.

## Guide 2 — Translation Lag / “One Beat Behind”

**Story and arc.** Two adult partners speak across one table; one understands a sentence a beat after the speaker's face changes. Use delayed echo as narrative causality and zeugma around “carry” — a phrase carries meaning and one person carries the delay. Unease becomes devotion when lag stops being treated as failure and becomes the interval in which the listener checks rather than assumes. The category chain progresses from word to tense to intention to permission to love, with no answer to the last noun.

**Sound and form.** At 102 BPM, 7/8 questions collide with 4/4 delayed answers. A clipped digital harpsichord states each category; prepared synthetic viola harmonics answer one eighth-note late. Twin adult female voices are left/right but never caricatured as cultural difference. The harmonizers approach at 2:18, stop short, and leave a slow comb-beat at the sternum. Runtime 4:15; map: tongue-click aperture → lag verse → delayed refrain → five category rooms → free-breath hinge → shortened replies → unanswered translation.

**Rhyme and hook.** Use enjambed internal slant rhyme with delayed terminal words; each response inherits one phoneme from the call but changes the ethical verb: “true” becomes “through.” Three hook routes: “Carry me through” with the answer one beat late; “A beat behind is still beside”; the same two-note motif arriving late but not lower. One SFX marks the harmonizers arresting before unison.

## Guide 3 — Name Pronunciation / “Say My Name Sideways”

**Story and arc.** An invented adult name passes through two mouths and lands differently because mouths have histories. Use apostrophe to the name and chiasmus: do not force the mouth to fit the name; let the name reveal the mouth. The sensory field is tongue against the ridge behind the teeth, a consonant that changes pressure, and the small heat of embarrassment. Devotion emerges when correction becomes invitation: “show me where the sound rests.”

**Sound and form.** A 104 BPM microtonal chamber lattice alternates 7/8 spoken-name calls and 4/4 sung responses. Pizzicato synthetic strings and clipped key-clicks render the consonant; green bowed-resonator pads carry the vowel. Twin harmonizers trace each name contour, approach unison during the hinge, and stop at the exact altered consonant. Runtime 3:55; map: isolated name → mouth-map verse → name refrain → category test → dry correction confession → invitation refrain → final unnamed question.

**Rhyme and hook.** Ordinary lines use pararhyme keyed to consonant families; chorus pairs “right/light” so the one-phoneme swap changes possession into gentleness. Hooks: “Say my name your way”; an antiphonal invented-name pair; “Right is not the only kind of listening.” The Hyper-Skeptic requires explicit responsive listening so pronunciation difference never excuses indifference. One SFX is a single key-click at the phoneme turn.

## Guide 4 — Distance Resonance / “The Far Note Holds”

**Story and arc.** Two adult women occupy separate rooms connected by vibration, not by fantasies of perfect presence. Use spatial metonymy — wall, wire, sternum — and negative capability: neither singer knows whether distance protects or starves the bond. The opening stance begs for closure; after the midpoint, the singer recognizes that “come close” can become control. The answer changes one phoneme toward “keep close,” an ongoing act rather than a demand to collapse space.

**Sound and form.** At 101 BPM, 7/8 calls move from hard-left dry voice to 4/4 answers hard-right with a long narrow-band resonance between them. Low synthetic viol, clipped digital harpsichord, muted violet pulse, and green harmonizer haze remain chamber-sized. At 2:12, both harmonizers narrow toward unison and halt; the beating partial becomes the only bridge. Runtime 4:20; map: two-room aperture → distance inventory → far-note refrain → category collapse → registerless hinge → resonance return → unanswered presence test.

**Rhyme and hook.** Use noncontiguous near-rhyme across speakers, never resolved inside one stanza. Hooks: “The far note holds”; “Come close / keep close”; wordless dyads whose difference tone is louder than either source. The final question “Present?” receives dry breathing from both rooms and no lexical answer. One SFX marks the resonant wire.

## Guide 5 — Wildcard / Grammar Losing Its Nouns

Treat relational categories as a grammar that progressively loses nouns while verbs remain: know, ask, wait, listen. Build 7/8 question cells from clipped synthetic consonants and let 4/4 answers shed semantic closure. The adult female duet remains close and responsive; the risk is excessive cleverness, so every formal loss must reveal tongue, jaw, or sternum pressure. Use mesodiplosis and consonance, no neat end rhyme. The final category is replaced by a held vowel that stops short of matching pitch.

The first singer treats the gap as lack; the second hears it as load-bearing difference; the turn admits both are partly right. Alternate asymmetric and common meter without smoothing the join. Let digital-harpsichord attacks disappear one by one as categories fail, but keep both breaths present through the last unanswered prompt.
