## Golden Seed anchors chosen before writing

### Primary — “Nail an Obscure Emotion”

Preserve:

- one precise emotional target per pair rather than six songs named by one broad mood;
- immediate, danceable or bodily hook pressure without reducing the song to a trend object;
- risk through transitions, form, and production behavior—not a wall of decorative glitches;
- each song must make the listener want a second play because the hook and movement hold, not because the premise requires explanation.

Adapt:

- the six emotional targets are bound to today’s CONTENT/SONIC/MATERIAL tri-source;
- the set is split 3 NEWS / 3 EXISTENCE and 3 ACCESSIBLE / 3 AMBITIOUS at assignment time;
- each pair gets a different formal translation of the legal-diagram source, preventing shared scaffolds.

### Secondary — “Straightening Our Spines”

Preserve:

- mundane system noise becomes moral and bodily rhythm;
- the female lead changes stance in real time rather than announcing the final thesis at line one;
- one sterile/administrative element becomes an unforgettable acoustic device;
- Indignation remains physical and specific without collapsing into generic rage.

Adapt:

- no lab-coat/battle-armor, pen/refusal, blackout-drop, or “No, YOU move” copying;
- today’s systems are invoice, scan pane, magnitude label, task timer, tuning grid, and mute icon;
- pleasure may coexist with anger, but no attack/war report becomes a dance payoff.

### Structural archetype support

- ACCESSIBLE slots may draw separately from **THE MEASUREMENT**, **THE ARRIVAL**, and **THE CATALOG**, never one archetype for all three.
- AMBITIOUS slots may use SWITCHBOARD logic only once; the other two require original architecture.
- No pair may use THE LAST WITNESS on a recent real harmed person; the Human Subject Standard forbids that route.

## Why this lineage fits today

Today’s inputs repeatedly put living signals inside grids: medical billing around rescue, scanning around private speech, earthquake magnitude labels over unequal depths, Hantavirus proteins arranged in square tiles, and a legal-journal cover where numbered grass grows out of a case ledger. The winning move is not “systems bad.” It is the stranger and more human truth: a system can record a pulse without exhausting what the pulse means.
