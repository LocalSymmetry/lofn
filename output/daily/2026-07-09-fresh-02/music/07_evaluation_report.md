## Sequential panel process

### Panel 1 — Concept

The six concept constructs tested whether each song moved beyond “people are more than numbers.” Their main disagreement concerned portable hooks: one side preferred clean principles, while the Hyper-Skeptic rejected any principle not paid for by a bodily consequence. The ruling favored songs whose hook changed what a system may do rather than merely naming a moral. **Direction:** keep six different costly consequences and six different standing bodies.

### Panel 2 — Medium

The six medium constructs removed scores from any package where the signature device could be unplugged without changing meaning. They rewarded meter break, mute, downbeat circulation, register exchange, unequal sub-decay, and near-unison only when the event was structurally irreversible. **Direction:** technique earns points as dramaturgy, never as novelty inventory.

### Panel 3 — Context and audience

The audience panel tested one-minute recall, ethical framing, and short-clip survival. It protected clear accessible hooks while refusing to penalize the ambitious arm for cognitive difficulty that carried real artistic identity. The Hyper-Skeptic forced a human-subject recheck on both news-derived harms and rejected any candidate that needed victim testimony. **Direction:** use dignity and curiosity as the entry, never vulnerable lives as atmosphere.

### Transformations

- Group transformation — **FOCUS:** score only the load-bearing difference between siblings; polish shared by all four cannot decide the pair.
- Skeptic transformation — **REFLECT:** ask what fails if the song’s central claim is accurate but incomplete, not villainously false.
- Surviving synthesis: the winner in each pair makes its formal defect the listener’s physical proof.
