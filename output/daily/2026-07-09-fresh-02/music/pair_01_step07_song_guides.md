## Guide 1 — Arrival, Then Price

Use dramatic irony and delayed naming: the wrist, adhesive, idling fan, and curb arrive before the paper's purpose is spoken. Apprehension should become gratitude, then indignation, then a dignity that does not erase the first gratitude. A clean green-synth bass, glass FM chords, dry kick, and two brittle thermal clicks establish a synthetic civic space. Unequal 5/7/4 verse cells alternate with a six-line refrain; the first hook line overruns the bar and lands late. Favor consonance around `r`, `d`, and `v`, with slant rhyme only at cell endings. Hook options: a late landing on “Let arrival mean arrived,” a backing-vocal pickup that the lead outruns, or the band dropping beat four so the final stress makes the new downbeat. The printer's rhythm phase-locks to the wrist only at the defiant hinge, then disappears. End with the speaker rubbing the pale square under the strip.

## Guide 2 — Inventory Error Without Error Language

Use metonymy and antanaclasis: “item” belongs to the paper, never the person; “count” changes from useful verb to overreaching authority. The singer remains beside the vehicle, physically upright, with cold air on newly bare wrist skin. Keep the accessible pulse at 114 BPM but make the verses more clipped, using 4/5/7 cells before a long, meter-crossing principle line. Translucent voltage-organ chords and clipped synthetic hand percussion leave space for a chest-to-mix adult female lead. The receipt clicks form tidy triplets until the hinge, where one skipped click lets the pulse lead. Hook options: “Keep every pulse off the shelf,” “The hand comes home as a hand,” or “Name what helped, not what I am.” Use zeugma once, pairing a literal label with a social consequence, but never call the body an invoice. End on a hand closing around the removed adhesive, making disposal—not denial—the act.

## Guide 3 — Paper Outlives the Siren

Use temporal braid and object permanence: the audible emergency ends while thermal paper keeps feeding. Gratitude is present tense; the paper's afterlife creates apprehension and slow indignation. Lower the pulse to 110 BPM and widen the harmony only when the singer distinguishes care from collection. Short 7/4/5 cells should give way to a refrain whose longest line crosses the bar; internal rhyme stays sparse and end rhyme is avoided. Use pale FM bells, a rounded synthetic sub, fan-like filtered noise, and receipt rasp heard from changing distances. Hook options: “Let help finish helping,” “Leave the door open after the siren,” or “The rescue ends with me standing.” The hinge makes delayed printer echoes converge with the current wrist pulse, proving that paper time and body time are not the same. End with the vehicle gone, one square of wrist skin cooling in ordinary air.

## Guide 4 — Mutual-Aid Revaluation

Use chiasmus and cumulative syntax: “the ride carries the body / the body carries the meaning” may guide structure but must not appear as a slogan. The singer's perspective expands only after individual bodily authority is established; any `we` is earned by visible anonymous hands passing a plain envelope without names or biography. At 112 BPM, use radiant green FM plucks, elastic synthetic bass, dry handclaps, and one restrained receipt transient per stanza. Let 5/7/4 cells accumulate into an affirmative refrain with a late final stress. Hook options: “Make the door a way home,” “Let every arrival arrive,” or “Keep the road open both ways.” At the hinge, many asynchronous hand taps settle around the singer's pulse while the receipt clicks fall out, reversing who organizes time. The ending is not charity triumph; it is a practical public rule embodied by an open hand and an unlocked threshold.

## Guide 5 — Vocal Truth / Dry-Line Test

Treat the lead as documentary without testimony: adult female alto-to-mezzo, A3–D5, close enough to hear adhesive tug and breath reset, but never coded as helpless. Use interrupted anaphora, where three lines approach the same claim and the fourth changes the verb. Arrange mono 5/7/4 cells against wider refrains, reserving stacked self-harmony for earned dignity. The receipt may clatter, yet every variation must survive an a cappella read of its decisive line. Hook options should use open vowels and four strong stresses; the final stress arrives after the expected bar without sounding musically mistaken. Use breath, `mm`, a dry tongue click, and a released exhale as body noises with distinct functions. If removing the receipt-pulse hinge leaves the moral turn intact, redesign the hinge rather than decorating it.

## Guide 6 — Wildcard: Civic Organism

Imagine the rescue route as a public circulatory score, not as a body metaphor pasted onto billing. Organic protrusion from the legal diagram becomes one melodic line that exceeds its measure; the grid remains audible but no longer governs the landing. Use additive phrase lengths, a single harmonic brightening from E Mixolydian toward A major color, and clattering transients that feel like dry seeds before they become pulse-adjacent. Keep all imagery at wrist, curb, paper, door, road, and anonymous hands. Three possible hook treatments: the lead sustains across a missing kick, backing voices enter one beat too early and are corrected by the lead, or the bass delays its tonic until the late word. The ending must alter authority rather than erase cost. Hyper-Skeptic check: if “public promise” could be swapped for any cause, return to adhesive, serration, idling fan, and the exact act of arrival.

## Panel synthesis

The panel favored Guide 1's emotional clarity, Guide 3's temporal distinction, Guide 4's consequential act, and Guide 5's dry-line standard. The Hyper-Skeptic rejected “the body is not inventory” as pre-solved rhetoric. Moderator ruling: four songs retain separate causal engines and section maps while sharing only the assigned receipt-to-pulse law and meter-breaking public hook.
