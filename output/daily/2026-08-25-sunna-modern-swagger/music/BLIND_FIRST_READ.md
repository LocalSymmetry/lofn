# Blind-First Read — Selected Six

**Read state:** written after blind calibration and identity reveal, but before research, panel, selection, prior-QA, credit-file, or run-history review.  
**Scope:** only the six current `music/selected/*.md` delivery files.

## Portfolio first impression

All six communicate an event rather than merely a style: an object or gap creates a wager; another adult answers with an action or changed phrase; the answer alters one non-bass musical dimension; Sunna then names a specific future want. The hooks are distinct and immediately retrievable. No selected song reads as a one-note emotional corpse on first pass, and every central motif changes meaning before the end.

The portfolio does carry a visible house skeleton that final QA must interrogate rather than excuse: **body/object hook at zero → missing beat/slot/note → one adult reply → one non-bass consequence over firewalled mono sub → exact chorus → future appetite → hard one-bar receipt.** The six consequences are audibly different on paper, but the dramatic grammar repeats often enough to be a substantive Somatic flag.

## 1. Watch Me Begin — P06 / AMBITIOUS / EXISTENCE

**Event communicated:** A flattering `CLASSIC ENCORE` placard tries to declare Sunna a revival. She tears off `ENCORE`, keeps `BEGIN`, and passes the strip to an adult. The adult answers “Begin with me,” the spectrum reopens top-down, the current clipped chassis restarts, and Sunna turns refusal into a mutual first-night invitation.

**Somatic read:** Strong. The hook is immediate, the placard is physically legible, and “Not proof that I will last / Just appetite, not past” supplies a genuine second emotional movement from indignation to vulnerable hope. `ENCORE` changes from praise to trap to discarded object; `BEGIN` changes from private refusal to reciprocal invitation. No named-corpse condition fires.

**Flags:** “Begin wears the high shine / With warms the middle line / Me brings back the floor” risks production-explainer lyric, though the adult action remains clear without it. House skeleton is visible in the missing beat, adult answer, isolated consequence, exact chorus, and latch receipt. The first-night stakes and torn-label event keep it from feeling interchangeable.

## 2. Answer Me Faster — P02 / ACCESSIBLE / EXISTENCE

**Event communicated:** At a copy counter, Sunna issues fast written commands to an adult rival. The rival returns questions with increasing agency, folds and tears the ruby receipt across the missing fourth clap, then writes the title as a question first. Sunna accepts the verb being bent and asks for another round tomorrow.

**Somatic read:** Strong with one caution. “Answer me faster / Question me faster” is a memorable reciprocal mutation, and the rival’s movement from shadow to equal depth changes the relationship rather than decorating it. The honest hinge—“I do not want my echo / I want your wild advice”—adds a second movement beneath the flirtatious game. The title, paper, and question motif transform coherently.

**Flags:** The 4/3/2/1 apparatus and repeated `I write:` construction sometimes make the lyric sound like a rule demonstration. “New nerve, new wager” is house diction rather than a discovered phrase. The missing-clap seam, independent sub, exact chorus, tomorrow appetite, and printer stop reproduce the shared portfolio grammar; the genuine command-to-question change is the song’s defense.

## 3. Make Our Move — P03 / ACCESSIBLE / EXISTENCE

**Event communicated:** In an empty rehearsal room, Sunna sends “make one move” across a tape line and asks for an adult’s changed reply rather than a copy. The adult returns “make our move”; `/aʊr/` broadens the offbeat stab, the chorus reorganizes into 3 / 2+1 call-and-answer, and the pair resend toward tomorrow.

**Somatic read:** Passes first read but is the portfolio’s most exposed piece. “I want yours, not mine twice” is excellent, the one→our mutation is instantly graspable, and the permanent beat-one vacancy gives the hook bodily identity. The adult’s choice changes the title and relationship. Yet the emotional world remains intentionally small, and several lines count the mechanism rather than deepen the wager.

**Flags:** “Our opens round / The broad stab blooms round” and “Three from me / Two, then one from you” verge on production/process exposition. The blue tape and counting language never develop as richly as the stronger object ladders. House-pattern exposure is high: vacancy, adult return, timbre consequence, bass firewall, exact chorus, future resend, receipt. Final Somatic review should ask whether lightness is skilled frivolity or merely low stakes.

## 4. Late Suits You — P04 / AMBITIOUS / NEWS

**Event communicated:** At a cold planetarium console, Sunna deliberately keeps the fourth lamp dark and sends three tightening message cards. A false machine flash arrives; then the adult finally says `AGAIN`, spending the one withheld rave-piano chord and two ghost pulses. The exact chorus lands, and Sunna asks for the same chosen-delay game next week.

**Somatic read:** Strong. The title is compact and adoptable, chosen delay is an unusual adult desire, and the false-arrival/mouth-arrival distinction creates real suspense. The movement from fear of blankness to appetite for another delay is a second movement, not reassurance. The dark slot changes from uncertainty to chosen space.

**Flags:** “One bright chord answers: AGAIN / Two ghost pulses bow” and “My ribs receive the boom” risk allowing production consequence to narrate the feeling. The four-card structure and answer-triggered seam visibly share the house grammar. Still, the false countdown and desire for chosen lateness make the event distinct from the other five.

## 5. Stay for the Whole — P01 / ACCESSIBLE / NEWS

**Event communicated:** Four rooftop queue tickets are assembled into an eclipse circle while Sunna fears being mistaken for her bright edge. An adult takes the last blue arc, completes the circle, and promises to wait for all of her through the black center. The promise becomes a stacked pickup into an exact chorus; after the eclipse, Sunna asks the adult to stay for the ordinary roof and ordinary lips.

**Somatic read:** Very strong. The title is one-breath adoptable, the eclipse is comprehensible without research, and “Do not love me by the crescent / Come want the whole thing too” turns cosmic image into personal stakes. The post-eclipse vending lights and ordinary lips create the clearest second movement in the set. Ticket, circle, crescent, and whole all transform rather than recur inertly.

**Flags:** “I want you underneath” is momentarily ambiguous, and the color/list/count passages show construction seams. It still shares the standard gap→adult action→harmonic consequence→exact chorus→future→rail receipt architecture. Its defense is that the answer changes the singer’s fear and the ordinary aftermath, not merely the mix.

## 6. Name Me After I Arrive — P05 / AMBITIOUS / EXISTENCE

**Event communicated:** At a beauty-supply mirror wall, Sunna rejects printed future labels and states a modest real want: reserve a back-room studio at dawn on June third for one first client. An adult stamps only the date `RESERVED` and returns the naming to her. Sunna signs in green; her own action releases the withheld fifth-note crown, and the unchanged exact chorus returns as authored arrival.

**Somatic read:** Excellent. “Name me after I arrive” is the strongest title proposition in the six, and the material logistics—chair, towel, rent, buses, first client—make hope specific without turning it into reassurance. The adult answers the booking but does not define Sunna; Sunna authors the musical crown herself. Printed label → chosen line → reserved date → green signature is a complete transformation ladder.

**Flags:** The exact chorus appears three times and carries heavy load, but its meaning genuinely changes after `RESERVED` and the signature, so this is deliberate return rather than collapse. The shared house grammar remains visible, especially the held note, adult action, firewalled sub, exact chorus, and printer receipt. This song partly breaks it by separating the adult’s answer from Sunna’s self-authored consequence.

## First-read Somatic / house-pattern findings to carry forward

1. **No one-note arc:** all six have a second movement; the weakest is P03, whose movement is reciprocity but whose stakes remain small.
2. **No inert motif:** ENCORE→BEGIN, command→question, one→our, blank→chosen late, crescent→whole, and labels→signed arrival all transform.
3. **No repeated-line collapse:** exact choruses function as intentional returns. P05’s three returns deserve scrutiny but not an automatic flag.
4. **Substantive house-pattern flag:** all six use nearly the same causal scaffold. Distinct consequence classes may satisfy portfolio engineering while still sounding authored by one template; final QA must compare the raw/Step-10 portfolio and soundcraft evidence before clearing this.
5. **Production-explainer lyric flag:** P03 is the clearest case; P04 and P06 contain smaller instances. The songs remain legible if those lines are ignored, but their presence is a Somatic risk.
6. **First-person/hope:** all six make Sunna the subject and name a future appetite. This is strongest where the want becomes ordinary and material (P01, P05, P06), not where the production mechanism is the main event.
