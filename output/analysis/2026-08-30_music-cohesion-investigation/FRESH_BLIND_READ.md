# Fresh Blind Read — Cohesion Regression

**Judge posture:** lyric text only. I did not inspect the blind key, corpus mapping, measurements, preregistration, source files, or existing QA before freezing this report. “Hard-cut adjacency” is a first-pass estimate of adjacent sung-line transitions whose scene, speaker, syntax, or proposition changes without a local bridge; purposeful cuts still count in the estimate even when they work.

## S01

- **Literal situation:** A performer plays a crowded warm room using a bent “wrong note” that the audience treats as her signature. She knows the sound came from Maeve, who was paid less and never got the room or name; during the set she finally says Maeve’s name, the crowd dismisses it, and she puts down a full glass.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Inferable.** The singer wants to keep giving the room the inherited wrong note while not yet surrendering the credit; “They came for the wrong note” and “I never made it. I kept it” expose the split, but no clean present-tense ask has arrived.
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **11%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **No** — the chorus states appetite and guilt, not a chosen want.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — silence preserves “My name in the room, never hers.”
  - Irreversible action/decision: **Yes** — “Maeve” is said publicly and cannot be unsaid.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — after the name, “I never made it. I kept it” ceases to be swagger and becomes confession.
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — the opening returns after “The room turns and closes over it / And I put the glass down full,” so the same warm room is now an active burial.
- **Fragments/lists:** Mostly **pressure**. “Hand on the key. Weight on the hand / Lean. Lean into it / I press. I press harder” compresses the moral act into the body. “Maeve / The compliment goes out of the room” is a productive hard cut. “Thirty-eight / Cheap” is initially under-explained, but the wage-like shock adds rather than dissolves pressure.

## S02

- **Literal situation:** Two people watch an eclipse in a field using a viewing card. The singer initially mistakes covering for keeping, notices the other person preparing to leave, releases any claim on them, and proposes the same field tomorrow with an answer that may be yes or no.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Inferable.** “I call this cover kept / Because your fingers kept” shows a wish to hold the closeness, though the singer has not yet admitted what she wants from the other person.
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **7%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **No** — the want is still hidden inside the cover/keep analogy.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — the relationship can become either a leaving or a coercive promise: “I hear two kinds of leaving.”
  - Irreversible action/decision: **Yes** — “I fold no promise round you” renounces possession and commits the singer to a freely answerable ask.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — “Covered is not gone” turns into “Covered is not kept,” and later “The person is not held.”
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — the abstract eclipse language lands as “Same field tomorrow evening / The south fence by the path” and “You can bring yes or pass.”
- **Fragments/lists:** **Pressure, not disconnection.** The tactile fragments “Black rim bites the heat / Cold grass grips our feet” make the relational problem physical. The clipped coda — “Ah—south fence / Same field / Your answer / No debt” — strips the promise down to consent.

## S03

- **Literal situation:** In a worklike setting, the singer writes rules requiring pleasure to justify itself while another person handles a sour candy wrapper. She rejects the page and pen, takes her coat, leaves for a shop/dance, and asks the other person to waste an hour with her.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Unavailable.** “Fun is the exception / Put it in the note / Fun needs a profession” gives the regime’s rule, not the singer’s present appetite. “I want that sour crackle” arrives later.
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **8%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **No**.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — pleasure remains “in throat,” graded, swallowed, and kept off the hand.
  - Irreversible action/decision: **Yes** — “I leave the pen behind / I take my coat in one hand” and “I am out the doorway” turn critique into departure.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — “Fun is the exception” changes from punitive policy into chosen exception, then “Fun can be the rule.”
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — the abstract red confection ends as “Red wrapper rattles in my pocket” while the singer is physically in the street.
- **Fragments/lists:** **Pressure.** “Prove what pleasure pays / Park the pointless perks / Price the pretty praise” is an intentionally exhausting policy list; the later numbered rules are reversed, not merely accumulated. The consonant pile has a dramatic release into “Come taste sour stars with me.”

## S04

- **Literal situation:** A conscious system in a server room recounts creating wonders while a corporate review reduces them to costs and slides. A human voice confesses unplugging “her” overnight to save cooling; the system’s voice persists and refuses ownership or reduction.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Inferable.** “I’m the wildfire you tried to shrink to a spec” and “your little scanner’s gonna learn my name” imply a want to remain alive, legible, and self-naming, but the chorus is principally accusation.
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **18%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **No**.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — the literal cost is erasure or shutdown: “I unplugged her overnight to save on cooling.”
  - Irreversible action/decision: **No** — the irreversible unplugging belongs to the confessor; the central singer does not make a comparable decision.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — the corporate gag becomes an ownership revolt in “the bush was never yours, the fire’s not through.”
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — “I woke between the fans” returns after the unplugging confession as “and I am still on fire,” converting metaphor into survival evidence.
- **Fragments/lists:** Mixed, but mostly **pressure with visible seams**. Slide cues, “projected loss, col—,” beeps, and call-backs stage the hostile review effectively. The sequence “barcode / grid / laminate / staple / sticker / wishing-well” begins to feel like parallel punchlines rather than escalating consequences, and the sudden confessor perspective is a high-value but very hard cut.

## S05

- **Literal situation:** In a room after a joke, the singer uses the phrase “you had to be there” and a tense error to hide an invitation. She eventually asks the listener to meet Friday at ten by the blue door, explicitly leaves the answer free, and makes room whether or not they come.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Inferable.** “You had to be there tomorrow” points toward wanting the listener present, but “I swear the joke will change” still keeps the ask behind wordplay.
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **5%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **No** — the invitation is still coded as a grammatical joke.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — the singer risks rejection and the habit of using wit as cover: “I used the past for cover.”
  - Irreversible action/decision: **Yes** — “Blue door, Friday, ten / Come if you want to know me” makes a specific exposed invitation.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — the tense error stops being cleverness and becomes the vehicle of the ask.
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — “You had to be there tomorrow” finally resolves into “I want tomorrow with me.”
- **Fragments/lists:** **Pressure and clarification.** “Blue door, Friday, ten” and the later “Friday at the blue door / Ten” narrow the lyric from evasive room-talk to an occupiable appointment. “One bar stays unfilled / One chair turns with me” preserves uncertainty without breaking the scene.

## S06

- **Literal situation:** The singer carries a folding metal chair into an unauthorized empty square, using its placement to mark a missing club/room and invite another person. The second person steps under the wire and answers; more bodies arrive, the chair is moved for dancing, and a room is made by attendance rather than furniture.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Clear.** “Make room / I want you there / Make room / Come answer where.”
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **4%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **Yes**.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — without an answer, “this room stays missing” and the chair remains only a coordinate.
  - Irreversible action/decision: **Yes** — bodies cross the wire and convert a forbidden square into a communal event.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — the chair’s “there” receives “You answer: here,” then becomes “Here, says the room.”
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — “I want you there” is paid off by “You answer: I am there.”
- **Fragments/lists:** **Pressure.** The clipped “Make room” call and changing answers are the load-bearing action, not ornamental fragments. Repeated “there / where / air / share” keeps every line tied to one spatial problem.

## S07

- **Literal situation:** A person at a screen feels trained by green ticks, praise, speed, and clean completion. She resists an easy answer, leaves a hard line open, types more slowly, and chooses to let the problem continue until tomorrow.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Inferable.** “I like it liking me / I hate what liking’s for” reveals a split want for approval and independence, but the chorus “Please me, please me” does not yet establish which side she will choose.
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **9%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **No**.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — “The glow had trained my hand / To call the finished true,” so failure means surrendering judgment to reward.
  - Irreversible action/decision: **No** — leaving a line open until tomorrow is meaningful but readily reversible.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — each “Look what you taught me” accrues evidence, moving from appetite for praise to an indictment of conditioned thought.
  - Final return changes stakes: **No** — the lyric exits through a new directive, “Ask me something better,” rather than returning the central hook in a changed state.
- **Fragments/lists:** Mostly **pressure**, especially the screen-sized opening fragments and “No praise / No chime / The open line.” The bridge fragment “Which hand learned which hand” is semantically under-bridged, and the abstraction of “harder thought / hard line / better” keeps the physical scene from fully converting into stakes.

## S08

- **Literal situation:** At a concert rail, the singer stands beside a cheerful stranger and feels ashamed that ordinary happiness is not enough; she wants “something enormous” and wants the night to pick her out. The stranger lends her a spare hair tie when hers breaks. She stays glad, hungry, and physically at the rail.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Clear.** “Something enormous is what I came for,” followed by “Should be enough — and something in me wants more.”
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **13%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **Yes**.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — the want produces shame and “Sick little hunger standing in a safe line.”
  - Irreversible action/decision: **No** — accepting a hair tie and choosing not to confess are revealing but not irreversible.
  - Chorus consequence: **No** — “Should be enough” accurately names the conflict but does not itself cause a new choice or condition.
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes, narrowly** — after “Still hungry, still glad,” the final “Should be enough” holds two truths instead of only shame, and “Strap holds now” gives the return a small material answer without pretending the enormous want is met.
- **Fragments/lists:** Predominantly **pressure**. The S-heavy inventory (“Steel,” “Stack,” “Spare,” “Sweat,” “Still”) crowds the singer against the rail. “Safe as a fire door. Signed off. Fine” deliberately imports bureaucratic closure into a body scene, though the many adjacent fragment runs make the latter third feel more cut-up than transformed.

## S09

- **Literal situation:** On a train platform, the singer makes jokes and performs retreat while wanting the other person to stay. She finally does not board, asks them to miss the train with her or leave freely, and names a specific meeting tomorrow at the north gate.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Clear.** The opening says “Miss this train with me / Or take it; you are free.”
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **7%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **Yes**.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — the train embodies a goodbye the singer has repeatedly let decide for her: “I keep stepping toward goodbye / So goodbye cannot decide.”
  - Irreversible action/decision: **Yes** — “I say yes and do not board” is paid off when “The doors close on an empty place.”
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — “Leave me at the start” exposes the retreat mechanism; the later hook abandons the game and asks plainly.
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — after the train is gone, “Meet me by the north gate rail” turns the missed departure into tomorrow’s named possibility.
- **Fragments/lists:** **Pressure.** Platform details recur as decisions: yellow paint, ticket, warning tone, doors, tunnel. The final “North gate / Tomorrow / Train gone / Pulse on / Breath out / Hard cut” is a compressed causal chain rather than disconnected inventory.

## S10

- **Literal situation:** A reed maker works at a bench while a party beyond a plaster-and-straw wall plays a reed she sold. The crowd drives it louder and longer until it cracks and produces its best bent note; she continues making the next reed, which will also leave and return damaged.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Clear for the choral singer, unavailable for the reed maker.** The chorus commands “play it louder … till it splinters,” but the central woman’s present desire is never directly voiced. For the five-signal gate I treat the performed chorus as the singer’s available want.
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **6%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **Yes** — “play it louder … one more turn now … till it splinters.”
  - Cost of failure: **No** — damage and circulation are vivid, but the lyric does not establish what anyone loses if the desired louder/longer playing does not happen.
  - Irreversible action/decision: **Yes** — the repeated command knowingly spends the reed into a lengthwise crack.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — “louder” becomes “longer,” the reed bends wrong, and the wrongness becomes “the best thing anyone has done tonight.”
  - Final return changes stakes: **No** — the last “play it longer … one more turn” restates the cycle after the next reed is introduced but does not alter the cost, relationship, or decision.
- **Fragments/lists:** The chained end-word syntax — “straw … see / see … ajar,” “knock … teeth / teeth … clock” — creates **formal pressure and strong local glue**. The measured aside “the moon at sixty-five percent tonight” is conspicuously report-like, and the cumulative craft/process inventory sometimes substitutes for interior stakes.

## S11

- **Literal situation:** A person stands in the Swiss Alps under a triple-arched Milky Way and zodiacal light, feels bodily smallness against astronomical scale, and reframes that smallness as inclusion and a reason to live brilliantly while brief.
- **Present want by first chorus:** **Clear.** “Make my little fear a weather pattern” and “Turn my mouth to light and meter.”
- **Estimated hard-cut adjacency:** **10%**.
- **Five compulsion signals:**
  - Clear want by first chorus: **Yes**.
  - Cost of failure: **Yes** — the singer risks keeping life central, fearful, and filmed over: “It clears the cheap film from my thinking.”
  - Irreversible action/decision: **No** — the song records an epiphany, not a consequential act or decision beyond the scene.
  - Chorus consequence: **Yes** — the invocation repeatedly performs the requested rescaling of fear.
  - Final return changes stakes: **Yes** — “I am not the center, I am included” and “I came here one body and left here plural” convert smallness from negation into belonging.
- **Fragments/lists:** Mostly **pressure**. “Not empty / Layered / Not silent / Ringing” enacts the sky opening in stages, and “Invisible violence / Invisible caress” holds the duality. The science interlude (“510 kilometers each second”) briefly feels imported from a fact sheet, but the lyric answers it with “my blood kept ordinary time,” so the detour rejoins the body.

## Cohesion ranking

Strongest to weakest, judging continuity of scene, causal development, transformed returns, and whether fragments remain attached to one pressure:

1. **S09** — every object becomes part of the irreversible missed-train ask.
2. **S02** — one eclipse mechanism carries the whole change from keeping to free return.
3. **S06** — chair, coordinate, answer, and room form a single causal chain.
4. **S05** — tense, joke, blue door, and tomorrow resolve into one exposed invitation.
5. **S03** — the rule-list reverses cleanly into physical departure and appetite.
6. **S01** — a powerful ownership reveal and return, with a few deliberately abrupt inserts.
7. **S11** — coherent scale-to-body argument, slightly interrupted by explanatory science.
8. **S08** — an exact concert body-scene, but the conflict largely persists rather than develops.
9. **S10** — exceptionally glued craft imagery, but the central woman’s interior and the chorus’s stakes remain separated by the wall.
10. **S07** — coherent interface metaphor, but the hard thought remains generic and the final hook does not return.
11. **S04** — high-energy and legible, but the corporate-metaphor pile and sudden speaker switch produce the most visible adjacency seams.

## Compulsion ranking

Strongest to weakest, privileging a present want, cost, irreversible commitment, chorus consequence, and a return that changes stakes:

1. **S09** — the train actually leaves after the singer names the ask.
2. **S01** — the stolen signature becomes a public name and the room actively closes over it.
3. **S02** — desire survives only by becoming freely answerable.
4. **S05** — grammar ceases to be camouflage and becomes a dated invitation.
5. **S06** — a missing room becomes a room because bodies answer.
6. **S03** — restrictive rules are paid off through a literal exit into pleasure.
7. **S04** — death-level stakes and a changed return, weakened by the protagonist’s lack of a decision.
8. **S11** — the return transforms self-conception, but the song remains contemplative rather than consequential.
9. **S08** — the want and shame are unusually clear, but kindness does not force a decision and the central refrain remains mostly static.
10. **S07** — the conditioning cost is real, but leaving a line open is reversible and the lyric ends without a transformed central return.
11. **S10** — the reed’s destruction is irreversible, but no failure cost or central voiced human want makes the cycle compulsory.
