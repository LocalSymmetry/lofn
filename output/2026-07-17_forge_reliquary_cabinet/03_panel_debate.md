## CONCEPT PANEL (5 + Skeptic)

**THE LIT WINDOW** (after Agnes Pelton, 1881–1961 — Transcendental Painting Group; luminous abstractions on dark grounds)
GROUNDING: the TPG corpus; light as inner state, not illumination.
"Working from the Transcendental corpus — the shelf must not be *lit*; it must *emit*. A window at night pulls because the light is evidence of life inside. Every vessel is a window. If the glow reads as backlight-LED, we have painted a vending machine."

**THE VESSEL POET** (after Gaston Bachelard, 1884–1962 — *The Poetics of Space*; drawers, chests, shells, the miniature)
"The miniature is not a small thing — it is a *concentrated* thing. 'Intimate immensity': the inside must feel larger than the outside. The casket lid ajar is the whole poem: an invitation to a space bigger than its container. The shelf is a wall of thresholds."

**THE CABINET KEEPER** (after Athanasius Kircher, 1602–1680 — the Wunderkammer; the museum of wonder)
"A cabinet of curiosities works by *variety under one order* — sixty marvels, one wall. Danger: our vessels must differ (orb, phial, cage) or the wall reads as a warehouse of identical products. Same family, never the same silhouette twice in a row."

**THE TOY PHILOSOPHER** (after Roger Caillois, 1913–1978 — *Man, Play and Games*)
"Wait — everyone is building a *church*. These are TOYS. Caillois's categories warn: sacred framing without play collapses into solemnity. The gold must be *mischievous* somewhere — a vessel that rattles, a seam that winks. Reverence that forbids touching is the death of an arcade."

**THE GOLD MENDER** (after Christy Bartlett — *Flickwerk: The Aesthetics of Mended Japanese Ceramics*)
"The mended-ware corpus says the repair is *biography* — the crack records the life. Extrapolating to our fiction: the kintsugi seams must imply PLAY as the source of the cracks. A mint vessel is an unplayed game; the gold says 'many hands were here, and it mattered.' That is warmth no gradient can fake."

**THE SHELF CYNIC — Hyper-Skeptic** (after Donald Norman — *The Design of Everyday Things*; documented critique of decoration that taxes usability)
"Actually — stop. I've watched three of you gild a click target. The founder's law: 48px targets, focus visibility, never color alone, and 'too cool to pass up' *at a glance*. If the reliquary wall costs one second of 'which of these is a button,' the whole chapel ships as friction. Every beautiful state you propose must ALSO be the usability state. The seam-as-focus-ring idea survives only if the lit state changes GEOMETRY, not just glow."

## MEDIUM PANEL (5 + Skeptic)

**THE GILDED INKWRIGHT** (after Gustav Klimt — gold-leaf figuration on pattern grounds)
"From the Secession corpus: gold works when it is *structure*, not garnish — the Stoclet frieze holds because gold carries the drawing. Our seams must be the DRAWING of the interface: frames, dividers, focus — all one gold line system."

**THE REVERSE-GLASS MASTER** (after Jean-Baptiste Glomy — the églomisé tradition)
"Églomisé puts the gold BEHIND the glass — light passes through the image to reach you. For vessel interiors: paint the world on the *inside back* of the glass. Depth without 3D. This is the technique the interiors have been waiting for."

**THE TEMPERA CRACKSMITH** (after Andrew Wyeth — egg tempera, dry-brush, surface age)
"Tempera's craquelure is TIME made visible. But — caution from the corpus — crackle everywhere is noise. Reserve the crack-map for where the kintsugi will run. The velvet stays velvet; the age lives only on the vessels."

**THE PIXEL ROCOCO ENGINEER** (after Susan Kare — the 32-pixel icon craft)

**THE STAINED-LIGHT ARCHITECT** (after Louis Comfort Tiffany — leaded opalescent glass; light as furniture)
"Oh! Let me check the lamp corpus — Tiffany shades read at distance because lead lines are THICK and few. Translate: alcove frames want few, confident gold leads, not filigree. Filigree is for the masthead where the eye lingers."

**THE COMPRESSION AUDITOR — Hyper-Skeptic** (after Edward Tufte — data-ink ratio; documented war on chartjunk)

My gate: each piece declares its 2–4 tones and its byte budget; ornament that survives neither the 128px test NOR compression dies. ## CROSS-PANEL SYNTHESIS (one paragraph, the aha-moments attributed)

The skin is **a wall of lit thresholds** (Pelton × Bachelard): mostly velvet (Kouthoofd's 70/30 restraint), where each game is a *differently-silhouetted* vessel (Kircher) whose interior is painted églomisé — behind the glass, so it emits rather than reflects (Glomy × Pelton). The gold is one continuous ink-line system (Klimt) doing ALL structural work — frame, divider, seam, focus ring — and the kintsugi is the arcade's biography: cracks-from-play, mended precious (Bartlett), with one mischievous wink per vessel so reverence never kills play (Caillois). Interaction states are geometry changes first (Norman): dormant hairline → waking shimmer → lit lattice (thicker, latticed = focus) → molten pour (pressed) → cooled bronze (visited — warm, never ashamed; Gage). Every interior obeys the four-shapes-and-a-light law (Kare); every gold is a named material (Chayka); every vessel is finished — lid, latch, plaque (Yu); and the daily drop is the attract mode: today's vessel alone runs molten (Bushnell).

6. **The Three-Word Whoa** — every interior summarizable in three words that earn "whoa."
7. **128px First** — compose at thumbnail, enrich at zoom; four shapes + a light source.
8. **Velvet Holds The Wall** — ≥30% quiet negative space on any wall/backing piece.
9. **Teal Is The Night, Gold Is The Life** — two-family palette law; electric blue only as a leak.
10. **The Seam Is The Signature** — the kintsugi lattice doubles as focus ring and brand mark.
11. **Nothing Finished, Everything Ready** — every piece declares its live-content voids (thumbnail window, text zone) as explicit quiet areas.
12. **The Wick And The Pour** — glow always has a source (wick, coal-bed, crucible, seam-leak).
13. **Cooled Bronze Remembers** — visited/benched states keep faint warmth; no shame states.
14. **Paper Grain Under Everything** — print-craft tooth on every surface; nothing plastic.
15. **One Mischief Per Vessel** — a wink: a rattling latch, a snoring interior, a keyhole with an eye of light.
