## Mood
Answered longing at civic scale: the one great forbidden journey passes the door of the woman who spent thirty years punching permission slips for six-stop journeys. Mid-eclipse holds the town's breath — noon gone silver, shadow bands crawling the cobbles — and into that held breath the sea's family arrives, unhurried, certain, at chimney height. Relief still checking the door: her body says yes before her face believes it, and her eyes are already following the pod toward the harbor she knows is dry. The splinter keeps the yes honest; the whales' calm keeps it kind.

## Story cue
An Erzgebirge-style terraced mining row, 1970s–80s East Germany: slate roofs, smoke-dark plaster, laundry lines strung wall to wall, tram rails in the cobbles — her route, her fares, her thirty years of stamped permissions. The town's harbor dried up a generation ago; the boats never left their keels. During the eclipse the gray whales come anyway, swimming the street's air as if the old water still stood there, trusting the way home to reopen. The migration needs no papers. Two neighbors' windows are lit; everyone else will only ever hear about it.
