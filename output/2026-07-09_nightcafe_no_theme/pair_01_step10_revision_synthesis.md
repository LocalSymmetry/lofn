```
A gum bichromate print with brush-coated borders holds field and sky in one closed aubergine envelope, values drawn near, so the long-demolished kitchen standing open in Kansas Turkey Red wheat carries the frame's single warm mass. No path leads to it; wheat presses unbroken against the doorsill. Its doorway pours humble candle gold in digital-oil impasto raised only there, the lit rooms receding doorway after doorway, deeper than the house is wide, each jamb found sharp while the dusk loses its edges. Halyna, 63, a Ukrainian-American farm woman in a man's canvas chore coat, stands at the pasture gate lower left, silver braid holding the day's last amber, her hand open just above the rail it released. Spilled flour dusts threshold and field grass, the one pearl note. The attic window stays dark. Open wheat rests at left under fresco crackle. Thirty years demolished, and supper is still warm.
```

### RANK 2 — V4 · The Stair and Attic — **150 words** — Flux 2 9B Fast · 4:5

```
A gum bichromate print, pigment brushed past its borders, sets the dusk — Turkey Red wheat, gable, crackled sky — in one deep aubergine key, so the climb of lit windows reads as a single golden gesture: doorway, hearth window, stair window rising in digital-oil impasto, above them the attic window alone dark, the one cool note the gold cannot answer. Through the stair glass, lit treads climb toward that unlit room and stop at its shadow. Halyna, 63, a silver-braided Ukrainian-American farm woman in a man's canvas chore coat, stands small at the lower-left gate, weight leaning home, face lifted three-quarter, her hand open just above the rail it released. Flour dusts threshold and field grass, a pearl seam beneath the gold. Wheat grows to the doorsill; no path. Open land rests along the bottom. Every room lit itself for her except one; she is looking at that one.
```

### RANK 3 — V2 · The Hearth-Room — **150 words** — Flux 2 9B Fast · 4:5

```
A gum bichromate print, raw brush strokes at its hand-coated borders, keeps the darkening Kansas wheat and sky as one quiet aubergine mass so the hearth-room of the demolished kitchen burns as the lone warm center, seen through the open doorway from the pasture gate. Digital-oil impasto thickens only inside: a black stove burning, a kettle at first steam, preserve jars, a second doorway opening on a third lit room the little house never had, each jamb crisp against the dusk's lost edges. Halyna, 63, a silver-braided Ukrainian-American woman in a man's canvas chore coat, stands lower left in the doorway's reach, face three-quarter to the light, her weathered hand hovering just off the top gate rail. Flour lies across the threshold, pearl dust on the field grass. The attic window above stays unlit. Wheat and crackled sky rest at left. The kettle went on before she reached the gate.
```

### RANK 4 — V3 · The Laid Table — **150 words** — Flux 2 9B Fast · 1:1

```
A gum bichromate print in aubergine and candle gold, hand-brushed emulsion borders visible, holds the evening field in one hushed near-valued mass and gives the standing kitchen's window and open door the only warmth, facing the viewer squarely. Inside, dense digital-oil impasto: an oilcloth table laid for two — loaf, butter dish, two enamel cups — one chair pulled out, faint steam curling off both cups, rooms receding door after door past where the outer wall stops, sharpest at that impossible depth. Halyna, 63, a Ukrainian-American farm woman in a man's canvas coat, silver braid over one shoulder, stands at the gate lower left, hand just lifted from the rail, knuckles still curved, face three-quarter. Flour spreads pearl across doorsill into field grass. Above the bright window, the attic stays dark. Low sun grazes the wheat heads; open field rests at left. No one has eaten here in thirty years.
```
