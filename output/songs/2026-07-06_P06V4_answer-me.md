# Answer Me
*(the one dug-for, rendered only as absence)*

## MUSIC PROMPT (Suno style — 966 chars)
The sparsest of the set: a hollowed lo-fi dream-plugg address braided with dark ambient and industrial-gospel, in D minor near 74 BPM, where the beloved exists only as absence. A low-passed cloud-808 root-fifth drone and a storefront angel-choir pad hang over close-mic hands-in-gravel, irregular and un-quantized; almost no lead, only a single gospel Rhodes note left ringing where a reply should be. Call-and-response is built with true silence, the response slots empty air, once a full one-beat hole. A female vocalist in her late twenties, extremely close-mic'd, half-sung and breaking with Cry-and-Slide cracks, addresses a you who never answers; reverb tails only the unanswered word. A detuned GameBoy lead appears once then cuts, like a voice that should reply and is gone. Disorienting, terror-adjacent tenderness, the pressure of earth over a body. A stained-glass shimmer blooms over the minor drone at the end, grace without relief. No climax, no build.

## Bold Choice
The call-and-response is built out of *true silence* — the response slots are literally empty air, and the empty slot IS the grief. Apophasis is simultaneously the craft and the human-subject safeguard: the buried person is never named or described, and the bridge turns the refusal-to-name into the last act of love — "I could give the world your name. I never, ever will." The safety constraint became the song.
